Selling an Inherited House in California: Decide Within a Year Whether Anyone Will Live There, Then Pick the Fastest Legal Path

Since Proposition 19, a California house left to the kids keeps the parent's low property-tax base only if a child makes it their own home within a year. Everyone else gets reassessed to today's value. That one rule turns "we'll figure it out later" into an expensive choice. Here's how to make it, and how California's newer $750,000 primary-residence petition, a living trust or full probate gets you to a sale. Or ask us a question, free.

A parent died and left a house in California, and most of the advice you've heard is about probate: how long it takes, how much it costs, whether you can avoid it. Those questions matter, and we'll get to them. But in California the first decision isn't about court. It's about taxes, and it has a deadline.

For decades, children who inherited a California house could keep a parent's Proposition 13 tax base, even if they rented the house out. Proposition 19, which took effect for transfers on or after February 16, 2021, narrowed that sharply. Today the low base survives only if a child actually lives there. That makes "keep it as a rental" and "keep it while we think" much more expensive, and it should shape every other step.

🏷️ The First Decision: Will an Heir Make It Their Home Within a Year?

Under Prop 19, a transfer of the family home from a parent to a child (or from a grandparent to a grandchild whose parent has died) is excluded from reassessment only if:

  • 🏡 It was the parent's principal residence, and
  • 🔑 The child makes it their own principal residence and files for the homeowners' or disabled veterans' exemption within one year of the transfer, and
  • 📏 The value fits under the cap. If the market value exceeds the parent's factored base year value plus an adjusted exclusion amount, the excess is added to the base. The State Board of Equalization set that amount at $1,044,586 for transfers from February 16, 2025, through February 15, 2027.

If no child moves in, the county assessor reassesses the house to its market value as of the date of death. On a house a parent bought decades ago, that can multiply the tax bill several times over, and the assessor will also send a supplemental bill covering the period since the death.

So the family needs an honest answer early: does anyone actually want to live in this house? If yes, that person files with the assessor and the family can take its time. If no, every month of holding the house is a month at the new, higher tax, and the economic case for selling sooner gets stronger. Many families also discover that "I'll move in" was a hope, not a plan, and switching to a sale later costs a year of carrying costs.

⚠️ The warning that matters most: renting an inherited California house to a tenant, or leaving it empty "until the market improves," usually means paying property tax on today's value from the date of death. Run that number before the family agrees to wait. If the heir who wants to keep it won't live there, a buyout or a sale is often the better deal for everyone.

🧾 The Assessor's Paperwork Has Its Own Clock

California asks for two filings that heirs often miss:

  1. Change in Ownership Statement (Death of Real Property Owner), form BOE-502-D. It's due to the county assessor within 150 days of the death, or, if the estate is probated, when the inventory and appraisal is filed. It tells the assessor who inherits.
  2. Claim for the parent-child exclusion, form BOE-19-P, if a child is moving in and wants to keep the base. Filing deadlines are tied to the transfer date and the supplemental or regular bill, so file as soon as the move-in is real.

Neither form transfers title. That still has to happen through a trust, a deed, a court petition or probate, which is the next question.

🏛️ Under $750,000: The Primary-Residence Petition Instead of Full Probate

For deaths on or after April 1, 2025, California expanded a shortcut that used to be useless for houses. Under Probate Code § 13151, heirs or beneficiaries can ask the superior court for an Order Determining Succession to the decedent's primary residence if its value is $750,000 or less, as appraised by a probate referee. Before that change, the limit for real property was $184,500 (deaths from April 2022 through March 2025), so almost no California house qualified.

How it works in practice:

  • 📅 The petition (Judicial Council form DE-310) can be filed at least 40 days after the death.
  • 🏠 It covers the decedent's primary residence, not rentals or commercial property.
  • 📣 The heirs and beneficiaries named in the petition get notice, and anyone can object.
  • ⚖️ A judge holds a hearing. If nobody disputes it and the requirements are met, the court issues an order that's recorded with the county recorder, and the successors own the house.
  • 🖋️ The successors can then sell like any owners, with all of them signing the deed.

Compared with full probate, this skips letters of administration, the inventory-and-accounting cycle and the statutory fees described below. It still involves a court filing fee, the probate referee's fee, and usually a lawyer, but it typically finishes in a few months instead of a year or more. It doesn't work if any successor will refuse to sign the deed later, since there's no personal representative to sign for the estate.

California's other small-estate tools are mostly not for houses. The $208,850 affidavit for personal property, for deaths on or after April 1, 2025, collects bank accounts and the like. A separate affidavit for real property of small value is capped at $69,625.

🧭 Before Any Court Filing, Check How the Title Was Held

Many California houses never need a court at all:

📁 A living trust

Most common in California

If the deed is in the name of a trust, the successor trustee can usually sell without court involvement, after the notices the Probate Code requires trustees to send heirs and beneficiaries. This is by far the smoothest path.

📑 A revocable transfer-on-death deed

Check the recorder

California allows TOD deeds under Probate Code § 5600 and following, and legislation passed in 2021 extended them to deeds executed before January 1, 2032. The deed must have been recorded before the death. The beneficiary records an affidavit of death, and the house passes outside probate, although heirs can challenge a TOD deed in some cases.

💍 Community property with a surviving spouse

Spousal petition

When the house passes to a surviving spouse, a spousal property petition or a recorded affidavit for community property with right of survivorship usually handles it without full probate. The children's turn comes at the second death.

⚖️ Over $750,000, or Not the Family Home: Full Probate

When none of the shortcuts fit, a personal representative is appointed by the probate division of the superior court in the county where the decedent lived. Two California rules shape the sale:

  • 🔓 Independent administration. If the court grants full authority under the Independent Administration of Estates Act, the personal representative can sell the house without court confirmation by sending heirs a Notice of Proposed Action at least 15 days before acting. Any heir who objects in writing can force the sale back into court.
  • 🔨 Court confirmation. Without full authority, or after an objection, the sale goes to a confirmation hearing where outside bidders can overbid in open court. The first overbid must be at least 10 percent of the first $10,000 of the accepted price plus 5 percent of the rest. A private sale generally has to be for at least 90 percent of the appraised value.

California also sets probate fees by statute, based on the gross value of the estate, not the equity. Under Probate Code § 10810, the personal representative and the attorney each may receive 4 percent of the first $100,000, 3 percent of the next $100,000, 2 percent of the next $800,000 and 1 percent of the next $9 million. On a $600,000 house with a $300,000 mortgage, the fees are calculated on $600,000: $15,000 for the personal representative and $15,000 for the attorney, unless someone waives.

🏡 Worked Example: That Sacramento House, Sold Two Ways

Mom died in June 2025. No trust, a simple will leaving the house to her two children, and the house is her primary residence. The probate referee appraises it at $540,000, so it qualifies for the § 13151 petition. Neither child wants to live there. Fully updated, it might sell for $600,000; it needs a roof, electrical panel and kitchen, about $55,000.

  • 📈 Petition, then list as-is: the order is recorded about four months after filing. Listed as-is, the house sells for around $520,000. After about 5 percent commission and 1.5 percent seller costs, the children net roughly $486,000, less five to seven months of reassessed carrying costs ($4,600 to $8,900) and the petition's costs. Probate's statutory fees, about $13,800 each to a personal representative and attorney on $540,000, never apply.
  • 💵 Petition, then sell for cash: at 60 to 80 percent of the $600,000 after-repair value, offers would range from $360,000 to $480,000. On this house we'd likely be near $430,000 to $455,000, closed in two to three weeks after the order, with no repairs or clean-out.

On these numbers, listing nets the family something like $30,000 to $50,000 more. Unless the house has problems a financed buyer can't get past, such as foundation movement, unpermitted additions or a tenant who won't leave, or the siblings need to close quickly, they should list.

🙋 Reasons to Skip a Cash Buyer in California

California's buyer pool is deep, and a decent house in most metro areas will draw financed offers. If the house is sound, the heirs agree and nobody needs speed, an agent will almost always beat us. If a child genuinely wants to live there, keeping the Prop 19 base may be worth more than any sale.

A cash sale makes sense when the house needs work lenders won't finance, when a reassessed tax bill is eating the estate, when heirs live out of state and can't manage a renovation, or when a court-confirmation sale needs a buyer willing to wait for the hearing and risk being overbid.

📋 What To Do This Week as a California Heir

  1. 🏠 Have the "will anyone live there?" conversation now, and write down the answer.
  2. 🔎 Pull the recorded deed: is it in a trust, a TOD deed, community property with right of survivorship, or the parent's name alone?
  3. 🧾 Calendar the BOE-502-D filing, due within 150 days of the death.
  4. 📏 If there's no trust and the house was the primary residence, get a sense of value: under $750,000 may mean the § 13151 petition instead of probate.
  5. 🔐 Secure the house, tell the insurer it's vacant, and keep the utilities on.
  6. 🗣️ If you open probate, ask the lawyer to request full authority under the Independent Administration of Estates Act.
  7. 👨‍👩‍👧 If siblings disagree about selling, read our guide to heirs who can't agree on a California house before anyone files.

✅ Bottom Line on a California Inheritance

In California, start with the tax question, not the court question. If an heir will truly live in the house, file with the assessor and keep the parent's base. If not, the house is likely headed for reassessment, and the sooner the family has someone with authority to sell, the less it costs. For a primary residence worth $750,000 or less, the § 13151 petition usually gets there faster and cheaper than probate. For bigger estates, ask for full independent-administration authority so the sale doesn't need a confirmation hearing.

Then list it if it's sound. If it isn't, or the family can't wait, ask us, and we'll tell you which path you're on before we talk price.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Do I have to pay higher property taxes on a house I inherit in California?

Usually, unless you move in. Under Proposition 19, a parent's family home keeps its Prop 13 base only if a child makes it their principal residence and files for the homeowners' or disabled veterans' exemption within one year. Otherwise the assessor reassesses it to market value as of the date of death.

How much of the parent's tax base can a child keep under Prop 19?

If the child moves in, the base carries over unless the market value exceeds the parent's factored base year value plus an adjusted exclusion amount. For transfers from February 16, 2025, through February 15, 2027, that amount is $1,044,586; any value above it is added to the base.

What is California's $750,000 petition for an inherited home?

For deaths on or after April 1, 2025, Probate Code § 13151 lets successors ask the superior court for an order determining succession to the decedent's primary residence if a probate referee values it at $750,000 or less. After a hearing, the recorded order transfers title without full probate.

How soon after a death can we file the California primary-residence petition?

At least 40 days after the death, using Judicial Council form DE-310. The heirs and beneficiaries named in the petition receive notice, a judge holds a hearing, and if no one disputes it and the requirements are met, the court issues an order that is recorded with the county.

Can a rental property use the $750,000 succession petition?

No. The expanded limit applies to the decedent's primary residence. Rentals and commercial property don't qualify for that limit, so they usually need full probate unless they were held in a trust, a transfer-on-death deed or another form that passes outside probate.

What form do heirs file with the California assessor after a death?

A Change in Ownership Statement (Death of Real Property Owner), form BOE-502-D, due within 150 days of the death or, in probate, when the inventory and appraisal is filed. A child keeping the parent's base also files the parent-child exclusion claim, form BOE-19-P.

How are probate fees calculated on a California house?

Under Probate Code § 10810, the personal representative and the attorney each may receive 4% of the first $100,000 of the estate, 3% of the next $100,000, 2% of the next $800,000 and 1% of the next $9 million. The base is gross value, so a mortgage doesn't reduce the fee.

Does a California executor need court approval to sell the house?

Not if the court granted full authority under the Independent Administration of Estates Act. The personal representative then sends heirs a Notice of Proposed Action at least 15 days before selling. If an heir objects, or authority is limited, the sale goes to a court confirmation hearing with open overbidding.

Does California still allow transfer-on-death deeds?

Yes. Revocable TOD deeds under Probate Code § 5600 and following remain available for deeds executed before January 1, 2032. The deed must be recorded before the owner's death, and the beneficiary then records an affidavit of death to show title has passed.

Is listing or a cash sale better for a house inherited in California?

If the house is sound and the heirs agree, listing usually nets more, often by tens of thousands of dollars. A cash sale makes more sense when the house needs repairs lenders won't finance, a reassessed tax bill is draining the estate, or the heirs need a fast, certain closing.
