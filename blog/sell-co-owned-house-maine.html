Selling a Co-Owned House in Maine: Read the Deed First, Because Maine Has No Tenancy by the Entirety and Assumes You Own in Common

Maine sorts co-owners with one short statute: a deed or will to two or more people creates a tenancy in common unless it plainly says joint tenancy or survivorship. Even "tenants by the entirety" wording only makes a joint tenancy here. What your deed says decides whether you can walk away with your share tomorrow, and what a Maine winter will cost you if you can't.

By Dustin Ray, Founder of Pages of Purpose LLC. Not legal advice — laws differ by state, and Maine statutes and court practice change. A Maine-licensed attorney, licensed where the property sits, should review your deed before you record, sign or file anything.

📜 One Maine Statute Decides What You Own: 33 M.R.S. §159

Before anything else, pull the deed. Maine's rule on co-ownership is short and it controls every option on this page. Under Title 33, section 159, a conveyance or devise of land to two or more people creates a tenancy in common unless the deed or will expresses that the owners take as joint tenants or with a right of survivorship. If the deed simply says "to Anne Smith and Robert Smith," Anne and Robert are tenants in common, each with a share they can sell, mortgage or leave by will.

The same statute handles the phrase married couples often see on deeds drafted elsewhere: a conveyance to people "as tenants by the entirety" creates a joint tenancy in Maine. Maine doesn't create entireties estates at all. That matters for married co-owners in two ways. There is no special shield that stops one spouse's creditors from reaching that spouse's interest, as there is in states like Maryland. And there is no rule that one spouse can't act alone — a married joint tenant in Maine has the same power to end survivorship as any other joint tenant.

⚠️ The most important warning on this page: do not assume you know how you hold title. "Joint owners," "co-owners" and "we're both on it" mean nothing legally. The exact words on the recorded deed at the county registry of deeds decide whether your share passes by survivorship, whether you can leave it to your kids, and how fast you can sell it.

✂️ Ending Survivorship Without Asking: The Self-Deed

If your deed does create a joint tenancy, you are not stuck with it. Section 159, as amended in 2011, lets a joint tenant convey to himself, or to himself and others, as tenants in common. That deed creates an estate in common and severs the joint tenancy. No court, no consent from the other owner.

Why would you do it? Because as long as the joint tenancy stands, your share disappears into the other owner's hands if you die first. A parent who added a child "for convenience" years ago, or two siblings who bought a house together, may decide they would rather each leave their own half to their own family. Severing doesn't sell anything; it just turns a survivorship interest into an ordinary share you can sell or devise. Talk to a Maine attorney before recording one — severing can have tax and estate-planning consequences, and it signals to the other owner that a fight may be coming.

🧍 Selling Just Your Share in Maine

A tenant in common can sell an undivided interest without the other owners' permission. The buyer steps into your shoes: he owns your fraction, shares the right to use the whole property, and can ask a court to partition if the remaining owners won't deal.

Maine's real estate transfer tax follows the share. Under 36 M.R.S. §4641-A the tax is $2.20 for each $500 of value, split evenly between the grantor and the grantee, and it is figured on the value of the property actually transferred, so a deed of a one-half interest is taxed on that half's value. A 2025 law added a higher rate on value above $1,000,000, which matters for shorefront and island property. Two exemptions in §4641-C are worth knowing: deeds between spouses, between parent and child, or between grandparent and grandchild with no consideration are exempt, and a partition deed among co-owners is exempt except for any value received beyond a party's proportional share. That second rule makes an agreed division of a family property far cheaper than a sale.

The honest problem with selling a fraction is price. Few buyers want half a house they can't live in. Investors who buy shares price in the cost and time of a partition case. Expect a meaningful discount below your share of the whole-house value; how much depends on how cooperative the other owner is likely to be.

🌲 Family Camps, Woodlots and the 30-Day Timber Notice

A lot of Maine co-ownership isn't a house in town. It is a lakefront camp three generations have shared, or a woodlot that came with the farmhouse. Maine has rules for these that people learn the hard way.

  • 🪓 Cutting timber. Under 14 M.R.S. §7505, a co-tenant who cuts timber or commits waste without first giving the other co-owners 30 days' written notice — or who does it while a partition case is pending — is liable for treble damages. The cousin who quietly sells a stand of pine to pay the camp's taxes can owe three times its value.
  • 🏚️ Improvements. If one co-owner had exclusive use of part of the land by agreement and built on it — a bunkhouse, a dock, a garage — Maine's partition statute (14 M.R.S. §6514) gives that part to the owner who improved it in a division and credits the value of the improvement.
  • 💸 Rent. Under 33 M.R.S. §953, a co-owner who collects more than his share of rents or income without the others' consent, and refuses to pay up after a demand, can be sued for the others' share. That covers summer rentals of a shared camp.
  • 🧾 Expenses. The Maine Supreme Judicial Court has held that a co-tenant who pays more than his share of taxes, mortgage or repairs is entitled to contribution, which can be offset by the value of his exclusive use (Palanza v. Lufkin, 2002 ME 143).

❄️ What a Shared Maine House Costs Through a Winter

Maine adds a cost most states don't: heat. An empty house can't simply be shut off for the winter in Bangor or Presque Isle; either someone keeps the furnace running or someone pays to winterize the plumbing. Here is a realistic monthly picture for a vacant $250,000 house in central Maine:

  • 🏛️ Property tax at a commonly cited effective rate of around 1.1–1.2% of value: about $230–$250 a month. Maine's homestead exemption — $25,000 of just value, adjusted by the town's assessment ratio — only applies to owners who live there as their permanent residence, and for a co-owned home it is split among the owners who do.
  • 🔥 Heating oil or propane to keep the house at 50–55°F from November to April: often $250–$500 a month in the cold months.
  • 🛡️ Vacant-dwelling insurance, usually more than a standard policy: $100–$200 a month.
  • ❄️ Plowing, roof-raking and spring cleanup: $75–$200.

That is $600 to $1,100 a month in winter and less in summer — roughly $7,000 to $11,000 a year. A frozen pipe in February can cost more than a year of all of it. If one co-owner is paying these bills alone while the other refuses to sell, document every payment; it supports a contribution claim later.

💵 A Lewiston Two-Family With Two Owners

Two brothers own a two-family in Lewiston as joint tenants. Repaired, it would sell for about $300,000; it needs roughly $40,000 in work, including a boiler. One brother lives in the first-floor unit and collects the rent from the second; the other lives out of state.

  • ✂️ Step one for the out-of-state brother: a self-deed under §159 converts his half to a tenancy in common, so it won't pass to his brother if he dies first.
  • 💸 The rent: if the brother in residence keeps all the second-floor rent after a written demand, §953 lets the other sue for his share.
  • 🏷️ Agreed listing: as-is, it might sell for $250,000, netting about $115,000 each after commissions and transfer tax.
  • 💵 Cash sale of the whole: cash buyers typically pay 60–80% of repaired value. At 70% of $300,000 that is $210,000, or $105,000 each, with no boiler replacement and no winter of showings.
  • 🧩 Selling only his half: the out-of-state brother can do it alone, at a discount, and be done in weeks.

If the brothers can agree, listing wins. Our offer makes sense when the boiler is gone, the tenant situation scares off retail buyers, or one brother has stopped responding.

✅ Bottom Line

In Maine, the deed decides everything. Without express survivorship language you are a tenant in common and can sell or leave your share as you like. With it, you can still sever on your own with a self-deed. There is no tenancy by the entirety to protect or trap married owners. The extra Maine risks are seasonal and rural: a house that freezes while the owners argue, and timber cut without the 30-day notice that triggers treble damages.

If everyone will sign and the house is sound, list it. If not — or if you simply want out of your share before another heating season — ask us, and we'll tell you honestly which route leaves you with the most.

❓ Maine Co-Owner Questions, Answered

Are co-owners in Maine joint tenants or tenants in common?

Under 33 M.R.S. section 159, a deed or will to two or more people creates a tenancy in common unless it expressly says the owners hold as joint tenants or with a right of survivorship.

Does Maine recognize tenancy by the entirety?

No. Under section 159, a deed to people as tenants by the entirety creates a joint tenancy in Maine, so married co-owners get no separate entireties protection.

Can one joint tenant in Maine end the right of survivorship alone?

Yes. Section 159 lets a joint tenant deed the interest to himself, or to himself and others, as tenants in common. That creates an estate in common and severs the joint tenancy without the other owner's consent.

Can I sell my share of a Maine house without my co-owner's permission?

A tenant in common can sell an undivided share without the other owners' consent. Selling the whole house requires every owner's signature, unless a court orders a sale in a partition case.

How much is the Maine transfer tax when a co-owner sells a share?

The Maine real estate transfer tax is $2.20 per $500 of value, split evenly between grantor and grantee, figured on the value of the interest actually transferred. A 2025 law added a higher rate on value over $1 million.

Is a partition deed between Maine co-owners subject to transfer tax?

A partition deed among co-owners given without consideration is exempt under 36 M.R.S. section 4641-C, except for any value a party receives beyond his or her proportional share.

Can a co-owner cut timber on shared Maine land?

Under 14 M.R.S. section 7505, a co-tenant who cuts timber or commits waste without giving the other co-owners 30 days' written notice, or while a partition case is pending, is liable for treble damages.

Does my Maine co-owner owe me rent for living in the house?

Maine law lets a co-owner who collects more than his share of rents or income from third parties, and refuses after demand, be sued for the others' share under 33 M.R.S. section 953. Whether an owner who simply lives there owes the others rent depends on the facts, so ask a Maine attorney.

Does the Maine homestead exemption apply to a co-owned house?

The $25,000 homestead exemption, adjusted by the town's assessment ratio, applies only to owners who live in the house as their permanent Maine residence and have owned a Maine homestead for the prior 12 months. For a co-owned home it is divided among the owners who live there.

Should I list my co-owned Maine house or sell to a cash buyer?

If every owner will sign and the house is in good condition, listing usually nets more. A cash sale fits better when the house needs major work, sits vacant through the winter, or a co-owner won't cooperate.
