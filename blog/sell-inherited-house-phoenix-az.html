Inherited a Phoenix House? Arizona's New $300,000 Affidavit May Let You Skip Probate

Arizona tripled its small-estate limit for real estate in 2025. For affidavits filed on or after September 26, 2025, heirs can transfer a house with a net value of up to $300,000, measured on the county's full cash value minus liens, without opening a probate case, once six months have passed since the death. Add Arizona's widely used beneficiary deeds and an informal probate system that rarely needs a judge, and the paperwork is seldom what holds up a Phoenix inheritance. The house's condition, a Phoenix summer and the current market usually are.

By Dustin Ray, Founder of Pages of Purpose LLC. Not legal advice. Arizona probate law and Maricopa County practice change, laws differ by state, and every estate is different. Talk to an attorney licensed in Arizona, where the property is, before you sign or record anything.

When someone inherits a house, the first question is usually "how long will probate take?" In Phoenix, the better first question is "do we need probate at all?" Arizona gives heirs more ways to avoid a court case than most states, and the 2025 change to the small-estate affidavit for real property moved a large share of ordinary Phoenix houses into that category.

This guide follows the order the decisions actually happen in: whether the house can transfer without probate, what probate looks like in Maricopa County when you do need it, how community property affects a surviving spouse, what it costs to hold the house through an Arizona summer, and when listing beats selling for cash.

📝 Arizona's $300,000 Affidavit Can Replace Probate for Many Phoenix Houses

A.R.S. 14-3971 lets heirs transfer Arizona real property by affidavit, without appointing a personal representative, when the estate qualifies. House Bill 2116, signed on March 31, 2025, raised the real property limit from $100,000 to $300,000 for affidavits filed on or after September 26, 2025. The main requirements:

  • At least six months since the death. The affidavit can't be filed sooner.
  • Net value of $300,000 or less. Value isn't the market price. It's the full cash value on the Maricopa County Assessor's rolls for the year of death, minus liens and encumbrances (such as the mortgage balance) as of the date of death.
  • No personal representative appointed or application pending, and the heirs filing are the people entitled to the property under the will or Arizona's intestacy law.
  • Debts and expenses accounted for. The affidavit includes statements about funeral expenses and unsecured debts; those responsibilities don't disappear because there's no probate.

The affidavit is presented to the court for certification and then recorded with the Maricopa County Recorder. Once recorded, the heirs hold title and can sell like any other owners.

Why this matters so much in Phoenix: full cash values on the assessor's rolls tend to run below market prices. A house that would sell for $420,000 might carry a full cash value around $330,000. If there's a $60,000 mortgage, the net is $270,000 and the house may qualify. Before the 2025 change, that house would have needed probate.

Check the dates before you rely on it. The $300,000 limit is tied to when the affidavit is filed (on or after September 26, 2025), and the six-month waiting period still runs from the date of death. Value is measured from the assessor's full cash value for the year of death, not from a Zillow estimate or an appraisal. Have an Arizona probate attorney confirm the numbers before anyone signs.

🔎 Search the Recorder for a Beneficiary Deed First

Arizona was one of the early states to allow beneficiary deeds (A.R.S. 33-405), and they're common in the Phoenix area, especially among retirees. A beneficiary deed lets an owner name who gets the house at death while keeping full ownership during life. It must have been recorded before the owner died to work.

If one exists, the house passes to the named beneficiary outside probate. The beneficiary typically records a death certificate and an affidavit to confirm the transfer, and can then sell. There's no six-month wait and no value cap. Search the owner's name on the Maricopa County Recorder's website; families often find one their parent never mentioned.

Also look for a living trust. A house deeded to a trust passes by the trust's terms, and the successor trustee can sell without any court involvement.

🏛️ When You Do Need Probate: Informal Administration in Maricopa County

If there's no beneficiary deed, no trust, and the house's net value is over $300,000 (or other assets require it), the next step is usually informal probate in the Maricopa County Superior Court's probate department. Informal probate is handled largely on paper through the court's registrar rather than at a contested hearing.

  • Appointment. An application for informal probate and appointment of a personal representative is filed with the will (if any). The filing fee is roughly $300. Letters of appointment often issue within a few weeks in an uncontested case.
  • Power to sell. In an unsupervised administration, an Arizona personal representative can generally sell real estate without separate court approval of the sale, as long as they act in the estate's interest.
  • Creditor notice. The personal representative publishes notice to creditors; claims are generally barred if not presented within four months after first publication. That sets the minimum time before the estate can close, but it doesn't stop a sale.
  • Timeline and cost. A simple informal probate typically runs six to nine months to closing, and many Phoenix probate attorneys handle uncontested informal estates for $2,500-$5,000. Formal probate (when a will is contested or heirs dispute who should serve) takes longer and costs more.

💍 Community Property and the Surviving Spouse

Arizona is a community property state, which matters in two ways for an inherited Phoenix house.

Who inherits without a will

Under A.R.S. 14-2102, a surviving spouse inherits the entire intestate estate when all of the deceased's children are also the spouse's children. If the deceased had children from another relationship, the spouse takes no part of the deceased's half of the community property (it goes to the children) and one-half of the deceased's separate property. Blended families are where Phoenix inheritances get complicated: a stepparent and adult stepchildren can end up co-owning the house.

The double step-up

For federal income tax, inherited property generally gets a "stepped-up" basis equal to its value at death. In community property states, a surviving spouse can get that step-up on both halves of community property, not just the deceased spouse's half. A widow or widower who sells a long-held Phoenix house soon after the death may owe little or no capital gains tax. Ask a CPA to confirm before you sell.

🧾 Your Property Tax Bill Won't Reset, but One Credit Ends

Unlike Florida, California or Michigan, Arizona doesn't reassess a home just because it changed hands. Taxes are based on the limited property value, which can rise no more than 5% a year under Proposition 117, and a change of ownership isn't one of the events that resets it. Heirs keep the same capped value the owner had.

Two things can still change:

  • Primary residence status. If no owner lives in the house, it's no longer classified as an owner-occupied primary residence, and the state homeowner's rebate on school taxes (worth up to $600 a year) stops. If the house is rented out, it's classified as a rental.
  • Personal exemptions end. Senior valuation protection and the widow, widower or disability exemptions belong to the person, not the house. An heir has to qualify independently.

Phoenix property taxes are low by national standards. A typical house worth $400,000 on the market might pay $2,000-$3,000 a year, often roughly 0.5-0.7% of market value. Taxes are rarely what makes an inherited Phoenix house expensive to hold.

☀️ What a Vacant House Costs Through a Phoenix Summer

Heat is. A closed-up house in Phoenix in July can reach temperatures that damage flooring, cabinets, electronics and anything stored inside. Most insurers and property managers expect the air conditioning to keep running, typically set in the mid-80s.

  • Electricity: $200-$400 a month in summer from APS or SRP just to keep a vacant house cooled, less in winter.
  • Pool: $120-$200 a month for service. A neglected pool turns green within weeks and can draw city code complaints.
  • Insurance: a vacant-home policy commonly runs $100-$200 a month; a standard homeowner policy may limit coverage after 30-60 days of vacancy.
  • HOA dues: $50-$250 a month in many Phoenix-area subdivisions. Arizona HOAs can place liens for unpaid assessments.
  • Yard, pest control and checks: $100-$200 a month, more for desert landscaping that's been left to overgrow.
  • Property tax: about $170-$250 a month on a typical house.

That's around $750-$1,400 a month, and the biggest risk isn't on the list: an AC unit that dies in July with nobody checking the house.

🔆 Solar Leases, HOAs and Other Phoenix Title Snags

Several issues come up on inherited Phoenix houses far more often than in other markets:

  • Leased or financed solar. Many Phoenix houses have rooftop solar under a lease, power purchase agreement or loan. A buyer has to assume the lease or the heirs have to pay it off, and the solar company has to approve the transfer. Some lenders and buyers balk. Pull the solar contract early.
  • Unpermitted additions. Enclosed patios, garage conversions and casitas built without permits are common in older Phoenix neighborhoods and can complicate financing and appraisals.
  • Aging AC and roofs. A 15-year-old AC unit is near the end of its life in this climate, and foam or tile roofs often need underlayment work. Buyers' inspectors will find both.
  • HOA transfer and disclosure fees. Arizona HOAs charge disclosure and transfer fees at sale; budget a few hundred dollars.

⚖️ Listing vs. Cash in the Current Phoenix Market

🔀 Four Ways Forward

List it on the MLS

Best for updated houses

If the house has a newer AC unit and roof, the heirs agree, and nobody needs cash in the next two months, list it. Arizona has no state real estate transfer tax, so beyond commission, closing costs are modest. On a sound house, this nets more than any cash offer, including ours.

Fix it up, then list

Only with money, time and agreement

Replacing an AC unit ($9,000-$15,000), flooring and paint can add more than it costs, but only if one heir is willing to manage contractors in the heat and the others agree to fund it. Many families start this and stall.

Sell as-is for cash

Best when condition or people are the problem

A cash sale makes sense when the house needs work nobody wants to fund, when a solar lease or unpermitted addition scares off financed buyers, when heirs live out of state, or when someone needs their share quickly.

One heir buys out the others

Good if someone wants it

An heir who wants to live in or rent the house can buy out the others at an agreed value. If heirs can't agree on anything, see our guide on what to do when heirs can't agree, and on how partition lawsuits work as a last resort.

💵 What a Cash Offer Looks Like on a Maryvale House

Cash buyers generally pay around 60-80% of after-repair value, depending on the work needed and the complexity of the title. A realistic example:

A 1972 three-bedroom block house in Maryvale. Updated houses nearby sell for about $350,000. This one needs a new AC unit, roof underlayment, flooring, a kitchen refresh and pool resurfacing: about $55,000. It has a leased solar system with eight years left.

  • Cash offer: roughly $220,000-$250,000 (about 63-71% of repaired value), closing in two to three weeks once the heirs have title, with the solar lease handled at closing.
  • Listing as-is: maybe $285,000-$300,000 if a buyer will take on the solar lease, minus about 5-6% commission ($14,000-$18,000), title and escrow costs, and three to four months of carrying costs ($3,000-$5,000). Net around $260,000-$275,000.
  • Fix and list: perhaps $345,000, minus $55,000 in repairs, commission and five to six months of carrying costs. Net around $260,000-$270,000, with more risk and work.

On this house, listing as-is likely nets $20,000-$40,000 more than a cash sale if the heirs can wait and the solar lease transfers smoothly. The cash route is worth that gap only if speed, distance, or the solar and repair issues make a listing impractical. On a house in good shape, listing wins by more.

  3. Note the date of death and the date six months later, when an affidavit could first be filed.

✅ Bottom Line

In Phoenix, the legal path for an inherited house is often shorter than families expect. Check first for a beneficiary deed or trust. If there isn't one, Arizona's 2025 law lets heirs transfer a house worth up to $300,000 in net assessed value by affidavit after six months, and informal probate covers most of the rest without a judge. The property tax bill won't reset the way it would in many states.

That leaves the real decision: condition and timing. If the house is updated and the heirs agree, list it. If the AC is failing, a solar lease complicates the sale, or the family is scattered or in conflict, ask us. We'll tell you honestly which path nets more for your house.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What is the small estate limit for real property in Arizona?

For affidavits filed on or after September 26, 2025, A.R.S. 14-3971 allows heirs to transfer Arizona real property by affidavit when its net value is $300,000 or less. Before that change, the limit was $100,000. At least six months must have passed since the death.

How is value measured for the Arizona real property affidavit?

It uses the full cash value shown on the county assessor's rolls for the year of death, minus liens and encumbrances such as the mortgage balance at the date of death. Market value, appraisals and online estimates do not count.

What is a beneficiary deed in Arizona?

A deed under A.R.S. 33-405 that names who receives the property at the owner's death while the owner keeps full ownership during life. It must be recorded before death. The beneficiary takes title outside probate, with no six-month wait or value limit.

How long does informal probate take in Maricopa County?

A simple, uncontested informal probate typically takes six to nine months to close. Letters of appointment often issue within a few weeks, and creditors have four months after first publication of notice to present claims. A personal representative in an unsupervised estate can usually sell the house before the estate closes.

Can a personal representative sell a house in Arizona without court approval?

In an unsupervised administration, generally yes. An Arizona personal representative can sell estate real estate without a separate court order approving the sale, as long as they act in the estate's best interest.

Who inherits a Phoenix house if there's no will and a surviving spouse?

Under A.R.S. 14-2102, the spouse inherits everything if all of the deceased's children are also the spouse's children. If the deceased had children from another relationship, those children take the deceased's half of the community property, and the spouse takes one-half of the deceased's separate property.

Will the property tax bill rise after an Arizona inheritance?

Generally not because of the inheritance itself. Arizona taxes homes on a limited property value that can rise no more than 5% a year, and a change of ownership does not reset it. If no owner lives in the house, though, the homeowner's rebate on school taxes ends, and personal exemptions held by the deceased do not transfer.

What is the double step-up in basis for Arizona community property?

For federal income tax, a surviving spouse can receive a stepped-up basis on both halves of community property at the first spouse's death, not just the deceased spouse's half. That can sharply reduce capital gains tax if the spouse sells. Confirm with a CPA.

Does a leased solar system make it harder to sell an inherited Phoenix house?

It can. The buyer must assume the lease or the heirs must pay it off, and the solar company must approve the transfer. Some financed buyers and lenders are wary of solar leases, so get the contract early and ask the company about its transfer process.

Is listing or a cash sale the better move for an inherited Phoenix house?

If the house is updated, the AC and roof are in good shape and the heirs agree, listing usually nets more. A cash sale makes sense when the house needs major work, a solar lease or unpermitted addition complicates financing, the heirs live far away or disagree, or someone needs their share quickly.
