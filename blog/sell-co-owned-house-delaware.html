Selling a Co-Owned House in Delaware: The Court of Chancery, a Court-Appointed Trustee, and Your Half of a 4 Percent Transfer Tax

When co-owners in Delaware can't agree, the case doesn't go to an ordinary trial court. It goes to the Court of Chancery, the same equity court known for corporate law, which appoints a trustee to sell the house and then sorts out who paid what. Each side usually pays its own lawyer, and the seller's half of Delaware's realty transfer tax comes off the top. Here's how that shapes your options as a co-owner. Or ask us a question, free.

You're on the deed to a Delaware house with someone who won't sell, won't buy you out, or won't respond. It could be an ex-partner, a brother, or a group of cousins who inherited it together. Before you weigh a buyout against a lawsuit or a sale of your share, it helps to know where a Delaware co-ownership fight actually ends up, because that shapes every negotiation that comes before it.

🏛️ Delaware Sends Co-Owner Disputes to the Court of Chancery

In most states, a partition lawsuit goes to the general trial court. In Delaware, it goes to the Court of Chancery. Under 25 Del. C. § 721, any one or more joint tenants, tenants in common or parceners can petition the Court of Chancery for partition. Chancery is a court of equity with no juries, and it decides these cases on written submissions and short hearings.

The statute still reads like the 1800s. Under § 724, once the court decrees that partition will be made, it can issue a commission to three freeholders of the county, who go to the property and divide it fairly. If they decide a division would hurt the owners, they make no division and instead appraise the whole property at its true value in money. Under § 729, if no division is made, the court orders the property sold by a trustee it appoints, and the statute's default is a public sale to the highest bidder, with the court approving the sale before the trustee signs a deed. Section 735 says the costs of the proceeding are taxed by the court and paid from the sale proceeds or by the parties according to their interests.

In practice, for a single house, Chancery often skips straight to a sale and lets its trustee use a more modern process. That's exactly what happened in a recent New Castle case.

📅 One Real Case, Start to Finish: A New Castle House

In re Partition of Real Estate of Davis (Del. Ch. 2023) is a useful picture of a typical Delaware co-owner case because it's so ordinary. Two former partners bought a house together in New Castle in 2004. One moved out for good in 2015. Years later he filed a partition petition without a lawyer. Here's how the timeline ran:

DateWhat happened
December 2021Partition petition filed
March 2022Initial hearing
May 2022Court finds physical division impractical and orders a sale
August 2022Court-appointed trustee asks approval of a private sale for $180,000; court approves it
October 2022Sale closes
November 2022Court approves the return of sale
April 2023Evidentiary hearing on how to split the money
July 2023Final decision on each owner's share

Two lessons stand out. The house itself was sold within about ten months of filing, through a trustee's private sale the court approved rather than a courthouse auction. And the fight over the money took another nine months after closing. That second phase, the accounting, is where most of a Delaware co-owner's time and legal fees go.

🧮 How Chancery Splits the Check

In Davis, the Vice Chancellor adjusted each owner's share for what they had actually paid. Each owner's mortgage principal payments were compared, with the higher payer credited for half the difference. The owner who had paid the property taxes and insurance, about $18,900 in all, was credited for half of it. And the owner who made repairs and improvements valued at $12,500 got half of that value added to her side.

Those numbers show how Delaware's equity court thinks about co-ownership: shared expenses are shared, and whoever carried more than their half gets reimbursed for the other owner's half. If you've been paying alone, your receipts are worth real money. If you've been the one not paying, expect your share to shrink.

💼 Each Side Usually Pays Its Own Lawyer

Some states make all owners share the filing lawyer's fees in a partition case. Delaware's Court of Chancery starts from the American Rule: each side pays its own attorney. In Davis, the court noted two exceptions, bad faith litigation conduct and the common benefit doctrine, found neither applied, and left each party to bear its own fees and costs.

That has two consequences. A co-owner who drags out the accounting doesn't automatically shift their lawyer's bill to you. But neither can you count on the house paying for your lawyer. A contested Delaware partition commonly costs each side several thousand dollars at the low end and $15,000 to $30,000 or more when every credit is fought over. Run that against the dollars actually in dispute before you spend them.

📄 What Your Deed Says About Your Rights

Delaware's default rule favors tenancy in common. Under 25 Del. C. § 701, a deed or will to two or more people, other than executors or trustees, doesn't create a joint tenancy unless it expressly grants the property to be held "as joint tenants and not as tenants in common." Married couples are the major exception, because Delaware recognizes tenancy by the entirety, the form of ownership that treats spouses as one owner.

If you're married and divorcing, the house is handled by the Family Court, not by a Chancery partition case. Under 13 Del. C. § 1513(c), property acquired during the marriage is presumed marital no matter whose name is on the deed or which form of co-ownership it's in. 🏷️ Selling Just Your Share, Without a Lawsuit

A Delaware tenant in common, or a joint tenant, can sell their undivided interest without the other owner's signature. The buyer steps into the seller's position, including the right to petition Chancery. People choose this when:

  • ⏱️ They need money in weeks, not the 18 months a petition plus accounting can take.
  • 🧘 They don't want to sit across from an ex or a sibling at an evidentiary hearing.
  • 💸 They've stopped paying their share and know the accounting will cut against them, so a clean exit is worth more than a fight.
  • 📍 They live out of state and can't manage a Delaware court case.

The trade-off is price. A buyer of a share is buying the accounting and the possible Chancery case, so the offer will be well below your fraction of market value. Get more than one written offer. Our guide on whether a co-owner can sell a house explains how share buyers price that risk.

🧾 Delaware's Realty Transfer Tax: Half of 4 Percent Comes From You

Delaware has one of the highest realty transfer taxes in the country, and it applies to sales of a share just as it does to the whole house. Under 30 Del. C. § 5402, the state rate is 2.5 percent where the county or town has adopted the full 1.5 percent local tax, which most have, for 4 percent in total. The statute splits the tax equally between seller and buyer, so the seller's half is typically 2 percent of the price.

On a $280,000 house, that's $11,200 in total and $5,600 from the sellers. On a $90,000 sale of a half interest, the seller's share is about $1,800. Factor it into every comparison, including a buyout between co-owners, and ask the settlement attorney whether any exemption applies to your particular transfer.

💵 A Dover House Owned 50/50, by the Numbers

Two siblings inherited a three-bedroom house in Dover. Fixed up, it would sell for about $310,000; as-is, about $280,000. Neither lives there.

  • 🧾 Property tax. Delaware's effective property tax rate on homes is about 0.55 percent, one of the lowest in the country, according to the Tax Foundation. On $280,000, that's around $1,540 a year, or $128 a month. The counties' recent reassessments have moved individual bills, so check the actual one.
  • 🛡️ Vacant-home insurance: about $100 to $200 a month.
  • 🔌 Utilities, lawn and upkeep: about $120 to $250 a month.

Holding the empty house costs the siblings roughly $350 to $580 a month, or $4,200 to $7,000 a year.

  • 📈 Listed together as-is: $280,000 less about 6 percent commission and the sellers' 2 percent transfer tax leaves about $257,600, or $128,800 each before other closing costs.
  • 💵 Sold together for cash: at 60 to 80 percent of the $310,000 repaired value, $186,000 to $248,000. On this house we'd likely be around $215,000 to $230,000, closed in a few weeks, before the sellers' transfer tax.
  • ⚖️ Through Chancery: a trustee's sale near $270,000 after a year, less the trustee's costs, the sellers' transfer tax and each sibling's own lawyer, plus a year of holding costs.
  • 🧩 One sibling's half, alone: likely $75,000 to $100,000.

📋 What To Do This Week as a Delaware Co-Owner

  1. 📄 Get the recorded deed from the county Recorder of Deeds and read exactly how the owners are named.
  2. 🧾 Total every mortgage, tax, insurance and repair payment each owner has made, with proof. Chancery's accounting runs on these numbers.
  3. 🔎 Order an appraisal or broker opinion of value.
  4. ✉️ Send the other owner a written proposal: list together, a buyout at the appraised value with credits, or you'll consider selling your interest or petitioning.
  5. 💍 If you're married to the co-owner, talk to a family lawyer before anything else.
  6. ⚖️ If you're leaning toward Chancery, ask a Delaware lawyer for a realistic fee estimate for both the sale phase and the accounting.

✅ Bottom Line

In Delaware, a co-owner can sell their own interest without permission, and any tenant in common or joint tenant can petition the Court of Chancery to force a sale. Chancery usually orders a single house sold by a court-appointed trustee, then decides the split based on who actually paid what, and each side normally pays its own lawyer. The seller's half of the 4 percent transfer tax comes out either way. Those facts make a negotiated listing or buyout the best result for most co-owners, and a sale of your share the fastest exit when the other owner won't move.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Which court handles partition of a house in Delaware?

The Court of Chancery. Under 25 Del. C. 721, any joint tenant, tenant in common or parcener can petition the Court of Chancery for partition. Chancery is an equity court with no juries.

Does a Delaware partition sale have to be a public auction?

The statute's default under 25 Del. C. 729 is a public sale by a court-appointed trustee to the highest bidder. In practice Chancery has approved private sales by its trustee, as in In re Partition of Real Estate of Davis (2023), where a $180,000 private sale was approved.

How long does a partition case take in Delaware?

In the 2023 Davis case, the petition was filed in December 2021, a sale was ordered in May 2022 and the house closed in October 2022. The final split of the money wasn't decided until July 2023.

Who pays the lawyers in a Delaware partition case?

Usually each side pays its own, under the American Rule. Chancery can depart from that for bad faith litigation or under the common benefit doctrine, but in Davis it found neither applied.

How does Chancery adjust each owner's share in Delaware?

By who actually paid. Owners who paid more than their half of mortgage principal, taxes and insurance are credited for the excess, and improvements are credited by their value. Keep records of every payment.

Does a Delaware deed to two people create a joint tenancy?

Not unless it says so expressly. Under 25 Del. C. 701, a conveyance to two or more people creates a joint tenancy only if it grants the property to be held as joint tenants and not as tenants in common.

Can I partition a house with my spouse in Delaware?

Not while you're married if you own as tenants by the entirety. The Family Court divides the house in a divorce, and 13 Del. C. 1513(c) presumes property acquired during the marriage is marital regardless of how title is held.

How much is the realty transfer tax when selling a share in Delaware?

Usually 4 percent in total, split equally between seller and buyer under 30 Del. C. 5402, so the seller typically pays 2 percent. On a $90,000 sale of a half interest, that's about $1,800 from the seller.

Can I sell my half of a Delaware house without the other owner?

Yes, if you're a tenant in common or joint tenant. The buyer becomes a co-owner and can petition Chancery. Because the buyer takes on the dispute, offers come in well below your fraction of market value.

Should Delaware co-owners list the house or sell it for cash?

If both owners will sign and the house is in good shape, listing usually nets the most. A cash sale fits a house that needs work, an owner who won't cooperate, or owners who'd rather not spend a year in Chancery.
