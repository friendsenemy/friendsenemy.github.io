Partition Lawsuits in Georgia: A 20-Day Notice, Five Freeholders, and a Jury When Owners Fight Over Who Owns What

Georgia's partition statute is older and more procedural than most. Before anyone files, the other owners get written notice. Five local landowners can be sent to divide the land. And when owners disagree about the shares themselves, an objecting owner can have that question tried by a jury. For many Georgia families, the real fight isn't whether to sell. It's who owns how much. Here's how a Georgia partition case actually unfolds, and where it tends to go wrong. Or ask us a question, free.

Most people picture a partition case as a lawsuit that ends with a forced sale. In Georgia it's better to picture a sequence with several checkpoints: a notice letter, a hearing on title, a visit from court-appointed partitioners, possible objections tried to a jury, and only then a division, a buyout or a sale. Each checkpoint is a place where a co-owner can slow things down, and a place where a careful owner can settle on good terms.

📬 In Georgia, Partition Starts With a 20-Day Letter

Under O.C.G.A. § 44-6-162, the owner applying for a writ of partition must give the other owners at least 20 days' notice of the intention to apply. If another owner is a minor, has a guardian, or holds through a trust, the notice goes to the guardian or trustee. If an owner lives outside Georgia, the court can order service by publication.

The application itself, under § 44-6-160, goes to the superior court of the county where the land sits. It must describe the property and define each owner's share and interest. Any one common owner can apply, whether they got their interest by inheritance, purchase or some other way.

That notice period matters in practice. It's a 20-day warning that the other side is ready to file, and it's often the last cheap moment to negotiate. Once the court is examining title and appointing partitioners, every step costs money and the shares described in the petition become the battleground.

⚠️ Get the shares right before you send the notice. The petition has to state each owner's share. If you guess wrong about who inherited what, or overlook a relative who holds a fraction, you invite an objection, a jury trial on title, or a judgment that a missed owner can later try to set aside.

🧑‍🌾 Five Freeholders Walk the Land

Once the notice has been given, § 44-6-163 directs the court to examine the petitioner's title and share and then have the superior court clerk issue a writ of partition directed to five freeholders of the county, who serve as partitioners. That's more than most states use; many rely on three commissioners or a single referee.

Section 44-6-164 sets out how they work. The partitioners can hire a surveyor. They must give all parties, if possible, at least eight days' notice of when they'll execute the writ. They take an oath, and then the partitioners, or a majority of them, divide the land in entire tracts or parcels as they judge most beneficial to the owners and in proportion to their shares. They have three months from the writ's issuance to return it to the superior court.

For farmland, timber tracts and large rural parcels, which Georgia has plenty of, this is a real division process, and a family that wants to keep land in pieces can use it. For a single house in Macon or a townhome in Gwinnett, it usually becomes the step where everyone confirms the house can't be fairly split, and the case moves to the appraisal, buyout and sale procedure.

🧑‍⚖️ Disputing Who Owns What? Georgia Lets a Jury Decide

This is the feature of Georgia partition that surprises people most. Under § 44-6-165, an owner against whom partition is sought can file objections to the applicant's right, to the writ, or to the partitioners' return. They can raise "any good and probable matter in bar" of the partition, or show that the petitioner doesn't own as much as claimed or any of it. When they do, "the issue shall be tried by a jury."

That means the hardest disputes in Georgia partition cases are often title disputes, not valuation disputes. Common examples:

  • 📜 An heir claims a deed from the late parent gave them the whole house, and the others say the deed was never delivered.
  • 🧾 A relative who paid the taxes for 20 years claims to own more than an equal share.
  • 👪 Children from two marriages disagree about who counts as an heir.
  • 🖊️ Someone produces a handwritten agreement about who gets the house.

If no one objects, or a jury rejects the objections, § 44-6-166 makes the partitioners' return the judgment of the court, final and conclusive as to every party who was properly notified. If the objections are sustained, or the court sees injustice or inequality in the division, it orders a new partition, which then becomes "firm, good, and conclusive forever" against the notified parties.

A jury trial adds months and real legal cost. If you expect your relatives to dispute the shares, it's often worth settling the title question first, through a quiet title action or a written agreement among the heirs, before anyone applies for partition.

📜 When a Will or an Agreement Takes Partition Off the Table

Georgia's statutory partition applies when common owners hold land "and no provision is made, by will or otherwise, as to how such lands and tenements shall be divided." That clause has teeth. If the will that left you the house says how it's to be divided, or the owners signed an agreement covering division or sale, the statutory writ may not be available, and the owners may have to follow what the will or agreement says.

Georgia also gives judges a safety valve. Section 44-6-170 lets the court frame its own proceeding in an extraordinary case the statute doesn't cover, and to "deny a sale or partition altogether if it is manifest that the interest of each party will not be fully protected." Courts don't use it casually, but it means a Georgia partition isn't automatic. If a sale would leave one owner badly treated, that owner has an argument.

🏚️ A House That Can't Be Split: Appraisal, Buyout, Then a Courthouse Sale

When the property can't be divided fairly by metes and bounds, Georgia uses a distinct procedure in § 44-6-166.1: three appraisers set the price, owners can switch sides within a short window, and the owners who didn't ask for the sale get a fixed period to pay the others their share of that price. If they don't, § 44-6-167 sends the property to a public sale by court-appointed commissioners, held at the county's place of public sales.

We walk through that day-by-day clock in our guide to selling a co-owned house in Georgia. For this page, the takeaway is the money. Under § 44-6-168, the commissioners return the proceeds to the court, which divides them among the owners in proportion to their interests after deducting the expenses of the proceedings. Commissioners who fail to pay the money into court can be held in contempt. A courthouse sale also tends to draw investors rather than families, which usually means a lower price than a listing.

👪 Family Land Gets a Different Track

Georgia was one of the early adopters of the Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act, codified at §§ 44-6-180 through 44-6-189.1. When inherited property qualifies as heirs property, the court must apply that act's process instead: a court-determined value, a buyout right for owners who didn't request the sale, a preference for dividing the land, and an open-market sale through a broker rather than a courthouse auction if a sale is ordered. Those protections matter in rural Georgia, where generations of heirs often share family land without anyone having probated an estate. For the family dynamics, see our guide to heirs who can't agree, and if no estate was ever opened, start with what happens to a house when there's no will.

🕰️ The 12-Month Window for an Heir Nobody Told

Here's a Georgia rule that protects absent owners and, indirectly, buyers. Under § 44-6-171, if an owner was absent from the state during the case or wasn't notified, that owner can move to set aside the partition judgment within 12 months after it's entered. A minor or an owner without capacity gets 12 months after the disability ends or a guardian is appointed. After that, the judgment is binding on them as if they'd been present. And in no event do those later proceedings affect the title of a good-faith buyer under a court-ordered sale.

Two practical consequences follow. First, the filing owner should work hard to identify and notify every heir, including the cousin in another state, because a missed owner can reopen the division. Second, a buyer at a court-ordered sale is protected even if a missed heir later challenges the judgment. That missed heir's remedy is a claim against the proceeds and the parties, not the house.

💵 A Macon Bungalow, Three Ways

Five siblings inherited their mother's bungalow in Macon-Bibb County. One sister, who lived there and paid the taxes for years, says she owns half. The other four say everyone owns a fifth. Fixed up, similar houses sell for about $190,000. As it stands, with an old roof and dated systems, maybe $150,000.

  • 🧾 Holding costs. Georgia assesses property at 40 percent of fair market value and applies the local millage rate. At an illustrative combined rate of 30 mills, a $150,000 house would carry about $1,800 a year in property tax, around $150 a month, before any exemptions; check the actual bill with the Macon-Bibb County Tax Commissioner. The mother's homestead exemption ends with her, so the bill may rise. Add $100 to $175 a month for insurance on an older house, $120 to $220 for utilities, and upkeep.
  • ⚖️ Partition with a title fight: the sister objects to a one-fifth share and demands a jury. Eighteen months and perhaps $20,000 to $40,000 in combined legal fees later, the jury decides the shares, and the house sells at a courthouse sale for, say, $135,000. After expenses, each sibling's slice is smaller than it would have been in almost any settlement.
  • 💵 Cash sale after the siblings agree on shares: at 60 to 80 percent of the $190,000 repaired value, a cash offer would fall between $114,000 and $152,000. For this house we'd expect roughly $120,000 to $135,000, closing in a few weeks, split however the siblings agree, with credits for the sister's tax payments negotiated rather than litigated.
  • 🏡 Listing after the shares are settled: as-is, maybe $145,000 to $155,000 before commission and closing costs, over two or three months.

The honest lesson: in this family, the price of the house is the smaller problem. Settling the shares, by agreement or with a mediator, is worth more than any buyer's offer. Once that's done, a listing will usually beat us on a house in this condition by a modest amount, and a cash sale wins only if speed or repairs matter more.

🧭 Choosing Your Route

🤝 Settle the shares, then sell or buy out

Usually the best outcome

A signed agreement on who owns what, with credits for taxes and repairs, avoids a jury trial and lets the family choose how to sell.

📬 Send the 20-day notice and apply

Right when someone won't engage

The statute gives every owner a defined process and a final judgment. Expect months, and years if title is contested.

🏡 List the house together

Best price for a sound house

If the house is in decent shape and all owners will sign, a listing nets more than a courthouse sale or a cash buyer.

💵 Sell your interest or the whole house for cash

Fastest exit

A buyer of one owner's interest steps into the title dispute, so the price reflects it. A whole-house cash sale needs every owner's signature or a court order.

  1. 📄 Pull the deed history from the clerk of superior court's real estate records in the county where the property sits.
  2. 👪 Build a family tree of every heir and what fraction each holds, and note anyone who lives out of state or is a minor.

  5. 🗣️ Propose a written agreement on shares before anyone sends a partition notice.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Do I have to notify my co-owners before filing for partition in Georgia?

Yes. Under O.C.G.A. 44-6-162, the owner applying for a writ of partition must give the other owners at least 20 days' notice of the intention to apply. Guardians or trustees receive it for minors and trust beneficiaries, and out-of-state owners can be served by publication if the court orders.

Who divides the property in a Georgia partition case?

Five freeholders of the county appointed as partitioners under 44-6-163. They can hire a surveyor, must give at least eight days' notice of when they'll act, and return the writ within three months.

Can a co-owner get a jury trial in a Georgia partition?

Yes. Under 44-6-165, an owner can object to the applicant's right, the writ or the partitioners' return, including by showing the petitioner doesn't own the share claimed, and that issue is tried by a jury.

Can a will stop a partition in Georgia?

It can. Statutory partition applies when no provision is made by will or otherwise as to how the land is to be divided. If the will or an owners' agreement covers division, the owners may have to follow it instead.

Can a Georgia court refuse to partition or sell?

Yes. Section 44-6-170 lets the court deny a sale or partition altogether if it is manifest that each party's interest will not be fully protected.

What happens if an heir wasn't notified of a Georgia partition?

Under 44-6-171, an absent or unnotified owner can move to set the judgment aside within 12 months after it's entered. A good-faith buyer's title from a court-ordered sale is not affected.

How is the money divided after a Georgia partition sale?

Under 44-6-168, the commissioners return the sale proceeds to the court, which divides them among the owners in proportion to their interests after deducting the expenses of the proceeding.

Does Georgia have a separate process for inherited family land?

Yes. Georgia adopted the Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act at 44-6-180 through 44-6-189.1, which adds a court-determined value, a buyout right and a preference for an open-market sale when property qualifies as heirs property.

Who pays the lawyers in a Georgia partition case?

Usually each owner pays their own attorney. The expenses of the proceeding, such as appraisers, partitioners and sale costs, are deducted from the proceeds before division.

Should I sell to a cash buyer or go through a Georgia partition?

If the shares are agreed and the house is in good shape, a listing usually nets more. A cash sale makes sense when repairs, an absent owner or carrying costs make speed worth a discount, but it still needs every owner's signature or a court order.
