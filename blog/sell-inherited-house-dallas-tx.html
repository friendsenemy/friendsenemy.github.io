Inherited a Dallas House? Your Real Deadline Is the Next January 1 Tax Appraisal

Texas has no inheritance tax and no state income tax, so it pays for schools with some of the highest property taxes in the country. For decades, an older Dallas homeowner's bill is softened by over-65 exemptions, a frozen school tax and a cap on appraisal increases. When they die, those protections end for the heirs at the next January 1. Here's what that does to the bill, who can keep the breaks, and how to plan around it. Or ask us, free.

By Dustin Ray, Founder of Pages of Purpose LLC. Not legal or tax advice — laws differ by state, Texas property tax law changes almost every legislative session, and your appraisal district and tax office have the final word on your account. Talk to an attorney or tax professional licensed in Texas, where the property is, before you file or sign anything.

The first thing most Dallas families ask after a parent's death is how long probate will take. That matters, and we'll cover it. But in Dallas County there's a second clock that catches families off guard, and it often runs faster than any court: the property tax appraisal on January 1.

Texas taxes property based on who owns it and how it's used on January 1 of each year. A parent who was 65 or older and lived in the house typically had several large exemptions, a frozen school tax and a cap on how fast the appraisal could climb. On the first January 1 after the death, the heirs don't get any of that unless they qualify on their own. The result can be a tax bill that triples or quadruples in a single year. Planning around that date is the most valuable thing most Dallas heirs can do.

🗓️ Why January 1 Matters More Than Probate in Dallas County

Here's what a long-time owner who was 65 or older typically had working for them, and what disappears for the heirs:

  • The school district homestead exemptions. After the 2025 changes approved by Texas voters, Section 11.13 of the Tax Code exempts $140,000 of a homestead's value from school taxes, plus another $60,000 for owners who are 65 or older or disabled. In Dallas ISD, where the school rate was about 99 cents per $100 of value in 2025, that $200,000 of exemptions alone is worth close to $2,000 a year.
  • The school tax ceiling. An over-65 owner's school taxes are frozen at the amount due in the year they qualified, so they don't rise with appraisals or rate changes. Many local taxing units in Dallas County offer their own over-65 exemptions or freezes too.
  • The 10% homestead appraisal cap. A homestead's taxable appraisal can't rise more than 10% a year, even if the market jumps more. In many Dallas neighborhoods that have climbed fast, a long-time owner's capped value sits well below what the house would sell for.

None of these pass to children or other heirs. When the appraisal district values the house as of the next January 1, it will be owned by an estate or by heirs who, in most cases, don't live there. The exemptions come off, the ceiling ends, and the appraisal can jump to full market value.

The deadline in plain terms: if a Dallas homeowner dies in, say, June 2026, the 2026 bill is generally figured on their January 1, 2026 status. The 2027 bill, which arrives in October 2027 and is due by January 31, 2028, will reflect the heirs' status as of January 1, 2027. Decisions you make before that date, such as who moves in, who files what, and whether to sell, can change the bill.

🧓 Who Can Keep the Breaks: A Surviving Spouse and the Heir Who Moves In

A surviving spouse who was 55 or older

Section 11.13(q) of the Tax Code lets a surviving spouse keep the deceased spouse's over-65 exemption from the same taxing units if the spouse was 55 or older when the owner died and the house remains the spouse's homestead. The tax ceiling carries over too. The spouse usually has to file a new application with the Dallas Central Appraisal District with a copy of the death certificate. If your stepmother is 58 and still lives in the house, the tax bill may not change at all, but her homestead right may also keep the heirs from selling. Our guide to heirs who can't agree on a Texas house explains that surviving-spouse homestead right.

An heir who lives in the house

Since a 2019 change in the law (Senate Bill 1943), an heir who inherited the home and lives in it as their principal residence can claim the full residence homestead exemption, even before any probate or recorded deed. The heir files the appraisal district's homestead application with the heir property affidavit (Comptroller Form 50-114-A), the death certificate and a utility bill. If other heirs also live there, they sign an affidavit consenting to the application. Appraisal districts can't demand a recorded affidavit of heirship or a court order for this exemption.

That heir gets the general homestead exemption and, from the following year, the 10% appraisal cap starts protecting them. They get the over-65 exemption and school tax ceiling only if they're 65 or older themselves. A 40-year-old son who moves into his late mother's house keeps some of the protection, but not all of it.

📈 The Appraisal Catch-Up When the 10% Cap Disappears

The cap matters more in Dallas than in many Texas cities because so many older neighborhoods appreciated quickly over the last decade. A house in Lake Highlands, Oak Cliff or Lakewood that a widow bought in 1985 may have a capped value tens of thousands of dollars below market. When the homestead ends, the next appraisal notice from the Dallas Central Appraisal District can reflect the full market value at once.

Heirs can protest. Texas protest deadlines generally fall on May 15 or 30 days after the appraisal notice is delivered, whichever is later. An inherited house is often the best kind to protest, because the appraisal district usually hasn't seen the inside. Photos of a cracked slab, an original 1960s kitchen, roof damage or a failing sewer line are exactly the evidence an appraisal review board can use. A successful protest won't restore the parent's exemptions, but it can take hundreds or thousands off the new bill.

🧾 Getting Title Clear Enough to Sell

You can't sell an inherited house until someone has legal authority to sign the deed. In Texas the heirs own the house from the moment of death, but a buyer's title company will want proof of who they are. The usual paths:

  • A transfer on death deed. Search the Dallas County Clerk's real property records first. If your parent recorded a transfer on death deed under Chapter 114 of the Estates Code, the named beneficiaries can usually sell after recording a death certificate and an affidavit, without probate.
  • A will and probate. Wills are probated in the Dallas County Probate Courts. Texas usually lets an independent executor sell without court approval, and a muniment of title can transfer the house quickly when there are no unpaid debts. Our Dallas probate guide covers the timelines and costs in detail.
  • No will. Families often use an affidavit of heirship under Section 203.001 of the Estates Code, signed by two disinterested witnesses who knew the family, and recorded in the county records. Many Texas title companies will insure a sale based on it, especially after time has passed, though some want a court determination of heirship. Texas small estate affidavits generally can't transfer real estate other than the homestead. Our guide to what happens to a house when there's no will explains intestate succession.

Watch one more deadline. A Texas will generally must be offered for probate within four years of death. After that, the options narrow to muniment of title in limited situations or heirship proceedings, and the paperwork gets harder.

🏚️ Dallas Houses, Dallas Dirt: What You're Likely Inheriting

Much of Dallas sits on expansive Blackland Prairie clay that swells when wet and shrinks in drought. Slab foundations from the 1950s through the 1980s often show it: cracked brick, doors that won't latch, sloped floors. Foundation repairs on a typical Dallas ranch house commonly run $5,000 to $20,000, and more when plumbing under the slab was damaged too. North Texas hail is the other common problem, since many roofs on inherited houses have storm damage the parent never claimed.

Those issues matter for two reasons. They can stop financed buyers, whose lenders and insurers want a sound roof and slab. And a vacant house with a known problem is expensive to insure. Vacant-dwelling policies in Dallas County commonly run $150 to $300 a month, and a standard homeowner's policy may not cover a house that's been empty for more than 30 to 60 days.

🔀 Your Options, With Honest Verdicts

List with a Dallas agent

Best price on a sound house

If the foundation and roof are in decent shape and the heirs agree, list it. Dallas still draws plenty of owner-occupant buyers for updated or updatable houses, and you'll net more than any cash offer. Allow 60 to 120 days from listing to closing, plus whatever time the title paperwork takes.

Keep it in the family

Best when an heir wants to live there

An heir can buy out the others or move in and file the heir property exemption. Put the arrangement in writing: who pays taxes and insurance, whether the occupant pays rent, and what happens if someone later wants out. Unwritten family deals are where many partition suits start.

Sell to a cash buyer

Best for foundation or roof problems, or a deadline

When the slab has moved, the roof is shot, the house is full of a lifetime of belongings, or the heirs live out of state and don't want to manage a listing, a cash sale closes in two to four weeks with no repairs or cleanout. You give up some price for speed and certainty.

Wait

Rarely the right call

Waiting makes sense for a few weeks while you gather documents. It stops making sense when the house sits empty through a January 1 appraisal, because the carrying costs come out of every heir's share.

💵 Before-and-After Tax Math and a Cash Offer on a Dallas House

Here's an example. A widow, 84, owned a 1962 brick ranch house in Lake Highlands for 40 years. Its market value as-is is about $290,000; fully repaired, about $340,000. With her capped appraisal, the $200,000 in school exemptions, local over-65 exemptions and a school tax ceiling set years ago, her annual bill was low; for a long-time owner in her position, $1,500 to $2,500 a year is common.

The heirs' bill. Inside the City of Dallas and Dallas ISD, the combined 2025 rate was about $2.23 per $100, adding the city (about 70 cents), Dallas ISD (about 99 cents), Dallas County (about 22 cents), Parkland Hospital (about 21 cents) and Dallas College (about 11 cents). With no exemptions, a $290,000 appraisal produces a bill of about $6,460 a year, or $538 a month. That's three to four times what their mother paid.

Monthly carrying cost while vacant: about $538 in taxes, $150 to $300 for a vacant-dwelling policy, about $150 for utilities, and $100 to $150 for lawn care and basic upkeep. Call it $950 to $1,150 a month.

Listing as-is. The house needs about $25,000 of foundation work and a $15,000 roof. A buyer who can finance it might pay $280,000 after inspections and repair negotiations. Subtract about 8% for commissions and closing costs ($22,400) and five months of carrying costs ($5,250). Net to the heirs: about $252,350.

Cash sale. Cash buyers typically pay 60% to 80% of after-repair value, depending on condition and the neighborhood. Here, a realistic offer might be $225,000, about 66% of $340,000, closing in three weeks with the buyer paying most closing costs and handling the cleanout. Net: about $223,000.

The listing nets about $29,000 more, if the heirs agree, the title paperwork is ready, and a buyer's lender accepts the foundation report. The cash sale wins if the slab problem turns out bigger than the estimate, if no lender will finance it, or if the heirs would rather have certainty this month than more money in the spring.

🙅 When a Cash Sale Is the Wrong Call

If the house is in good condition, the heirs agree, and nobody needs the money right away, list it. A Dallas house that a family can show in decent shape will usually bring more on the open market than from any cash buyer, including us. If an heir wants to live there, the heir property exemption and a family buyout can make that affordable. And if a surviving spouse 55 or older is living there, the tax bill may not change at all; there may be no reason to sell, and possibly no legal way to force one.

📋 What To Do This Week

  1. Look up the property on the Dallas Central Appraisal District website. Note the exemptions on the account, the market value and the capped appraised value.
  2. Find the most recent tax statement from the Dallas County Tax Office and confirm whether the current year's bill is paid. Texas tax bills are generally due by January 31, with penalties and interest starting February 1.
  3. Search the Dallas County Clerk's records for a transfer on death deed, and look for a will.
  4. Decide as a family, before the next January 1 if possible, whether an heir will move in, whether to rent, or whether to sell.
  5. If an heir is moving in, file the homestead application with the heir property affidavit promptly.
  6. Check the homeowner's insurance policy for vacancy limits and call the carrier.
  7. Get both an as-is value and a repaired value, and a foundation inspection if you see cracks.

✅ Bottom Line

In Dallas, the property tax calendar is often a tighter deadline than probate. A parent's over-65 exemptions, frozen school tax and 10% appraisal cap protect them, not their heirs, and they fall away at the first January 1 after the death unless a surviving spouse 55 or older keeps them or an heir moves in and qualifies. Look up the account, decide before that date if you can, protest a high appraisal with photos of what's wrong, and choose the exit that fits the house and the family. If it's a sound house and everyone agrees, list it. If it isn't, or if you need it done, ask us for an honest number.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Do we lose Mom's over-65 tax freeze when she dies in Texas?

Generally yes, for children and other heirs. The over-65 exemptions and the school tax ceiling belong to the qualifying owner. They stay with a surviving spouse who was 55 or older at the death and keeps living there, but otherwise they end, and the house is taxed on the heirs' status as of the next January 1.

Can my stepfather keep the over-65 exemption on the Dallas house?

If he was 55 or older when your mother died and the house remains his homestead, Texas Tax Code Section 11.13(q) lets him keep the over-65 exemption from the same taxing units, and the tax ceiling carries over. He typically files a new application with the Dallas Central Appraisal District with a death certificate.

I live in my late father's Dallas house. Can I get the homestead exemption without probate?

Yes, if you inherited it and it is your principal residence. Since Senate Bill 1943 in 2019, an heir can claim the full residence homestead exemption by filing the homestead application with the heir property affidavit (Form 50-114-A), a death certificate and a utility bill. Other heirs who live there must consent in writing.

When will the Dallas County tax bill go up after the owner dies?

Texas exemptions are based on ownership and use as of January 1. The bill for the year of death usually reflects the owner's status that January 1. The following year's bill reflects the heirs' status, so that is typically when exemptions and the school tax ceiling disappear and the appraisal can jump to market value.

Can we protest the appraisal on an inherited Dallas house?

Yes. The protest deadline is generally May 15 or 30 days after the appraisal notice is delivered, whichever is later. Photos and repair estimates for foundation movement, roof damage or outdated systems are strong evidence, because the appraisal district usually values the house without seeing the inside.

What happens if nobody pays the property taxes on an inherited Dallas house?

Texas property taxes are generally due by January 31, and penalties and interest begin February 1 and grow each month, with collection fees often added later in the year. Unpaid taxes are a lien on the house, and taxing units can eventually sue to foreclose. Paying the taxes from estate funds or at closing protects every heir's share.

Do we need probate to sell an inherited house in Dallas?

Not always. If there is a recorded transfer on death deed, the beneficiaries can usually sell without probate. Without a will, many families use a recorded affidavit of heirship, which many title companies accept. With a will, probate through the Dallas County Probate Courts, often as an independent administration or a muniment of title, is the usual route.

Will we owe inheritance or capital gains tax when we sell an inherited Texas house?

Texas has no inheritance tax and no state income tax. For federal purposes, inherited property generally receives a stepped-up basis equal to its value at death, so selling soon after for about that value usually produces little or no capital gain. Ask a tax professional about your situation.

How much will a cash buyer pay for an inherited Dallas house with foundation problems?

Cash buyers typically offer about 60% to 80% of the house's after-repair value, depending on the extent of foundation and roof work, the neighborhood and how quickly they can close. On a house worth $340,000 repaired that needs $40,000 of work, an offer around $220,000 to $235,000 is realistic.

Is it smarter to list an inherited Dallas house or take a cash offer?

If the house is in decent shape, the heirs agree and the title paperwork is ready, listing usually nets more even after commissions. A cash offer makes more sense when foundation or roof problems will block financing, when the heirs are out of state and can't manage a listing, or when carrying costs after the tax reset are eating the equity.
