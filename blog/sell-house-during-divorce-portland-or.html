Selling a House During a Divorce in Portland, Oregon: When Even the Premarital House Is on the Table

Oregon doesn't sort a divorcing couple's property into neat marital and separate piles. A judge can divide the property of either or both spouses, including a house one of you owned before the wedding, as is "just and proper." That changes how Portland couples should think about selling, keeping or buying out the house. Here's the law, the Multnomah County process and the real numbers.

By Dustin Ray, Founder of Pages of Purpose LLC. Not legal advice. Laws differ by state, and Oregon property division depends heavily on the facts of each marriage. Talk to a family law attorney licensed in Oregon, where the property is, before you sign a listing agreement, a sale contract or a settlement.

In many states, the first question in a divorce is whether the house is marital or separate property. If one spouse bought it before the wedding, that spouse often starts with a strong claim to it. Oregon is different, and Portland couples are often surprised. Under ORS 107.105(1)(f), a judge divides "the real or personal property, or both, of either or both of the parties as may be just and proper in all the circumstances." Of either or both. The house one of you owned first is on the table along with everything else.

That doesn't mean premarital property gets split down the middle. It means the outcome depends on the facts, which makes a negotiated sale, with a realistic number both spouses can see, more valuable here than in states with bright-line rules. For the general picture across the country, read our national guide to selling a house during a divorce. This page is about Portland and Oregon.

🗂️ Oregon Puts Every Asset on the Table, Even the House You Owned First

Oregon isn't a community property state like California, and it doesn't limit the court to marital property the way many equitable-distribution states do. The statute gives the court authority over property of either spouse. Oregon appellate courts have built a framework for how premarital and separately held property is treated, and judges look at how the asset was acquired, whether it was kept separate, and what each spouse contributed to it.

For a Portland house, that means questions like these matter:

  • 🔑 Who bought it, and when? A house bought before the marriage starts differently than one bought during it, but it isn't off limits.
  • 🏦 Whose money paid the mortgage? Marital income paying down a premarital mortgage for years strengthens the other spouse's claim.
  • 🛠️ Who improved it? A kitchen remodel or a new roof paid with joint funds commingles the asset.
  • 📝 Was it ever retitled? Adding a spouse to the deed is strong evidence the house became shared.

🧾 How Judges Divide It: Equal Contribution, Gifts and the Cost of Selling

ORS 107.105(1)(f) contains several rules that shape the house negotiation directly:

  • ⚖️ A presumption of equal contribution. Both spouses are presumed to have contributed equally to property acquired during the marriage, whether it's held jointly or separately (subsection (C)). Work as a homemaker counts as a contribution (subsection (B)). The presumption can be rebutted.
  • 🎁 Gifts and inheritances. Property one spouse received by gift or inheritance during the marriage, and kept separate, doesn't get that presumption (subsection (D)). A house inherited from a parent and never put in joint names is treated differently from a house bought with paychecks.
  • 💸 The cost of selling counts. Subsection (G) tells the court to consider reasonable costs of sale, taxes and other costs. A spouse arguing for the house's full Zillow value, ignoring commission, repairs and capital gains, is arguing against the statute.
  • 🤝 A kind of co-ownership. Once the petition is filed, the spouses' rights in marital assets become "a species of co-ownership," and the judgment's transfer of property is treated as a partition (subsection (E)).

The practical takeaway: in Oregon, a credible net-proceeds number, after commission, repairs and taxes, is a negotiating tool. It's also what the court is told to consider.

🔒 Filing Triggers an Automatic Order: ORS 107.093

Once a dissolution petition is filed and served, ORS 107.093 automatically restrains both spouses until judgment, dismissal or further order. Among other things, neither spouse may transfer, encumber, conceal or dispose of property the other has an interest in without the other's written consent or a court order. That covers selling or refinancing the house. The order also requires notice and an accounting for extraordinary expenditures, and it freezes insurance coverage and beneficiaries.

There are exceptions for the usual course of business, necessities of life, attorney fees, real estate and income taxes, and a few others, so you can keep paying the mortgage and property taxes. A violation is enforced through remedial contempt. Separately, ORS 107.095 lets the court restrain disposal of property, award temporary use of the house to one spouse, and order who pays which liens while the case is pending.

⚠️ Most important warning: once the petition has been served, don't sign a listing agreement or sale contract on your own, even if only your name is on the deed. Get your spouse's written consent or a court order first, and have both attorneys review the contract. A sale signed in violation of the automatic order invites contempt and can sink the deal.

🏠 Your Deed Probably Says Tenancy by the Entirety

Oregon still recognizes tenancy by the entirety. Under ORS 93.180, a conveyance to two people married to each other creates a tenancy by the entirety unless the deed clearly says otherwise. Many Portland couples who bought together hold title that way without knowing it. In that form both spouses own the whole, neither can sell alone, and the survivor takes everything if one dies.

What matters during a divorce is that the judgment deals with title clearly. Whether the house is awarded to one spouse or ordered sold, ask your attorney to make sure the judgment and any deed that follows match, so a buyer's title company can close without asking questions. If the divorce stalls and the house is held by former spouses as co-owners, our guide on whether a co-owner can sell without the other applies, and under ORS 105.205 a co-owner can ask a court to partition or sell. 🏛️ Filing in Multnomah County

Portland divorces are filed in Multnomah County Circuit Court, which moved into the Central Courthouse at 1200 SW 1st Avenue in 2020. The filing fee for a dissolution petition is $301 statewide under the Oregon circuit court fee schedule effective January 1, 2026. Under ORS 107.075, if you married in Oregon, one spouse must live here when you file; otherwise one spouse must have lived in Oregon for six continuous months.

Couples in Beaverton or Hillsboro file in Washington County, and those in Lake Oswego, Milwaukie or Oregon City usually file in Clackamas County. The property rules are the same statewide.

🧮 What a $520,000 Portland House Nets

Redfin put Portland's median sale price at about $518,000 for the three months ending September 2026, and Realtor.com showed a median of around 65 days on market. Take a 1920s bungalow in Southeast Portland worth about $520,000 updated. It needs a sewer line, electrical work and a dated kitchen, about $60,000 of work. The couple is separated and one spouse has moved out.

Carrying costs while you argue. Oregon's Measure 50 caps growth in a property's maximum assessed value at 3 percent a year, so assessed value is often well below market value. The Oregon Department of Revenue's data shows Multnomah County taxes averaging about $11.71 per $1,000 of market value, about 1.17 percent. On this house that's around $500 a month. Add homeowners insurance of $120 to $200 a month, and more if it sits vacant, utilities of $150 to $250, and basic upkeep. Holding the house through a contested divorce easily costs $900 to $1,100 a month on top of the mortgage, and someone has to pay it.

Selling costs. Multnomah County has no real estate transfer tax; Oregon's constitution has barred new ones since 2012. Washington County is the exception, at $1 per $1,000 of the sale price. On a listing, commission and seller-paid costs commonly run 5 to 7 percent.

Cash versus listing. Cash buyers usually pay 60 to 80 percent of repaired value, here $312,000 to $416,000. With $60,000 of work, a fair cash offer would land around $365,000 to $390,000, closing in a few weeks with no repairs, showings or disclosure negotiations. Listing as-is might bring $455,000 to $470,000, less 5 to 7 percent in selling costs and two or three more months of carrying costs. That's still roughly $50,000 to $70,000 more, if the spouses can cooperate on price, showings and repair requests. Fixing it up first could add more, but only if they agree on who pays.

💸 The Tax Layers on a Big Gain

Long-held Portland houses can carry large gains, and Oregon adds layers most states don't:

  • 🇺🇸 Federal exclusion. Section 121 of the tax code excludes up to $250,000 of gain per person, $500,000 on a joint return if both meet the use test. Under section 121(d)(3)(B), a spouse who moved out can still count as using the home while the other lives there under a divorce instrument. Timing the sale around the year you file jointly can matter.
  • 🌲 Oregon income tax. Oregon taxes capital gains at its regular income tax rates, up to 9.9 percent.
  • 🏙️ Metro Supportive Housing Services tax. 1 percent on Metro-taxable income above $128,000 single or $205,000 joint for 2026.
  • 🧒 Multnomah County Preschool for All tax. 1.5 percent on income above $125,000 single or $200,000 joint, plus another 1.5 percent above $250,000 or $400,000.

A large taxable gain in one year can push a seller over those thresholds. Ask a tax professional before you pick the closing date.

📄 Divorcing Sellers Still Owe a Disclosure

Oregon requires most sellers to give buyers a seller's property disclosure statement under ORS 105.464 and 105.465. ORS 105.470 exempts court-appointed receivers, personal representatives, trustees, conservators and a few others, but not spouses selling their own home in a divorce. Under ORS 105.475 the buyer can revoke the offer within five business days after receiving the disclosure, and if no disclosure is given, until closing. When two spouses who aren't speaking have to agree on what the form says about the roof and the basement, a delay here is common. Agree on who fills it out before you list.

📋 What To Do This Week in Portland

  1. Pull your deed from the county and see how title is held. A deed to both spouses is probably a tenancy by the entirety.
  2. Gather the purchase date, the source of the down payment and records of major improvements; in Oregon all of it can matter.
  3. If the petition has been served, don't list or sign anything without written consent or a court order under ORS 107.093.
  4. Get a realistic as-is value and a repaired value, and estimate net proceeds after commission, repairs and taxes.
  5. Talk to a tax professional about the section 121 exclusion, Oregon income tax and the Metro and Preschool for All taxes before choosing a closing year.
  6. Agree in writing who pays the mortgage, taxes and insurance until the sale.
  7. Compare an agent's estimated net with a written cash offer, and share both with both attorneys.

✅ Bottom Line for Portland Couples

Oregon gives judges broad authority: property of either spouse can be divided as is just and proper, including a house one of you owned before the marriage, and property acquired during the marriage is presumed to reflect equal contributions. Because the outcome depends on facts, couples who agree on the house usually do better than those who litigate it. Once a petition is served, ORS 107.093 means a sale needs written consent or a court order. The court is told to consider the costs of selling and taxes, so bring a real net number to the table, including Oregon's income tax and Portland's Metro and Preschool for All taxes on a large gain. If the house is in good shape and you can cooperate, list it. If it needs work or cooperation has run out, one written cash offer both of you can see may be the cleaner path.

❓ Portland and Oregon Divorce Home Sale FAQ

Can an Oregon judge divide a house I owned before we got married?

Yes. ORS 107.105(1)(f) lets the court divide the real or personal property of either or both spouses as is just and proper in all the circumstances, so premarital property is not automatically excluded. How it is divided depends on the facts of the marriage.

Does Oregon split marital property 50/50?

Not automatically. Oregon presumes both spouses contributed equally to property acquired during the marriage, whether it is held jointly or separately, but the presumption can be rebutted and the final division must be just and proper.

Is an inheritance protected in an Oregon divorce?

Property received by one spouse as a gift or inheritance during the marriage, and kept separate, does not get the equal-contribution presumption under ORS 107.105(1)(f)(D). The court can still consider it in a just and proper division.

Can we sell the house after filing for divorce in Oregon?

Yes, with both spouses' written consent or a court order. Under ORS 107.093, once the petition is filed and served, both spouses are restrained from transferring or encumbering property the other has an interest in without that consent or order.

Does the automatic restraining order in Oregon stop me from paying the mortgage or property tax?

No. ORS 107.093 has exceptions for the usual course of business, necessities of life, attorney fees and real estate and income taxes, so ordinary bills on the house can be paid.

How much does it cost to file for divorce in Multnomah County?

The statewide filing fee for a dissolution petition in Oregon circuit court is 301 dollars under the fee schedule effective January 1, 2026. Attorney fees are separate and usually far larger.

Does a divorcing couple have to give the buyer a seller disclosure in Oregon?

Yes. ORS 105.470 exempts court-appointed receivers, personal representatives, trustees and similar sellers, but not spouses selling their own home. Under ORS 105.475 the buyer can revoke within five business days after receiving the disclosure.

Is there a transfer tax when selling a house in Portland?

No transfer tax applies in Multnomah County. Oregon's constitution bars new real estate transfer taxes, and Washington County is the exception, charging 1 dollar per 1,000 dollars of the price because its tax predates the ban.

Do Portland residents pay extra tax on a big home-sale gain?

Possibly. Gain above the federal exclusion is taxed by Oregon as income, and the Metro Supportive Housing Services tax and the Multnomah County Preschool for All tax apply to income above set thresholds, so a large gain can push a seller into them.

Does the court consider the costs of selling the house when dividing property in Oregon?

Yes. ORS 107.105(1)(f)(G) tells the court to consider reasonable costs of sale of assets, taxes and other costs when dividing property, which is why a realistic net-proceeds estimate matters in negotiations.
