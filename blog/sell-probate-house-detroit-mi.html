Selling a Probate House in Detroit: Michigan Lets the Personal Representative Sell Without a Judge, Until Someone Asks for Supervision

Michigan's probate code gives a personal representative in an unsupervised estate broad power to sell real estate without a hearing, a court-approved price, or every heir's signature. In Wayne County, most estates start that way. That's fast, but it also puts a lot of weight on who gets appointed and on keeping the family from asking the court to step in. Here's how a Detroit probate house actually gets sold, what slows it down, and when listing beats a cash offer.

If you've just been named personal representative of a parent's estate in Wayne County, you probably have a set of Letters of Authority, a house you don't live in, and relatives with opinions. The good news is that Michigan gives you more power to act than many states do. The less welcome news is that you're expected to use it carefully, and any interested family member can ask the court to rein you in.

This guide is about the mechanics of a Detroit probate sale: what you can do on your own authority, what puts a judge back in the middle, what to do about an occupant, how Medicaid estate recovery fits in, and how to pick between listing and a cash buyer.

🔓 An Unsupervised Personal Representative Can Usually Sell Without a Hearing

Michigan's Estates and Protected Individuals Code (EPIC) sets out what a personal representative may do without court order. Under MCL 700.3715, the list includes acquiring and disposing of estate assets, including land, for cash or on credit, at public or private sale. In an unsupervised estate, the default in most Wayne County cases, that means you can generally list the house, accept an offer, and sign a personal representative's deed without asking a judge to approve the price.

That's a different starting point from states where the will's wording decides whether the executor needs court permission. In Michigan, the code itself supplies the power. A will can restrict it, and the court can restrict it, but most of the time the personal representative already has it once Letters of Authority issue.

🏛️ How the personal representative is appointed in Wayne County

  • 📝 Informal proceedings. Most estates are opened by an application to the probate register at the Wayne County Probate Court. No hearing is needed when the application is uncontested and the applicant has priority. Appointment can come within a few weeks.
  • ⚖️ Formal proceedings. If the will's validity is disputed, someone with priority objects, or heirs can't be located, the case goes before a judge. That adds weeks or months before anyone can sell.
  • 📬 Notice duties. After appointment, the personal representative must give notice of the appointment to the heirs and devisees, and must publish notice to creditors. Under MCL 700.3801, creditors then generally have four months to present claims.

🪪 What a title company in Detroit will ask for

Expect the closing title company to want a recently certified copy of your Letters of Authority, a death certificate, and confirmation that the estate is unsupervised and that no restraining order or supervision petition is pending. If the will restricts your powers, they'll want to read the restriction. If the house is still in the name of someone who died before your parent, you may need two estates opened.

🛑 When an Heir Asks the Court to Step In

The flip side of a personal representative's broad power is that every interested person has tools to check it.

  • 👁️ Petition for supervised administration. An interested person can ask the court to supervise the estate. In supervised administration, the personal representative can't distribute assets without a court order, and the court can direct how the estate is handled. Many title companies will want the court's approval of a sale in a supervised estate before they insure it.
  • ⛔ Restraining order. An heir who believes a sale is a mistake can ask the court to restrain the personal representative from a particular act, such as selling the house to a particular buyer or at a particular price.
  • 🔄 Removal. A personal representative who mismanages the estate or acts against the heirs' interests can be removed and surcharged.
⚠️ Communicate before you contract. The fastest way to turn a quick unsupervised sale into a months-long supervised one is to sign a purchase agreement without telling the heirs. Send everyone the appraisal or broker opinion, the offer, and the net to each heir before you sign. A personal representative who sells the family house for well under market without explanation invites a supervision petition and personal liability.

Costs to open and run a simple informal estate include a petition filing fee of about $175, an inventory fee that rises with the estate's value, publication costs, a death certificate or two, and attorney fees that commonly run $2,000 to $6,000 for one house and few complications. The court may require a bond unless the will waives it. A personal representative is entitled to reasonable compensation, though family members often waive it.

🚪 When a Relative Is Living in the Estate's House

This is one of the most common Detroit probate complications: a sibling, cousin or grandchild has lived in the house for years, sometimes caring for the parent, and doesn't want to leave.

Under EPIC, a personal representative has a right to take possession of the decedent's property when administration requires it, including the house. That doesn't let you change the locks on someone who lives there. If talking doesn't work, removing an occupant generally runs through a summary proceeding in district court; for a Detroit address, that's the 36th District Court. Expect weeks to a few months, and court costs.

Before going that route, consider the alternatives:

  • 🤝 Let the occupant buy it. If they can get a mortgage or bring cash, a sale to the occupant at appraised value, with credit for their share as an heir, often ends the dispute cleanly.
  • 📆 Agree on a move-out date tied to closing, with a cash payment from the proceeds for moving costs. It's usually cheaper than eviction.
  • 🏷️ Sell with the occupant in place. Some cash buyers, including us, will buy an occupied house and handle the occupant after closing, priced accordingly.

If the occupant paid the taxes, insurance or major repairs, that may affect what they're owed in the final accounting. Keep records and let the probate attorney sort credits.

🏥 Medicaid Estate Recovery and Michigan's Half-of-Average Exemption

If the deceased owner received Medicaid long-term care, the Michigan Department of Health and Human Services may file a claim against the estate under Michigan's estate recovery law, MCL 400.112g. Three points matter for a Detroit house:

  • 🏠 Protected occupants. The statute bars recovery from the home while certain people lawfully live there: a surviving spouse, a child under 21 or a blind or disabled child, a caretaker relative who lived there at least two years before the owner went into care and whose care kept them home, or a sibling with an ownership interest who lived there at least a year before admission.
  • 💲 The hardship exemption. The statute requires a hardship definition that includes an exemption for the part of the home's value at or below 50% of the average home price in the county at the time of death. Many Detroit houses are worth less than half of Wayne County's average price, so a large part, or all, of a modest Detroit house may be exempt if the family applies.
  • 📜 What passes outside probate. Michigan's program has generally pursued assets in the probate estate. A house that passed by a valid Lady Bird (enhanced life estate) deed has generally stayed out of reach. Have an elder law or probate attorney confirm how the house passed before assuming either way.

Ask whether a claim exists before you list. A Medicaid claim that surfaces after the sale can hold up distribution of the proceeds.

💵 Listing or a Cash Sale From the Estate

The personal representative's duty is to get a fair price for the heirs, not the fastest one. In Detroit, though, what counts as fair depends heavily on the house's condition, because repair costs are often a large slice of the value.

📈 List it

The right call for a sound house

If the house is updated or close to it, in a neighborhood where financed buyers are active, list it. A personal representative who can show the heirs a fully marketed sale has also protected themselves from a later complaint about price.

💵 Take a cash offer

Often right for an occupied, unrepaired, or tax-delinquent house

Get at least two cash offers and a broker opinion of as-is value, and share them with the heirs. Then the decision is documented and the heirs can see the trade-off between price and speed.

🤝 Distribute the house instead of selling

Right when one heir wants it and the rest agree

An estate doesn't have to sell. The personal representative can deed the house to an heir, with equalizing cash to the others, if the heirs agree on value. Check the tax consequences first; our Detroit inherited house guide explains how uncapping and the homestead exemption work after a transfer.

🧮 A worked example

Take a two-story house on the east side worth about $130,000 renovated, needing about $40,000 of work, with a grandson living in it. A cash buyer working at 60% to 80% of repaired value, adjusted for the occupant, would likely offer $75,000 to $90,000. Listing it would mean first getting the occupant out, possibly through the 36th District Court, and then selling as-is to a cash or rehab-loan buyer for perhaps $85,000 to $100,000, less around 6% in costs, three to five months later. At roughly $600 a month in taxes, insurance, utilities and upkeep, plus attorney time for the eviction, the gap between the two often shrinks to a few thousand dollars. With a vacant house in good shape, listing would win clearly.

📋 What To Do This Week

  1. Get several certified copies of your Letters of Authority and confirm whether the estate is supervised or unsupervised.
  2. Publish notice to creditors and send notice of your appointment to the heirs and devisees.
  3. Secure and insure the house in the estate's name, and check whether property taxes are delinquent with the Wayne County Treasurer.
  4. Find out whether anyone lives there and on what terms, and start a conversation before anything else.
  5. Ask about a Medicaid claim if your parent received long-term care, and whether the hardship exemption may apply.
  6. Get a broker opinion and at least one cash offer, and share both with every heir in writing.
  7. Keep a ledger of every estate expense you pay; you'll need it for the accounting.

✅ Bottom Line

In Michigan, an unsupervised personal representative usually has the power to sell the estate's house without asking a judge, which makes a Detroit probate sale faster than in many states. Use that power openly: share the numbers with the heirs, deal with any occupant before or through the sale, and check for a Medicaid claim early. Keeping the family informed is what keeps the estate unsupervised.

If the house is in good shape and there's no occupant, list it; the market will likely pay more than we will. If someone is living there, the house needs major work, or taxes are behind, ask us for a written offer and set it next to a broker's opinion so the heirs can see the trade-off.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Does a personal representative in Michigan need court approval to sell a house?

Usually not in unsupervised administration. MCL 700.3715 authorizes a personal representative to dispose of estate assets, including land, at public or private sale without a court order, unless the will or the court restricts that power. In supervised administration, title companies commonly expect court approval.

What is supervised administration in Michigan?

It is a probate proceeding in which the court supervises the personal representative, who cannot distribute estate assets without a court order. Any interested person can petition for it, and it usually makes a sale slower.

Can an heir stop the sale of a probate house in Detroit?

An heir can petition the Wayne County Probate Court for supervised administration, ask for an order restraining the personal representative from a specific sale, or seek removal of a personal representative who is mismanaging the estate. An heir generally cannot block a fair sale simply by refusing to sign.

How long does probate take in Wayne County?

Informal appointment often takes 2 to 6 weeks, the creditor claim period runs 4 months from publication, and a simple estate often closes in 6 to 12 months. The house can usually be sold well before the estate closes.

How long do creditors have to file claims in a Michigan estate?

Under MCL 700.3801, after notice to creditors is published, creditors generally have four months from the date of publication to present claims.

How do I get a relative out of a probate house in Detroit?

Start by negotiating a move-out date or a buyout. If that fails, the personal representative can generally pursue possession through a summary proceeding in district court, which for a Detroit address is the 36th District Court. Do not change the locks on an occupant.

Will Michigan Medicaid take my parent's house after death?

Michigan's estate recovery program under MCL 400.112g may claim against the estate if your parent received covered long-term care. Recovery is barred while certain relatives lawfully live in the home, and the hardship rules include an exemption for the part of the home's value at or below 50 percent of the average home price in the county.

Does a Lady Bird deed avoid probate in Michigan?

A valid enhanced life estate deed, often called a Lady Bird deed, passes the house at death to the named beneficiaries without probate. Michigan's estate recovery program has generally pursued probate assets, so a house passing by such a deed has generally been outside it.

What does it cost to probate an estate with a house in Wayne County?

Expect a petition filing fee of about 175 dollars, an inventory fee scaled to the estate's value, publication costs, and attorney fees that commonly run 2,000 to 6,000 dollars for a simple estate with one house. Contested or supervised estates cost more.

Should a personal representative list the house or sell it for cash?

If the house is in good condition, vacant and in a neighborhood with financed buyers, listing usually nets more and best documents a fair price. A cash sale fits when the house needs heavy repairs, someone is living in it, taxes are delinquent, or the estate needs a quick and certain close. Getting a broker opinion and cash offers and sharing them with heirs protects the personal representative either way.
