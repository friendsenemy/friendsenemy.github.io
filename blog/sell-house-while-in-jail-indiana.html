Selling an Indiana House From Jail or Prison: Witnesses Instead of a Notary, and the Abandoned-House Fast Track

Indiana is one of the few states that lets a power of attorney be signed in front of witnesses instead of a notary, which can matter a lot inside a prison. But for a house, the recorder won't accept the agent's deed until that power of attorney is itself recorded. And an empty house whose owner can't show up in court can be foreclosed on an expedited track. Here's how those rules work together.

When the owner of an Indiana house is at Pendleton, Wabash Valley, the Indiana State Prison in Michigan City or a county jail, the hardest part of selling is usually not finding a buyer. It's getting one valid signature from inside, in a form that the county recorder will accept, before the house deteriorates or the lender's case moves forward.

Indiana's power of attorney law gives families an option most states don't: signing before witnesses rather than a notary. It also adds a recording rule that catches people at the end. This page walks through both, then the part of Indiana foreclosure law that applies specifically to empty houses. For the general rules that apply in every state, start with our national guide to selling a house while incarcerated.

🖋️ Indiana Lets Witnesses Stand In for the Notary, With One Catch for Houses

Under IC 30-5-4-1, a power of attorney is valid if it is in writing, names an attorney in fact, gives that person power to act, and is signed by the principal either in the presence of a notary public or in the presence of witnesses as described in the sections that follow. Indiana added the witness route in 2020, and for an owner in prison it can solve the most stubborn logistics problem: finding a notary who is allowed in, at a time the facility allows, with ID the notary will accept.

The witness rules are specific (IC 30-5-4-1.3). Anyone competent to be a witness in Indiana may attest. But a witness who has an interest makes the power of attorney void if it can't be proved without that witness. A witness has an interest if he or she is:

  • 🙅 the attorney in fact or a successor attorney in fact named in the document;
  • 🙅 someone the document gives another power or a beneficial interest; or
  • 🙅 the spouse or a descendant of one of those people.

In plain terms: if the owner's sister is going to be the agent, neither the sister, her husband nor her children should witness. Use people with nothing to gain, such as a caseworker, a chaplain, an attorney's staff member or a visiting friend with no role in the document.

Now the catch. For real estate, the witnessed document has to get into the county records, and IC 30-5-3-3 controls that:

  • 📌 An attorney in fact must record the power of attorney before presenting any document that must be recorded, such as a deed.
  • ⛔ The county recorder may not accept a document executed and presented by an attorney in fact whose power of attorney is unrecorded.
  • 🔗 The deed must reference the book and page or instrument number where the power of attorney is recorded.
  • 🧾 A witnessed (not notarized) power of attorney can be recorded only if a "proof" signed by at least one attesting witness, and complying with Indiana's notary law, is attached.

So the witness route moves the notary from inside the prison to outside it. The owner signs before witnesses at the facility, and afterward one of those witnesses signs a proof in front of a notary on the outside. Choose at least one witness you can find again, and who is willing to meet a notary within a few days. A witness who was another inmate, or a guard who won't get involved, can leave you with a valid document you can't record.

⚠️ Plan the recording before the signing. In Indiana, a perfectly valid power of attorney that can't be recorded will not get a deed accepted by the recorder. Decide in advance whether the owner signs before a notary or before witnesses, and if witnesses, who will sign the notarized proof afterward.

📑 What Makes an Indiana Power of Attorney Recordable

IC 30-5-3-3(d) says a power of attorney must meet the general recording requirements, "including notary and preparation statements," to be recorded (the witnessed-document exception above is the only shortcut). In practice, the county recorder will look for:

  • ✍️ The preparer's name ("This instrument was prepared by...") at the end.
  • 🔒 The standard affirmation that Social Security numbers have been redacted, which Indiana recorders require on recorded documents.
  • 📏 Margins and legibility that meet the recorder's formatting rules, since a hand-corrected form may be rejected.
  • 🏠 Enough description of the authority granted. Indiana's statute lists real property transactions as a category of power (IC 30-5-5-2), and a document can grant it by referring to that section. A title company will still want the house described.

Have an Indiana attorney or the title company prepare the document so it's recordable on the first attempt. A rejected filing costs a week; a power of attorney that has to be re-signed inside costs a month.

🏢 Getting Signatures at IDOC Facilities and County Jails

The Indiana Department of Correction houses people at facilities across the state, and many others are held in county jails pending trial or serving short sentences. Before you mail anything, call the facility and ask three questions:

  1. Can an outside notary or outside witnesses attend a legal or professional visit, and how far ahead must it be booked?
  2. Does the facility have staff notaries who will notarize personal legal documents for inmates?
  3. What identification will the inmate have available on the day of signing?

County jails usually move faster but often prohibit passing papers hand to hand. In that case the document goes in through the mail or property room, the owner signs while the notary or witnesses watch, and the paper comes back out the same way. Build in a week for each direction. Our guide on getting a notary into a jail or prison covers ID problems in more detail.

🏚️ The Abandoned-House Fast Track

Indiana forecloses mortgages through the courts. Ordinarily that gives an owner time: after the lender files, no sheriff's sale can happen for three months (IC 32-29-7-3), the homeowner can ask for a settlement conference within 30 days after receiving the summons (IC 32-30-10.5-8), and after judgment the sheriff publishes notice for three weeks, starting at least 30 days before the sale. Six months to a year from filing to sale is common.

An empty house can skip much of that. Indiana's chapter on abandoned property in foreclosure (IC 32-30-10.6) lets a court find a mortgaged house abandoned and enter judgment foreclosing it. Under IC 32-30-10.6-5, prima facie evidence of abandonment includes:

  • 🔌 Gas, electric, water or other utility service has been shut off.
  • 🪟 Windows or entrances are boarded up, or multiple panes are broken.
  • 🗑️ Trash or debris has piled up, or the house is falling below minimum safety and sanitation standards.
  • 🔑 The lender changed the locks and the owner didn't ask to get in for at least 15 days.

And regardless of the house's condition, the owner's failure to file written objections or appear on the date set in the court's order is itself prima facie evidence of abandonment. For an owner in prison who can't easily appear, that's the risk. Once a court enters the abandonment judgment, the sheriff must schedule the sale within 60 days, instead of the 120-day outer limit for ordinary foreclosures.

What that means for families:

  • 💡 Keep at least minimal utilities on and the yard and windows maintained. Shutoffs and debris are evidence against you.
  • 📨 If foreclosure papers arrive, respond in writing before the date in the court's order, even if the response is only an objection to the abandonment finding while you arrange a sale. The owner can write from prison; a lawyer is better.
  • 📞 Request the settlement conference within 30 days of the summons. It gives you time and a forum to propose a sale.

📈 Indiana's Tax Caps and the Empty House

Indiana's constitution caps property tax bills as a percentage of gross assessed value: 1% for a homestead, 2% for other residential property, and 3% for most other property. Homestead treatment depends on the house being the owner's principal place of residence. If the county auditor decides an incarcerated owner's house no longer qualifies, the homestead deduction can be removed and the cap rises from 1% to 2%.

On a $180,000 house in Marion County, that's the difference between a bill of at most about $150 a month and one that can approach $300 a month. Add vacant-home insurance ($100 to $250 a month), utilities kept on ($100 to $200), and upkeep ($75 to $150), and an empty Indianapolis-area house can cost $450 to $900 a month before the mortgage. Unpaid taxes eventually lead to the county's annual tax sale, which adds penalties and redemption costs of its own.

🧾 Paperwork Indiana Adds at Closing

Beyond the deed, Indiana requires a sales disclosure form filed with the county auditor for most sales. The agent under a recorded power of attorney can sign it. If the owner co-owns with a spouse, Indiana recognizes tenancy by the entirety for married couples, so both spouses must sign the deed. If the owner shares title with siblings or an ex, each co-owner signs for his or her own share, and an unwilling co-owner may require a partition action or a sale of just the incarcerated owner's share. See whether a co-owner can sell without the other for how that works.

Judgments from the criminal case (court costs, fines, restitution) and child-support liens are paid from the proceeds if they're liens on the house. Ask the title company for a search early so the net figure you're comparing is real.

📋 What To Do This Week

  1. 🔎 Look up the deed and any liens at the county recorder, and note how the title is held.
  2. 📞 Ask the facility whether an outside notary can come in; if not, line up disinterested witnesses and one who will sign a notarized proof afterward.
  3. 🖊️ Have an Indiana attorney or title company prepare a recordable power of attorney with real property authority and a successor agent.
  4. 💡 Keep the utilities on and the house secured, so it doesn't look abandoned.
  5. 📨 If there's a foreclosure summons, request a settlement conference within 30 days and respond before any court-set appearance date.
  6. 🧾 Check with the county auditor whether the homestead deduction is still on the tax bill.
  7. 💵 Get one agent's as-is valuation and one written cash offer, then compare nets.

✅ Bottom Line

Indiana gives an incarcerated owner a useful tool, a power of attorney signed before disinterested witnesses instead of a notary, but a house can't be sold under it until it's recorded, and a witnessed document needs a notarized witness proof to get there. Meanwhile, an empty house with its utilities off and an owner who doesn't answer the court can be declared abandoned and sold within about 60 days of judgment. Get a recordable power signed, keep the house looking lived-in, answer any court papers, and then compare a listing with a cash offer on real numbers.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Does an Indiana power of attorney need a notary if the owner is in prison?

No. IC 30-5-4-1 allows a power of attorney to be signed either before a notary or before witnesses. For selling a house, though, it must be recorded, and a witnessed document can be recorded only with a notarized proof signed by one of the witnesses.

Who can witness an Indiana power of attorney?

Anyone competent to be a witness in Indiana, except people with an interest: the named agent or successor agent, anyone the document gives a power or benefit to, and the spouse or descendants of those people. Using an interested witness can make the document void.

Will the county recorder accept a deed signed by an agent whose power of attorney isn't recorded?

No. Under IC 30-5-3-3, the recorder may not accept a document signed and presented by an attorney in fact whose power of attorney is unrecorded, and the deed must reference where the power of attorney is recorded.

How do you record a witnessed power of attorney in Indiana?

Attach a proof signed by at least one of the attesting witnesses that complies with Indiana's notary law. In practice the witness signs a short statement before a notary confirming the owner signed in his or her presence.

How long does an Indiana foreclosure take?

Indiana foreclosures go through court. No sheriff's sale can occur until three months after the complaint is filed, and the sheriff advertises for three weeks before the sale. Six months to a year from filing is common unless the house is found abandoned.

Can an incarcerated homeowner ask for a foreclosure settlement conference in Indiana?

Yes. The summons tells the homeowner of the right to request a settlement conference, and the request is due within 30 days of receiving it under IC 32-30-10.5-8. An incarcerated owner can make the request in writing or through a lawyer.

Can an Indiana court declare an incarcerated owner's house abandoned?

It can. Under IC 32-30-10.6-5, shut-off utilities, boarded or broken windows, accumulated debris, or the owner's failure to object or appear by the court's date are prima facie evidence of abandonment. The sheriff's sale must then be scheduled within 60 days.

Will property taxes go up on an empty Indiana house while the owner is in prison?

They can. Homesteads are capped at 1% of gross assessed value, other residential property at 2%. If the county decides the house is no longer the owner's principal residence and removes the homestead deduction, the cap and the bill can roughly double.

What documents does an Indiana home sale need besides the deed?

A sales disclosure form filed with the county auditor, a payoff for any mortgage or liens, and, if an agent signs, the recorded power of attorney referenced in the deed. The title company usually prepares these.

Is listing better than a cash sale for an Indiana house when the owner is incarcerated?

Often, yes, if the house is occupied or in decent shape and someone can manage a listing. A cash sale is worth its discount when the house is empty and deteriorating, a foreclosure case is active, or nobody outside can handle showings.
