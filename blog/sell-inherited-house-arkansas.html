Selling an Inherited House in Arkansas: The Family Home Doesn't Count Toward the $100,000 Small-Estate Limit

Arkansas does something most states don't: when heirs add up an estate to see whether it qualifies for the small estate affidavit, the law tells them to leave the decedent's homestead out. That can put a $250,000 house on a path that skips a personal representative and a court order to sell. Here's how the shortcut works, where it breaks, and how to sell once you have it. Someone in your family died owning a house in Arkansas. Maybe it's the place on a quarter-acre in Springdale where you grew up, maybe a brick ranch in Fort Smith that's been empty since the funeral. The deed is still in their name, and you've probably heard that nothing can happen until "probate" finishes, which sounds like a year of court dates.

In Arkansas that's often not true, and the reason is one sentence in the small-estate statute that changes the math for a lot of families. This guide starts there, because it decides which path you're on. Then it covers when that path closes, what a full estate looks like if you need one, what the empty house costs while you decide, and how the two main ways to sell compare once someone has authority to sign.

🏠 Arkansas Leaves the Homestead Out of the $100,000 Test

Most states have a small-estate affidavit, and most of them are useless for a house. Either real estate is excluded outright, or the dollar limit is so low that any house blows through it. Arkansas's version, Ark. Code § 28-41-101, is different in two ways.

  • 📏 The limit is $100,000, net of debts. The test is the value, "less encumbrances," of everything the person owned at death. A mortgage balance comes off the top.
  • 🏡 The decedent's homestead is excluded from that count. The 2025 statute says that when calculating the value, "the value of the decedent's homestead and the value of any statutory allowances for the benefit of a spouse or minor children, if any, shall be excluded."

Put those together and a family whose mother left a $240,000 house she lived in, a $9,000 checking account and a 2014 Buick may fit under the limit, because only the checking account and the car are counted. In many neighboring states the same estate would need a full administration with a court-appointed personal representative before anyone could sign a deed.

The affidavit still has to describe the house. It must include a legal description and valuation of any real property "including the homestead," the names of anyone living on it, and the names, addresses and relationships of the people who will receive it. It's filed with the probate clerk of the circuit court in the county where an estate would be opened. The statute sets a $25 filing fee and $5 per certified copy, and says no court order is necessary.

⚠️ The warning that matters most: the affidavit includes a sworn statement that there are no unpaid claims against the person or the estate, and that the Arkansas Department of Human Services furnished no benefits or has been reimbursed. If your parent was on Medicaid in a nursing home, that line is not a formality. Signing it falsely can make the heirs personally answerable for the debt and can unwind the shortcut. Ask DHS for an estate recovery answer before anyone signs, and if there is a claim, plan to pay it from the sale.

📅 The Affidavit's Calendar: 45 Days, 30 Days, Then Three Months

The affidavit route is quick on paper, but it runs on a fixed clock, and a buyer's title company will be watching the same clock.

  1. Day 45 after the death. The affidavit can't be filed until 45 days have passed, and only if nobody has asked the court to appoint a personal representative.
  2. Within 30 days after filing. Because the estate includes real property, the heir who filed must publish a notice of the death and the affidavit in a local newspaper, using the form the statute sets out.
  3. Three months after the first publication. Creditors who haven't presented claims by then are barred.

So the realistic earliest date for a clean sale on an affidavit is about four and a half to five months after the death, and that's if you file on day 45 and publish the same week. Some title insurers will close sooner with an indemnity or an escrow holdback; others won't touch an affidavit sale until the claim period has run. Call the title company before you sign a purchase contract, not after, and ask exactly what it needs to insure a deed from the heirs named in the affidavit.

One more practical point: on the affidavit route there's no personal representative, so the deed is signed by every heir who inherits. If four siblings inherit equally, four siblings sign, plus the spouse of any married sibling to release dower or curtesy if the title company asks. An heir in another state can sign in front of a notary there. An heir who won't sign stops the sale.

🚧 Four Things That Push an Arkansas Estate Into Full Probate

The homestead exclusion doesn't help everyone. These are the situations where Arkansas families usually end up opening a full estate instead:

💵 Other property pushes the total over $100,000

Affidavit unavailable

The exclusion covers the homestead, not a rental house, a lake cabin or a farm the person didn't live on. A second property, a brokerage account or a paid-off truck can push the counted total past $100,000 on its own. If the inherited house wasn't the person's home, it counts in full.

🧾 There are debts the heirs can't clear

Affidavit risky

Credit cards, a hospital bill, a reverse mortgage that's come due, or a Medicaid recovery claim. The affidavit requires a statement that there are no unpaid claims. When there are debts that the house has to pay, an estate with a personal representative and a creditor process is the cleaner way.

🤝 One heir won't sign, or can't be found

Needs a court

With no personal representative, every heir signs the deed. A missing grandchild or a holdout sibling makes the affidavit route a dead end. Our guide to what to do when heirs can't agree to sell covers the options, and in Arkansas they run through the circuit court.

📜 A will needs to be probated

Usually full estate

A will can be attached to the affidavit without an extra fee, but if the will leaves the house differently than the heirs expect, or names an executor the family wants to use, most lawyers will open an estate so the executor gets letters.

When an estate is opened, the circuit court's probate division appoints a personal representative, and under Ark. Code § 28-51-103 a sale of estate real property generally happens under a court order unless the will grants the power to sell. That adds a petition and an order to the timeline. It's routine, but it's a step many states don't have.

📑 Check the Courthouse for a Beneficiary Deed Before You File Anything

Before anyone files an affidavit, search the county recorder's records for a beneficiary deed. Arkansas allows one under Ark. Code § 18-12-608. If the owner signed and recorded it before death, the house passes directly to the named beneficiary, outside probate and outside the affidavit, and a will can't override it.

The details that trip families up:

  • 🗂️ It has to have been recorded before the death. A signed beneficiary deed found in a drawer afterward doesn't work.
  • ✍️ If there are two, the later-signed one controls.
  • 🧾 The beneficiary usually records an affidavit with the death certificate so the title company can see that title has passed.

If a beneficiary deed exists, the beneficiary can generally sell like any owner, and the rest of this guide's probate discussion may not apply. If the beneficiary deed names two siblings, both must sign.

💸 What an Empty Arkansas House Costs: The Tax Freeze Doesn't Pass to Heirs

Arkansas property tax is modest by national standards, but two rules make the first bill after a death bigger than heirs expect.

  • 🧊 The age-65 freeze ends with the owner. Arkansas lets a homeowner who's 65 or older, or disabled, freeze the assessed value of a homestead. The Department of Finance and Administration says plainly that one homeowner's freeze "cannot pass or convey to a subsequent owner." If Dad's assessment was frozen in 2012, the heirs will be taxed on the current value.
  • 🏷️ The homestead credit goes away when nobody lives there. The homestead credit is up to $500 a year, and the legislature authorized up to $600 starting with 2026 tax bills. It applies only to an owner's principal residence. A vacant inherited house doesn't get it.

Arkansas assesses real estate at 20 percent of market value and applies local millage to the assessment. For tax year 2025 (collected in 2026), the state's millage table lists 57.3 mills inside Fayetteville, 57.5 in Fort Smith, 62 in Bentonville's school district and 70 in Little Rock.

🧮 A worked example on that Fayetteville house

Say the house would sell for $300,000 fully updated. It needs a roof, HVAC and flooring, about $38,000, and the family is in three states.

  • 📈 Listing as-is: a buyer might pay around $255,000. After a 5–6 percent commission and about 1.5 percent in seller costs, the heirs net roughly $237,000 to $240,000, plus three to five months of carrying costs ($1,600 to $4,200) and the work of clearing the house.
  • 💵 Cash sale: at 60–80 percent of the $300,000 after-repair value, offers would run $180,000 to $240,000. On this house, after the repairs, our offer would probably land near $205,000 to $215,000, with nothing to clean and no commission.

On those numbers, listing nets the family about $25,000 to $35,000 more. That's real money. A cash sale makes sense here only if the heirs value the time and the clean-out more than that gap, or if something forces speed, such as a Medicaid payoff, a tax certification or a sibling who needs their share now.

📋 What To Do This Week With an Inherited Arkansas House

  1. 🔎 Search the county recorder's index for a beneficiary deed in the deceased owner's name.
  2. 🧮 Add up everything the person owned except the home they lived in, minus debts against it. Under $100,000? The affidavit may fit.
  3. 🏥 If they received Medicaid long-term care, request an estate recovery answer from DHS before anyone signs an affidavit.
  4. 📞 Call a title company and ask what it needs to insure a deed signed by heirs under § 28-41-101, and whether it will wait for the three-month claim period.
  5. 🔐 Change the locks, notify the insurer that the house is vacant, and keep the utilities on.
  6. 🧾 Pull the current tax bill from the county collector, and pay it if October 15 is near.
  7. 🗺️ If any heir won't sign or can't be found, talk with an Arkansas probate attorney about opening an estate instead.

✅ The Bottom Line for Arkansas Heirs

Arkansas gives heirs a shortcut most states don't: the home the person lived in doesn't count toward the $100,000 small-estate limit. If everything else is under that line, there are no unpaid debts and every heir will sign, an affidavit filed at the circuit clerk's office and a newspaper notice can get you to a sale without a personal representative or a court order. If any of those pieces are missing, open an estate and let a personal representative get the order to sell.

Either way, the house should probably be listed if it's in good shape and the family agrees. If it isn't, or the family is stretched across the country, ask us. We'll tell you which route you're on before we talk about a price.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Can an Arkansas small estate affidavit transfer a house?

Yes. Ark. Code § 28-41-101 lets heirs collect and distribute an estate without a personal representative if the net value is $100,000 or less, and the affidavit must include a legal description and valuation of any real property. After filing, the heir must publish notice within 30 days so creditors can present claims.

Does the house count toward Arkansas's $100,000 small estate limit?

Not if it was the decedent's homestead. The statute says the value of the decedent's homestead and any statutory allowances for a spouse or minor children are excluded when calculating the $100,000. A rental house, cabin or other property the person didn't live in counts in full.

How long do I have to wait to file a small estate affidavit in Arkansas?

At least 45 days after the death, and only if no petition to appoint a personal representative is pending or has been granted. If the estate includes real property, notice must be published within 30 days after filing, and creditors then have three months from the first publication.

Who signs the deed when an Arkansas house passes by small estate affidavit?

The heirs who receive the house sign it, because there is no personal representative. If several heirs inherit together, all of them sign, and a title company may ask a married heir's spouse to sign to release dower or curtesy. One heir who refuses can stop the sale.

What does it cost to file a small estate affidavit in Arkansas?

The statute sets a $25 clerk's fee for filing the affidavit and $5 for each certified copy, with no extra fee for attaching a will. Publishing the required newspaper notice costs extra, and most families also pay for title work and, often, a lawyer to prepare the affidavit.

Can we use the affidavit if Mom was on Medicaid in a nursing home?

Only after the Medicaid question is resolved. The affidavit requires a statement that the Department of Human Services furnished no benefits or has been reimbursed. Ask DHS for an estate recovery answer first, and if there is a claim, many families pay it from the sale proceeds at closing.

Does Arkansas allow transfer-on-death deeds for houses?

Yes. Arkansas calls them beneficiary deeds, under Ark. Code § 18-12-608. The deed must be recorded with the county recorder before the owner dies to be valid. If there are two, the one signed last controls, and a will cannot override a recorded beneficiary deed.

Does an Arkansas personal representative need a court order to sell the house?

Generally yes. Under Ark. Code § 28-51-103, estate real property is sold under court order when the sale is in the estate's interest, unless the will gives the personal representative power to sell. An Arkansas probate attorney can tell you whether your will does.

Will the property taxes go up after my parent dies in Arkansas?

Often, yes. An age-65 or disability assessment freeze cannot pass to a new owner, and the homestead credit of up to $500 a year (up to $600 starting with 2026 bills) applies only to an owner's principal residence. A vacant inherited house usually loses both.

Should we list an inherited Arkansas house or sell it for cash?

If the house is in good condition and every heir agrees, a listing with a local agent usually nets more, often by tens of thousands of dollars. A cash sale tends to make sense when the house needs major repairs, the heirs live far away, or a tax or Medicaid payoff needs a firm closing date.
