Partition Lawsuits in Maine: The Statute Only Divides Land, So a Forced Sale of a House Comes From the Court's Equity Power

Read Maine's partition chapter from start to finish and you won't find the word "sale" authorizing one. It provides for commissioners who split land and for owelty payments that even out the shares. When a house can't be split, Maine judges order a sale under their general equity power instead. Here is what that means for co-owners and heirs, and when you should skip the lawsuit altogether.

🗺️ A Statute Built for Dividing Land: Maine's Chapter 719

Maine's partition law lives in Title 14, chapter 719, sections 6501 through 6525. Section 6501 says co-owners holding as tenants in common or joint tenants, in fee or for life, "may be compelled to divide the same by a civil action for partition." The rest of the chapter describes how to divide: after a judgment for partition, the court appoints three or five disinterested commissioners to set off each owner's share (section 6511); a majority of them can sign the report (section 6513); the court may confirm it, send it back, or set it aside; and once confirmed, the judgment is recorded at the registry of deeds (section 6521).

What chapter 719 does not contain is a section authorizing a court to sell the property. That surprises people who have read about partition in other states, where the statute spells out a sale by a referee, sheriff or trustee. In Maine the sale power comes from somewhere else — the general equity jurisdiction of the Superior Court under 14 M.R.S. §6051, which covers disputes between "part owners" of real and personal property (subsection 7) and gives the court broad equity powers (subsection 13). The District Court has parallel equity jurisdiction over part owners under 4 M.R.S. §152.

⚠️ Why this matters: because a forced sale in Maine rests on the judge's equitable discretion rather than a step-by-step statute, the result depends heavily on the facts you put in front of the court — who has lived there, who paid what, whether the property can realistically be split. A co-owner who walks in unprepared can lose leverage they didn't know they had.

A 2022 Superior Court decision shows how it works in practice. In Tompkins v. Buhro, the court relied on section 6051 and an 1895 Law Court case, Williams v. Coombs, 88 Me. 183, found that physical division of the property was impractical, and held that "the only viable method of partition is equitable partition by sale." For an ordinary single-family house, that is the usual destination. For rural acreage, commissioners and a survey are a real alternative.

⚖️ Owelty: Maine's Built-In Way to Keep the Property Whole

Section 6515 gives the commissioners a tool that solves many family disputes without a sale. When a parcel is worth more than one owner's share and can't be divided without great inconvenience, the commissioners can assign the whole thing to one owner, who pays the others money to even it out. That payment is called owelty. The commissioners' report isn't accepted until the money is paid or secured.

Owelty is the Maine answer to "I want to keep Mom's house and my brother wants his money." It is effectively a court-supervised buyout at a value the commissioners set. The owner keeping the property needs to be able to pay or secure the difference — usually with a mortgage — and should come prepared with an appraisal, because the commissioners' valuation drives everything.

Two related rules shape the numbers:

  • 🏗️ Improvements stay with the improver. If an owner had exclusive possession of part of the property by mutual consent and improved it, that part goes to that owner and the improvement's value is credited (section 6514).
  • 🏦 Liens follow the owner. A mortgage or lien on one owner's share stays attached to whatever that owner receives (section 6525).

🏛️ Heirs Have a Second Door: Partition in Probate Court

When the co-owners are heirs or devisees of an estate that hasn't closed, they don't have to start a separate civil suit. Under the Maine Uniform Probate Code, 18-C M.R.S. §3-911, heirs, devisees or the personal representative may petition the county Probate Court for partition before the estate is closed. The Probate Court partitions "in the same manner as" a civil partition action and may direct the personal representative to sell property that can't be divided without prejudice. The expenses are shared in proportion to the parties' interests.

For a family whose parent's estate is already in Probate Court, this is often simpler: the judge already knows the estate, the personal representative already has authority, and a sale can be folded into the administration. Maine's small-estate affidavit (18-C §3-1201) won't help here — it covers only personal property, not a house.

🔍 Where and How a Maine Case Gets Filed

Under section 6502, a partition complaint can be filed in the Superior Court or the District Court for the county where the land lies. The complaint has to describe the property, the type of estate, the share the plaintiff claims, and the other co-tenants and where they live. Because partition affects title, the case is docketed as a proceeding affecting title (14 M.R.S. §2401), and a buyer's title search will turn it up.

The broad sequence:

  1. 📄 Complaint and service on every co-owner, including relatives in other states.
  2. 🧮 Contribution accounting. Under Palanza v. Lufkin, 2002 ME 143, a co-owner who paid more than a fair share of taxes, mortgage or repairs is entitled to contribution, which can be offset by the value of that owner's exclusive use.
  3. 👥 Judgment for partition, then either commissioners to divide (with owelty if needed) or an equitable order to sell.
  4. 📝 Commissioners' report or sale, confirmed by the court and recorded.
  5. 💵 Division of proceeds after costs. Section 6516 lets the court split the partition expenses equitably among the owners.

Maine has not adopted the Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act, so there is no statutory appraisal-and-buyout procedure of the kind many other states now use. Owelty and the court's equitable discretion fill that space.

🪵 Woodlots, Sawmills and Waiting Out the Case

Maine's partition law still carries traces of a timber economy. Section 6506 even lets co-owners of a sawmill get a division by time-share. The rule that matters today is 14 M.R.S. §7505: a co-owner who cuts timber or commits waste while a partition case is pending — or without 30 days' written notice to the other co-owners — is liable for treble damages. If you are fighting over a farmhouse with a woodlot, nobody should run a skidder until the case is over.

⌛ How Long and How Much in Maine

There is no official timeline. A contested Maine partition commonly runs a year or more from complaint to a confirmed sale or division, longer if commissioners and a survey are involved or a co-owner contests the accounting. Each side usually pays its own attorney, and in a fought case those fees can run well into five figures; the court allocates the partition expenses themselves among the owners.

The property costs money the whole time. Take a vacant $220,000 house near Bangor. At a commonly cited effective tax rate of around 1.1–1.2%, with no homestead exemption because no owner lives there, property tax runs about $200–$220 a month. Heat through a Maine winter to keep the pipes from freezing can add $250–$500 a month for half the year; vacant-dwelling insurance $100–$200; plowing and upkeep $75–$150. Over a 15-month case, that is easily $9,000 to $14,000 before attorney fees.

Transfer tax is one place Maine is kind to partition. Under 36 M.R.S. §4641-C, a partition deed among co-owners given without consideration is exempt, except for value a party receives beyond his proportional share. A court-ordered sale to an outside buyer pays the ordinary $2.20 per $500, split between seller and buyer.

💵 A Farmhouse Outside Augusta, Worked Through

Three siblings inherited a farmhouse on twelve acres in Kennebec County. Repaired, it would sell for about $280,000. It needs a new septic system and roof — about $50,000. One sibling, who has lived there and paid the taxes for four years, wants to keep it.

  • ⚖️ Owelty: commissioners value the property as-is at $225,000. The sibling who stays owes each of the other two about $75,000, less a contribution credit for the taxes paid beyond her share, offset by the value of her years of exclusive use. If she can finance about $150,000, she keeps the farm.
  • 🌾 Division: the commissioners could carve off two building lots and leave the farmhouse on the rest — but only if each piece holds roughly equal value, which the septic problem complicates.
  • 🏷️ Agreed listing: as-is, perhaps $220,000 after costs, about $73,000 each, if all three sign.
  • 💵 Cash sale: cash buyers usually pay 60–80% of repaired value. At 70% of $280,000, that is $196,000 — about $65,000 each, but in weeks, without a septic replacement or a year of carrying costs.

🛑 Skip the Lawsuit When the Owners Will Talk

Partition is a tool for deadlock, not a first step. If your co-owners will sign a listing, list it. If one of them wants the house, negotiate the owelty number yourselves with an appraisal and a Maine attorney drafting the deed — the same exemption from transfer tax applies to a voluntary partition deed. ✅ Bottom Line

Maine's partition chapter was written to divide land: commissioners, surveys and owelty payments. A forced sale of a house comes from the judge's equity power, so facts and preparation matter more here than in states with a rigid sale statute. Heirs with an open estate can partition in Probate Court instead. There is no heirs-property buyout statute, but owelty does much of the same work for the owner who wants to keep the place.

If the owners can talk, settle and list. If one wants to keep it, price the owelty. If nobody will move, or the house needs a septic system and a roof before anyone will buy it, ask us for a number to compare.

❓ Maine Partition: Ten Common Questions

Which Maine court handles partition cases?

Under 14 M.R.S. section 6502, a partition complaint can be filed in the Superior Court or the District Court for the county where the land is located. Heirs of an open estate can also petition the county Probate Court under 18-C M.R.S. section 3-911.

Can a Maine court order a co-owned house sold?

Yes, although chapter 719 itself provides for division by commissioners. Courts order a sale under their equity jurisdiction over disputes between part owners, 14 M.R.S. section 6051 for the Superior Court and 4 M.R.S. section 152 for the District Court, when physical division is impractical.

What is owelty in a Maine partition?

Under 14 M.R.S. section 6515, when a parcel is worth more than one owner's share and can't be divided without great inconvenience, it may be assigned to one owner who pays money to the others to equalize the shares. The commissioners' report isn't accepted until that payment is made or secured.

How many commissioners does a Maine partition use?

After a judgment for partition, the court appoints three or five disinterested commissioners to set off the shares under section 6511, and a report signed by a majority is valid under section 6513.

Has Maine adopted the Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act?

No. Maine's statutes contain no version of the Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act. Owelty payments and the court's equitable discretion fill much of the same role.

Can heirs partition a house through the Maine Probate Court?

Yes. Before an estate is closed, heirs, devisees or the personal representative may petition for partition under 18-C M.R.S. section 3-911, and the court may direct the personal representative to sell property that can't be divided without prejudice.

Who pays the costs of a Maine partition?

Section 6516 lets the court divide the partition expenses equitably among the owners. Each owner usually pays his or her own attorney, and contribution for taxes, mortgage and repairs is sorted out in the accounting.

Is a Maine partition deed subject to the real estate transfer tax?

A partition deed among co-owners given without consideration is exempt under 36 M.R.S. section 4641-C, except for any value a party receives beyond his proportional share. A sale to an outside buyer pays the ordinary transfer tax.

Can a co-owner cut timber while a Maine partition case is pending?

Not safely. Under 14 M.R.S. section 7505, a co-tenant who cuts timber or commits waste while a partition action is pending, or without 30 days' written notice to the other co-owners, is liable for treble damages.

Should I file for partition in Maine or sell my share?

If the other owners will negotiate, a settlement or agreed listing usually leaves everyone with more than a court process. Partition makes sense when talks have failed; selling your share for cash makes sense when you need out quickly and can accept a discount.
