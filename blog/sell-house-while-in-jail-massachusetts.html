Selling a Massachusetts House From Jail or Prison: The Homestead Signature, Houses of Correction and an Attorney-Run Closing

Every Massachusetts home carries at least an automatic homestead, and ending it at a sale takes a deed signed by the owner and by any non-owner spouse who lives there. When one spouse is serving time in a county house of correction or a state prison, title attorneys have to decide whether that spouse still "resides" in the home, and most will simply ask for the signature. Here's how to plan for it.

🏡 The Homestead Question: Does a Spouse in Prison Still Live There?

Massachusetts homestead law is Chapter 188 of the General Laws. Under § 10(a)(1), one way a homestead ends is by a deed conveying the home to a non-family member, signed by the owner and, if any, by a non-owner spouse or former spouse who resides in the home as a principal residence on the date of the deed. A normal sale ends the homestead that way.

The words "resides in the home as a principal residence" are where incarceration creates doubt. A spouse in a house of correction for a year hasn't moved anywhere else and may plan to come home. A spouse serving a long state sentence is less clearly a resident. The statute doesn't speak to incarceration directly, and closing attorneys don't like to guess on a title they're certifying. In practice they tend to do one of three things:

  • ✍️ Ask for the spouse's signature on the deed, or a power of attorney letting someone sign for him. This is the safest and most common answer.
  • 📝 Accept an affidavit from the owner about where the spouse lives and why he isn't a resident, if the facts are clear, such as a long sentence and a separation.
  • 📄 Ask for a release of any homestead interest signed before a notary, which is functionally the same as getting his signature.

The easy fix is to plan for the signature from the start. A spouse who can sign once, in front of a notary, a power of attorney that covers releasing homestead rights saves the family from a delayed closing or a buyer who walks.

⚠️ Most important warning: tell the closing attorney at the very beginning that a spouse is incarcerated and whether he lived in the home before he went in. If the homestead question surfaces at the last minute, it can cost weeks, and a buyer with a rate lock may not wait.

📑 Automatic vs. Declared Homestead

Since the 2011 rewrite of Chapter 188, every owner-occupied home in Massachusetts has an automatic homestead exemption of $125,000 without filing anything. An owner can raise that to a declared homestead of $500,000 by recording a declaration at the registry of deeds. A declaration under § 5 has to be signed by each owner who benefits from it, and it lists the non-titled spouse and other family members who live there.

Two things follow for a sale. First, a recorded declaration tells the buyer's attorney exactly which family members claimed the home, so check it at the registry. If it lists the incarcerated spouse, expect the attorney to want his signature. Second, homestead protection is against certain creditors. It doesn't stop a mortgage lender from foreclosing, and it doesn't block taxes. It also doesn't prevent the owner from selling, as long as the right people sign.

🔑 Title Forms: Entirety, Joint Tenancy and Sole Ownership

  • 💑 Tenants by the entirety. Massachusetts allows married couples to hold title this way. Both spouses own the whole, so both sign the deed, whoever is incarcerated.
  • 👥 Joint tenants or tenants in common. Each co-owner signs for his or her own interest. If siblings co-own a house and one is inside, everyone else can't sell the whole house without that person's signature or a court order. See can a co-owner sell without the others.
  • 🏠 Sole owner. The owner signs, and the homestead rule above decides whether a non-owner spouse signs too.
  • 🏦 Trust. Many Massachusetts homes sit in nominee or revocable trusts. The trustee signs, with a trustee certificate recorded at the registry. If the trustee is the one in prison, look to the successor trustee named in the trust.

⚖️ Houses of Correction vs. State Prison

Massachusetts splits sentenced people between two systems, and the split affects how a sale is planned. Sentences of up to two and a half years are generally served in a county house of correction run by the sheriff. Longer felony sentences go to a state prison run by the Department of Correction.

  • 🏢 Houses of correction. Each county sheriff runs its own facility, such as the Suffolk County House of Correction in Boston, the Plymouth County Correctional Facility, the Middlesex facility in Billerica or the Hampden County facility in Ludlow. Professional visits, including notaries, are arranged through each sheriff's office. Because sentences are short, some families wait for release instead of selling. That only works if the mortgage, taxes and insurance can be covered until then.
  • 🏛️ Department of Correction. Men serve longer sentences at institutions such as MCI-Norfolk, MCI-Shirley, Souza-Baranowski, Old Colony in Bridgewater and MCI-Concord. Women are held at MCI-Framingham. Ask the inmate's institutional parole officer or records staff how outside notaries are scheduled.
  • ⛓️ Federal. Federal Medical Center Devens holds federal prisoners in Massachusetts. See the federal prison guide.
  • 🚓 Pretrial. People awaiting trial are usually held in the same county facilities. A defense attorney who already visits can often help line up a notary.

✒️ The Durable Power of Attorney and the Registry of Deeds

Massachusetts powers of attorney for property fall under the Uniform Probate Code's durable power provisions, in Chapter 190B, Article V, Part 5. For a house sale, the document has to satisfy the closing attorney and the registry of deeds:

  • 🖋️ Signed and notarized. The principal signs before a notary with a proper acknowledgment. Without it the registry won't record the power, and the deed signed under it won't be insurable.
  • 🗂️ Recorded with the deed. When an agent signs a deed, the power of attorney is recorded at the registry of deeds for the county or district where the house sits, together with the deed. Bring an original.
  • 🏡 Broad enough. It should let the agent sell real estate, sign the deed and settlement statement, release homestead rights, deal with the lender, and handle the deeds excise.
  • ⏳ Recent. Closing attorneys often ask for an affidavit from the agent that the power hasn't been revoked and the principal is alive, and they may balk at a very old document.

Massachusetts closings are run by attorneys, which helps. The buyer's or lender's closing attorney can often tell you in advance exactly what the power of attorney must say. Ask before the notary visit, not after. See how to get a notary in jail or prison for the logistics.

🏚️ Foreclosure: Right to Cure, Land Court, Then Auction

Massachusetts lenders usually foreclose by power of sale, without a full lawsuit, but there are steps first:

  • 📨 Right-to-cure notice. Under Chapter 244, § 35A, the lender sends a notice giving the owner 90 days to cure the default before it can accelerate the loan.
  • 🎖️ Servicemembers case. Most lenders then file a short case in the Land Court to confirm the owner isn't protected by the federal Servicemembers Civil Relief Act.
  • 📰 Notice and auction. The sale is advertised in a local newspaper for three weeks and the owner gets mailed notice before the auction.

From the first missed payment to an auction often takes a year or more. That leaves room to sell. The notice itself also reminds the owner that the property can be sold before the foreclosure sale and the proceeds used to pay the mortgage off. 🧾 A Brockton Two-Family, by the Numbers

Say there's a two-family in Brockton worth about $520,000 after repairs. It needs a new heating system, electrical updates, a porch and kitchens, about $60,000, and one unit is empty. Massachusetts towns set their own property tax rates per $1,000 of assessed value under Proposition 2½. Here's a rough monthly picture:

  • 🧾 Property tax: about $425 to $575 a month for a property like this, depending on the city's current residential rate and assessment. Check the assessor's database.
  • 🛡️ Insurance: $175 to $350 a month, more if a unit is vacant or the owner can't sign a renewal.
  • 🔥 Heat, water and upkeep: $250 to $450 a month. Oil or gas heat has to stay on through winter, and frozen pipes in a two-family can flood both units.
  • ⚖️ Lawyer: $500 to $1,500 for a power of attorney and a homestead review, plus the seller's attorney fee at closing.

That's about $1,350 to $2,900 a month in the worst stretch, partly offset if the other unit is rented. At closing, the seller pays the deeds excise, $2.28 per $500 of price, or $4.56 per $1,000, in every county except Barnstable, which has its own rate. On $520,000, that's about $2,370.

A cash buyer typically pays 60% to 80% of repaired value, depending on condition and rents. Here that's about $312,000 to $416,000, as-is, with tenants in place.

🙋 When We'd Tell You to List

Massachusetts has some of the tightest housing markets in the country. Ordinary houses in decent condition, from Worcester's three-deckers to Quincy condos, often sell quickly and for strong prices. If the homestead signature is handled, nobody is foreclosing, and a relative or attorney can manage the offer, inspection and closing, list the house. You'll almost always net more than with a cash buyer, including us. A cash sale makes sense when the house needs heavy work, tenants make showings hard, the foreclosure is moving, or the person inside can sign only once and needs a certain closing.

📋 What To Do This Week in Massachusetts

  1. Pull the deed and any declaration of homestead from the registry of deeds, and note how title is held.
  2. Write down whether a non-owner spouse lived in the home before going in, and how long the sentence is.
  3. Find out whether the person is in a county house of correction, a DOC institution or federal custody, and call to ask about notary visits.
  4. Have a Massachusetts attorney draft a durable power of attorney that covers selling, releasing homestead rights and dealing with the lender.
  5. Look for a right-to-cure notice or a Land Court servicemembers filing.
  6. Get a mortgage payoff and the current tax bill, and keep the heat on and the insurance active.
  7. Compare an agent's estimated net, after commission and excise, with a cash offer.

✅ The Massachusetts Takeaway

In Massachusetts, the deed is the hard part, not the foreclosure. Every owner-occupied home has at least a $125,000 automatic homestead, and a sale ends it only with a deed signed by the owner and any non-owner spouse living there. When that spouse is incarcerated, assume the closing attorney will want his signature and get a notarized power of attorney or release before you list. Know whether the person is in a county house of correction or a DOC prison, because a short sentence may make waiting the better choice. Foreclosure here starts with a 90-day right to cure and a Land Court filing, which leaves time to sell. If the house is in good shape, list it. If it isn't, or the calendar is tight, ask us.

❓ Massachusetts Homestead, Prison and Home Sale FAQ

Does my husband have to sign the deed if the Massachusetts house is only in my name?

Possibly. Under M.G.L. c. 188, section 10, a sale ends the homestead with a deed signed by the owner and any non-owner spouse who resides in the home as a principal residence. If he lived there before going in, most closing attorneys will want his signature or a release.

Does an incarcerated spouse still reside in the home for homestead purposes?

The statute doesn't address incarceration directly, so the answer depends on the facts. Because closing attorneys certify title, they usually ask for the signature rather than take the risk.

What is the automatic homestead exemption in Massachusetts?

Every owner-occupied home has an automatic homestead of $125,000. Recording a declaration of homestead at the registry of deeds raises it to $500,000.

Does a homestead stop a sale or a foreclosure in Massachusetts?

No. It protects equity from certain creditors, but it doesn't stop a mortgage lender from foreclosing or prevent the owner from selling when the right people sign.

What is a house of correction in Massachusetts?

A county jail run by the sheriff where people serve sentences of up to two and a half years. Longer sentences are generally served in state prisons run by the Department of Correction.

Does a Massachusetts power of attorney have to be recorded to sell a house?

Yes, in practice. When an agent signs the deed, the notarized power of attorney is recorded at the registry of deeds with the deed so the sale can be insured.

How long does foreclosure take in Massachusetts?

Often a year or more. The lender must first give a 90-day right-to-cure notice under c. 244, section 35A, usually files a servicemembers case in Land Court, and then advertises the auction for three weeks.

Who pays the deeds excise when selling a Massachusetts house?

The seller. It's $2.28 per $500 of price, or $4.56 per $1,000, everywhere except Barnstable County, which has its own rate.

Who handles a Massachusetts real estate closing?

An attorney. Closing attorneys review title, including homestead and power of attorney issues, so ask them early what documents they'll need from the person inside.

Should we list the Massachusetts house or take a cash offer?

List it if the house is in decent shape, the homestead signature is handled and nobody is foreclosing. A cash sale fits when the house needs major work, tenants complicate showings, or the person inside needs one certain closing.
