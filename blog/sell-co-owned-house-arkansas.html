Selling Your Share of a Jointly Owned Arkansas House: Why Your Spouse May Have to Sign Even Though Only You Are on the Deed

Arkansas still recognizes dower and curtesy, so when a married co-owner sells an undivided share, the buyer's title company will usually want the seller's husband or wife to sign a release, even if that spouse never owned any of it. That one rule shapes what a single co-owner can do alone, and it sits on top of Arkansas's deed presumptions and its automatic divorce rule. Here's how it all fits together. You own part of a house in Arkansas, and the other owner either won't sell or won't answer. Maybe it's a brother who lives in it, a former partner, or cousins who inherited it with you. You've read that a co-owner can sell their own share without anyone's permission. In most states that's close to the full answer. In Arkansas there's a wrinkle that catches married sellers at the closing table, so we'll start with it.

💍 The Signature Nobody Expects: A Non-Owner Spouse's Dower or Curtesy

Arkansas is one of a small number of states that still has dower (for a widow) and curtesy (for a widower). Under Ark. Code § 28-11-301, when a married person dies leaving a spouse and children, the surviving spouse is entitled to a life estate in one-third of the lands the deceased owned during the marriage, unless the spouse relinquished that right. The right reaches land sold during the marriage without the spouse's proper release.

That has a direct effect on a co-owner who is married. Say you and your sister inherited a house in Hot Springs as tenants in common, and you're married. Your husband isn't on the deed and has no ownership today. But if you sell your half and later die, his potential dower or curtesy in that half could follow the land into the buyer's hands. That's why Arkansas title companies routinely ask a married seller's spouse to sign the deed, or a separate instrument, to relinquish dower, curtesy and any homestead claim.

  • ✅ If your spouse will sign: this is a five-minute formality at closing or in front of any notary.
  • ⚠️ If you're separated and your spouse won't sign: a buyer for your share may still close, but expect a lower price or a title policy exception. Talk to an Arkansas lawyer before you sign a contract you can't perform.
  • 🧾 If your co-owner is married: the same applies to them when they sell. A buyout of your sister's half may need your sister's husband's signature, too.

⚠️ The warning that matters most: before you promise a closing date to any buyer, including us, list every owner on the deed and every owner's current spouse. In Arkansas the number of signatures a title company wants is often larger than the number of names on the deed, and a missing spouse is the most common reason a "simple" sale of a share stalls.

🧾 Read the Deed First: What Arkansas Presumes From the Wording

Your options depend on what kind of co-ownership the deed created, and Arkansas has a clear default rule. Under Ark. Code § 18-12-603, every interest granted or devised to two or more people is a tenancy in common "unless expressly declared in the grant or devise to be a joint tenancy." Married couples are treated differently: a deed to a husband and wife is generally presumed to create a tenancy by the entirety under Arkansas case law.

Your interest still exists, but a buyer will want the chain of title cleaned up first. Our guide to what happens to a house when someone dies without a will explains how heirs end up as co-owners.

🔓 Selling Only Your Share as a Tenant in Common

A tenant in common owns an undivided fraction of the whole house. You can sell, give away or leave that fraction in a will without asking the other owners. The buyer steps into your shoes: they become a co-owner with your brother or cousin, with the same right to use the house and the same right to ask a court to divide or sell it.

What that means in practice:

  • 🏷️ Price. Nobody pays half the value of the house for half of a house they can't live in or finance. Buyers of fractional interests price in the cost and time of a buyout negotiation or a partition case. Expect an offer meaningfully below your share of market value, and get more than one.
  • 🧑‍🤝‍🧑 Your co-owner gets a new partner. Many co-owners who wouldn't talk to a sibling will negotiate with a buyer who has no emotional history with the house. Many others won't, and the buyer then files for partition.
  • 🌾 Large rural parcels have a waiting period. Under Ark. Code § 18-60-404, someone who buys an undivided interest in a parcel of at least 10 acres and isn't related to another owner within the fourth degree can't file for partition for three years, unless the buyer owns 50 percent or more. That rule makes shares of family farmland harder to sell to outsiders. A typical house lot is well under 10 acres, so it rarely applies to a house in town.

🔗 Joint Tenants: A Sale of Your Interest Ends Survivorship

If the deed expressly says joint tenancy, each owner's interest goes to the survivors at death instead of through a will. In Arkansas, as in most common-law states, a joint tenant who conveys their interest severs the joint tenancy as to that interest. The buyer becomes a tenant in common with the remaining owners.

That has two practical consequences. First, a co-owner who is worried about dying before the other owner and losing their share to survivorship can end that risk by conveying their interest, even to a trust or another family member. That's a step to take with a lawyer, because a transfer done only to defeat survivorship can be challenged in some situations. Second, if you're the one who benefits from survivorship, understand that it isn't guaranteed: your co-owner can end it without your consent.

💑 Tenancy by the Entirety: Locked Until Death or Divorce

A married couple who own as tenants by the entirety own the house together as a unit. Neither spouse can force a sale or get a buyer to pay real money for a one-spouse interest while the marriage lasts. Arkansas then gives two specific exits.

📜 Divorce dissolves the entirety automatically

The usual exit

Under Ark. Code § 9-12-317, when a circuit court enters a final divorce decree, any estate by the entirety or survivorship is automatically dissolved unless the order says otherwise, and the ex-spouses are treated as tenants in common. The court can divide the property under the marital-property statute, § 9-12-315, and must explain in writing any unequal split. Our guide to selling a house during a divorce covers the sale itself.

⚖️ A felony during the marriage can change the split

Arkansas-specific

The same statute says that if one spouse was found guilty of, or pleaded guilty or no contest to, a felony during the marriage and within three years of the divorce filing, and the other spouse didn't benefit from it, the judge may award the property to the other spouse or divide it in any proportion the judge finds fair. Few states have a rule like this.

🏠 The homestead exception to post-divorce partition

Read before filing

Arkansas's partition statute, § 18-60-401, lets former spouses who held by the entirety petition to partition after a divorce, except when the property is a homestead "occupied by either of the divorced persons." If your ex still lives in the house, ask an Arkansas lawyer how that exception applies before filing anything, because it can stop a partition case that would otherwise be routine.

⚖️ When the Other Owner Won't Sell: Partition in Circuit Court

Any tenant in common, joint tenant or divorced former spouse (subject to the homestead exception) can ask the circuit court in the county where the land is to partition it. The court can divide the land if that's fair, or order it sold if division would cause "great prejudice" to the owners, which is almost always the answer for a single house. Arkansas lets the court order a public auction or a negotiated sale, including through a real estate broker. For inherited family land, the Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act, which Arkansas adopted in 2015, adds an appraisal and a chance for the other heirs to buy out the owner who asked for the sale.

One Arkansas cost rule matters even before you file: the court allows a reasonable fee to the attorney who brought the partition case, taxed as costs and paid by all the owners in proportion to their shares. Our partition lawsuit explainer covers how these cases run nationally, and a partition case usually ends in a settlement long before an auction.

💵 The Numbers on a Jonesboro House Owned 50/50

Take a three-bedroom house in Jonesboro that two cousins inherited equally. Fixed up, it would sell for about $220,000. As it stands, with an older roof and dated kitchen, a retail buyer might pay $185,000.

  • 🧾 Carrying it while you disagree. Arkansas assesses property at 20 percent of market value. The 2025 millage inside the Jonesboro school district in Jonesboro is 41.2 mills, so at an assessor's value of $190,000 the tax is about $1,566 a year, or $130 a month, with no homestead credit if neither cousin lives there. Add vacant-home insurance at $100–$180 a month, and utilities and lawn care at $120–$250, and an empty house costs the two of you roughly $350 to $560 a month.
  • 📈 Selling the whole house together, as-is: $185,000 less about 7 percent in commission and costs leaves roughly $172,000, or $86,000 each.
  • 💵 Selling the whole house to a cash buyer together: at 60–80 percent of the $220,000 after-repair value, $132,000 to $176,000. On this house, we'd likely be around $150,000 to $160,000, or $75,000 to $80,000 each, closed in a few weeks with no repairs.
  • 🧩 Selling only your half while your cousin refuses: a share buyer is buying a lawsuit or a negotiation. Offers for a 50 percent interest here might run $45,000 to $65,000. That's well below $86,000, and it only makes sense if the alternative is years of paying half the bills on a house you can't use, or paying your own lawyer to file for partition.

The honest summary: if both owners will sign, sell the whole house together, usually through an agent. Selling only a share is the right tool when one owner is stuck, not a way to get top dollar.

📋 What To Do This Week as an Arkansas Co-Owner

  1. 📄 Get a copy of the recorded deed from the circuit clerk's or county recorder's office and read exactly how the owners are named.
  2. 💍 Write down every owner's marital status and current spouse, and ask each married owner whether their spouse will sign a dower or curtesy release.
  3. 🧾 Pull the tax bill and insurance policy, and note who has been paying them, with receipts.
  4. ✉️ Send the other owner a short written proposal: sell together, buy me out at a stated price, or I'll consider selling my share.
  5. ⚖️ If you're divorced or divorcing, read the decree or ask your divorce lawyer what it says about the house before doing anything else.
  6. 🔎 Get a broker's opinion of value, so every number you discuss starts from the same place.

✅ Where This Leaves an Arkansas Co-Owner

In Arkansas you can sell your own share of a house held as tenants in common or joint tenants without the other owner's consent. What you may not be able to do is sell it without your spouse, because dower and curtesy reach your share. Married couples who own by the entirety are locked together until death or a divorce decree, which converts their ownership into shares automatically. And when nobody will budge, the circuit court can order the house sold, with the filing lawyer's fee shared by every owner.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Can one co-owner sell a jointly owned house in Arkansas without the other?

No owner can sell the whole house alone. A tenant in common or joint tenant can sell their own undivided interest without the others' consent, and the buyer becomes a co-owner. Married couples who own by the entirety can't do even that in any practical way while the marriage lasts.

Does my spouse have to sign if only my name is on the deed to my share?

Usually, in Arkansas. Because Arkansas still has dower and curtesy under Ark. Code § 28-11-301, a married seller's spouse can have a potential life interest in land owned during the marriage. Title companies routinely ask the non-owner spouse to sign a release of dower, curtesy and homestead.

What does an Arkansas deed to two unmarried people create?

A tenancy in common. Ark. Code § 18-12-603 says an interest granted to two or more persons is a tenancy in common unless the deed expressly declares a joint tenancy. Each owner can sell or leave their share to anyone.

What happens to a tenancy by the entirety when an Arkansas couple divorces?

Under Ark. Code § 9-12-317, a final divorce decree automatically dissolves any estate by the entirety or survivorship unless the court order says otherwise, and the former spouses are treated as tenants in common. The court can then divide the property and must explain any unequal split in writing.

Can a felony conviction affect how an Arkansas divorce court splits the house?

Yes. If one spouse was found guilty of, or pleaded guilty or no contest to, a felony during the marriage and within three years of the divorce filing, and the other spouse didn't benefit from it, the judge may award the property to the other spouse or divide it in any proportion that is equitable.

Can I partition a house with my ex-spouse if they still live in it?

Possibly not. Arkansas's partition statute, § 18-60-401, allows former spouses who held by the entirety to seek partition after divorce, except when the property is a homestead occupied by either of the divorced persons. An Arkansas attorney should review the decree and the facts before you file.

Who pays the lawyer in an Arkansas partition case?

Under Ark. Code § 18-60-419, when the court orders partition in kind or a sale, it allows a reasonable fee to the attorney who brought the suit for work that benefits everyone. The fee is taxed as costs and paid by all owners in proportion to their interests, and it can't exceed $40,000.

Can a stranger who buys my share of family farmland force a partition in Arkansas?

Not right away if the parcel is 10 acres or more. Ark. Code § 18-60-404 bars a buyer who isn't related to another owner within the fourth degree from suing for partition for three years after buying an undivided interest, unless the buyer owns at least 50 percent.

How much will someone pay for my half of an Arkansas house?

Less than half the house's value, because the buyer inherits a co-owner, the carrying costs and the time it takes to negotiate a buyout or win a partition case. Offers for a 50 percent share often land well below half the as-is market value. Get more than one written offer.

Should Arkansas co-owners list the house or sell for cash?

If every owner and every owner's spouse will sign and the house is in good condition, listing with a local agent usually nets the most. Selling to a cash buyer, or selling only one share, makes more sense when an owner refuses, the house needs major work, or the owners want a fast, fixed result.
