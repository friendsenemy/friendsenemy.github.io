Selling an Iowa House From Prison or Jail: Passing the Abstract Examination, the Spouse's Signature and Restitution Liens

Iowa bars private title insurance. Instead, an Iowa attorney examines the abstract and writes a title opinion, and anything that looks wrong becomes an objection that has to be cured before closing. When the owner is incarcerated, that examining attorney is the person you have to satisfy. Here's what they look for and how to give it to them.

By Dustin Ray, Founder of Pages of Purpose LLC. Not legal advice. Laws differ by state, and Iowa title examination, power of attorney and restitution questions depend on the facts. Before a power of attorney, purchase agreement or deed is signed, get advice from an attorney licensed in Iowa, where the house sits.

In most states, when a homeowner is incarcerated, the family worries about getting a notary into the facility. In Iowa that's usually the easy part. The harder part is a person you may never meet: the attorney who examines the abstract for the buyer. Iowa doesn't allow title insurance. Title is proved by an abstract, a running history of every recorded document affecting the land, and an Iowa attorney reads it and writes an opinion. If something looks off, it becomes a written objection. A sale from prison produces more objections than most, and each one has to be cured before anyone gets paid.

For the basics that apply in every state, see our national guide to selling a house while incarcerated. This page is about Iowa.

📚 Iowa Has No Title Insurance, So a Lawyer Reads Every Page

Iowa Code 515.48 excludes title insurance from what insurance companies may sell in Iowa, and the Iowa Supreme Court upheld the ban in 1977. In its place, the state runs Iowa Title Guaranty, a division of the Iowa Finance Authority, which is the only entity authorized to offer title coverage in the state. Its own program manual puts the system simply: in Iowa, the status of title is determined by abstract examination, and only attorneys may render title opinions.

For a seller, that means three practical things:

  • 📘 The abstract gets updated. The seller's abstract is brought current by an abstracter so it shows everything recorded since the last sale, including any judgments against the owner.
  • 🧑‍⚖️ An attorney examines it. The buyer's attorney reviews it and lists objections in a title opinion.
  • 🛠️ Objections must be cured. A missing signature, an unrecorded power of attorney, an unreleased lien: each has to be fixed with a document, an affidavit or a payoff.

This isn't bad news. A careful examination protects the buyer and the seller. But it means a sale from prison will be read closely, and the paperwork needs to anticipate what the examiner will ask for.

📝 The Iowa Statutory Power of Attorney Form

Iowa adopted the Uniform Power of Attorney Act as chapter 633B, effective July 1, 2014. Under section 633B.105, the power of attorney must be signed by the principal and acknowledged before a notary public. A notarized signature is presumed genuine, and the agent can't be the notary. Iowa doesn't require witnesses.

Section 633B.301 provides the "Iowa Statutory Power of Attorney Form," which is what examining attorneys expect to see. When it grants real property authority, section 633B.204 lets the agent sell, convey and quitclaim the property, and it expressly covers transferring or releasing the principal's homestead rights under 561.13 and chapter 597. That last part matters, as the next sections explain.

Chapter 633B doesn't require a power of attorney to be recorded to be valid. But under Iowa Code 558.41, an unrecorded instrument doesn't bind later purchasers without notice, and an examiner wants the abstract to show the agent's authority. In practice, record it with the deed.

💑 The Spouse Must Join, Even Off Title

Iowa Code 561.13 says that if the owner is married, a conveyance or encumbrance of the homestead is not valid unless the spouse signs the same or a like instrument, or a power of attorney for signing it. The spouse doesn't have to be on the deed. Iowa's homestead has no dollar cap; it's limited by area instead, half an acre inside a city plat or 40 acres outside one, under section 561.2.

That produces two common prison situations:

  • 🏠 The owner is inside, the spouse is home. The owner signs through an agent, and the spouse signs in person. If the spouse is also the agent, the documents can be drafted so the spouse signs both ways.
  • 🔒 The spouse is inside, the owner is home. The owner can't sell the homestead alone. The incarcerated spouse has to sign, or sign a power of attorney covering the release of homestead rights. Section 633B.204 makes that power available.

Section 561.13 lists exceptions, including a divorce decree that disposes of the property. If the marriage is ending, read our guide to selling a house during a divorce before you decide who signs what.

🏢 Notarizing Inside an Iowa DOC Institution

Iowa DOC policy IO-OR-05 requires each institution to have at least one employee qualified to administer oaths and acknowledgments, and a person's attorney can also notarize during a legal visit. A newer policy, AD-CR-04, effective May 2025, says designated staff check that a signature is valid and, if it is, notarize it free of charge. Under Iowa's notary law a notary may still use reasonable discretion to decline a notarization, so send a complete document with no blanks and a cover letter explaining what it is.

County jails aren't covered by DOC policy. If the person is held in a county jail awaiting trial or serving a short sentence, call the jail administrator and ask how outside notaries or attorney visits are handled.

🧾 Restitution Judgments and Liens

Iowa is direct about restitution. Under Iowa Code 910.7A, a restitution order is a judgment and lien against all property of the defendant, may be recorded, and is enforceable like a civil judgment. Section 910.10 also lets the prosecutor or the victim file a separate restitution lien against identified property. Iowa splits restitution into categories: category A covers fines, penalties and surcharges, and category B covers items like court costs, court-appointed attorney fees and crime-victim compensation reimbursement, which are ordered subject to the person's ability to pay.

Judgments in Iowa are liens on real estate for ten years under section 624.23, which also says they don't attach to a homestead except as section 561.21 allows, with a 30-day written demand procedure to clear a lien claimed on a homestead. Whether a particular restitution lien reaches a homestead is a legal question for an Iowa attorney. For planning purposes, assume the examiner will want any recorded restitution paid or resolved at closing.

🏷️ The Homestead Credit and an Empty House

Iowa's homestead tax credit, under chapter 425, reduces the bill for an owner-occupied home. Section 425.2 continues the credit only while the property is used as a homestead by the owner or the owner's spouse on July 1 and occupied by one of them for at least six months of the year. An owner who stops using it as a homestead has to tell the assessor by July 1.

If a spouse still lives there, the credit can continue. If the house is empty during a long sentence, it's likely to lose the credit, which raises the tax and should factor into how long you hold it.

🧠 Why a Conservatorship Rarely Fits

Some families ask whether a court can simply appoint someone to sell. Iowa's 2019 guardianship and conservatorship reform requires clear and convincing evidence that the person's decision-making capacity is so impaired that they can't make or carry out important financial decisions (Iowa Code 633.553), and the court must consider less restrictive alternatives. Being incarcerated doesn't meet that standard. In Iowa the realistic routes are a power of attorney signed inside, a signature in person, or waiting.

🧮 A Des Moines House, by the Numbers

Consider a two-bedroom house on Des Moines's south side worth about $240,000 repaired. It needs a roof, a water heater and flooring, about $35,000. The owner has a five-year sentence, is unmarried, and the house is empty.

Holding costs. Iowa's average effective property tax rate on owner-occupied homes is about 1.33 percent. On this house that's roughly $265 a month, and more once the homestead credit is lost. In Des Moines the consolidated levy for taxes payable in 2026-27 is about $43.74 per $1,000 of taxable value, which is assessed value after Iowa's residential rollback. Add vacant-home insurance of $100 to $200 a month, and utilities of $125 to $225 a month to keep the heat on through an Iowa winter, plus lawn and snow care. Expect $550 to $750 a month to hold it empty.

Selling costs. The transfer tax under Iowa Code 428A.1 is 80 cents for each $500 over the first $500, so a $170,000 sale owes about $271. The deed is filed with a declaration of value and, under section 558.69, a groundwater hazard statement about wells, septic systems and similar conditions, signed by the seller or the seller's agent. Updating the abstract and the attorney's title work carry modest fees. A power of attorney from an Iowa attorney typically costs a few hundred dollars.

The cash offer. Cash buyers usually pay 60 to 80 percent of repaired value, here $144,000 to $192,000. With $35,000 of work, a fair offer would land around $160,000 to $175,000. A retail sale as-is might bring $195,000 to $205,000, minus commission and several months of holding. On a house that only needed cosmetic work, listing would usually win.

🧭 Iowa Options Side by Side

Best fit for most

Notarized by a staff notary or an attorney on a legal visit, recorded with the deed, with homestead release authority if a spouse is involved. Cures the biggest title objection in advance.

✍️ The owner signs the deed in person

Simple when timing allows

If the buyer and closing are ready, a deed signed and notarized at the facility avoids the power of attorney entirely. It needs careful coordination with the facility and the closing attorney.

🏛️ Conservatorship

Usually not available

Iowa's capacity standard means incarceration alone won't support one. Don't plan around it.

⏳ Waiting for release

Short sentences only

Reasonable for a few months with a spouse living in the home. Expensive for years with an empty house and a lost homestead credit.

🗣️ Our Honest Advice on Listing

If the house is in decent shape, there's no restitution or other judgment on the abstract, the spouse is cooperative and a valid power of attorney is in place, list it with an agent. Retail buyers will pay more than a cash buyer for a house that needs little, and an Iowa closing will go smoothly once the title objections are anticipated. Selling to a cash buyer like us makes sense when the house needs real repairs, it sits empty through winter, judgments need to be paid from the proceeds, or the family needs one buyer who will wait while objections get cured.

When the house is owned with siblings or other relatives, read can a co-owner sell without the others. Iowa moved partition into statute in 2018 (Iowa Code chapter 651). The default is partition by sale, and the chapter includes heirs-property protections, including an appraisal and a buyout right for cotenants. 📋 What To Do This Week in Iowa

  1. Find the abstract. Ask family, the mortgage lender, or the attorney who handled the purchase. If it's lost, an abstracter can help.
  2. Check the county recorder for any recorded restitution lien and ask the clerk of court about judgments.
  3. Confirm whether the owner is married, which decides whether a spouse must sign under 561.13.
  4. Have an Iowa attorney prepare the statutory power of attorney with real property and homestead authority.
  5. Ask the institution or jail how notary requests work, and send a complete document with no blanks.
  6. If the house is empty, notify the assessor if needed, keep the heat on and switch to vacant-home insurance.
  7. Compare an agent's estimated net with a cash offer.

✅ Iowa Bottom Line

In Iowa the examining attorney is the gatekeeper. Without title insurance, an attorney reads the abstract and lists objections, and a sale from prison raises predictable ones: the agent's authority, a spouse's signature on the homestead, and restitution or other judgments. Prepare for all three before you list. Use the Iowa statutory power of attorney form, notarized inside, with homestead release authority, and record it with the deed. A house in good repair with clean title should go to an agent. An empty house needing work, with liens to settle, is where a cash buyer is worth considering.

❓ Iowa Title, Prison Notary and Home Sale FAQ

Why is there no title insurance when selling a house in Iowa?

Iowa law excludes title insurance from what insurers may sell, and the Iowa Supreme Court upheld that ban. Instead, an Iowa attorney examines the abstract and gives a title opinion, and the state's Iowa Title Guaranty program offers coverage.

What is a title objection in an Iowa sale?

It is a problem the examining attorney finds in the abstract, such as a missing spouse signature, an unrecorded power of attorney or a judgment lien. Objections have to be cured, usually with a document, a payoff or an affidavit, before the sale closes.

Does an Iowa power of attorney have to be notarized?

Yes. Iowa Code 633B.105 says the power of attorney must be acknowledged before a notary public, and a notarized signature is presumed genuine. The agent cannot be the notary.

Can the agent under an Iowa power of attorney release homestead rights?

Yes, if the power grants real property authority. Iowa Code 633B.204 expressly includes transferring or releasing the principal's homestead rights under 561.13 and chapter 597.

Does my spouse have to sign if the Iowa house is only in my name?

If it is your homestead and you are married, yes. Iowa Code 561.13 says a conveyance of the homestead is not valid unless the spouse signs the same or a like instrument, or a power of attorney to sign it.

Will an Iowa prison notarize documents for an inmate?

Iowa DOC policy requires each institution to have at least one employee qualified as a notary, and the 2025 policy on signatures says valid signatures are notarized free of charge. County jails set their own rules.

Is restitution a lien on an Iowa house?

Iowa Code 910.7A says a restitution order is a judgment and lien against the defendant's property and may be recorded. A separate restitution lien can also be filed under 910.10. The examining attorney will expect it to be paid or resolved at closing.

Does an Iowa house keep the homestead tax credit while the owner is in prison?

Under Iowa Code 425.2 the credit requires the owner or the owner's spouse to use the house as a homestead and occupy it at least six months of the year. If a spouse still lives there, it can continue. An empty house likely loses it.

Can an Iowa court appoint a conservator because the owner is in prison?

Not on that basis alone. Iowa Code 633.553 requires clear and convincing evidence that the person's decision-making capacity is impaired, and the court must consider less restrictive alternatives such as a power of attorney.

How much is the transfer tax on an Iowa home sale?

Eighty cents for each 500 dollars of price over the first 500 dollars, under Iowa Code 428A.1. On a 170,000 dollar sale that is about 271 dollars. A declaration of value and a groundwater hazard statement are filed with the deed.
