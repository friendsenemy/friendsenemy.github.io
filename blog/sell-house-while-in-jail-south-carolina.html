Selling a South Carolina House From Jail or Prison: Two Witnesses, a Recorded Power of Attorney and an Attorney at the Table

South Carolina treats a power of attorney almost like a will: it has to be signed in front of two witnesses and then acknowledged or proved, and once the owner can no longer act it only works after it is recorded. Every closing is run by a South Carolina lawyer who will check all of it. Get the signing right inside SCDC or a county detention center and the rest of the sale is ordinary.

By Dustin Ray, Founder of Pages of Purpose LLC. This is not legal advice. Laws differ by state, South Carolina's probate code and corrections procedures change, and every closing attorney and title insurer applies its own requirements. Talk to an attorney licensed in South Carolina, where the property is, before anyone signs a power of attorney or a deed.

✍️ A Power of Attorney Signed Like a Will

South Carolina adopted the Uniform Power of Attorney Act in 2016, effective January 1, 2017, as Article 8 of the Probate Code. Most states that adopted the uniform act only ask for a signature, with a notary optional. South Carolina added its own requirement. Under S.C. Code § 62-8-105, a power of attorney must be:

  1. signed by the principal, or by someone else in the principal's presence at his direction;
  2. attested with the same formality, and the same witness requirements, as a South Carolina will; and
  3. acknowledged or proved under § 30-5-30, the statute that governs recording deeds.

A South Carolina will needs two witnesses. So, in practice, a power of attorney an agent will use to sell a house needs the owner's signature, two witnesses who watch him sign, and then either a notary's acknowledgment or a witness's sworn statement proving the signature. A document missing any of the three is the most common reason a closing attorney says no.

🧾 "Acknowledged or proved" gives you two ways to finish

Section 30-5-30 lets a document be recorded if it is acknowledged, or if its execution is "proved by the affidavit of a subscribing witness" sworn before an officer who can give oaths. South Carolina lawyers call that second route a probate. It matters behind a fence. If the owner signs in front of two witnesses but no notary is in the room, one of those witnesses can later sign the probate affidavit in front of a notary outside the facility. That is often easier than getting a notary through security.

The witnesses should be adults with nothing to gain from the sale. The agent named in the document should not be one of them. Ask the closing attorney who will close your sale before anyone signs. Some will draft the document for you, and many prefer to see a draft before it goes in.

⚠️ The most important warning: don't mail a store-bought form into a facility and hope. A form with one notary and no witnesses, or witnesses with no acknowledgment or probate, gets rejected at closing, and the owner has to sign again. Each round trip through facility mail and visitation can cost weeks, which matters if taxes are past due or a foreclosure is already filed.

🗄️ When the Document Has to Be on Record First

South Carolina adds a second rule most families don't know about. Under § 62-8-109(c), once the principal is incapacitated, the agent may act only if the power of attorney has been recorded "in the same manner as a deed" in the county where the principal lives. Before recording, the agent's authority can't be used.

Being in prison is not incapacity. A healthy owner serving a sentence can still make decisions, so this rule doesn't apply to most of our sellers. But a title company won't take your word for it, and illness behind bars is common. Closing attorneys almost always record the power of attorney with the Register of Deeds anyway, because the buyer's title insurer wants the agent's authority in the public record alongside the deed. Plan on recording it from the start. It costs a modest county fee and avoids an argument at the closing table.

🏛️ Which office records it

Most South Carolina counties call their land records office the Register of Deeds. A few use the Register of Mesne Conveyances (Charleston's office is the RMC). Either way, it is the county where the house sits. The recorded power of attorney then shows up in the chain of title next to the deed the agent signs.

🔒 Getting Signatures Inside SCDC and County Detention Centers

Where the owner is held decides how the signing works:

  • 🏢 County detention centers. People awaiting trial or serving short sentences are held by the county, such as Alvin S. Glenn in Richland County or the Charleston County Detention Center. Notary access is set by each jail. Some have staff notaries, while others let an outside notary in through the professional-visit process. Call the jail's administration line, not general visitation.
  • 🏛️ SCDC institutions. After sentencing, the person usually moves to the Department of Corrections, at places like Broad River in Columbia, Perry in Pelzer, Lieber in Ridgeville or Lee in Bishopville. Requests for legal-document help usually go through the person's caseworker. Expect the institution to control who can be a witness, when, and in what room.
  • ⛓️ Federal prisons. South Carolina residents can be held at FCI Edgefield, FCI Estill, FCI Williamsburg or out of state. Our federal prison guide covers Bureau of Prisons notary requests.

The witness rule makes facility logistics harder than in states that only need a notary. You need three people in the room, or two witnesses plus a later probate affidavit. Before you schedule anything, ask the facility whether its staff can serve as witnesses and whether it has a notary. Then ask the closing attorney whether staff witnesses will be acceptable. For the mechanics of getting a notary into a lockup, see how to get a notary in jail or prison.

⏱️ Sign before the transfer if you can

The easiest time to sign is while the person is still in the county jail and lawyers can visit freely. A transfer to an SCDC reception center can mean weeks of intake and classification with limited visits. If sentencing is coming, get the power of attorney done first.

⚖️ Your Closing Is Run by a Lawyer, and That Helps

South Carolina is one of the states where a real estate closing has to be supervised by a licensed attorney. That comes from the South Carolina Supreme Court's unauthorized-practice cases, starting with State v. Buyers Service Co. in 1987. In most states a title company runs the closing. Here, a lawyer reviews the title, the power of attorney, the deed and the money.

For a family dealing with a prison, that lawyer is useful. The same attorney who will judge whether the power of attorney is good enough can often draft it, tell you whether staff witnesses are acceptable, and record it. Ask whether the closing attorney represents you, the buyer or the lender, and if it isn't you, consider hiring your own attorney to protect the owner's interest. A buyer's or lender's closing attorney isn't working for the owner.

South Carolina also has a deed recording fee, a transfer tax. It is normally charged at $3.70 per $1,000 of price, the state's $2.60 plus the county's $1.10, and by local custom the seller usually pays it. On a $150,000 sale that's $555.

🏚️ The 4% Rate an Empty House Can Lose

This cost catches families off guard. Under S.C. Code § 12-43-220(c), an owner-occupied legal residence is assessed at 4% of fair market value. Most other residential property, including rentals and second homes, is assessed at 6%. A legal residence is also exempt from school operating millage, which is often the largest line on a South Carolina tax bill. Together, the two changes can more than double the bill on the same house.

The 4% ratio depends on the owner actually living there. If the owner is serving a long sentence, the house is rented out, or the assessor learns it is vacant, the county can review the legal residence classification. Some families sell partly to stop paying a bill that has jumped. If the county sends a notice about the residence status, don't ignore it. Answer it, and ask the auditor what happens when the owner is incarcerated rather than living somewhere else.

If the taxes go unpaid, the county tax collector sells the property at the delinquent tax sale, usually in the fall, and the owner has one year to redeem. It's a real deadline, but slower than many states. It still adds interest the owner pays to redeem, and it puts the title in a buyer's hands if nobody acts.

💵 A Columbia House: Hold, List or Sell As-Is

Take a three-bedroom house in northeast Richland County worth about $210,000 fixed up. It needs a roof, HVAC and floors, about $35,000. While it sits empty, expect roughly:

  • 🧾 Property tax: about $110 to $140 a month if it keeps the 4% legal residence ratio, and often $250 to $350 a month if it is reclassified at 6% without the school operating exemption. Ask the Richland County auditor for the actual figures.
  • 🛡️ Vacant-dwelling insurance: $110 to $250 a month. A standard homeowner's policy may not cover a house that has been empty for more than 30 or 60 days.
  • 💡 Utilities and upkeep: $150 to $250 a month for power, water and yard work, more in summer when the house needs air conditioning to keep down mold.
  • ⚖️ Attorney fees: roughly $300 to $800 for a power of attorney drafted for the purpose, and South Carolina closing fees of about $700 to $1,500, depending on the attorney.

That's about $400 to $900 a month just to hold the house. A cash buyer typically pays about 60% to 80% of repaired value for a house that needs this much work. Here, that is about $126,000 to $160,000, and after the work we'd have to do, $135,000 to $150,000 is a realistic range.

🏦 When the Lender Files: South Carolina's Court Foreclosures

South Carolina mortgages are foreclosed in court. The lender sues in the county's Court of Common Pleas, the case is usually referred to the master-in-equity, and the house is sold at a public auction, typically on the first Monday of the month. An owner in prison is served like anyone else, so make sure someone outside is watching the mail.

Two features are worth knowing. First, the case takes months, which gives the agent time to sell if the power of attorney is already in place. Second, under § 15-39-720, when the lender asks for a deficiency judgment, bidding doesn't close on sale day. It stays open for 30 days, so others can raise the bid. A sale before the auction almost always beats waiting for that. For general tactics, see how to stop a foreclosure by selling.

South Carolina's homestead exemption in § 15-41-30 protects some of a debtor's equity in a residence from judgment creditors. The base figure is $50,000 per debtor, adjusted for inflation over time. It doesn't stop a mortgage foreclosure or a tax sale, and it doesn't help much if the house isn't the debtor's residence.

🛤️ Four South Carolina Routes

1. Witnessed and recorded POA, then a normal listing

Best price, if the house is sound

Two witnesses, a notary or a probate affidavit, recording with the Register of Deeds, and a relative works with a listing agent. If the house is in good shape and the mortgage is current, this usually nets the most.

2. Same POA, then a cash sale as-is

Fastest with repairs

For houses that need major work, are behind on taxes, or are heading to a master-in-equity sale. Fewer showings and no repairs to manage from a distance.

3. Owner signs the deed directly

Works for short stays

If the owner is in a county jail for a short time and the closing attorney can arrange witnesses and a notary, the owner can sign the deed himself and skip the power of attorney. South Carolina deeds are customarily witnessed by two people too, so the same three-person signing applies.

4. Co-owner sale or a partition action

When the owner won't cooperate

If the person inside co-owns the house and won't sign, a co-owner can ask the court to partition it. It is slow and expensive, but it is a way out. See whether a co-owner can sell without the other and how partition lawsuits work.

📋 This Week's South Carolina Checklist

  1. Pull the deed from the county Register of Deeds and confirm exactly whose names are on it.
  2. Choose the closing attorney now, and ask them to draft or approve the power of attorney before it goes into the facility.
  3. Call the facility's administration line and ask about notaries, who can serve as witnesses, and how legal visits are booked.
  4. Sign with two witnesses and either a notary or a later probate affidavit, then record it in the county where the house is.
  5. Check the tax bill and the county's records for the legal residence classification, and pay anything delinquent before the fall tax sale.
  6. Get two opinions of value: a listing agent's and a cash offer. Compare the net, not the headline price.

✅ The Short Version

In South Carolina, the paperwork is the hard part, and it is fixable. A power of attorney that will hold up at closing needs two witnesses and either a notary or a witness's probate affidavit, and it should be recorded where the house is. Bring the closing attorney in before anyone signs, because that lawyer decides whether the document works. Watch the legal residence assessment and the tax sale calendar while you do. Once the signing is done, the choice between listing and a cash sale comes down to the condition of the house and how much time you have.

❓ South Carolina Questions Families Ask Us

Does a South Carolina power of attorney have to be witnessed?

Yes. Under S.C. Code 62-8-105, a power of attorney must be attested with the same formality and witness requirements as a South Carolina will, which means two witnesses, and it must also be acknowledged before a notary or proved by a witness affidavit under 30-5-30.

My son signed a POA in jail with only a notary. Is it good enough to sell his house?

Probably not for a South Carolina closing. Without two witnesses it doesn't meet 62-8-105, and the closing attorney is likely to reject it. He will need to sign again in front of two witnesses, with a notary or a later witness affidavit.

What is a probate of a deed or power of attorney in South Carolina?

It's a sworn affidavit by one of the subscribing witnesses, made before a notary or other officer, stating that he saw the document signed. Section 30-5-30 accepts it in place of the signer's own acknowledgment, which helps when no notary can get into the facility at the time of signing.

Does the power of attorney have to be recorded before my relative can sell?

If the owner has become incapacitated, yes: 62-8-109(c) says the agent can act only after the power of attorney is recorded like a deed. If the owner is healthy, recording isn't strictly required by that section, but closing attorneys nearly always record it with the deed anyway.

Why does a lawyer have to handle the closing in South Carolina?

The South Carolina Supreme Court has held that conducting a real estate closing is the practice of law, so a licensed attorney must supervise it. That attorney reviews the power of attorney, so involve them early.

Can prison staff be witnesses for a South Carolina power of attorney?

Often they can, but it depends on the facility's policy, and the closing attorney has to be comfortable with it. Witnesses should be adults with no stake in the sale, and the agent named in the document shouldn't be one of them.

Will the property taxes go up while the owner is in prison?

They can. A legal residence is assessed at 4% and exempt from school operating millage; other residential property is assessed at 6%. If the county decides the house is no longer the owner's legal residence, the bill can more than double.

How long before a South Carolina tax sale takes the house?

Delinquent property is sold at the county's tax sale, usually in the fall, and the owner then has one year to redeem by paying the bid amount plus interest. After that, the buyer can get a tax deed.

How long does a South Carolina foreclosure take?

Usually several months or more, because it goes through the Court of Common Pleas and typically the master-in-equity. When the lender seeks a deficiency judgment, bidding stays open 30 days after the auction under 15-39-720.

Should we list the house or sell it for cash?

If the house is in good condition and the mortgage is current, listing will usually net more, even after commission. A cash sale makes sense when the house needs major work, taxes or foreclosure are on a deadline, or nobody can manage showings and repairs.
