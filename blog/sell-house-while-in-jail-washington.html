Selling a Washington House When One Spouse Is in Jail or Prison: Community Property, Two Acknowledgments and the DOC

In Washington, a house bought during a marriage is presumed to belong to both spouses no matter whose name is on the deed, and RCW 26.16.030 says neither one can sell it unless both join the deed and both acknowledge it. When one spouse is in a county jail or a Department of Corrections prison, that second acknowledgment is the piece families have to plan around.

By Dustin Ray, Founder of Pages of Purpose LLC. Nothing on this page is legal advice. Laws differ by state, Washington statutes and DOC policies change, and title insurers make their own calls on community property. Talk to an attorney licensed in Washington, the state where the property is, before signing a power of attorney, a deed or a quitclaim.

Escrow officers in Washington see this situation often. A wife wants to sell the house in Spokane Valley. The deed shows only her name. Her husband is serving a sentence at Airway Heights. The preliminary title report comes back with a requirement: the husband must join in the deed, or sign a quitclaim, before the sale can close. She didn't know she needed him. That requirement comes from Washington's community property law, and it affects more sales involving incarceration than anything else in this state.

This guide is about that requirement: when it applies, how to meet it from inside a DOC facility, and what it costs to wait. For the general picture across states, see our national guide to selling a house while incarcerated.

🤝 The House Probably Belongs to Both of You

Washington is one of nine community property states. Under RCW 26.16.030, property acquired during a marriage or state registered domestic partnership is community property, unless it falls into the separate-property categories in RCW 26.16.010 and 26.16.020: property owned before the marriage, or acquired later by gift or inheritance.

Whose name is on the deed doesn't decide it. A house bought in 2015 by a couple married in 2010 is presumed community property even if only one spouse signed the purchase papers. The spouse who's left off title still owns half of the community.

Then comes the sale rule. RCW 26.16.030(3) says neither spouse "shall sell, convey, or encumber the community real property without the other spouse ... joining in the execution of the deed," and that the deed "must be acknowledged by both spouses." Two signatures and two notarized acknowledgments. One spouse can't sell community real estate alone.

📅 Separate property is different, but title companies still ask

If the house was bought before the wedding, or inherited, it's usually the owner's separate property, and the other spouse doesn't have to join the sale. But mortgage payments from community income, a refinance during the marriage, or a remodel paid from wages can give the community a claim. Title insurers don't sort that out themselves. They usually ask the non-owning spouse to sign a quitclaim deed or a separate-property acknowledgment, just to be safe. So even with separate property, plan on needing something signed by the spouse who's inside.

⚠️ Most important warning: don't sign a purchase agreement until you know whether both spouses have to sign. If the spouse inside won't or can't sign, the sale may not close, and a buyer who has given notice to move can claim damages. Get a preliminary title report first; it costs little and tells you what the title insurer will require.

🔁 Two Situations, Two Signing Plans

  • 🏠 The spouse at home wants to sell. The incarcerated spouse has to join. He can sign the deed himself in front of a notary at the facility, sign a quitclaim or separate-property acknowledgment if the house is separate, or sign a power of attorney naming someone to sign for him.
  • 🔒 The incarcerated spouse owns the house and wants to sell. He needs someone outside with authority, usually under a power of attorney, and the spouse at home still joins and acknowledges the deed for herself.

Washington doesn't bar a spouse from acting as the other spouse's agent. But a title insurer may be uneasy when one person signs a community property deed twice, once for herself and once as agent. Many prefer to see a different agent, or a power of attorney that specifically authorizes conveying the couple's community real estate. Ask escrow before drafting.

If you're divorcing, the decree assigns the house and ends community ownership going forward. See our guide to selling a house during a divorce.

🖊️ Washington's Witness Option, and Why a Notary Is Still Better

Washington adopted the Uniform Power of Attorney Act in 2017, codified at RCW 11.125. Under RCW 11.125.050, a power of attorney has to be signed and dated, and the signature must be either acknowledged before a notary or attested by two competent witnesses. The witnesses can't be related to the principal or the agent by blood, marriage or registered domestic partnership, and they can't be the principal's home care providers or care providers where he lives.

That witness option is useful for medical and financial decisions. For a house, it is the weaker choice. The agent will sign a deed that has to be recorded with the county auditor, and recording offices and title insurers want an acknowledged power of attorney. A witnessed-only document may be valid, but escrow is likely to reject it for a real estate closing. Use a notary.

For a Washington house, the power of attorney should:

  1. expressly grant authority over real property, and if possible name the specific property and the authority to sign the deed, the excise tax affidavit and closing papers;
  2. say whether the agent can convey community real property and join for the principal;
  3. be acknowledged before a notary; and
  4. be recorded with the county auditor (or the recorder's office in counties that have one) where the house is, usually at closing.

🏛️ Notaries in DOC Facilities and County Jails

Most people sentenced to more than a year go to a Department of Corrections facility after processing at the Washington Corrections Center in Shelton, or the Washington Corrections Center for Women in Gig Harbor. Long-term facilities include the Washington State Penitentiary in Walla Walla, Monroe Correctional Complex, Coyote Ridge in Connell, Airway Heights near Spokane and Stafford Creek near Aberdeen. Pretrial detainees and short sentences stay in county jails, such as the King County Correctional Facility in Seattle or the Spokane County Jail. Federal detainees are often at FDC SeaTac.

DOC facilities usually handle legal paperwork through the incarcerated person's counselor or a designated staff notary, and outside notaries need approval to visit. County jails vary a lot. Ask early, and send the documents in only after escrow has approved them. The steps are in our guide to getting a notary in jail or prison.

Timing tip: the reception center stay in Shelton is a busy, restricted period. If sentencing is coming, have the power of attorney signed in the county jail before the transfer.

🧾 The Real Estate Excise Tax the Seller Pays

Washington charges a real estate excise tax on every sale, and by custom the seller pays it. Under RCW 82.45.060, the state rate is graduated: 1.1% on the portion of the price up to the first threshold, which has been $525,000 since 2023 and is adjusted every four years, then 1.28% on the next tier, with higher rates above $1.5 million and above $3 million. Cities and counties add a local rate, often 0.25% to 0.5%.

On a $230,000 sale in a city with a 0.5% local rate, the excise tax is about $3,680. Escrow collects it at closing with the excise tax affidavit, which the agent can sign under a proper power of attorney. It applies to a cash sale and a listing alike. It's a cost of selling in Washington, not a reason to pick one route over another.

💵 Spokane Numbers

Take a 1950s house in north Spokane worth about $340,000 updated. It needs a roof, a furnace and a bathroom, about $45,000. While it sits empty:

  • 🧾 Property tax: about $250 to $310 a month, based on a levy near 1% of assessed value in much of Spokane County.
  • 🛡️ Vacant-dwelling insurance: $125 to $275 a month. Freeze damage is a real risk in eastern Washington winters, so keep the heat on and have someone check the house.
  • 💡 Utilities and upkeep: $175 to $300 a month, including heat, water, sewer, garbage and snow removal.
  • ⚖️ Lawyer: $350 to $900 for a power of attorney and quitclaim review. Escrow fees are separate and are usually split or paid by the buyer in a cash sale.

Cash buyers usually pay 60% to 80% of the repaired value, so $204,000 to $272,000 here. With $45,000 of work, $215,000 to $240,000 is a realistic range.

🏦 Washington's Foreclosure Steps and the Mediation Program

Most Washington homes are financed with deeds of trust, and the lender usually forecloses without a lawsuit under the Deeds of Trust Act, RCW 61.24. Before the trustee can sell, the borrower gets a notice of default, and a notice of trustee's sale must be recorded and sent at least 90 days before the sale. In practice, the first missed payment to the auction usually takes most of a year.

Washington's Foreclosure Fairness Act lets an owner-occupant be referred to foreclosure mediation by a housing counselor or attorney. For a family dealing with an incarceration, mediation can buy time to get both spouses' signatures and close a sale. If the lender sells nonjudicially, the owner's right to redeem is cut off, and the lender generally can't pursue a deficiency on that deed of trust. See how to stop a foreclosure by selling.

Unpaid property taxes move slower. Washington counties generally start tax foreclosure once taxes are three years delinquent. That's not a reason to ignore them, because interest and penalties add up.

🛡️ The homestead exemption, briefly

Since 2021, RCW 6.13.030 has set the homestead exemption from judgment creditors at the greater of $125,000 or the county's median sale price for a single-family home in the prior year. It can protect equity from ordinary judgment creditors while the owner is inside. It doesn't stop a mortgage foreclosure or a tax foreclosure.

✅ Bottom Line for Washington

In Washington, ask when the house was bought before asking whose name is on the deed. A home bought during the marriage is presumed community property, and RCW 26.16.030 requires both spouses to join the deed and acknowledge it. Even separate property usually requires a quitclaim from the other spouse before a title insurer will sign off. Get a notarized power of attorney or deed signed at the facility, have escrow approve it first, and keep an eye on the trustee's sale timeline. Once both signatures are handled, the house can be sold like any other.

❓ Washington Community Property and Jail Sale Questions

My husband is in prison and the Washington house is only in my name. Does he have to sign?

Probably, if you bought it during the marriage. It's presumed community property, and RCW 26.16.030 says neither spouse can sell community real property without the other joining the deed and acknowledging it.

What if I owned the house before we married?

Then it's likely your separate property, and his joinder isn't legally required. Title insurers still often ask the other spouse for a quitclaim deed or separate-property acknowledgment, because community funds may have paid the mortgage.

Does a Washington power of attorney need a notary?

RCW 11.125.050 allows either a notary acknowledgment or two qualified witnesses. For a real estate sale, use a notary, because the document will be recorded with the deed and escrow will expect an acknowledged power of attorney.

Who can't be a witness on a Washington power of attorney?

Witnesses can't be related by blood, marriage or registered domestic partnership to the principal or the agent, and can't be the principal's home care providers or care providers where he lives.

Can I be my incarcerated husband's agent to sell our community home?

Washington law doesn't forbid it, but some title insurers are uncomfortable with one person signing twice. A power of attorney that expressly authorizes conveying community real property, or a different agent, avoids that problem. Ask escrow first.

Who pays the real estate excise tax in Washington?

By custom the seller does. The state rate is 1.1% of the price up to the first threshold, currently $525,000, with higher tiers above that, plus a local rate that is often 0.25% to 0.5%.

How long does a Washington foreclosure take?

Usually most of a year from the first missed payment. Under RCW 61.24 a notice of trustee's sale must be recorded and sent at least 90 days before the sale, and an owner-occupant can be referred to foreclosure mediation.

Can a DOC prison provide a notary?

Many DOC facilities have staff notaries or let approved outside notaries visit, usually arranged through the person's counselor. County jails differ widely. Ask before sending documents in.

Does the Washington homestead exemption stop a foreclosure?

No. It protects equity, at least $125,000 or the county median home price, from ordinary judgment creditors, but it doesn't stop a mortgage lender or a county tax foreclosure.

Should we list the house or sell to a cash buyer?

List it if it's in decent shape, the loan is current and both spouses can sign; a listing will usually net more. A cash sale fits a house that needs major work, a scheduled trustee's sale, or a spouse who can sign only once.
