Selling a New Jersey House From Jail or Prison: Why the Tax Collector, Not the Bank, Usually Sets Your Deadline

New Jersey's mortgage foreclosures are among the slowest in the country, often taking years in the Chancery Division. Its property taxes are the highest, and unpaid taxes are sold each year as tax sale certificates that can be foreclosed in as little as six months to two years. When the owner is in a county jail or a state prison, that second clock is usually the one that matters.

🧾 Nearly $10,000 a Year Keeps Coming While the Owner Is Away

New Jersey's average residential property tax bill has been close to $10,000 a year in recent years, the highest in the nation. Taxes are billed quarterly by the municipality, due February 1, May 1, August 1 and November 1. When an owner goes to prison, those bills often go unpaid. Mortgage escrow usually covers them until the loan defaults. Then the lender stops paying, or stops advancing, and the municipality's tax collector starts the tax sale process.

🏷️ How a New Jersey tax sale certificate works

Each municipality holds a tax sale, often once a year, for properties with delinquent taxes and municipal charges such as water and sewer. Investors bid the interest rate down from as high as 18%, and can bid premiums above that, for the right to collect. The winner pays the delinquent amount and gets a tax sale certificate, a lien on the house. If no investor bids, the municipality keeps the certificate.

Under N.J.S.A. 54:5-86, as amended by P.L. 2024, c. 39:

  • 🏛️ A municipality holding the certificate can file to foreclose the right of redemption after six months.
  • 💼 A private investor holding the certificate can file after two years from the date of the sale.
  • 📈 Meanwhile, the holder can pay later taxes and add them to the lien, and interest and allowed costs keep growing.

If the foreclosure ends in final judgment and the owner hasn't redeemed, the right to redeem is cut off. Before 2024, that usually meant the certificate holder took the house and all of its equity. After the U.S. Supreme Court's 2023 decision in Tyler v. Hennepin County, New Jersey rewrote the law. Now an owner can demand, in writing, that the property be sold at a sheriff's sale or online auction instead, so surplus value above the lien comes back to the owner. That protects some of the equity, but a forced auction still usually brings less than a market sale.

⚠️ Most important warning: a letter from a company you've never heard of that mentions a "tax sale certificate" or "right of redemption" is not junk mail. Find out who holds the certificate, when it was sold, and whether a foreclosure complaint has been filed. A private holder's two-year clock may already be running out. Selling the house, with the certificate paid off at closing, keeps the full equity. Waiting for an auction doesn't.

🐢 The Mortgage Foreclosure Moves Slowly

New Jersey forecloses mortgages through the courts. Under the Fair Foreclosure Act, a residential lender must send a notice of intention to foreclose at least 30 days before filing. The case is filed in the Superior Court, Chancery Division. Uncontested cases are processed by the Office of Foreclosure, and owner-occupants can ask for the court's foreclosure mediation program. It is common for a New Jersey foreclosure to take two years or more from filing to sheriff's sale.

After judgment, the sheriff of the county sells the house. Under N.J.S.A. 2A:17-36, the debtor can get two adjournments of the sale as of right, each of up to 30 days. After the sale, the owner can still redeem by paying the judgment amount, generally within the 10-day period before the sheriff's deed is delivered.

All that time is helpful to a family with someone inside, as long as the property taxes are under control. The mortgage case rarely forces a rushed decision. Unpaid taxes can. For general strategy, see how to stop a foreclosure by selling the house.

📝 The New Jersey Power of Attorney and the Spouse's Possession Right

New Jersey's Revised Durable Power of Attorney Act sets a simple formality rule. Under N.J.S.A. 46:2B-8.9, a power of attorney must be in writing, signed, and acknowledged in the manner set forth in N.J.S.A. 46:14-2.1, the same acknowledgment used for deeds. For a sale, the acknowledgment before a notary or attorney isn't optional. The agent's deed will be recorded with the county clerk, and the title company will want the power of attorney recorded too.

For a sale, the document should expressly authorize selling real property, ideally describe the specific property, and let the agent sign the deed, the seller's residency certification (the GIT/REP forms) and the closing statement. Ask the title company to approve the draft first.

🏡 The non-owner spouse's right of joint possession

New Jersey abolished dower and curtesy for property acquired after 1980 and replaced them with a different protection. Under N.J.S.A. 3B:28-3, a married person is entitled to joint possession with the spouse of real property they occupy jointly as their principal matrimonial residence. A buyer takes subject to that right unless the spouse releases it, a court ends it, or another listed event occurs, such as divorce, death or voluntary abandonment.

In practice, when the house is the couple's main home, title companies will ask the spouse who isn't on the deed to sign a release, or to join the deed. If that spouse is the one in prison, plan for that signature too. Incarceration usually isn't treated as voluntary abandonment, so don't count on the right having ended. If a divorce is pending, see selling a house during a divorce.

🏢 Signing Inside NJDOC, County Jails and Fort Dix

  • 🏢 County jails. Pretrial detainees and short sentences stay in county facilities, such as the Essex County Correctional Facility in Newark or the Mercer County Correction Center. Each sets its own rules for notary and attorney visits.
  • 🏛️ NJDOC. State-sentenced people are processed at the Central Reception and Assignment Facility in Trenton, then sent to institutions that include New Jersey State Prison in Trenton, East Jersey State Prison in Avenel, Northern State Prison in Newark, South Woods in Bridgeton, Bayside in Leesburg and Edna Mahan for women in Clinton. Legal-document requests generally go through the person's social worker or institutional staff.
  • ⛓️ Federal. FCI Fort Dix holds many federal prisoners from New Jersey. See our federal prison guide.

New Jersey lets attorneys take acknowledgments as well as notaries, which helps. The owner's criminal defense attorney, who can already visit, may be able to take the acknowledgment on the power of attorney during a legal visit. Ask. For other options, see how to get a notary in jail or prison.

💵 A Hamilton Township House, by the Month

Take a Cape Cod in Hamilton Township, Mercer County, worth about $320,000 after repairs. It needs a roof, an oil-to-gas conversion and a kitchen, about $55,000. Assuming an effective tax rate around 2.5%, a common level in central New Jersey suburbs, here's what it costs to hold:

  • 🧾 Property tax: about $650 a month, around $8,000 a year. Check the actual bill on the municipal tax collector's page.
  • 🛡️ Vacant-dwelling insurance: $150 to $325 a month.
  • 💡 Utilities and upkeep: $175 to $325 a month, including heat through the winter so pipes don't freeze, plus water and sewer charges the municipality can also sell at tax sale.
  • ⚖️ Lawyer: $400 to $1,200 for a power of attorney, title coordination and an attorney-review contract. In North Jersey, an attorney often handles the whole closing.

That's roughly $1,000 to $1,500 a month before mortgage interest. Ten months of waiting can eat $10,000 to $15,000 of equity. If a tax sale certificate is outstanding, it's also accruing interest at the bid rate.

The seller also pays New Jersey's realty transfer fee, a graduated fee based on price, with reduced rates for qualifying seniors, disabled and low-income sellers. A cash buyer typically pays 60% to 80% of repaired value, so $192,000 to $256,000 here. With $55,000 of work, $200,000 to $230,000 is realistic.

🔀 New Jersey Paths, Ranked by Urgency

Taxes current, no certificate sold

List it

With a recorded power of attorney and the spouse's release if needed, list with an agent. Expect a three-business-day attorney review period after signing a broker-prepared contract, which is standard here. This usually nets the most.

Certificate sold, no foreclosure filed yet

Sell soon, redeem at closing

The owner can still redeem by paying the certificate amount, interest and costs, and a sale does exactly that at closing. A quick listing can work if the house shows well. If it needs heavy work, a cash closing avoids months of added interest.

Tax foreclosure complaint filed

Act now

Redemption is still possible until final judgment, but costs rise. Get a payoff figure from the holder or its attorney, have the power of attorney signed, and close fast. If that can't happen, look into the owner's right to demand a sheriff's sale or auction so surplus equity is protected.

Co-owned with relatives who won't agree

Partition in the Chancery Division

✅ New Jersey in One Paragraph

In New Jersey, the bank is rarely what forces the decision. Mortgage foreclosures take years in the Chancery Division, but property taxes that average close to $10,000 a year keep coming, and once a tax sale certificate is sold, the holder can file to foreclose in six months if it's the municipality or two years if it's an investor. The 2024 law protects some surplus equity through an auction, but a sale on your own terms protects more. Record an acknowledged power of attorney, get the spouse's release if the house was the couple's home, pay off any certificate at closing, and choose between listing and cash based on how far along the tax clock is.

❓ New Jersey Tax Sale, Foreclosure and Jail Sale FAQ

What is a tax sale certificate in New Jersey?

When property taxes or municipal charges go unpaid, the municipality sells a lien on the house at its tax sale. The buyer, an investor or the town, gets a tax sale certificate that earns interest and can eventually be foreclosed if the owner doesn't redeem it.

How soon can a tax sale certificate holder foreclose in New Jersey?

Under N.J.S.A. 54:5-86, a municipality holding the certificate can file after six months, and a private holder can file after two years from the date of the tax sale.

Can my father still sell his house after a tax sale certificate was sold?

Yes, as long as the right of redemption hasn't been cut off by final judgment. The certificate is paid off from the sale proceeds at closing, with interest and allowed costs.

What changed in New Jersey's tax sale law in 2024?

P.L. 2024, c. 39, passed after the Supreme Court's Tyler v. Hennepin County decision, lets an owner facing tax lien foreclosure demand in writing that the property be sold at a sheriff's sale or auction, so surplus equity above the lien can be paid to the owner.

How long does a mortgage foreclosure take in New Jersey?

Often two years or more. Lenders must send a notice of intention to foreclose, file in the Chancery Division, get a judgment and then hold a sheriff's sale, which the debtor can adjourn twice for up to 30 days each.

Does a New Jersey power of attorney have to be notarized?

It must be acknowledged in the manner set out in N.J.S.A. 46:14-2.1, before a notary or another officer authorized to take acknowledgments, such as a New Jersey attorney. For a sale, it should also be recorded with the county clerk.

My husband is in prison and the house is only in my name. Does he need to sign?

If it was your principal matrimonial residence together, he likely has a right of joint possession under N.J.S.A. 3B:28-3, and the title company will usually want him to release it before a buyer takes title.

Can a defense attorney notarize documents in jail?

New Jersey attorneys can take acknowledgments, so a lawyer already visiting may be able to acknowledge the power of attorney. Confirm with the attorney and the facility first.

Who pays the realty transfer fee in New Jersey?

The seller. It's graduated by price, and qualifying senior, disabled and low-income sellers can pay reduced rates.

Should we list the New Jersey house or take a cash offer?

List it if taxes are current, the mortgage isn't in default and someone can manage attorney review and showings. A cash sale fits when a tax certificate is accruing interest, a foreclosure has been filed, or the house needs major work.
