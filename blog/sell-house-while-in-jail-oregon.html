Selling an Oregon House From Jail or Prison: You Keep the Right to Sell, but Not the Right to Act for Others

Oregon law says a felony conviction doesn't take away the right to hold, receive and transfer property. So an owner at Coffee Creek or Snake River can still sell his own house. What a felony prison sentence does suspend is the right to hold a position of private trust, which matters when the person inside is the executor of a parent's estate or the trustee of a family trust. Figuring out which role he's in is the first step in Oregon.

By Dustin Ray, Founder of Pages of Purpose LLC. Not legal advice. Laws differ by state, and how Oregon courts apply the private-trust rule to a particular estate or trust depends on the facts and the documents. Talk to an attorney licensed in Oregon, where the property is, before anyone signs a power of attorney, a deed or a sale agreement.

🗽 Oregon Lets a Prisoner Keep and Sell Property

Oregon is explicit on this point. ORS 137.275 says that, except as otherwise provided by law, a person convicted of a felony doesn't suffer civil death or disability, loss of civil rights, or forfeiture of estate or property. The person keeps all rights, including the right to hold, receive and transfer property, to make contracts, and to bring and defend lawsuits.

So an Oregon owner serving a prison sentence can still sell his house, sign a contract, sign a deed, and name an agent to do those things for him. Nobody needs a court's permission, and the State doesn't take the house because of the conviction. Some states still have older laws that cloud that answer. Oregon's statute settles it.

🚫 What a Felony Sentence Does Take: Positions of Private Trust

The next statute is where Oregon becomes unusual. Under ORS 137.281, when a person is sentenced to a term of incarceration in a felony case, he loses certain rights from the date of sentencing until release or until the conviction is set aside. They are holding public office, serving on a jury, voting, and holding a position of private trust. The rights come back automatically on release.

"Position of private trust" isn't defined in the statute, but the common reading covers fiduciary roles held for other people, such as:

  • 📜 Personal representative of someone else's estate, Oregon's term for an executor or administrator.
  • 🏦 Trustee of a living trust or family trust that holds a house.
  • 🧑‍⚖️ Conservator or guardian for another person.
  • ✍️ Agent under someone else's power of attorney.

Note the limits. The rule is about felony cases with a sentence of incarceration. A person held pretrial in the Multnomah County Detention Center, or serving a misdemeanor jail sentence, isn't covered by it. And it's about acting for others, not about the person's own property.

⚠️ Most important warning: if the person in prison is the named executor, trustee or agent for a house that belongs to someone else, don't build a sale around his signature. A buyer's title company will likely refuse it, and a deed he signs in that role may be challenged. Plan for a successor to act instead.

🧩 Owner, Executor, Trustee or Agent?

Sort the situation into one of these four boxes before anyone drafts anything.

He owns the house himself

He can sell

ORS 137.275 protects his right to transfer property. He signs a power of attorney inside, or signs the deed and closing papers in front of a notary. If he's married and holds title with his spouse as tenants by the entirety, which Oregon recognizes, the spouse signs too.

He's the named personal representative

Someone else should serve

While a felony prison term runs, he likely can't hold that position. Another heir, or an alternate named in the will, can ask the circuit court's probate department to appoint them instead. He can still sell his own inherited share later, once the estate distributes it. For family disagreements, see when heirs can't agree.

He's trustee of a family trust

The successor trustee steps in

Most trust documents name a successor trustee for when the trustee can't serve. That person signs the sale. If the trust names no one, an Oregon lawyer can explain the court route to appoint one.

He's the agent under a parent's power of attorney

Use a backup agent

A well-drafted power of attorney names an alternate. If it doesn't and the parent is still competent, the parent can sign a new one naming someone else.

🪪 The Power of Attorney and the County Clerk

For an owner who will sell through an agent, Oregon's power of attorney rules are in ORS Chapter 127. The practical requirements for a house sale come from the title company and the county clerk who records the deed:

  • 🖋️ Notarized. The principal's signature should be acknowledged before a notary so the document can be recorded and relied on.
  • 🗃️ Recorded. When an agent signs a deed, the power of attorney is recorded with the county clerk where the house sits, usually just before the deed.
  • 🏠 Specific. It should authorize selling real property, name the property by address and legal description, and allow the agent to sign the deed, the settlement statement and tax withholding forms.
  • 🔁 Pick the agent with the trust rule in mind. If the owner is incarcerated, the agent must be someone outside. If a spouse is also in custody on a felony sentence, that spouse can't be the agent either.

Oregon notaries can also perform remote notarizations by video under state rules, but prison phone and video systems rarely allow it. Plan on an in-person notary. Our guide to getting a notary in jail or prison explains how outside notaries are usually approved.

🌲 Coffee Creek, Salem, Ontario and Sheridan

  • 🏛️ Oregon Department of Corrections intake. Everyone sentenced to state prison goes first to Coffee Creek Correctional Facility in Wilsonville, which is also the state's prison for women. Men then move to institutions such as Oregon State Penitentiary and Oregon State Correctional Institution in Salem, Snake River in Ontario, Two Rivers in Umatilla or Eastern Oregon in Pendleton.
  • 📍 Timing at intake. A notary visit at Coffee Creek is often easier to arrange from the Portland area than a trip to Ontario later. If the sale is likely, sign the power of attorney before transfer.
  • 🚔 County jails. Multnomah County holds detainees at the Multnomah County Detention Center downtown and Inverness Jail. Washington, Clackamas, Marion and Lane Counties run their own jails, each with its own professional-visit rules.
  • ⛓️ Federal. FCI Sheridan holds federal prisoners in Oregon. See our federal prison guide.

📉 Oregon's Two Foreclosure Clocks

🏦 The lender's clock

Most Oregon home loans use a trust deed, which lets the trustee sell without a lawsuit. The trustee must give notice of the sale at least 120 days before the sale date. Many larger lenders must first offer a resolution conference through the Oregon Foreclosure Avoidance Program before foreclosing on a residential trust deed. The owner can usually stop the sale by curing the default until shortly before the sale, and a sale of the house to a buyer that pays the loan off works the same way. Four months of notice is longer than many western states give, which helps a family working through prison mail.

🧾 The county's clock

Oregon property taxes are due November 15, with a discount for paying the full year by then. Under ORS 312.010, real property becomes subject to foreclosure for delinquent taxes once three years have passed since the taxes became delinquent. After the county gets its judgment, there's generally a two-year redemption period before the county takes a deed. That's slow, but for someone serving a long sentence it's a real deadline, and interest runs the whole time.

🧮 A Salem House, Month by Month

Take a 1950s ranch in northeast Salem worth about $420,000 after repairs. It needs a roof, a heat pump, new flooring and some dry rot work, around $45,000. Oregon's Measure 50 limits how fast a home's assessed value can grow, generally 3% a year, so long-held houses often carry tax bills well below what their market value would suggest. Rough monthly carrying costs:

  • 🧾 Property tax: about $280 to $380 a month for a house like this. Check the Marion County tax statement.
  • 🛡️ Vacant-dwelling insurance: $125 to $300 a month.
  • 🌧️ Utilities and upkeep: $175 to $325 a month. Western Oregon's wet winters punish empty houses with moss, gutters and moisture problems.
  • ⚖️ Lawyer: $500 to $1,500 for a power of attorney, or more if a new personal representative or trustee must be appointed.

That's roughly $580 to $1,000 a month before the mortgage. One good thing about selling in Oregon: the state constitution bars new real estate transfer taxes, and only Washington County still charges one, at $1 per $1,000. Escrow and title fees are customary costs instead.

A cash buyer typically offers 60% to 80% of repaired value, or about $252,000 to $336,000 here. In Salem's market, a listing would likely do better if the house can be shown and financed.

💬 Where a Normal Sale Wins

Portland's suburbs, Salem, Eugene and Bend all have steady buyer demand. If the house is in fair shape, the right person has authority to sign, and someone can manage showings and the inspection, list it with a local broker. That will usually beat any cash offer, ours included, by a meaningful amount. A cash sale is the better tool when the house needs repairs lenders won't finance, a trustee's sale is scheduled, or the family needs a single closing date while an estate or trust is changing hands.

📋 What To Do This Week in Oregon

  1. Pull the deed and any will or trust, and decide whether the person inside is the owner or a fiduciary for someone else.
  2. If he's a personal representative or trustee and was sentenced on a felony, contact an Oregon probate lawyer about appointing a successor.
  3. If he's the owner, have a power of attorney drafted and arrange a notary at the jail, Coffee Creek or his institution.
  4. Check for a trustee's notice of sale and the date, and look up the property tax status with the county.
  5. Insure the house as vacant and set up someone to check on it every week.
  6. Get a loan payoff and compare a broker's net estimate with a cash offer.

✅ Bottom Line in Oregon

Oregon draws a clear line. A person serving a felony prison sentence keeps the right to own, receive and transfer property, so he can sell his own house through a notarized, recorded power of attorney. What he loses until release is the right to hold a position of private trust, so if he's the executor, trustee or agent for someone else's house, a successor should sign instead. Lenders must give at least 120 days' notice before a trustee's sale, and counties can foreclose for taxes after three years. If the house is sound and the right signer is in place, list it. ❓ Oregon Prisoner Property Rights and Home Sale FAQ

Can a prisoner in Oregon sell his own house?

Yes. ORS 137.275 says a person convicted of a felony keeps all civil rights, including the right to hold, receive and transfer property and to make contracts, except as otherwise provided by law.

What rights does an Oregon felony prison sentence suspend?

Under ORS 137.281, from sentencing until release, the person can't hold public office, serve on a jury, vote, or hold a position of private trust. The rights return automatically on release.

Can an incarcerated person be the executor of an estate in Oregon?

Generally not while serving a felony prison term, because being personal representative is a position of private trust. Another heir or alternate can ask the probate court to be appointed instead.

What if the person in prison is trustee of a family trust?

The successor trustee named in the trust document usually takes over and signs the sale. If none is named, an Oregon attorney can explain how a court appoints one.

Does the private trust rule apply to someone in county jail awaiting trial?

No. It applies when a person is sentenced to incarceration in a felony case, not to pretrial detention or misdemeanor sentences.

Does an Oregon power of attorney need to be recorded to sell a house?

When an agent signs the deed, title companies expect a notarized power of attorney recorded with the county clerk where the house is located, usually just before the deed.

How long does a trustee's foreclosure take in Oregon?

The trustee must give notice of the sale at least 120 days before the sale date, and many lenders must first offer a resolution conference, so the process usually takes five months or more.

When can an Oregon county foreclose for unpaid property taxes?

Under ORS 312.010, once three years have passed since the taxes became delinquent. After the county's judgment there's generally a two-year redemption period.

Is there a transfer tax when selling a house in Oregon?

Only in Washington County, at $1 per $1,000. Oregon's constitution bars new real estate transfer taxes elsewhere in the state.

Should we list the Oregon house or sell it for cash?

List it if the house is in fair condition, the right person can sign, and someone can manage showings. A cash sale fits when a trustee's sale is scheduled, the house needs major repairs, or an estate or trust needs a quick, certain closing.
