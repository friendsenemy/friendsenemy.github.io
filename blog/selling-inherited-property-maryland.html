How to Sell an Inherited House in Maryland

Who has the power to sign, what the Register of Wills needs, which taxes actually apply, and how long it takes. Written for families in the middle of it.

By Dustin Ray, founder of Pages of Purpose LLC. Updated November 2, 2026. Not legal or tax advice. Pages of Purpose is a real estate company, not a law firm. For your estate, talk to a Maryland probate attorney and a tax professional.

Someone you loved died and left a house in Maryland. Now you're the one fielding calls about the mortgage, the lawn, the mail and the question everyone keeps asking: can we sell it? Here is how the process works, in the order it happens.

Short answer: To sell an inherited house in Maryland, someone usually has to open an estate with the Register of Wills in the county where the person lived and be appointed personal representative. Once Letters of Administration are issued, the personal representative can sell the house without a court order unless the will or a court limits that power. Heirs can't sign a deed on their own.

Who has the right to sell an inherited house in Maryland?

The personal representative does. Maryland law says a dead person's property passes directly to the personal representative, who holds legal title for administration and distribution (Estates & Trusts §1-301).

"Personal representative" is Maryland's term for an executor or administrator. If there is a will, it usually names one. If there isn't, family members can ask to be appointed in a priority order the law sets out. Until the Register of Wills appoints someone and issues Letters of Administration, nobody can give a buyer good title. That includes the children, even if they are the only heirs.

Once appointed, the personal representative has broad power. Maryland's statute says they may "invest in, sell, mortgage, pledge, exchange, or lease property" and may act "without application to, the approval of, or ratification by the court" unless the will or a court order limits them (Estates & Trusts §7-401(a) and (n)). In plain terms, a Maryland executor selling a home does not normally need a judge to sign off.

The personal representative also owes duties to everyone in the estate. They have to get a fair price, keep records and report the sale on the estate's account. Heirs who think a sale is unfair can raise it with the Orphans' Court.

Step by step: how an inherited house gets sold in Maryland

Most Maryland estate sales follow the same path. The deadlines below come from the Register of Wills estate guide and the Estates & Trusts Article.

  1. Confirm how the house was titled. Pull the deed from Maryland Land Records or look the property up on SDAT. If the house was owned jointly with a right of survivorship, or held in a living trust, it may pass outside probate. If it was in the deceased person's name alone, it goes through the estate.
  2. Find the original will, if there is one. The original, not a copy, gets filed with the Register of Wills.
  3. Open the estate. File a petition with the Register of Wills in the county where the person lived. Whether it is handled as a small estate or a regular estate depends on the value of the probate assets.
  4. Get appointed and receive Letters of Administration. This document is your proof of authority. Title companies, lenders and buyers will all ask for it. Order several certified copies.
  5. Publish notice to creditors and secure the house. Change the locks, keep insurance in place (a vacant house may need a different policy) and keep paying property taxes. Maryland lets the personal representative pay taxes and insure estate property from estate funds (§7-401(j) and (k)).
  6. File the inventory within three months of appointment for a regular estate. The house goes on it at its date-of-death value.
  7. Choose how to sell. List with an agent, sell to a family member, or sell as-is to a cash buyer. Get an appraisal or a broker's opinion first so you can show the price was fair.
  8. Sign the contract and go to settlement as personal representative. The title company will want the Letters, a death certificate and the will. You sign the deed in your role as personal representative.
  9. Pay debts, file the account, distribute what's left. The first account is due within nine months of appointment. Sale proceeds pay valid debts and expenses first. Any inheritance tax is paid when the account shows distribution. Then the heirs get their shares.

Do you need full probate, or does a small estate work?

If the probate assets are worth $50,000 or less, or $100,000 or less when the surviving spouse is the only heir or beneficiary, the estate can use Maryland's simpler small estate process (Estates & Trusts §5-601). Everything above that is a regular estate.

One detail surprises a lot of families. For the small estate test, the house is valued at fair market value minus recorded debts secured by it, such as a mortgage (§5-601(d)). A house worth $180,000 with a $140,000 mortgage counts as $40,000. Assets that already passed outside probate, like jointly owned accounts or life insurance with a named beneficiary, generally don't count either.

Can you sell a house while it's still in probate in Maryland?

Yes. You don't have to wait for the estate to close. Once a personal representative is appointed, the house can be listed, put under contract and sold, and the money stays in the estate until the account is approved.

Timing is where people get tripped up. Creditors generally have until the earlier of six months after the death or two months after the personal representative mails or delivers notice to file a claim (Estates & Trusts §8-103). You can sell before that window closes. You just shouldn't hand all the proceeds to the heirs until you know what the estate owes.

This works the same way in Baltimore City, Baltimore County, Prince George's, Anne Arundel or any other county. Each county has its own Register of Wills office, but the state law is the same everywhere.

What if the house is still in the deceased person's name and no estate was opened?

Then the first job is opening one. It's a common situation: a parent died years ago, a sibling has been living in the house, taxes slipped, and the deed still shows the parent's name. Nobody can sell that house until someone has authority to sign for the estate.

The longer it waits, the harder it gets. Heirs die and their own heirs step into their shares. Tax bills and water liens keep stacking up. An unpaid Maryland property tax bill can end up in the county's tax sale, which adds redemption costs and a deadline. If that's where you are, read how to sell a Maryland house before the tax sale.

A probate attorney can tell you whether an estate can still be opened in your situation and who should serve. It can usually be fixed. It just takes longer than people expect.

What if the heirs can't agree on selling?

It depends on where the estate is. While the estate is open, the personal representative decides whether to sell, though heirs can object to the Orphans' Court. After the house has been deeded out to several heirs, they are co-owners, and a sale needs every owner's signature or a court-ordered partition.

Common ways families get unstuck:

  • 📝 One heir buys out the others at an appraised price.
  • 🏠 The personal representative sells during administration and the heirs split the cash instead of the house.
  • 🤝 An heir who wants out sells only their share.
  • ⚖️ As a last resort, someone files for partition in Circuit Court.

Do you pay inheritance tax on a house in Maryland?

Usually not, if you are close family. Maryland's inheritance tax is 10%, but spouses, children and other lineal descendants, stepchildren, parents, grandparents, siblings and a child's spouse are exempt (Register of Wills, Maryland taxes).

When a probate asset like the house goes to someone who owes the tax, it is paid when the personal representative files the account showing that distribution. The Register of Wills page linked above has the details.

Maryland also has a separate estate tax, which is paid by the estate, not the heir. According to the state's Department of Legislative Services, it effectively exempts the first $5 million of a taxable estate (2026 fiscal and policy note, SB 211). Most families selling a single inherited house never reach that number.

Do you owe capital gains tax when you sell an inherited house?

Often very little. Your tax basis in an inherited house is generally its fair market value on the date of death, not what the owner originally paid (IRS Publication 551). If you sell soon after the death for about that value, there may be little or no gain to tax.

The IRS also treats inherited property as held for more than one year, no matter how quickly you sell (IRS Publication 544). So any gain gets long-term capital gains rates. Get a date-of-death appraisal. It documents the basis, supports the estate inventory and backs up the sale price if an heir questions it later.

A hypothetical example

This is made up to show the math. It is not a real client. Say a mother in Charles County bought her house in 1990 for $95,000. She dies in 2026 with no will and three adult children. An appraisal puts the house at $310,000 on the date of death.

Her daughter opens the estate and is appointed personal representative. The house sells eight months later for $315,000, and selling costs come to $18,000. The gain is figured against the $310,000 stepped-up basis, not the $95,000 purchase price. After selling costs, the sale shows a small loss, so there is no capital gains tax. The children owe no Maryland inheritance tax because they are lineal descendants. After the debts and expenses are paid, they split what's left three ways.

What documents do you need to sell an inherited house in Maryland?

The title company will ask for proof of death, proof of authority and proof of what's owed. Gather these early:

  • Certified copies of the death certificate
  • Letters of Administration (or the small estate equivalent) from the Register of Wills
  • The will, if there was one
  • The current deed and the property's SDAT account number
  • Mortgage, HELOC and lien payoff statements
  • Recent property tax and water/sewer bills
  • The date-of-death appraisal
  • Photo ID for the personal representative
  • If the estate is already closed and the house was deeded to several heirs: every heir on the deed signs

Should you list the house or sell it for cash?

If the house is in decent shape, the heirs get along and the estate can carry the costs for a few months, listing with a good agent will usually net the most money. We tell people that directly.

A cash sale makes more sense when the house needs work nobody wants to pay for, when it's full of belongings, when taxes or liens are piling up, or when the estate can't keep paying for an empty property. It also helps when the sale itself is complicated: probate never opened, a missing heir, or heirs who disagree. Any cash buyer will pay less than a retail buyer would. What you get back is time, certainty and no repairs.

Frequently asked questions

Can I sell a house while it's in probate in Maryland?

Yes. Once the Register of Wills appoints a personal representative and issues Letters of Administration, the house can be listed, put under contract and sold while the estate is still open. Maryland law lets the personal representative sell estate property without a court order unless the will or a court limits that power. The proceeds stay in the estate until debts are paid and the account is filed.

Can an executor sell a house in Maryland without all heirs agreeing?

Usually, yes. In Maryland the executor is called the personal representative, and the law gives that person power to sell estate property without court approval. The personal representative still owes the heirs a fair price and an accounting, and heirs can object to the Orphans' Court. After the house has been deeded to the heirs, every heir on the deed has to sign.

How do I sell my deceased parent's house in Maryland without probate?

Only if the house passed outside probate, for example because it was owned jointly with a right of survivorship or held in a living trust. If the deed is in your parent's name alone, an estate has to be opened so a personal representative can sign the deed. Small estates with $50,000 or less in probate assets ($100,000 if the spouse is the sole heir) use a simpler process.

What documents are needed to sell an inherited house in Maryland?

Expect to need certified death certificates, Letters of Administration from the Register of Wills, the will if there is one, the current deed, mortgage and lien payoff statements, recent tax and water bills, a date-of-death appraisal and photo ID for the personal representative. If the estate already deeded the house to several heirs, all of them must sign.

Do I have to pay inheritance tax on a house in Maryland?

Not if you are close family. Maryland's inheritance tax is 10%, but spouses, children, grandchildren, stepchildren, parents, grandparents, siblings and a child's spouse are exempt. Nieces, nephews, cousins and friends generally pay 10% on what they receive. Maryland's separate estate tax only affects estates above about $5 million.

Do I pay capital gains tax when I sell an inherited house?

Often little or none. Your basis is generally the house's fair market value on the date of death, so you are taxed only on gain above that value. The IRS treats inherited property as held longer than one year, so any gain gets long-term rates. A date-of-death appraisal documents the basis.

How long does it take to sell an inherited house in Maryland?

Appointment as personal representative comes first, and the sale can close as soon as a buyer is ready after that. The estate itself takes longer: the inventory is due within three months of appointment, the first account within nine months, and creditors generally have up to six months after the death to file claims.

Who buys inherited houses in Maryland?

Three kinds of buyers: retail buyers found through a real estate agent, family members buying out the other heirs, and cash buyers who purchase as-is. Listing usually nets the most when the house is in good shape and the estate can wait. A cash buyer makes sense when the house needs work, carrying costs are piling up, or the title or family situation is complicated.
