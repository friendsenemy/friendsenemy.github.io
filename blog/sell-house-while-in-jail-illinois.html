Selling an Illinois House From Jail or Prison: The Power of Attorney Witness Rule, the Homestead Release and Chicago's Costs

Illinois requires a property power of attorney to be witnessed and notarized, and the witness can't be the owner's parent, sibling, child or spouse, or the agent's. That rule catches families during prison visits more than any other. Here's how to get the document right from an IDOC facility or Cook County Jail, why the deed needs a homestead release, how restitution liens work, what Chicago adds to the bill, and when listing beats a cash sale. Or ask us a question, free.

By Dustin Ray, Founder of Pages of Purpose LLC. Not legal advice — Illinois power of attorney, homestead, foreclosure and corrections rules change, and every case is different. Before you sign or file anything, talk to an attorney licensed in Illinois.

The question Illinois families ask first is whether a house can be sold at all while the owner is incarcerated. It can. Under 730 ILCS 5/5-5-5, a conviction doesn't cost the defendant their civil rights except the ones the statute lists, such as voting while imprisoned and holding certain offices. Owning a house, and deciding to sell it, isn't on that list.

The harder question is how to get a signature that a title company will accept. In Illinois, that usually comes down to one document, the property power of attorney, and one rule about who may witness it. This guide starts there, then covers notaries inside, the homestead release, restitution liens, foreclosure timing and Chicago-specific costs. For the nationwide basics, see our national guide to selling a house while incarcerated.

👥 Illinois Bars the Family From Witnessing the Power of Attorney

Under 755 ILCS 45/3-3.6, every Illinois power of attorney for property must be signed by a witness and notarized. The notary can't also be the witness. And the statute disqualifies a long list of people from serving as witness or notary:

  • 👨‍👩‍👧 a parent, sibling or descendant of the principal, or of any agent, or the spouse of any of those people;
  • 🧾 the agent or any successor agent;
  • 🩺 the principal's attending physician or mental health provider, or a relative of either;
  • 🏥 an owner or operator of a health-care facility where the principal lives, or a relative of one.

⚠️ Where prison signings fail: families often plan to have the power of attorney signed during a visit, with a sister or the owner's mother as witness. Under Illinois law, she can't be. If the agent is the owner's wife, her parents and children can't witness either. Line up an unrelated witness, such as a facility staff member, a friend, or someone from the lawyer's office, before the appointment, or the document can be rejected at closing.

The disqualifications make sense; they are meant to stop relatives from pressuring a vulnerable person. But for a family coordinating a signing inside a facility, they mean planning two outside people: a notary and an unrelated witness, who both have to be approved to enter.

📄 What the Statutory Short Form Needs to Do

Illinois's Statutory Short Form Power of Attorney for Property (755 ILCS 45/3-3) lets the principal grant powers by category, including real estate transactions. For a sale from prison, make sure:

  • 🏠 the real estate category is not struck out, and ideally the property's address and legal description are added in the special instructions;
  • 📅 the effective date is immediate, not "upon my incapacity," since an incarcerated owner isn't incapacitated;
  • 🖊️ the agent's name matches their ID exactly, because the title company will check it against the deed.

No Illinois statute we found requires the power of attorney itself to be recorded before an agent sells. In practice, title companies usually record it along with the deed, so send them the original. Ask your attorney or the title company before closing which they'll require.

🔏 Notaries Inside IDOC and Cook County Jail

State prisons

IDOC's rules on mail (20 Ill. Adm. Code 525) don't mention notaries, and the department doesn't publish a single notary policy. Its FY2025 annual report describes paralegal, notary and photocopying services in the law library of at least one facility, so ask the facility's law library or counselor how a notary is arranged. Whether every facility offers it, and how often, varies.

For sending documents in, privileged mail must be marked "Privileged" or "Legal Mail" with the sender's name, title and address, and it is opened in front of the person in custody. IDOC tells attorneys to get a one-time control number for privileged mail. A power of attorney sent by a relative goes through regular mail. Letting the family's lawyer mail it is usually faster.

Cook County Jail

The Cook County Department of Corrections inmate handbook lists notary service among the law library's services. Ask the law library or a social worker how to request it, and confirm that the witness you plan to bring can be approved for the visit.

🏡 The Homestead Release Clause, and Why the Spouse Signs

Illinois protects homestead equity, up to $15,000 per individual, or a proportionate share of $30,000 when two owners occupy the house (735 ILCS 5/12-901). The sale side of that rule matters more for a prison sale. Under 765 ILCS 5/27, a deed releases homestead rights only if it contains a clause expressly releasing or waiving them, and one spouse's release doesn't bind the other unless the other spouse joins in it.

So title companies in Illinois commonly ask the owner's spouse to sign the deed, even when the spouse isn't on title, to release homestead. If the owner is inside and the spouse is home, the spouse signs for themselves and the agent signs for the owner. If the spouse is the one inside, they'll need to sign the release, or give someone a power of attorney to sign it.

Married couples can also hold their home as tenants by the entirety. Under 765 ILCS 1005/1c, that tenancy is limited to the couple's homestead, and no deed, mortgage or lease of the property is effective unless both spouses sign. A divorce judgment converts it to a tenancy in common. If the couple is separating, our guide to selling during a divorce explains what changes.

⚖️ Restitution Is a Judgment Lien

Under 730 ILCS 5/5-5-6(m), a restitution order is a judgment lien in favor of the victim that attaches to the defendant's property, and it expires the same way a civil judgment lien does. For real estate, 735 ILCS 5/12-101 makes a judgment a lien only once a transcript, certified copy or memorandum is recorded in the county where the land is. That lien lasts up to seven years from entry or revival, and it can be revived.

If restitution or a civil judgment has been recorded, the title company will pay it out of the proceeds at closing. If it hasn't been recorded, it isn't a lien on the house yet, though the debt remains. Order a title search early so the family knows what the owner will actually net.

🧠 Why Guardianship Usually Isn't the Answer

Families sometimes ask whether a court can appoint a guardian to sell the house when the owner won't sign. In Illinois, a guardian of the estate requires a "person with a disability" under 755 ILCS 5/11a-2, which is defined by mental deterioration, physical incapacity, mental illness, developmental disability and a few other conditions. Being in custody isn't one of them. If the owner won't sign a power of attorney or a deed, there's usually no court shortcut; co-owners may need a partition case instead.

🏦 Illinois Foreclosure Gives the Family More Time

Illinois forecloses through the courts, and the statute builds in two windows:

  • 🔁 Reinstatement (735 ILCS 5/15-1602): within 90 days after all mortgagors are served or submit to the court, the owner can cure the default. If the court makes a written finding that it was used, it can't be used again for five years.
  • ⏳ Redemption (15-1603): for residential property, the redemption period ends on the later of seven months from service or three months from the foreclosure judgment.

Together that usually gives a family many months to sell before a sheriff's sale, longer than in states with trustee sales. It isn't a reason to wait: interest, late fees and lawyer fees all come out of the equity. See what happens to a mortgage during incarceration and how a sale can stop a foreclosure.

🧾 What Chicago Adds to the Bill

  • 🏚️ Vacant building registration. Chicago's municipal code (13-12-125) requires owners to register a building vacant more than 30 days. The fee is $300 per registration and renewal, doubled if late. Vacant houses also draw inspections and fines.
  • 📈 Property taxes. For tax year 2024, billed in 2025, the median Chicago homeowner's bill rose 16.7%, the largest jump in at least 30 years according to the Cook County Treasurer, with the steepest increases on the South and West Sides. A modest bungalow commonly pays $3,500–$6,000 a year, roughly $290–$500 a month.
  • 🏷️ Homeowner exemption. Cook County's homeowner exemption, which cuts $10,000 off a home's equalized assessed value, requires the owner to occupy the home as a principal residence on January 1. If nobody lives there, ask the Assessor whether the exemption still applies.
  • 🪙 Transfer stamps. Illinois charges 50 cents per $500, Cook County 25 cents per $500, and Chicago $3.75 per $500, generally paid by the buyer, plus $1.50 per $500 for the CTA portion, generally paid by the seller.
  • 📝 Attorney review. Chicago-area contracts on the Multi-Board 8.0 form give each side's attorney five business days to approve or propose changes. That's a good moment for the family's lawyer to confirm the power of attorney and homestead release are in order.

🧮 A Chatham Bungalow, Priced Two Ways

Take a 1920s brick bungalow in Chatham. Renovated, nearby bungalows sell for about $300,000. This one needs a boiler, tuckpointing, a kitchen and a bath, around $55,000, and it's been empty for four months.

Holding costs. Taxes around $350 a month, vacant-dwelling insurance $125–$200, heat through a Chicago winter $150–$250, plus the $300 vacant building registration. Call it $700–$850 a month.

Cash sale. Cash buyers pay about 60–80% of after-repair value. An offer here would likely be $180,000 to $240,000; call it $210,000, 70%. The seller's share of transfer stamps (state, county and the CTA portion, about $945 together), title and attorney fees of about $2,500, and one more month of holding leave the owner about $205,800, before any recorded liens.

Listing. As-is, it might sell for $255,000. Subtract about 5.5% commission ($14,000), about $1,150 in seller transfer stamps, $3,000 in title and attorney fees, and five months of holding ($4,000). The owner nets about $232,800, before liens.

That's about $27,000 more by listing, assuming the house is winterized, the boiler doesn't fail mid-listing, and someone can let inspectors in.

🔀 Picking the Path

List through the agent

Most money

If the house is livable, the taxes are paid and a relative can manage a listing, a retail buyer will usually pay more than any cash buyer, including us. That's the honest recommendation for a lot of Illinois families.

Sell for cash

Least handling

When the house needs a boiler or a roof, the mortgage is in foreclosure, or nobody local can manage showings and inspections through a winter, a cash sale with one signing date and no repair requests is often worth the lower price.

Owner signs directly

If a POA can't be arranged

The owner can sign the deed before a facility notary without naming an agent. It avoids the witness problem but leaves the closing at the mercy of prison mail and transfers.

📋 What To Do This Week in Illinois

  1. Pull the deed from the county recorder (the Cook County Clerk's recordings division in Cook) and check for recorded judgments or restitution.
  2. Confirm whether the owner is married and how title is held, since that decides who signs the homestead release.
  3. Have an Illinois attorney prepare a statutory short form power of attorney for property with real estate powers effective now.
  4. Find an unrelated adult who can serve as witness and get them approved for the visit, along with a notary.
  5. Ask the facility law library or counselor how notary service works there.
  6. If the house is in Chicago and empty more than 30 days, register it as a vacant building.
  7. Call the mortgage servicer with the power of attorney so the agent can get payoff and reinstatement figures.

✅ Where Illinois Leaves You

An Illinois house can be sold while its owner is in jail or prison. The sale lives or dies on the property power of attorney: it needs a notary and a witness, and the witness can't be the owner's or the agent's close family. Add a homestead release signed by the spouse, check the recorder for restitution and judgments, and register the house if it's a vacant Chicago building. If the house is in good shape and someone can manage a listing, list it. If it needs major work or foreclosure is underway, a cash sale is the simpler path.

❓ Illinois Prison Sale, POA and Homestead FAQ

Who can witness an Illinois power of attorney for property?

Any adult except those 755 ILCS 45/3-3.6 disqualifies: the principal's or an agent's parent, sibling or descendant, or the spouse of any of them; any agent or successor agent; the principal's attending physician or mental health provider or their relatives; and a health-care facility owner where the principal lives or their relatives. The notary can't also be the witness.

Does an Illinois power of attorney need to be notarized?

Yes. Under 755 ILCS 45/3-3.6 a power of attorney for property must bear a witness's signature and be notarized.

Does an Illinois power of attorney have to be recorded before the agent sells a house?

We found no statute that requires it, but title companies usually record it with the deed. Ask the title company which original or certified copy it needs before closing.

Does a person lose the right to own property when convicted in Illinois?

No. Under 730 ILCS 5/5-5-5 a conviction doesn't cost the defendant their civil rights except those the statute lists, such as voting while imprisoned and holding certain offices. The owner keeps the house and can sell it.

Do Illinois prisons provide notaries?

IDOC doesn't publish one notary rule, but its FY2025 annual report describes notary services in at least one facility's law library. Ask the facility's law library or counselor how it works there.

Does Cook County Jail have a notary?

The Cook County Department of Corrections inmate handbook lists notary service among the law library's services. Ask the law library or a social worker how to request it.

Why does my spouse have to sign the deed in Illinois if only I'm on title?

Under 765 ILCS 5/27, a deed releases homestead rights only through an express release clause, and one spouse's release doesn't bind the other unless that spouse joins. Title companies therefore ask a resident spouse to sign the homestead release.

Is restitution a lien on an Illinois house?

A restitution order is a judgment lien in favor of the victim under 730 ILCS 5/5-5-6(m). It becomes a lien on real estate once a transcript, certified copy or memorandum is recorded in that county, and it lasts up to seven years unless revived.

Can a guardian be appointed to sell an Illinois house because the owner is in prison?

Usually not. A guardian of the estate requires a person with a disability as defined in 755 ILCS 5/11a-2, and incarceration isn't one of the listed conditions.

How long does an Illinois foreclosure take if the owner is in prison?

Illinois forecloses in court. The owner has 90 days after service to reinstate, and residential redemption runs to the later of seven months from service or three months from judgment, so a sale is usually possible for many months, though fees keep growing.
