Selling Your Share of an Inherited Florida House: First Find Out Which Kind of Share You Own

In most states, "my share of Mom's house" means one thing: a fraction of the house, owned with your siblings. In Florida it can mean four different things, because of the state's homestead rules and probate process. A remainder behind a stepparent's life estate, a co-owner's share, an interest in protected homestead, and a beneficiary's interest in a house still in probate are valued differently, sold differently and bought by different people. Here's how to tell which one you have and what each is realistically worth.

"I just want to sell my part." We hear it from Florida heirs every week, and the first thing we have to work out with them is what "my part" actually is. Florida treats a parent's home differently from almost any other asset, and the way it passes can leave heirs holding interests that look alike on paper and are worth very different amounts in practice. Get this step right and the rest of the decision becomes much clearer.

🧩 In Florida You May Be Selling One of Four Different Things

Here are the four most common shapes an inherited Florida share takes:

  1. 🌅 A remainder behind a life estate. If the house was the parent's homestead and a spouse survived along with descendants, Section 732.401 of the Florida Statutes generally gives the spouse a life estate and the descendants a vested remainder. You own a share of what's left after the spouse dies. You can't live there, rent it out or force a sale while the life estate lasts.
  2. 🤝 A tenant-in-common share. You and your co-heirs own fractions of the whole house, each with an equal right to use it. This is the case when there was no surviving spouse, when the spouse elected under Section 732.401(2) to take a one-half interest as a tenant in common instead of a life estate, or when the house wasn't the parent's homestead and has been distributed.
  3. 🏛️ An interest in protected homestead with no administration needed. Protected homestead passes outside the probate estate. The heirs usually get a court order determining homestead status, and that order is what proves their ownership to a title company.
  4. 📑 A beneficiary's interest in a house still in probate. If the house wasn't protected homestead (a rental, a second home, or a homestead the owner could leave by will because no spouse or minor child survived), it may be an estate asset. Under Section 733.607, the personal representative can take possession of it if that's needed for administration, and your interest is subject to the estate's debts and expenses until it's distributed.

🌅 Selling a Remainder: You're Selling the Future

If your share is a remainder behind a stepparent's or parent's life estate, the most important fact is time. The life tenant has the right to live in the house, or rent it out, for the rest of their life. Under Florida's Chapter 738 rules, which Section 732.401 points to, the life tenant generally pays the ordinary expenses: recurring property taxes, insurance and routine maintenance. The remaindermen generally pay for improvements expected to outlast the life estate, like a new roof. So unlike most heirs, you may be paying almost nothing to hold your interest.

Valuing a remainder isn't guesswork. Lawyers, appraisers and the IRS value life estates and remainders using actuarial tables under Section 7520 of the Internal Revenue Code, which combine the life tenant's age with an interest rate that changes monthly. For a healthy life tenant in their early 70s, the tables often put the remainder at roughly half of the whole property's value, give or take, depending on the rate that month. Your share of the remainder is your fraction of that number.

Who will pay it? Realistically:

  • 👵 The life tenant. Buying the remainder gives them full ownership, which lets them sell, refinance or leave the house to their own children. They're often the best buyer.
  • 👫 Your co-remaindermen. A sibling who expects to keep the house someday may buy your share now.
  • 🏷️ Everyone together. If the life tenant wants to move, the life tenant and all remaindermen can sell the whole house on the open market and split the proceeds by an agreed or actuarial formula. That usually beats every other option.
  • 💵 An outside investor. Few investors buy remainders, because they get no use of the property and no cash flow until the life tenant dies, which could be decades away. Expect offers far below the actuarial value, if you get one at all. We look at them case by case, and we'll often tell you the life tenant is the better buyer.
The mistake to avoid: don't price a remainder as if it were a co-owner's share of the whole house. If your stepmother is 68 and in good health, your "third of Dad's house" may be worth far less today than a third of its market value, to anyone. Get an actuarial calculation before you negotiate, and offer it to the life tenant first.

🤝 Selling a Co-Owner Share: The Heirs Property Act Sets the Floor

If you hold a tenant-in-common share, you can sell it without anyone's consent, and your co-owners don't get a right of first refusal unless there's a written agreement giving them one. What they do get, if the case ever reaches partition, is the protection of Florida's Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act, adopted in 2020 at Sections 64.201 through 64.214.

Under Section 64.202, it generally covers property held by tenants in common with no binding partition agreement, where at least one owner got title from a relative and relatives hold at least 20% of the interests or make up at least 20% of the owners. When a co-owner asks for a partition sale, the court first sets the value under Section 64.206. Then, under Section 64.207, the other owners have 45 days to elect to buy that owner's interest at the court-set value multiplied by the owner's fraction, with a payment deadline at least 60 days after notice.

That buyout price has no fractional discount, which makes it the right benchmark for your share. If the house is worth $270,000 as-is and you own a third, the statutory number is $90,000. Your siblings may not pay that voluntarily, but if they want to keep the house, they'd pay it in court, plus a share of the fees under Section 64.081. Put a written offer to them at that number before you take a discounted offer from anyone.

Outside buyers pay less because they take on the remaining work: a 6- to 18-month partition case, shared fees that often run $10,000 to $40,000 or more in total, any occupant, and claims by co-owners who paid more than their share of taxes, insurance or repairs. Offers for an inherited Florida co-owner share often fall somewhere around 35% to 65% of its proportional as-is value, depending mostly on whether the house is vacant, whether title is clean, and how close a sale of the whole is.

📑 When the House Is Protected Homestead or Still in Probate

Protected homestead. Because protected homestead passes to the heirs at death outside the estate, the personal representative usually can't sell it, and creditors of the deceased generally can't reach it. But a buyer's title insurer will want proof of who the heirs are. That proof is usually a court order determining homestead, entered in a probate proceeding. If no one has asked for that order yet, your share is real but unproven, and outside buyers will either require the order first or discount heavily. Asking a probate attorney to petition for the order is often the single best way to raise what your share is worth. It typically costs a filing fee plus a few thousand dollars in attorney time.

An estate asset still in probate. If the house isn't protected homestead and an estate is open, it's under the personal representative's control while the estate needs it. Under Section 733.613, a personal representative without a power of sale in the will needs the court to authorize or confirm a sale. Your interest as a beneficiary is in what's left after debts and expenses. You can usually assign that interest, but few buyers outside the family will pay much for it until the house is distributed. If you need money before then, ask the personal representative whether the estate plans to sell the house; a sale of the whole by the estate usually serves you better than any sale of your interest.

The two-year mark. Under Section 733.710, two years after the death, the estate, the personal representative and the beneficiaries generally can't be held liable for claims against the deceased, with exceptions for timely filed claims and recorded mortgages and liens. Where an estate was never opened, that bar can make an heirs' sale simpler. Ask a Florida attorney how it applies to your facts.

🧾 Florida Closing Details on a Share Sale

  • 📮 Documentary stamp tax. Florida taxes deeds. In most counties the rate is $0.70 per $100 of the price (Miami-Dade uses a different rate), so a $50,000 share sale carries about $350 in doc stamps. Who pays is negotiable; in much of Florida the seller customarily does.
  • 💍 Your spouse's joinder, if it's your homestead. If you live in the inherited house as your own homestead and you're married, Article X, Section 4(c) of the Florida Constitution requires your spouse to join in a sale. That applies to a sale of your share too.
  • 📜 Proof of title. Expect the buyer to want a death certificate, the probate or homestead order, and possibly affidavits about the family. Gaps in the paper show up as a lower price.
  • 🧮 Federal tax. Florida has no state income tax. Federally, inherited property generally takes a basis equal to its value at death, so a share sold soon after the death often has little taxable gain. A remainder's basis can be more complicated; ask a tax professional.

💵 Two Worked Examples

A co-owner share in Jacksonville. Three siblings inherited a house outright, with no surviving spouse. It would be worth $330,000 updated, needs $60,000 of work, and is worth about $270,000 as-is. One sibling lives there. You own a third.

  • 📏 Proportional as-is value: $90,000.
  • 🤝 Sibling buyout at an appraised price: about $90,000, less any taxes or repairs you owe the others.
  • ⚖️ Partition and broker sale after about a year: $270,000 less about 6% in sale costs ($16,200) and $30,000 in combined fees leaves $223,800, so your third is about $74,600, a year later and before reimbursement claims.
  • 🏷️ Sale of your share to an investor: roughly $32,000 to $58,000, depending on the occupant and title, closing in weeks.

Carrying costs while you wait are real in Florida. Without a homestead exemption, property taxes often run 1.5% to 2% of just value, so $340 to $450 a month on this house, and insuring an occupied-by-one-sibling or vacant house in Florida is expensive. Your third of that is $150 to $250 a month or more.

A remainder in Lakeland. Dad died, your stepmother, 72, has a life estate in the $300,000 house, and you and your brother each own half of the remainder. If the tables value the remainder at roughly half of the house, the whole remainder is about $150,000, and your half is about $75,000 on paper. Your stepmother might pay something close to that to own the house outright. An outside investor, facing perhaps 15 years of waiting with no income, would offer a small fraction of it, if anything. Here, the best path is almost always a deal with the life tenant, or a joint sale of the whole house if she wants to move.

🧭 Choosing Your Route

👵 Make an offer to the life tenant

Best for most remainder holders

Get an actuarial value, then offer to sell your remainder share to the life tenant, or to join in a sale of the whole if they'd rather move. They gain full control; you get money now instead of someday.

🤝 Offer your co-owner share to your siblings

Best for most co-owners

Use the as-is value times your fraction, the same formula Section 64.207 would use in court. Put it in writing with a deadline.

🏛️ Get the homestead order first

If your title isn't proven yet

An order determining homestead turns an unproven share into an insurable one. It's often the cheapest way to raise what your share is worth to anyone.

💵 Sell your share for cash

When the family won't engage and you need out

Fast and final, at a real discount. Best for co-owner shares in houses that will sell eventually. Get more than one offer.

🙅 When Not to Sell Your Share

Don't sell a remainder to an investor without first offering it to the life tenant; you'll almost always do better. Don't sell a co-owner share at a deep discount when your siblings can buy you out or a sale of the whole is a few months away. Don't sell anything until you know which of the four kinds of interest you hold. And if you're a beneficiary of a house still in probate, wait for the personal representative's plan before selling an interest that's still subject to the estate's debts.

📋 What To Do This Week

  1. 🔍 Identify your interest. Was the house the parent's homestead? Did a spouse or minor child survive? Did the spouse make the six-month election? A Florida probate attorney can answer these in one meeting.
  2. 📂 Check the court file. Search the county clerk's probate records for an open estate or an order determining homestead.
  3. 🧮 Get the right valuation. An as-is appraisal for a co-owner share; an appraisal plus an actuarial calculation for a remainder.
  4. 🧾 Tally what you owe or are owed for taxes, insurance and repairs since the death.
  5. ✉️ Make a written offer to the life tenant or your co-owners at the value you calculated.
  6. 💵 If they decline, get at least two outside offers and compare them to a realistic partition net or the value of waiting.

✅ Bottom Line

In Florida, the first question isn't what your share is worth. It's what your share is. A remainder behind a life estate is a claim on the future, best sold to the life tenant. A co-owner's share has a clear benchmark, the Heirs Property Act buyout price, and is best offered to your siblings first. A share of protected homestead is worth more once a court has confirmed it, and an interest in a house still in probate is usually best left until the estate decides what to do. If your family won't engage and you hold a co-owner share you need to turn into money, a cash sale is a fair option, and we'll make you an offer. ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Can I sell my share of an inherited house in Florida without the other heirs?

Usually yes. A tenant-in-common share or a remainder interest can be sold by deed without the other owners' consent, unless a written agreement says otherwise. If the house is your own homestead and you are married, your spouse must join. An interest in a house still in probate is subject to the estate's administration until it is distributed.

What is a remainder interest and can I sell it?

When a Florida homestead passes with a surviving spouse and descendants, the spouse usually gets a life estate and the descendants get a vested remainder under Section 732.401. A remainder is the right to own the house after the life tenant dies. You can sell it, but the natural buyers are the life tenant and the other remaindermen, because outside buyers get no use or income until the life estate ends.

How is a remainder interest in a Florida house valued?

It is usually valued with IRS actuarial tables under Internal Revenue Code Section 7520, which combine the life tenant's age with an interest rate that changes monthly. The younger the life tenant, the smaller the remainder's present value. A Florida attorney, CPA or appraiser can run the calculation.

Who pays property taxes when a spouse has a life estate in a Florida house?

Under the Chapter 738 rules that Section 732.401 points to, the life tenant generally pays ordinary expenses such as recurring property taxes, insurance and routine maintenance. The remaindermen generally bear improvements likely to outlast the life estate, such as a new roof.

What price do my siblings have to pay to buy out my share in a Florida partition?

If the Heirs Property Act applies, the court sets the value of the whole property under Section 64.206. Under Section 64.207, the co-owners who did not ask for a sale can elect within 45 days to buy the requesting owner's interest at that value times the owner's fraction, with no discount for owning only part.

Can I sell my share of protected homestead before a court order?

You can sign a deed, but most buyers and title insurers will want an order determining homestead that confirms who inherited the property. Without it, your share is unproven and will be heavily discounted or unsellable. Asking a probate attorney to petition for the order often raises what your share is worth.

Can I sell my interest if the house is still in probate in Florida?

If the house is not protected homestead, it may be an estate asset under the personal representative's control while it is needed for administration, and your interest is subject to the estate's debts and expenses. You can usually assign your beneficial interest, but few outside buyers will pay much for it before distribution.

Do I pay documentary stamp tax when I sell my share in Florida?

Yes, a deed transferring your share is subject to Florida documentary stamp tax, generally $0.70 per $100 of the price in most counties, with a different rate in Miami-Dade. On a $50,000 share sale that is about $350. Who pays it is negotiable, though sellers customarily pay in much of Florida.

Will a cash buyer pay full value for my share of a Florida house?

No. Outside buyers of co-owner shares often pay somewhere around 35% to 65% of the share's proportional as-is value, because they take on partition time and costs, any occupant and title work. For a remainder interest, offers are usually much lower. Your family or the life tenant is often the better buyer.

Should I sell my share or wait for the whole house to be sold?

If a sale of the whole house by agreement is realistic within a few months, waiting usually nets more. If you hold a remainder and the life tenant is young and healthy, selling to the life tenant may be the only way to get money in the near term. If your co-owners will not engage and you need money now, selling your co-owner share is a reasonable choice.
