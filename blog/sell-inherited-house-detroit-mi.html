Inherited a Detroit House? Check the Wayne County Tax Clock Before Anything Else

Detroit heirs lose houses every year without ever deciding to sell, because property taxes went unpaid while the family sorted things out. Wayne County can foreclose on a house about two years after taxes go delinquent, and many inherited Detroit houses are already a year behind when the owner dies. Start with the tax record. Then decide whether to keep, list, or sell for cash, using Michigan's rules on uncapping and the homestead exemption to run the real numbers.

By Dustin Ray, Founder of Pages of Purpose LLC. Not legal advice. Michigan probate and property tax law and Wayne County procedures change, every family is different, and laws differ by state. Before you sign or file anything, talk to an attorney licensed in Michigan, where the property is.

In a lot of Detroit families, the house is the inheritance. It may have been bought in the 1960s or 1970s, paid off long ago, and lived in by a grandparent until the end. When that person dies, the deed often stays in their name for years. A relative may move in. The family assumes the house will be dealt with eventually.

The danger is that the property tax bill doesn't wait. Detroit has some of the highest property tax rates of any big city in the country, and Wayne County runs a tight, court-supervised tax foreclosure calendar. The first question for any Detroit heir isn't whether to sell. It's how many unpaid tax years are on the house, and when Wayne County could take it.

📅 How the Wayne County Tax Foreclosure Calendar Works

Michigan's General Property Tax Act and the Wayne County Treasurer's published timeline follow a predictable sequence. Using one year's taxes as an example:

WhenWhat happensWhat it means for heirs
Summer and winter of Year 1City of Detroit bills the taxesBills keep going to the deceased owner's address
March 1, Year 2Unpaid taxes turn over to the Wayne County Treasurer as delinquent; a 4% administration fee and 1% monthly interest applyYou can still pay, but it's getting more expensive
March 1, Year 3The property is forfeited to the County Treasurer; a forfeiture fee is added and interest rises to 1.5% a month, retroactive to the delinquency dateForfeiture isn't loss of title yet, but foreclosure is next
Late Year 3 into early Year 4Notices posted, show cause and judicial foreclosure hearings heldLast practical window for a payment plan or a sale
March 31, Year 4Last day to redeem by paying all taxes, interest and feesMiss it and the house is gone
April 1, Year 4Foreclosure; title passes to the County TreasurerThe family loses the house; a later claim may be possible only for surplus sale proceeds

The County Treasurer's own summary says it plainly: properties that are two years delinquent can be foreclosed. If the owner died with a year of taxes unpaid and the family took a year to grieve and argue, the house may already be in its last window.

⚠️ Pull the Wayne County tax status this week. Look up the parcel through the Wayne County Treasurer's online tax records and write down which tax years are delinquent. If any year is close to forfeiture or foreclosure, the deadline matters more than any other decision on this page. A payment plan, a partial payment, or a sale that closes before March 31 can save the house's value for the heirs.

💰 If foreclosure already happened

After the Michigan Supreme Court's 2020 Rafaeli decision, Michigan changed its law so former owners can claim surplus proceeds when a foreclosed property sells at auction for more than the taxes owed. The Wayne County timeline sets a July 1 deadline after foreclosure to file a notice of intent to claim those remaining proceeds. Heirs of a deceased owner should talk to a Michigan attorney right away about whether and how they can file. The surplus is usually a fraction of what a normal sale would have brought.

🧾 Michigan's Uncapping Rule and the Family Exemption

Michigan taxes property on taxable value, which is capped and generally can rise only by inflation or 5% a year, whichever is less, while the owner keeps it. When ownership transfers, the cap normally comes off, and taxable value "uncaps" to the state equalized value (half of the assessor's estimate of market value) the next year. For a house held since the 1990s, that jump can be large.

Since December 31, 2013, MCL 211.27a(7)(s) excludes from "transfer of ownership" a transfer of residential property to a person related to the transferor by blood or affinity to the first degree, as long as the property's use doesn't change after the transfer. Michigan Treasury guidance reads first degree to include spouses, parents and children. It does not include siblings or grandchildren. In practice, that often means:

  • ✅ A house passing from a parent to a child who keeps it as a residence generally keeps its capped taxable value.
  • ⚠️ A house passing from a grandparent to grandchildren, or between siblings, generally uncaps.
  • 🏷️ A sale to an outside buyer uncaps for that buyer, which matters for resale value but not for the heirs' own tax bill.
  • ❓ Renting the house out may count as a change in use. Ask the City of Detroit assessor's office before you assume the exemption survives a rental.

File the Property Transfer Affidavit with the assessor when title changes, and claim the exemption on it. Late filing carries its own penalties.

🏠 The principal residence exemption usually disappears

The other tax change after a death is the homestead exemption, called the principal residence exemption in Michigan. It exempts an owner-occupied home from up to 18 mills of school operating taxes. If no heir lives in the house as their principal residence, the exemption should be rescinded, and the bill rises by up to 18 mills on taxable value. Detroit's homestead millage was about 68 mills in 2024; a non-homestead house pays roughly 18 mills on top of that. An heir who moves in and owns an interest may be able to claim the exemption again.

📂 Whose Name Is on the Deed? Detroit's Tangled Title Problem

Detroit housing advocates use the term tangled title for a house where the person living there isn't the owner of record, most often because the owner died and nobody probated the estate. It blocks almost everything: you can't sell, refinance, get many repair grants, or enroll in some tax-relief programs without clear title. There are a few ways it gets untangled:

  • 📜 Check for a Lady Bird deed. Enhanced life estate deeds are common in Michigan and pass a house at death without probate. Search the Wayne County Register of Deeds records for any deed your parent signed late in life.
  • 🏛️ Open a probate estate in the Wayne County Probate Court. Most Detroit estates use informal, unsupervised administration, where a personal representative can be appointed quickly without a hearing. The personal representative can then sell the house and sign the deed for the estate.
  • 🤝 Get help from a Detroit nonprofit legal or housing counseling organization if the family can't afford an attorney. Several have worked on tangled titles for years.

Opening an estate in Michigan involves a court filing fee (about $175 for a petition, plus an inventory fee scaled to the estate's value) and, under MCL 700.3801, publishing notice to creditors, who then have four months to present claims. Attorney fees for a simple informal estate with one house commonly run $1,500 to $5,000; contested or supervised administration costs more. Michigan has no inheritance tax.

🔍 Keep It or Sell It? The Detroit Decision Is About Repairs vs. Value

In many Detroit neighborhoods, the cost of making a long-held house rentable or sellable at full price is a big share of what it's worth. A roof, a furnace, a sewer line, plumbing that's been winterized badly, and lead paint work can run $40,000 to $80,000 on a house that would be worth $110,000 to $180,000 fixed up. That ratio, more than any legal rule, decides the right route.

🏡 Keep it and live in it

Best when an heir will occupy it and can carry the taxes

If a child of the owner moves in, the family exemption from uncapping and a renewed principal residence exemption can keep the bill close to what the parent paid. Get title cleared first, then look at Detroit's homeowner tax relief programs for which an owner-occupant may qualify.

🏘️ Keep it as a rental

Only if you can fund repairs and meet city rental rules

Detroit requires rental properties to be registered and to pass inspection for a certificate of compliance before renting. Expect non-homestead taxes, possible uncapping, landlord insurance, and a repair budget. It works for heirs with cash and time. It burns money for everyone else.

📈 List it with an agent

Best for a sound house in a strong pocket

In neighborhoods like East English Village, Grandmont-Rosedale, the University District, Bagley, or parts of Jefferson Chalmers, decent houses draw retail and FHA buyers, and listing will usually beat any cash offer. Lenders will want title clean and the house to meet appraisal standards.

💵 Sell for cash as-is

Best when taxes are behind, the house needs heavy work, or title is still being cleared

A cash buyer can close around a foreclosure deadline, pay the delinquent taxes from the proceeds at closing, buy with an occupant still in place, and take the house without repairs. You give up price for speed and certainty.

🧾 Monthly carrying costs while the family decides

  • 🏛️ Property tax: on a non-homestead Detroit house with a taxable value of $40,000, roughly 86 mills is about $3,400 a year, or $290 a month.
  • 🛡️ Vacant-home insurance: commonly $100-$250 a month in Detroit, if you can get it.
  • 💡 Utilities: DTE and Detroit water and sewer, $100-$200 a month to keep heat and service on.
  • 🔒 Security and upkeep: boarding, lawn, snow, and regular checks, $100-$250. Vacant houses attract scrappers, and copper and furnaces disappear fast.

That's about $500 to $900 a month, plus any back-tax interest at 1% to 1.5% a month.

👪 When Some Heirs Want to Keep the House

If the heirs are split, the personal representative of an opened estate can often sell over objections, subject to the probate court's oversight if someone petitions. If the heirs already hold title together, any co-owner can file a partition action in Wayne County Circuit Court. Before anyone files, put the tax deadline, the repair estimate, and each route's net on one page for everyone. Our guide on what to do when heirs can't agree walks through buyouts and selling a single share, and partition lawsuits explained covers the court route.

📋 What To Do This Week

  1. Look up the Wayne County tax status and note every delinquent year and the next forfeiture or foreclosure date.
  2. Search the Register of Deeds for the current deed and any Lady Bird or quitclaim deed signed before death.
  3. Decide who will be personal representative, and open an estate in Wayne County Probate Court if one is needed.
  4. Ask the County Treasurer about a payment plan if foreclosure is near and you need time to sell.
  5. Insure and secure the house: winterize if no heat, change locks, and arrange checks.
  6. Get a contractor's repair estimate and a local agent's opinion so you can compare listing and a cash sale honestly.
  7. File the Property Transfer Affidavit when title changes, claiming the family exemption if it applies.

✅ Bottom Line

For a Detroit heir, the tax calendar comes first. Wayne County can foreclose roughly two years after taxes go delinquent, and an inherited house is often already partway there. Know your dates, clear the title, and then decide. A child who keeps the house as a home may keep the old taxable value under Michigan's family exemption; a vacant house loses its homestead exemption and costs more every month.

If the house is sound and in a neighborhood with retail buyers, list it; you'll probably net more than a cash offer. If taxes are behind, the house needs heavy work, or the title is still being untangled, ask us. We'll tell you what each route would net and whether ours is the right one.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

How long can property taxes go unpaid before Wayne County forecloses?

Under the Wayne County Treasurer's published timeline, unpaid taxes become delinquent on March 1 of the year after they were billed, the property is forfeited to the County Treasurer the following March 1, and if taxes, interest and fees are not paid by March 31 of the next year, the property is foreclosed on April 1. In short, a house about two years delinquent can be foreclosed.

Can I sell an inherited Detroit house that is behind on property taxes?

Yes. Delinquent taxes, interest and fees are typically paid from the sale proceeds at closing, as long as the sale closes before the foreclosure redemption deadline. Someone with authority to sign, such as a personal representative or heirs with clear title, must be in place first.

Do property taxes go up when you inherit a house in Michigan?

They can. A transfer of ownership normally uncaps the taxable value, but MCL 211.27a(7)(s) excludes transfers of residential property to first-degree relatives, such as a child, if the property's use does not change. Separately, the principal residence exemption is lost if no owner lives in the house, which adds up to 18 mills.

Does the uncapping exemption apply if grandchildren inherit?

Generally not. Michigan Treasury guidance treats first-degree relatives as spouses, parents and children, so transfers to grandchildren or between siblings usually uncap the taxable value.

What is a tangled title in Detroit?

It is a house where the occupant or family is not the owner of record, usually because the owner died and the estate was never probated. It prevents a sale, a refinance and some tax relief until title is cleared through probate, a valid deed, or a court order.

Which court handles probate for a Detroit house?

The Wayne County Probate Court. Most estates use informal, unsupervised administration, where a personal representative can be appointed without a hearing and can then sell the house for the estate.

How long do creditors have to make claims in a Michigan estate?

Under MCL 700.3801, after the personal representative publishes notice to creditors, creditors generally have four months from the date of publication to present claims.

Is there an inheritance tax in Michigan?

No. Michigan does not have an inheritance tax. Heirs may owe federal capital gains tax only on gain above the stepped-up value as of the date of death when they sell.

What happens if Wayne County already foreclosed on my parent's house?

The family loses title, but under Michigan law former owners may be able to claim surplus proceeds if the house sells at auction for more than what was owed. There is a deadline to file a notice of intent to claim, set in the county's timeline, so heirs should speak with a Michigan attorney quickly.

Should I list an inherited Detroit house or sell it for cash?

If the house is in good condition, title is clear, and it is in a neighborhood with retail buyers, listing usually nets more. A cash sale fits when taxes are delinquent, the house needs major repairs, someone is living in it, or the family needs a fast and certain close.
