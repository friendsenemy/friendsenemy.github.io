Selling a Baltimore Probate House Starts With One Number: Is the Estate Under $50,000?

Baltimore has something many probate systems don't: plenty of houses cheap enough to make the whole estate a small estate. Maryland's line is $50,000 in gross probate assets, or $100,000 if a surviving spouse takes everything. Which side you land on sets the paperwork, the fees and how long your family waits for its money. Here's how to find out, what a personal representative can sign without a judge, and when a cash sale isn't worth it.

In a lot of cities, nobody with a house in the estate needs to think about small-estate limits. The house alone blows past them. Baltimore is different. A boarded or tired rowhouse in parts of West or East Baltimore can appraise for less than a new car, while a similar house a dozen blocks away brings $250,000. So the first probate question here isn't only "was there a will?" It's "what is this house worth on the date of death?"

Maryland handles estates through the Register of Wills in each county and in Baltimore City, with the Orphans' Court for Baltimore City stepping in when someone disputes something or a matter needs a judge. Most estates never see a courtroom. They do need the right track, and the track depends on a number.

⚖️ The $50,000 Line and Why It Falls in the Middle of Baltimore's Market

The Register of Wills treats an estate as a small estate when the probate assets have a gross value of $50,000 or less. If the surviving spouse is the sole heir or legatee, the limit is $100,000. Two words in that rule catch families out.

  • Gross. A mortgage doesn't shrink the figure. A house worth $70,000 with $40,000 still owed isn't a $30,000 asset for this test.
  • Probate. The figure counts what goes through the estate. A house held with a surviving joint owner, retirement accounts with named beneficiaries, and life insurance payable to a person don't count. Add the car and bank account in the decedent's sole name, though, and a $46,000 house can tip over.

The value of the house is therefore worth pinning down early. The Register's guidance says real property in a small estate needs an appraisal by a qualified, disinterested appraiser, although the Register can offer alternative ways of valuing real estate. Don't guess from an online estimate. If you're not sure, call the Register's office before paying for an appraisal and ask what they'll accept.

Don't try to talk the house under the line. It's tempting to want a low number. But the value used in probate affects the heirs' stepped-up tax basis and any inheritance tax, and an appraisal that's far below a sale a few weeks later invites questions. Get an honest appraisal of the house as it stood on the date of death, in its real condition, and let the track follow from that.

✍️ What a Personal Representative Can Sign Without Asking a Judge

Here's the good news that many Baltimore heirs don't hear. Under Estates and Trusts § 7-401, a Maryland personal representative can act without application to, approval of, or ratification by the court, unless the will or a court order limits that power, and that authority includes selling property. So no judge has to approve your buyer, your agent or your price in an ordinary estate.

Three limits apply in practice.

  1. No letters, no deal. A personal representative has to be appointed by the Register or the Orphans' Court before disposing of any asset. A relative who signs a contract as "the executor" before that happens is promising something they can't deliver. A buyer can sign a contingent contract, but closing has to wait.
  2. Read the will. The will can restrict the power, or direct that a specific person receives the house. A specific gift complicates a sale, and the house may not be yours to sell without that person's agreement.
  3. Everything gets reported. In a regular estate, the closing disclosure from the sale goes in with the first account. An heir who thinks the sale was a giveaway can object, and the Orphans' Court can hear it. A personal representative owes duties to everyone interested in the estate. So a sale to a cousin, or to yourself, at a low price needs great care and usually a lawyer.

If nobody agrees on who serves or a will is in doubt, the matter moves from administrative to judicial probate, with hearings before the Orphans' Court. That's where a straightforward few months turns into something longer and costlier. If the argument is really about whether to sell, read our guide for heirs who can't agree. If a will never existed, start with what happens to a house with no will.

⏳ The Six Months Your Family Shouldn't Skip

Under Estates and Trusts § 8-103, creditor claims are generally barred unless presented within six months of death, or within two months after the personal representative sends a written notice to that creditor, whichever is earlier. The State's Medicaid recovery claim has its own timing, tied to publication of the personal representative's appointment.

Those dates don't stop you selling. They do affect when you should hand out money. A sensible approach for a personal representative is to sell when the house is ready and keep the net proceeds in an estate account until the claim period is over. If you pay your brothers in month two and a $30,000 Medicaid claim arrives in month five, you may have a personal problem as well as an estate problem. If your parent spent time in a nursing home on Medicaid, assume a claim may come and have an attorney check.

🧾 What the Estate Pays While the House Waits

Taxes, insurance and upkeep don't pause for probate, and the estate pays them. If the estate has no cash, somebody in the family usually fronts the money and should keep receipts for reimbursement. Baltimore City's real property rate is $2.248 per $100 of assessed value, and the State adds 11.2 cents. On a house assessed at $100,000, that's about $2,360 a year. Baltimore also sells tax liens at its annual tax sale, so a bill that goes unpaid can become an expensive, time-sensitive problem on its own. Vacant-home insurance, water, boarding and grass cutting will often bring the total to $400 to $700 a month for a typical rowhouse. On a $48,000 house, a year of that can take a fifth of the value.

Check too for ground rent and open violations, which are Baltimore-specific title items. Our Baltimore inherited house guide explains how to look them up before you set a price.

🔀 Choosing What to Do With the House

Personal representative lists it

Usually the most money

For a house in livable shape in a working market, list it once letters are issued. No court approval is normally needed, and a good agent who has handled estate sales will know the title company's needs. Expect 60 to 120 days to closing.

Sell to a cash buyer from the estate

Fits small estates and damaged houses

On a low-value house, a 5% commission and a long market time can eat much of what's there, and a retail buyer's lender may refuse the house altogether. A cash sale can close quickly after letters issue. It's a trade of price for certainty, so get more than one offer.

Distribute the house to the heirs

Works if they really want it

The personal representative can deed the house to the people entitled to it. That's fine if one of them will live there. If three heirs take title merely to avoid a decision, you've swapped an estate problem for a co-ownership problem.

Let it go

Sometimes the honest answer

If the taxes, liens and violations exceed the value, no heir is obliged to pay them out of pocket just because a relative died. Don't walk away casually, and talk to an estates attorney first. But nobody should pour savings into a house that can't repay them.

💵 Two Rowhouses, Two Sets of Numbers

Rowhouse A: appraised at $46,000 as of the date of death, the only probate asset. It's a small estate. The Register's fee is $0. The house needs about $25,000 of work and might bring $70,000 repaired. Cash buyers usually pay about 60% to 80% of repaired value, here $42,000 to $56,000. A listing to investors at $45,000 less a commission and settlement costs nets less than a similar cash sale, with more time and carrying cost. In this case cash, from more than one buyer, is hard to beat.

Rowhouse B: appraised at $205,000, sound, needs paint and a water heater. It's a regular estate, the fee is $200, and an attorney-assisted estate commonly costs a few thousand dollars. If it lists at $220,000 and sells for that, the estate nets roughly $205,000 after a 5% commission and the seller's part of settlement costs. A cash offer at about 70% of repaired value, around $155,000, gives up about $50,000. For a family without an emergency, that's a poor trade. We would tell you so.

📋 What To Do This Week

  1. Find the original will, and don't write on it. Maryland requires a will to be filed with the Register after death.
  2. List everything in the person's sole name, and check how title to the house was held.
  3. Call the Register of Wills for Baltimore City and ask how they want real property valued for your likely track.
  4. Decide who will serve, and apply for appointment. You can't sell until letters are issued.
  5. Confirm the insurance, pay or schedule the property tax, and secure the house.
  6. Note the six-month mark from the date of death on your calendar.
  7. Ask whether any Medicaid long-term care was paid, and tell your attorney if so.

✅ Bottom Line

In Baltimore, the value of the house picks your probate track, so establish it first and honestly. Under the line, the process is short and costs little. Over it, a personal representative still has broad power to sell without a judge once letters are issued. Keep money in the estate until creditor time has run. On a low-value or damaged house, a cash sale can be the sensible end of a small estate. On a sound house worth a couple of hundred thousand dollars, it probably isn't, and listing should be your default.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What counts as a small estate in Maryland?

An estate whose probate assets have a gross value of $50,000 or less, or $100,000 or less when the surviving spouse is the sole heir or legatee. The test uses gross value, so a mortgage doesn't lower it. Assets passing outside probate, such as jointly owned property with survivorship, aren't counted.

Does a house count toward the $50,000 small estate limit in Baltimore?

Yes, if it was in the decedent's sole name or otherwise passes through probate. Its full value as of the date of death counts. If the deed gave a surviving joint owner the house automatically, it doesn't. Pull the recorded deed first, since the wording decides this.

How do I value a Baltimore rowhouse for probate?

For a small estate, the Register's guidance calls for an appraisal by a qualified, disinterested appraiser, though the Register can provide alternative ways to value real property. In a regular estate, the personal representative may set the real estate value personally, but an independent appraisal is often wise for tax and family reasons.

Does a Maryland personal representative need court approval to sell a house?

Usually not. Estates and Trusts section 7-401 lets a personal representative act without court approval, including selling property, unless the will or a court order limits that power. The person must first be appointed and hold letters, and in a regular estate the sale is reported in an account.

How long does probate take in Baltimore City?

A small estate can often wrap up within a few months. A regular estate usually runs 9 to 18 months because of the inventory, the first account and the six-month creditor period. Modified administration aims for about a year. Disputes in the Orphans' Court can add a good deal more.

Can I sell before the six-month creditor period ends?

Generally yes, once letters are issued. The claim period limits when it's safe to distribute money rather than when you may sell. Many personal representatives close the sale, hold the net funds in the estate account, and pay heirs after claims have been resolved.

What does it cost to open an estate in Baltimore?

For estates opened since October 1, 2022, the Register's probate fee is $0 under $50,000, $100 from $50,000 to under $100,000, and $200 from $100,000 to under $500,000. Newspaper notices, an appraisal and attorney fees are separate. A simple regular estate with a lawyer commonly costs a few thousand dollars.

Who picks the personal representative if there's no will?

Maryland law sets an order of priority, with a surviving spouse and children near the top. People with equal priority may need to agree or have the Orphans' Court decide. When family members can't agree, that's often the point at which an estates attorney saves money rather than costing it.

What happens if the estate can't pay the property taxes on the house?

Taxes keep accruing, and Baltimore City can sell the lien at its annual tax sale, which adds costs and starts a clock on the owner's rights. Often a family member advances the money and is repaid from the sale. If the numbers are hopeless, ask an attorney before paying more.

Is a cash buyer a good idea for a small estate in Baltimore?

It can be. When a house is worth under $50,000 and needs major work, commissions and months of carrying costs take a big share, and retail financing may not be available. Compare at least two written offers. On a livable house that's worth much more, listing usually pays better.
