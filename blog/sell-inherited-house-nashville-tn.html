Selling an Inherited House in Nashville: Tennessee Has No Shortcut for the Deed, So Plan Around Signatures

Tennessee never adopted a transfer-on-death deed, and the small estate process in Davidson County expressly can't transfer real estate. So for most Nashville houses left in one person's name, the sale comes down to one practical question: will every heir sign, or does a personal representative need court-backed authority? Here is how each route works, what it costs, and why in this market listing usually beats a cash offer.

A lot of families arrive at this question already worn out. Someone has been handling the funeral, the mail, the lawn on Saturdays. Then a neighbor mentions that houses on the street are selling for twice what Mom paid, a cousin says probate takes two years, and somebody else read online that you can "just file a small estate affidavit." In Tennessee, two of those three things are usually wrong.

This guide is built around how a Nashville inherited house actually gets sold, starting with the two shortcuts Tennessee doesn't offer, then the two ways a deed can be signed, the Davidson County court process, a surviving spouse's rights, the money, and what happens when one heir won't go along.

🚫 No Transfer-on-Death Deed, and No Small-Estate Shortcut for the House

Many states let a homeowner record a transfer-on-death deed that hands the house to a named beneficiary at death with no court involvement. Tennessee isn't one of them. Bills to adopt a Uniform Real Property Transfer on Death Act have been introduced in the General Assembly several times and haven't passed. A 2025 Senate version stalled in committee, and a 2026 House version was withdrawn. If someone tells you your parent's house "passed by TOD deed" in Tennessee, ask to see it; it likely doesn't exist, or it was a different kind of document.

The second shortcut people reach for is the small estate process. Tennessee allows a simplified small estate for personal property of $50,000 or less (T.C.A. §§ 30-4-101 and following). But Davidson County's own small estate petition form states, in capital letters, that filing a small estate petition is not valid for the transfer of real estate. Real estate isn't counted toward the $50,000 limit, and it isn't moved by it either.

⚠️ The key point for Nashville heirs: unless the house was owned jointly with survivorship rights or held in a trust, neither of the common shortcuts will put it in a sellable position. Either every heir signs the deed, or a personal representative needs authority from the will or the court to sell. Plan around signatures from day one.

Before going further, look at the deed on the Davidson County Register of Deeds site. If it says the owners held "as tenants by the entirety" (a married couple) or "with right of survivorship," the surviving owner already owns the house and can sell with a death certificate. If it was deeded to a living trust, the successor trustee sells under the trust, with no probate at all. Everything below assumes the house was in the decedent's name alone.

🖊️ Two Ways the Deed Gets Signed

Tennessee treats real estate differently from bank accounts and cars. Under T.C.A. § 31-2-103, real estate of someone who dies without a will vests immediately in the heirs. Land left by will goes to the people named in the will, unless the will puts it under the personal representative's control. In plain terms, the house usually belongs to the heirs or beneficiaries from the moment of death, not to "the estate." That produces two routes to a sale.

👨‍👩‍👧 Route one: every heir or beneficiary signs

If the heirs own the house, they can sell it together. Each heir, and each heir's spouse where the title company requires it, signs the deed. Title companies typically want to establish who the heirs are, often with an affidavit of heirship from a disinterested person who knew the family, plus a death certificate, and they'll look at whether a will might surface or creditors might have claims against the property. Some will insure the sale without a probate estate; others want an estate opened and the creditor period to run first. Ask the title company early, because its answer sets your timeline.

This route is fast and cheap when the family is small and cooperative. It falls apart when an heir can't be found, has died leaving their own heirs, is a minor, lacks capacity, or simply won't sign.

⚖️ Route two: a personal representative sells

If the will gives the executor the power to sell real estate, the executor can sell after Davidson County Probate Court issues letters testamentary, without every beneficiary signing. If there's no will, or the will doesn't grant that power, a personal representative generally needs a court order to sell. Tennessee's statutes for selling a decedent's land to pay debts (Title 30, Chapter 2, Part 4) require the court to find that the personal property isn't enough to cover what the estate owes. When the debts don't require a sale, the house typically stays with the heirs, and you're back to route one.

Which route fits you depends on three things: what the will says about selling, how many heirs there are and whether they agree, and whether the estate has debts the house will need to pay.

🏛️ Davidson County Probate Court in Practice

In Nashville, estates are handled by the Probate Court, the probate division of the Seventh Circuit Court, which has exclusive jurisdiction over estate administration in Davidson County. The clerk's office is at 1 Public Square in downtown Nashville. As of January 1, 2026, the Circuit Court Clerk lists a filing fee of $334.50 for a petition to probate a will, a petition for letters of administration, or a small estate petition. Certified copies of letters and the required newspaper publication add modest costs.

Once letters issue, the clerk publishes a notice to creditors. Creditors generally have four months from the first publication to file claims, and under Tennessee law claims are barred twelve months after the date of death in most cases even if no estate is ever opened. Simple estates often wrap up in roughly six to twelve months; estates with disputes, missing heirs, or litigation run longer.

For the house, the most useful thing about opening an estate is often clarity: a personal representative with letters, a published creditor notice, and a court file that a title company can rely on. 💍 A Surviving Spouse Can Change the Math

If the decedent was married, the spouse's rights have to be sorted out before anyone can say who owns what share of the house.

  • 📜 With no will: under T.C.A. § 31-2-104, a surviving spouse with children shares the estate with them, but the spouse's share is never less than one-third. With no children, the spouse takes everything.
  • ⏳ With a will that leaves the spouse out or short: Tennessee's elective share (T.C.A. § 31-4-101) lets the spouse claim a percentage of the net estate that rises with the length of the marriage, from 10% for a marriage of under three years up to 40% for nine years or more. The spouse has nine months from death to petition for it.
  • 🏠 Homestead: Tennessee's homestead exemption, $35,000 for an individual under T.C.A. § 26-2-301, continues for a surviving spouse and minor children. In a city where most houses are worth many times that, it rarely blocks a sale, but it's one more interest the title company will want accounted for.

In blended families, where the house was Dad's before he remarried and his children from a first marriage expect it, these rules are where disagreements start. Get them answered by a Tennessee attorney before anyone signs a listing agreement.

📈 Nashville Prices Usually Make Listing the Better Deal

Here's where we argue against ourselves. The Nashville-area median sale price hit a record $495,000 in mid-2026, according to the REMAX National Housing Report. Many inherited houses were bought decades ago for a small fraction of that. When the asset is that valuable, a few percent of price is real money, and cash buyers, including us, generally pay about 60% to 80% of what a house would be worth fully repaired, landing lower in that range the more work it needs.

Two tax notes that often tip the decision toward selling soon after death rather than years later. Tennessee's inheritance tax is repealed for deaths in 2016 and later, so there's no state tax on the inheritance itself. And under federal tax rules, inherited property generally gets a "stepped-up" basis equal to its value at death, so heirs who sell soon after usually have little or no capital gain. Ask a tax professional about your specific situation; the longer the heirs hold the house, the more any new appreciation becomes taxable gain.

🧾 What Holding the House Costs in Davidson County

Metro Nashville taxes residential property on 25% of its appraised value. The Trustee lists current rates of $2.814 per $100 of assessed value in the Urban Services District and $2.782 in the General Services District, with satellite cities like Belle Meade, Berry Hill, Forest Hills, and Goodlettsville adding their own rate on top of the GSD rate.

  • 🏛️ Property tax: a $500,000 house in the Urban Services District is assessed at $125,000, for a tax bill of about $3,518 a year, roughly $293 a month.
  • 🛡️ Insurance: once the house is empty, most homeowner's policies limit coverage after a vacancy period. A vacant-dwelling policy usually runs $100 to $250 a month in Middle Tennessee.
  • 💡 Utilities: $150 to $300 a month to keep power, water, and climate control on through humid summers and the occasional hard freeze.
  • 🌿 Yard and upkeep: $100 to $200 a month; Metro Codes responds to overgrown lots and complaints about vacant properties.
  • ⚖️ Legal costs: a simple Tennessee probate often runs a few thousand dollars in attorney fees, more for contested or court-ordered sales; Nashville rates tend to sit at the upper end of state ranges.

Before any mortgage payment, an empty Nashville house typically costs the heirs $650 to $1,000 a month. That's real, but on a $500,000 house it rarely outweighs the price advantage of listing. It's the condition of the house and the cooperation of the heirs, not carrying costs, that should decide this.

⚔️ If One Heir Won't Sign: Partition and Tennessee's Heirs Property Act

Because the heirs usually own the house directly, one holdout can stop a route-one sale. The tool Tennessee gives the others is partition. Under T.C.A. § 29-27-101, any co-owner, even one with a small share, can ask a court to divide the property or order it sold. A single-family house can't be split, so in practice the remedy is usually a sale, with the proceeds divided by ownership share after costs.

Since July 1, 2022, Tennessee has also applied a version of the Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act to qualifying inherited property, starting at T.C.A. § 29-27-303. It gives co-owners who didn't file the case a chance to buy out the filer's share at a court-determined value before any sale is ordered, and it requires the court to consider factors beyond money. Tennessee's version leaves out the uniform act's preference for physically dividing the land, which matters more for farmland than for a house in Madison.

Partition costs money and time, and it often produces a lower price than a cooperative listing. It works best as the thing everybody wants to avoid. Read our partition lawsuit guide and our guide to what to do when heirs can't agree on selling. One more option: an heir who wants out can sell their share. A buyer of a fractional interest pays less than a proportional share of the whole, because it takes on the dispute, but it lets that heir leave without forcing anyone else's hand.

📋 This Week's Steps for a Nashville Inheritance

  1. Pull the deed from the Davidson County Register of Deeds and note exactly how the house was titled.
  2. Find the will and look for a power of sale for the executor.
  3. Write down every heir, including the children of any heir who has already died, and note anyone who is a minor or can't sign.
  4. Call a title company and ask what it would require to insure a sale by the heirs without a probate estate.
  5. Secure the house: change the locks, keep the utilities on, and move to a vacant-dwelling policy.
  6. Check for a mortgage or reverse mortgage and notify the servicer of the death in writing.
  7. Get two numbers: an agent's estimate of value and a written cash offer, then compare what each nets after commission, repairs, and time.

✅ Where This Leaves Nashville Heirs

Tennessee doesn't give Nashville families a recorded TOD deed or a small-estate filing that moves the house, so every sale of a house left in one person's name comes down to signatures: all the heirs together, or a personal representative with authority from the will or the court. Figure out which one you have, and ask a title company what it needs, before you do anything else.

After that, let the house decide. In most of Nashville, a sound house with cooperative heirs will bring noticeably more on the open market, and listing is the right call. When the house needs serious work, the heirs are scattered or stuck, or a partition fight is looming, a cash sale can be the cleaner exit. We'll tell you which situation you're in if you ask.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Does Tennessee have a transfer-on-death deed?

No. Tennessee has not adopted a transfer-on-death deed law. Bills to adopt one have been introduced repeatedly, including in 2025 and 2026, without passing. A house in one person's name generally passes to the heirs or the people named in the will.

Can I use a small estate affidavit to transfer a house in Davidson County?

No. Davidson County's small estate petition form states that filing a small estate petition is not valid for the transfer of real estate. The small estate process covers personal property of $50,000 or less.

Do the heirs own a Tennessee house right after the owner dies?

Generally yes. Under T.C.A. 31-2-103, real estate of someone who dies without a will vests immediately in the heirs, and real estate left by will goes to the beneficiaries unless the will gives the personal representative control. The estate can still need the house to pay debts.

Can heirs in Nashville sell an inherited house without opening probate?

Sometimes. If every heir signs the deed and a title company is willing to insure the sale based on the death certificate and heirship documentation, a sale can close without an estate. Some title companies require an estate to be opened and the creditor period to run first.

Which court handles probate in Nashville?

The Probate Court, which is the probate division of the Seventh Circuit Court, has exclusive jurisdiction over estate administration in Davidson County. The clerk's office is at 1 Public Square. As of January 2026, the filing fee for a petition to probate a will or for letters of administration is $334.50.

How long do creditors have to make claims against a Tennessee estate?

Usually four months from the first publication of the notice to creditors, and in most cases claims are barred twelve months after the date of death even if no estate is opened.

What share of the house does a surviving spouse get in Tennessee?

Without a will, a spouse with children shares with them but never receives less than one-third; with no children, the spouse takes everything. With a will, the spouse can claim an elective share of 10% to 40% of the net estate depending on how long the marriage lasted, by petitioning within nine months of death.

Is there an inheritance tax on a house inherited in Tennessee?

No. The Tennessee Department of Revenue states that the inheritance tax is repealed for deaths in 2016 and later. Federal estate tax applies only to very large estates.

What can I do if one heir refuses to sell the Nashville house?

Any co-owner can file for partition under T.C.A. 29-27-101, and for qualifying inherited property Tennessee's version of the Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act gives the other co-owners a chance to buy out the filer's share first. Partition is slow and expensive, so a negotiated buyout or the sale of one heir's share is often better.

Should we list the inherited house or sell it for cash in Nashville?

In most of Nashville, a house in sound condition with cooperative heirs nets noticeably more listed on the open market. A cash sale is worth comparing when the house needs major repairs, the heirs are spread out or in conflict, or a quick, certain closing matters more than the last dollar.
