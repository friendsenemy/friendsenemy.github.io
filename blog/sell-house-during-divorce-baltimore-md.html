Divorcing in Baltimore With Kids and a House? Decide About Use and Possession Before You Decide to Sell

Maryland gives divorce judges an unusual tool: a custodial parent can be awarded the family home for up to three years after the divorce, whoever's name is on the deed. That can freeze a sale, change who pays the mortgage and reshape what a buyout is worth. Here's who qualifies, how it interacts with Maryland's 2023 divorce law, and how to choose between keeping, buying out and selling.

By Dustin Ray, Founder of Pages of Purpose LLC. Not legal advice — laws differ by state, and Maryland family law has changed recently. Speak with an attorney licensed where the property is, in this case Maryland, before you agree to anything or sign a deed.

The argument usually starts at the kitchen table in Lauraville or Canton. One of you wants the house sold so you can both start over. The other says the kids need to finish at their school, and they're not leaving. In many states, that argument gets settled by dividing the equity and selling. In Maryland, there's a statute that may give the parent with the kids the stronger hand for a while, even if the other spouse owns most of the house.

So if there are minor children, don't start with what the house would sell for. Start by asking whether use and possession is likely, because it decides whether "sell now" is even available without a deal.

🏠 Use and Possession: The Three-Year Hold on a Maryland Family Home

Maryland's Family Law Article, Title 8, lets a court give one spouse exclusive use and possession of the family home. Under the statute and how it's summarized by practitioners and Maryland Legal Aid, here's what to know:

  • Who can get it. It's tied to children. Generally the applicant has custody of at least one minor child of the marriage, and the home is that parent's and the child's principal residence. A stepchild doesn't qualify the home on its own.
  • How long. Up to three years after the divorce. Under §§ 8-208 and 8-210, the award ends sooner if the custodial spouse remarries or the youngest child turns 18.
  • What the judge weighs. The children's stability, schools and community, the housing needs of both spouses, and each one's financial ability.
  • Who pays. The court can order the spouse who moved out to pay all or part of the mortgage, insurance, taxes and upkeep during the award.
  • What it isn't. It's possession, not ownership. The other spouse keeps their share of the equity, and it's realized when the award ends, unless a buyout has happened.
The practical effect: a house with use and possession generally can't be sold out from under the custodial parent during the award. If you're the one who needs your equity now, a three-year wait may hurt more than a smaller number today. That's real bargaining room for a settlement, and it cuts both ways.

👀 What the spouse without the kids should think about

If you've moved out, you may still be on the mortgage, may be ordered to contribute, and can't touch your equity. Ask your attorney whether you can trade away the three-year risk in a settlement, for example by agreeing to a fixed sale date in exchange for leaving certain payments in place. Federal tax law helps a little here. Under Internal Revenue Code § 121(d)(3)(B), a spouse who moved out can generally be treated as using the home during the period the other spouse has it under a divorce instrument, which can protect the capital gains exclusion when the sale finally happens. Confirm this with a tax professional before relying on it.

📅 The 2023 Law Took Away a Lot of the Waiting

Since October 1, 2023, Maryland has been a no-fault-only state with no separate limited divorce. Under Family Law § 7-103, the grounds are:

  • Six-month separation. You've lived separate and apart for six months. You can meet this while under the same roof, in separate bedrooms with separate finances.
  • Mutual consent. A signed agreement covers custody, support, alimony and property, and it's presented at the hearing. There's no waiting period.
  • Irreconcilable differences. Also no waiting period.

This matters for the house because you no longer need one spouse to leave to get the clock running, and a full agreement can bring a divorce quickly. Many couples can decide the house in the agreement and be done. In Baltimore City, contested cases go through the Circuit Court, and a contested trial date is typically many months away. If you want a faster outcome for the house, the agreement is the way.

🔑 Why Neither of You Can Quietly Sell

Most Baltimore married couples hold their house as tenants by the entirety. While you're married, neither one can sell or mortgage it alone. A deed needs both signatures. When the divorce becomes final, an entirety tenancy generally ends and you become tenants in common, each with an undivided share. That change has a catch: after the divorce, either of you can file a partition case in the Circuit Court to force a sale, and a co-owner's creditors can reach that share. So a decree that says nothing about the house leaves an open door. Our national divorce guide and partition guide cover that path. For statewide basics on equitable distribution, see our Maryland divorce house guide.

🤝 The Buyout Tool Maryland Gives the Judge

For years Maryland judges could value marital property and make a monetary award but had limited power to retitle a house. Family Law § 8-205 now lets a court transfer one party's interest in a jointly owned family home to the other, or authorize one to buy the other out on terms it sets, as long as the person keeping the house gets the other released from the lien.

That last condition is what stops most buyouts. A refinance in one name has to qualify on one income, and today's rate may be much higher than the old one. So check the refinance before spending money arguing for the house. Two cost points help. Under Tax-Property § 12-108(d), a deed between spouses or former spouses is exempt from recordation tax, and the state transfer tax has a similar exemption. Confirm the city's treatment with your title agent, since Baltimore's transfer tax is 1.5%, and a recordation-and-transfer bill on a normal sale would run about 3% in total.

💵 What the Numbers Might Look Like on a Baltimore Rowhouse

Take a Hamilton house worth $285,000 in good shape, with $150,000 owed. The couple's equity is $135,000. With the assessment at about $250,000, the combined city and state tax is around $5,900 a year, or roughly $490 a month. Add insurance at about $120 a month and repairs of $150 a month, and the house costs $760 a month before the mortgage payment.

  • List it at $285,000: about 5% commission and the seller's share of settlement costs take around $19,000, leaving perhaps $116,000 to divide, or $58,000 each if split evenly.
  • Buyout: at an even split, the staying spouse pays about $67,500 and must refinance around $217,500. On a single income that may not pass.
  • Cash sale at 70%: $199,500 less the mortgage leaves about $49,500, before smaller closing costs. That's around $25,000 each.
  • Wait three years under use and possession: the equity may grow, and some principal gets paid down, but the moved-out spouse waits, and repair bills may pile up without any agreement on who pays.

On a house in that shape, listing wins by a wide margin, and a cash buyer who tells you otherwise isn't being straight with you. The math shifts if there's a failing roof, unpermitted work or an arrears problem. Maryland courts don't divide marital property by a fixed rule either, so don't assume an even split. A monetary award can shift it.

⚠️ Mistakes That Cost Baltimore Couples Money

  1. Signing a quitclaim deed before the mortgage is handled. You give up the house and stay on the debt.
  2. Moving out without advice while custody is unsettled. That can shape a use and possession outcome.
  3. Leaving the house out of the agreement. Then you're co-owners with a partition case as the remedy.
  4. Letting the mortgage slide. Maryland foreclosures take time, but late payments damage both of you right away. Our guide on selling to stop foreclosure covers what to do if that's already happening.

📋 What To Do This Week

  1. Write down who's on the deed and who's on the mortgage. They may not match.
  2. Pull the latest mortgage statement and the city tax bill so you're using real numbers.
  3. If you have minor children, ask a Maryland family lawyer whether use and possession is likely in your case.
  4. Get a refinance preapproval if either of you hopes to keep the house.
  5. Get an appraisal or two agent opinions, and a repair list.
  6. Keep paying the mortgage and the insurance, whatever else is in dispute.
  7. Don't list, deed or borrow against the house without talking to your attorney if a case has been filed.

✅ Bottom Line

With children and a Baltimore house, the first question is whether use and possession is on the table. If it is, a sale may not be possible for up to three years without an agreement. Without kids, or once that's settled, the choice is between a buyout that can actually be financed and a joint sale. List a good house. Use a cash sale when condition, conflict or a behind-on-payments mortgage makes a retail sale unrealistic. Whatever you pick, put it in the signed agreement, including what happens if the plan falls through.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What is use and possession of the family home in Maryland?

It's a court award giving one spouse, generally the parent with custody of a minor child, the exclusive right to live in the family home for up to three years after divorce. It doesn't change who owns the home. The court can also order the other spouse to help pay the costs.

Can my spouse force a sale while I live in the house with our kids?

Not while a valid use and possession order is in effect, in most situations. Before any order exists, a sale still needs both signatures if you own as tenants by the entirety. After the order ends, either owner can ask for a sale. Speak to a Maryland attorney early so the order is requested in time.

When does use and possession end in Maryland?

At the latest, three years after the divorce. It ends sooner if the custodial spouse remarries or the youngest child turns 18. Once it ends, the home is usually sold or bought out as the decree or agreement directs, so write that plan down now.

Do I have to move out to get divorced in Maryland?

No. Since October 2023, you can meet the six-month separation ground under one roof if you live separate lives. Mutual consent and irreconcilable differences don't have a waiting period at all. Moving out can still affect custody and use and possession, so get advice first.

Can one spouse sell a tenants by the entirety house in Baltimore?

No. While you're married, it takes both signatures. After a final divorce, you generally become tenants in common and each can transfer only their own share, but a full sale still needs both owners or a court order.

Can a Maryland judge order one spouse to buy the other out of the house?

Under Family Law section 8-205, a court can transfer an interest in a jointly owned family home, or authorize a purchase on terms it sets, if the spouse keeping the home gets the other released from the lien. In practice, that usually means a refinance has to work.

Do we pay transfer taxes when one spouse deeds the house to the other in Baltimore?

Maryland exempts deeds between spouses and former spouses from recordation tax under Tax-Property section 12-108, and the state transfer tax has a similar exemption. Ask the title agent how Baltimore City's own transfer tax is treated, and whether debt taken over affects the answer.

Who pays the mortgage during a Maryland divorce?

Both borrowers stay liable to the lender whatever a court says between you. A judge can allocate payments, including during use and possession. If a payment is missed, both credit reports are affected, so get an agreement in writing and watch the mortgage account.

Can I still avoid capital gains if my ex lives in the house for three years?

Often yes. Federal law generally lets you count the time your former spouse uses the home under a divorce instrument, which can help you meet the two-out-of-five-year use test. Ownership rules and the size of the gain still matter, so check with a tax professional.

Should we list our Baltimore house or sell it for cash during the divorce?

List it if it's in decent shape, there's equity, and you can cooperate on showings. Consider cash if it needs major work, payments are behind, or the conflict makes a months-long sale unrealistic. Compare the net to each spouse, not only the sale price.
