Partition Lawsuits in Maryland: Since 2022, Every Case Starts With an Appraisal and a Buyout Window, Not an Auction

Maryland threw out its old partition statute in 2022. Cases filed since October 1, 2022 follow Real Property Title 14, Subtitle 7: the court sets a value first, co-owners who didn't ask for a sale get 45 days to buy out those who did, and a forced sale goes to a licensed broker on the open market before anyone thinks about an auction. Many websites still describe the old law. Here is the current one.

By Dustin Ray, Founder of Pages of Purpose LLC. Not legal advice — laws differ by state, and Maryland's partition statute and court rules are only a few years old, so case law is still developing. Ask a Maryland-licensed attorney, licensed where the property is located, to review your situation before you file or answer a complaint.

🆕 Maryland Repealed Its Old Partition Law: What Replaced It

If you searched "Maryland partition" and read that a circuit court may order a sale whenever property "cannot be divided without loss or injury" under Real Property §14-107, you were reading the old law. Senate Bill 92 of 2022 (Chapter 402) repealed §14-107 and enacted a new Real Property Title 14, Subtitle 7, "Partition of Real Property," sections 14-701 through 14-713. It took effect October 1, 2022, and applies to actions filed on or after that date. The Supreme Court of Maryland followed with new Maryland Rules 12-401 through 12-410, effective July 1, 2023.

The bill began life as Maryland's version of the Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act. Most states that adopt that act limit its protections to "heirs property" — land passed down in families, measured by percentage tests. Maryland's General Assembly dropped those tests. Section 14-702(a) says real property "shall be partitioned under this subtitle unless all of the cotenants agree otherwise in a record." In other words, the appraisal, buyout and open-market-sale protections apply to almost every co-owned property in Maryland — two friends who bought a condo together, ex-partners, investors, as well as families.

⚠️ The warning that matters most: co-owners who signed a written co-ownership agreement setting a different partition process may be outside the new subtitle. Everyone else is inside it. If you are relying on advice, a blog post or even a lawyer's letter that cites §14-107 or describes commissioners who divide the land, ask whether it reflects the post-2022 law.

📐 Step One Is Always a Value

Under §14-707, before the court considers anything else, it determines what the property is worth. The default is an appraisal by a disinterested, Maryland-licensed appraiser. There are three alternatives: an existing appraisal dated no more than six months before filing, if nobody objects; a value or method all cotenants agree on; or a court hearing, if an appraisal would cost more than it is worth. Rule 12-406 lets the court divide the appraisal cost among the parties.

The schedule is tight. The court sends notice within 10 days after the appraisal is filed; any party has 30 days to object; and the hearing on value is held no sooner than 30 days after notice. That value then drives the buyout price, the listing price and the floor on any sale. A co-owner who thinks the appraiser missed a new roof — or missed foundation damage — needs to object in that window with evidence, not later.

⏳ The 45-Day Buyout Window

Section 14-708 is the heart of the new law. If any cotenant asks the court for a sale, the cotenants who did not ask for a sale get 45 days from notice to elect to buy all the interests of those who did. The price is fixed: the court-determined value times each seller's fractional share. Electing buyers must pay into court by a deadline the court sets, at least 60 days after notice. If some electing buyers don't pay, the others get a 20-day follow-on round to pick up those shares.

For a Maryland family, the practical upshot is simple. The sibling who wants to keep the house doesn't have to outbid investors at a trustee's auction; she has to come up with her siblings' shares of an appraised value within a court deadline. If she can't finance it, the case moves on. If you want to keep the property, talk to a lender before the appraisal is even filed — 45 days to elect and roughly 60 to pay is not long to close a loan.

🧱 Division in Kind Is the Preference on Paper

If no buyout happens, §14-709 tells the court to order partition in kind — physical division — unless that would result in "great prejudice to the cotenants as a group." Section 14-710 lists the factors the court weighs, including whether the property can practicably be divided, family ownership history, sentimental attachment and who has paid the taxes and insurance; no single factor controls. Division in kind uses a writ of survey under Rule 12-408, and parties get 30 days to object to the survey.

For a rowhouse in Baltimore or a split-level in Bowie, division is almost never practical, so the case proceeds to a sale. For acreage on the Eastern Shore or in Southern Maryland, the factors can matter a great deal.

🏷️ The Sale Goes to a Broker, Not the Courthouse Steps

When the court orders a sale, §14-711 makes an open-market sale through a Maryland-licensed real estate broker the default. The parties have 10 days to agree on a broker; otherwise the court appoints one and sets a reasonable commission. The listing price can't be below the court-determined value. The broker must report any offer at or above that value within 7 days (§14-712). Under Rule 12-409 broker reports stay sealed until a status hearing, and the court holds an evidentiary hearing before approving the sale. A cotenant who buys is credited with his own share of the proceeds.

Only if no acceptable offer comes in can the court approve the best offer received, reset the value, or order a sale by sealed bids or auction. Those fallback sales follow the judicial-sale rules in Title 14, Chapter 300: a court-appointed trustee posts a bond unless excused (Rule 14-303); a public sale is advertised once a week for three weeks; and after the trustee reports the sale, the clerk publishes a notice that it will be ratified unless cause is shown within 30 days (Rule 14-305). Exceptions are due in that window; the court may refer the account to an auditor.

💍 Tenancy by the Entirety and Joint Tenancy

Maryland still recognizes tenancy by the entirety for married couples, and the committee note to Rule 12-404 confirms that entireties property can't be partitioned. Under Maryland case law, as the Judiciary's People's Law Library explains, a divorce converts a tenancy by the entirety into a tenancy in common — after which either former spouse can seek partition. During the divorce itself, Family Law §8-202 lets the divorce court decide ownership of jointly titled property and order a partition or a sale instead. Our guide to selling a house during a Maryland divorce covers that route.

Joint tenancy works differently. Under Real Property §2-117, no deed creates a joint tenancy unless it expressly says so, and the same committee note says a joint tenancy has to be severed into a tenancy in common before anyone can seek partition. Most Maryland co-owners who inherited a house are already tenants in common.

🌍 Where to File and Who Must Be Found

Rule 12-403 puts venue in the circuit court for a county where all or part of the property sits; Baltimore City is its own county for this purpose, so city cases go to the Circuit Court for Baltimore City. Section 14-703 lets the court appoint an attorney for a party, require additional parties to be joined, and require a title report covering at least 60 years — useful in heirs cases where a deed from 1970 left the house to eight children.

Missing co-owners are common. Under §§14-705 and 14-706, service by publication requires an affidavit of diligent search, and the plaintiff must post the summons and complaint on the property within 10 days. Publication can't be used against an unknown person who is in open and actual possession of the property.

💰 What a Maryland Partition Costs

The new subtitle contains no attorney-fee provision; each side generally pays its own lawyer, and contested cases commonly cost each party many thousands of dollars. Add the appraisal (split as the court orders), the broker's commission on an open-market sale, and Maryland's transfer and recordation taxes. The state transfer tax is 0.5% (Tax-Property §13-203). Counties add their own: Baltimore City charges a 1.5% transfer tax, and Prince George's County 1.4%; recordation taxes are set locally on top.

Carrying costs depend heavily on the jurisdiction. Baltimore City's real property tax rate is $2.248 per $100 of assessed value plus the state's $0.112 — about 2.36% a year, among the highest in Maryland. Prince George's charges $1.00 per $100 plus the state rate (municipal taxes can add more). If no owner lives there, the Homestead Tax Credit (which caps annual assessment increases at 4% in Baltimore City and 3% in Prince George's for owner-occupants) doesn't help.

For a vacant Baltimore City rowhouse assessed at $200,000, that is about $393 a month in property tax alone. Add vacant-property insurance ($100–$200), utilities ($75–$150) and basic upkeep ($100–$200), and the house costs $670 to $940 a month. A partition that takes a year — a realistic estimate once appraisal objections, the 45-day election, the payment deadline and a listing period are added up — burns $8,000 to $11,000 before attorney fees.

💵 A Baltimore City Rowhouse, Run Through the New Law

Two cousins each own half of a rowhouse in northeast Baltimore. Repaired, it would sell for $230,000; it needs about $45,000 of work. Cousin A lives there. Cousin B files and asks for a sale.

  • 📐 Value: the court-appointed appraiser sets the as-is value at $180,000.
  • ⏳ Buyout: A didn't ask for a sale, so A has 45 days to elect to buy B's half for $90,000 and roughly 60 days to pay it into court. If A can finance it, A keeps the house and the case ends.
  • 🏷️ Broker sale: if A can't, a licensed broker lists it at no less than $180,000. After a 6% commission and B's share of the transfer and recordation taxes, each cousin nets somewhere around $80,000–$84,000 — after a year of carrying costs split between them.
  • 💵 Cash sale before trial: cash buyers usually pay about 60–80% of repaired value. At 70% of $230,000, that is $161,000, or $80,500 each, with no appraisal fights, no listing and a closing in weeks. Close to the broker number, but without the year.
  • 🧩 B sells only his half: B can do that without A's consent; a buyer steps into the case and the 45-day buyout right still protects A.

✅ Bottom Line

Maryland partition changed completely in 2022. The old "loss or injury" statute is gone. Today a case starts with a court-determined value, gives the co-owners who didn't ask for a sale 45 days to buy the others out, prefers physical division where it works, and sends houses to a licensed broker on the open market before any auction. Tenancy by the entirety property still can't be partitioned while the marriage lasts.

If you want the house, plan to use the buyout. If everyone wants out and the house is sound, agree on a broker and list. If the house needs more work than buyers will finance, or a co-owner won't engage, ask us for a number to put next to the others.

❓ Maryland Partition Questions We Get Asked

Is Real Property section 14-107 still the Maryland partition statute?

No. Senate Bill 92 of 2022 repealed section 14-107 and enacted Real Property sections 14-701 through 14-713, effective October 1, 2022, for partition actions filed on or after that date.

Does Maryland's partition law apply only to heirs property?

No. Although the bill started as a Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act, section 14-702(a) applies the new procedure to real property generally, unless all of the cotenants agree otherwise in a record.

How does the 45-day buyout work in a Maryland partition?

Under section 14-708, if any cotenant asks for a sale, the cotenants who did not ask for a sale have 45 days after notice to elect to buy those interests at the court-determined value times each fractional share, and must pay into court by a deadline at least 60 days after notice.

How is the value set in a Maryland partition case?

Section 14-707 calls for an appraisal by a disinterested Maryland-licensed appraiser, unless the parties agree on a value or method, use an appraisal made within six months before filing with no objection, or the court holds a hearing because an appraisal would cost too much. Parties have 30 days after notice to object.

Will a Maryland court auction a co-owned house?

Not as the first step. Under section 14-711, a court-ordered sale is an open-market sale through a Maryland-licensed broker at no less than the court-determined value. Sealed bids or an auction are fallbacks if no acceptable offer comes in.

Can tenants by the entirety property be partitioned in Maryland?

No. The committee note to Maryland Rule 12-404 confirms that tenants by the entirety property is not subject to partition. Under Maryland case law, a divorce converts it into a tenancy in common, after which partition is possible.

Where is a Maryland partition case filed?

Under Maryland Rule 12-403, in the circuit court for a county where all or part of the property is located. Baltimore City is treated as a county, so cases there go to the Circuit Court for Baltimore City.

What happens to a Maryland partition sale after the auction?

If the case falls back to an auction or sealed-bid sale, the trustee reports the sale and the clerk publishes a notice that it will be ratified unless cause is shown within 30 days under Rule 14-305. Exceptions must be filed within that period.

Who pays attorney fees in a Maryland partition?

The new partition subtitle has no attorney-fee provision, so each party generally pays his or her own lawyer. The court can divide appraisal costs among the parties under Rule 12-406, and the broker's commission is set by the court.

Should I sell my share of a Maryland house instead of going through partition?

If the other owners will cooperate, an agreed listing or buyout usually nets more. Selling your share for cash fits when you need out quickly; a buyer of your share steps into your position, and the other owners keep their buyout rights if a partition is filed.
