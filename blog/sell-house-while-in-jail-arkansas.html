Selling an Arkansas House From Jail or Prison: Before Unpaid Taxes Send It to the Commissioner of State Lands

Arkansas doesn't sell tax liens to investors. When property taxes go unpaid for a year past the October 15 due date, the land is forfeited to the State and certified to the Commissioner of State Lands, who can auction it a year later. For an owner serving time at Cummins, Varner or McPherson, that slow, quiet clock is often what decides whether the family keeps any of the equity.

🏛️ Unpaid Taxes Turn Into State Land

Arkansas handles delinquent real estate taxes differently from states that sell tax lien certificates. Under Ark. Code § 26-37-101, land on which taxes haven't been paid for one year after the October 15 due date is forfeited to the State and certified by the county to the Commissioner of State Lands in Little Rock. On certification, legal title vests in the State, held in the Commissioner's care, subject to the owner's right to redeem.

The Commissioner's rules then set the next step. The sale date can be no earlier than one year after the parcel is certified. The office sends redemption notices and offers unredeemed parcels at a public auction. If a parcel doesn't sell, it goes into ongoing unsold-property auctions starting about 30 days later. The owner, or anyone with a legal interest, can redeem by paying the taxes, penalties, interest and costs up until 4:00 p.m. Central on the last business day before the sale.

Put together, an Arkansas house can go from the first missed October tax payment to a state auction in a little over two years. That sounds like a lot of time. For someone serving a three- or five-year sentence, it isn't. The first year usually passes before anyone notices, and the second year is when the letters go to an empty house.

⚠️ Most important warning: once a house is certified to the Commissioner of State Lands, the county collector can't take the payment anymore. Redemption goes through the Commissioner's office, and the deadline is the last business day before the auction. Look the parcel up on the Commissioner's website as soon as you suspect the taxes are behind.

📆 The Arkansas Tax Calendar, Year by Year

  • 🗓️ Year one. Taxes for the prior year can be paid starting in March and are due by October 15. After that date they're delinquent and a penalty is added.
  • 📮 Year two. If still unpaid a year after October 15, the county certifies the parcel to the Commissioner of State Lands and title passes to the State, subject to redemption.
  • 🔨 Year three. No sooner than a year after certification, the parcel is listed for the county's public auction. Bidders who buy get a deed from the Commissioner.
  • 💲 Surplus. After the 2023 U.S. Supreme Court decision in Tyler v. Hennepin County, governments can't keep a former owner's surplus equity from a tax sale. Ask the Commissioner's office how to claim any excess proceeds, but don't count on an auction price coming close to market value.

A sale before the auction solves this cleanly. The buyer's title company gets a redemption figure from the Commissioner, pays it at closing, and the owner keeps the rest of the equity.

🏦 The Bank Can Sell in About 60 Days

If there is a mortgage, Arkansas gives the lender two choices. It can sue in circuit court, or it can use the Statutory Foreclosure Act, Ark. Code § 18-50-101 and following, if the mortgage or deed of trust has a power of sale. Most lenders choose the statutory route because it's fast:

  • 📝 The lender or trustee records a notice of default and intention to sell, and mails it by certified and first-class mail within 30 days after recording.
  • ⏱️ Under § 18-50-104, at least 60 days must pass after the notice is recorded before the sale.
  • 📰 The notice of sale is also published in a local newspaper before the sale date.
  • 🚪 A statutory foreclosure sale generally cuts off the owner's right to redeem afterward, unlike the redemption period that can follow a judicial sale when the mortgage doesn't waive it.

Sixty days is short when every signature has to travel through prison mail. If a notice of default arrives, call the servicer, ask for the sale date and a payoff, and get the power of attorney done that week. 💍 Dower, Curtesy and the Homestead

Arkansas still recognizes dower and curtesy. Under Ark. Code § 28-11-301, a surviving spouse is entitled to a life interest in a share of the lands the other spouse owned during the marriage, generally one-third where there are children. Because that interest can attach to land titled to one spouse alone, Arkansas deeds routinely include the non-owner spouse's release of dower or curtesy and homestead.

For a family with someone inside, that means:

  • 🔒 Owner in prison, spouse at home. The owner signs through a power of attorney. The spouse outside signs the deed to release dower or curtesy and homestead, which is easy to arrange.
  • 🏠 House in the free spouse's name, other spouse inside. The title company will usually want the incarcerated spouse to release dower or curtesy too, either in person before a notary or through an agent under a power of attorney.
  • 📄 Divorce pending. Dower and curtesy rights continue until the divorce is final. A decree that awards the house to one spouse usually resolves it, so check the docket before closing.

The Arkansas homestead also gives the family home some protection from general creditors, though not from a mortgage lender or from property taxes. If you're unsure whether the person inside even has an interest, our spouse in jail guide walks through common situations.

🖊️ Signing the Arkansas Power of Attorney

Arkansas uses the Uniform Power of Attorney Act at Ark. Code § 28-68-101 and following, with a statutory form in § 28-68-301. Under § 28-68-105, a signature on a power of attorney is presumed genuine if the principal acknowledges it before a notary public or another person authorized to take acknowledgments. Every title company will want that notarization for a land sale.

For an Arkansas sale, the document should:

  • 🏡 Grant authority over real property, either by initialing that subject in the statutory form or by spelling it out, and identify the house by address and legal description.
  • 🧾 Let the agent deal with the Commissioner of State Lands, the county collector, the mortgage servicer and the title company.
  • 📚 Be recorded with the circuit clerk, who serves as recorder, in the county where the house sits, before or with the agent's deed.
  • 💍 Cover releasing dower, curtesy and homestead if the person inside is the non-owner spouse.

If the owner won't or can't sign, the alternatives are court-supervised and slower, such as a guardianship of the estate. Most families never need one if they ask early. See getting a notary in jail or prison for how outside notaries get in.

🔗 Cummins, McPherson, Pulaski County and Forrest City

  • 🏛️ Arkansas Division of Correction. State prisoners are held at units such as Cummins and Varner near Grady, Tucker, East Arkansas Regional near Brickeys, and Ouachita River in Malvern. Women are held at the McPherson Unit in Newport. Unit staff decide how outside notaries are scheduled, so start with the inmate's classification or records officer.
  • 🚔 County jails. Pretrial detainees in Little Rock are held at the Pulaski County Regional Detention Facility. Washington, Benton, Sebastian and Craighead Counties run their own detention centers. Many state-sentenced people also wait in county jails for a bed in a state unit, sometimes for months.
  • ⛓️ Federal. The Federal Correctional Complex in Forrest City holds many federal prisoners from the region. Read our federal prison guide for Bureau of Prisons procedures.

Because a person waiting in a county jail for transfer may be moved without warning, sign the power of attorney as soon as possible. Units in the Delta are a long drive from Little Rock and Northwest Arkansas, so put every signature into one notary visit.

💲 A Little Rock House on Paper

Consider a 1960s brick ranch in southwest Little Rock worth about $175,000 after repairs. It needs a roof, HVAC, plumbing work and updating, about $35,000. Arkansas property taxes are among the lower ones in the country: the assessed value is 20% of market value, multiplied by the local millage, minus the state homestead credit for an owner-occupied home. Here's a rough monthly picture:

  • 🧾 Property tax: about $90 to $150 a month for a house like this. Look up the actual bill on the Pulaski County collector's site.
  • 🛡️ Vacant-dwelling insurance: $100 to $250 a month, since a standard policy may lapse on an empty house.
  • 🌿 Utilities, mowing and upkeep: $125 to $275 a month. Code enforcement in Little Rock and North Little Rock cites overgrown lots, and those liens add up.
  • ⚖️ Lawyer: $300 to $900 for a power of attorney, a dower release plan and a title review.

That's about $315 to $675 a month before any mortgage, lower than many states. The bigger cost in Arkansas is usually the tax forfeiture itself, not the monthly bills. Arkansas also charges a real property transfer tax of $3.30 per $1,000 of value, about $580 on a $175,000 sale, and the contract decides who pays it.

A cash buyer typically pays 60% to 80% of repaired value. Here that's roughly $105,000 to $140,000, as-is, with delinquent taxes and any mortgage paid from the proceeds at closing.

🛤️ Four Arkansas Situations

Taxes current, no mortgage trouble

List it

With the power of attorney recorded and dower released, list with a local agent. Arkansas houses in decent condition sell, and this path usually returns the most money.

One year of taxes behind, not yet certified

Pay the county now if you can

Paying the county collector before certification is the cheapest fix. If the family can't, a sale before certification still pays the taxes at closing.

Already certified to the Commissioner

Sell or redeem before the auction

Get the redemption amount from the Commissioner of State Lands, sign the power of attorney, and close before the last business day before the auction. Listing may still work if the auction is many months away.

Notice of default recorded

Act within the 60 days

Ask the servicer for the sale date and payoff, and line up a buyer who can close inside it. A cash buyer is often the only way to finish when the notice is already a few weeks old.

👍 When a Regular Listing Is Better

We'd be doing you a disservice if we didn't say this plainly. If the taxes are current, there's no notice of default, the house is in fair shape, and a relative can let in an inspector and an appraiser, put it on the market. In Northwest Arkansas especially, demand has been strong, and an ordinary buyer will pay more than we will. A cash sale is the better tool when the auction or the 60-day sale date is close, the house needs work most buyers won't finance, or the family needs a single closing the person inside can count on.

📋 What To Do This Week in Arkansas

  1. Look up the parcel on the county collector's site and on the Commissioner of State Lands' site to see whether taxes are delinquent or certified.
  2. Check the circuit clerk's records for a recorded notice of default and intention to sell.
  3. Pull the deed and figure out whether a spouse who isn't on it needs to release dower, curtesy or homestead.
  4. Have an Arkansas attorney draft a power of attorney under § 28-68 that covers real estate and the tax redemption.
  5. Ask the unit or jail how to schedule a notary, and send the documents with a prepaid return envelope.
  6. Get a redemption figure and any mortgage payoff in writing.
  7. Compare an agent's estimated net with a cash offer, after commission, transfer tax and holding costs.

✅ Arkansas, Summed Up

In Arkansas, watch the tax bill as closely as the mortgage. Taxes unpaid for a year after October 15 forfeit the house to the State, and the Commissioner of State Lands can auction it a year after certification. Redemption is possible until the last business day before that sale. If there's a mortgage with a power of sale, the lender can sell about 60 days after recording a notice of default. Get a notarized power of attorney recorded, plan the spouse's dower and homestead release, and close before either clock runs out. If the house is sound and there's time, list it. ❓ Arkansas Tax Forfeiture, Foreclosure and Jail Sale Questions

What happens if property taxes aren't paid on an Arkansas house?

Taxes are due October 15. If they stay unpaid for one year after that date, the land is forfeited to the State and certified to the Commissioner of State Lands, who can offer it at public auction no earlier than one year after certification.

Can the owner still get the house back after it goes to the Commissioner of State Lands?

Yes, by redeeming. The owner or someone with a legal interest can pay the taxes, penalties, interest and costs through the Commissioner's office until 4:00 p.m. Central on the last business day before the auction.

Can we sell an Arkansas house that's already been certified to the State?

Usually, as long as it hasn't been sold at auction. The title company gets a redemption figure from the Commissioner and pays it at closing, and the owner keeps the remaining proceeds.

How fast can a bank foreclose in Arkansas?

Under the Statutory Foreclosure Act, the sale can happen once at least 60 days have passed since the notice of default and intention to sell was recorded, so it can be under three months from start to finish.

Does a spouse have to sign when selling an Arkansas house?

Usually. Arkansas still recognizes dower and curtesy under Ark. Code 28-11-301, so deeds normally include the non-owner spouse's release of dower or curtesy and homestead.

Does an Arkansas power of attorney need to be notarized?

Ark. Code 28-68-105 presumes a signature genuine when acknowledged before a notary, and title companies require that for a sale. It should also be recorded with the circuit clerk in the county where the house sits.

How does an inmate in the Arkansas Division of Correction get a notary?

Through the unit's staff, usually the classification or records office, which approves outside notaries or arranges a staff notary. Rules differ by unit, so call before sending papers.

What is the real estate transfer tax in Arkansas?

It's $3.30 per $1,000 of value, paid at closing. Who pays it is set by the sale contract.

Do Arkansas property taxes cost much while a house sits empty?

Less than in many states. Homes are assessed at 20% of market value and taxed at local millage, so a modest house may cost around $90 to $150 a month in tax, plus insurance and upkeep.

Should we list the Arkansas house or take a cash offer?

List it if the taxes are current, no foreclosure notice is pending and the house is in fair condition. A cash sale makes more sense when a state land auction or a 60-day foreclosure sale is close, or the house needs major work.
