Selling an Arizona House From Jail or Prison: Restitution Liens That Never Expire, Community Property and the Witnessed Power of Attorney

In Arizona, a victim or the state can record a restitution lien against an incarcerated owner's house for free, and a criminal restitution order doesn't expire until it's paid. Add community property rules that require both spouses to sign, and a power of attorney that needs a witness as well as a notary, and the order you do things in matters. Here's that order, real Phoenix numbers, and when listing beats a cash sale. Or ask us a question, free.

By Dustin Ray, Founder of Pages of Purpose LLC. Not legal advice — Arizona criminal restitution, community property, power of attorney and corrections rules change, and every case is different. Before you sign or file anything, talk to an attorney licensed in Arizona.

Families in Arizona usually call us with the same two questions: can the house be sold while my son, husband or mother is incarcerated, and how much of the money will actually reach us? The first answer is yes. A conviction doesn't take away anyone's ownership of a house. The second answer depends heavily on something families often don't check until the title report arrives: whether restitution has been recorded against the property.

This guide starts there, because Arizona's restitution rules are unusually strong, then moves through community property, the power of attorney, notaries inside, the mortgage clock and the rest. For the version of this that applies in every state, see our national guide to selling a house while incarcerated.

🧾 Arizona Restitution Liens: Free to File, No Expiration Date

Two Arizona statutes work together here.

The restitution lien (A.R.S. 13-806)

The state or a victim can file a restitution lien, and there's no filing or recording fee. Against real estate, it's perfected by filing with the county recorder where the property sits. It attaches to the defendant's real property in that county, both what they own now and anything acquired later. It ranks ahead of later interests but behind liens and interests that were already recorded, such as the existing mortgage.

The criminal restitution order (A.R.S. 13-805)

A criminal restitution order can be recorded and enforced like a civil judgment. Unlike an ordinary civil judgment, it doesn't need to be renewed and doesn't expire until it's paid in full. Interest runs at 10% a year when a victim enforces it and 4% when the state does.

⚠️ What this means for the sale: if restitution has been recorded in the county where the house is, the title company will pay it from the seller's proceeds at closing. There's no waiting it out, and in Arizona there's no deadline after which it drops off. Order a title report before you decide anything, so you know how much equity is actually left.

Restitution that hasn't been recorded isn't a lien on the house yet. But a sale doesn't make the debt go away, and the cash that lands in the owner's account can still be reached by other collection tools. A criminal defense or post-conviction lawyer can tell you whether paying restitution from the sale is the smart move, for example to improve parole or release conditions.

💑 Community Property Means Two Signatures

Arizona is a community property state. Property acquired during the marriage is generally owned by both spouses, whoever is named on the deed. A.R.S. 25-214(C)(1) requires both spouses to join in any transaction to sell or encumber an interest in community real property, and A.R.S. 33-452 says a conveyance of community property isn't valid unless both spouses sign and acknowledge it.

So if a married owner goes to prison, the spouse at home can't simply sign the deed alone, even if the spouse at home is the only one on title and bought the house during the marriage. The incarcerated spouse has to sign too, directly or through an agent.

Arizona makes that easier than you might expect. A.R.S. 33-454 lets either spouse give the other a power of attorney to sign, acknowledge and deliver any conveyance or mortgage affecting separate or community property, as long as the power is executed and acknowledged the way a deed is. The spouse at home can be the agent. If the marriage is ending, read our guide to selling during a divorce, because a decree can change who owns the house.

Separate property is different. A house one spouse owned before the marriage, or inherited, is generally that spouse's separate property. Title companies still often ask the other spouse to sign a disclaimer deed so nobody can later claim a community interest.

🖊️ An Arizona Power of Attorney Needs a Witness and a Notary

A.R.S. 14-5501(D) sets stricter execution rules than many states. A financial power of attorney must:

  • 📝 clearly identify the agent;
  • ✍️ be signed by the principal, or by someone signing in the principal's conscious presence at their direction;
  • 👤 be witnessed by one person who is not the agent, the agent's spouse, the agent's children or the notary;
  • 🔏 be acknowledged by the principal before a notary, with the witness signing a sworn affidavit. The statute supplies the certificate form.

To keep working if the owner later becomes incapacitated, it must say so. A power of attorney validly signed in another state is valid in Arizona, which helps when the owner is in a federal facility out of state.

The witness rule is where prison signings break down. The notary can't double as the witness, and the agent's family can't be the witness either. Ask the facility in advance whether a second staff member, another visitor or a case manager can serve as the witness, and bring the form already filled in except for signatures.

🗄️ Record the Power of Attorney With the County Recorder

A Maricopa County Superior Court FAQ says that if a power of attorney directs the agent to transfer real property, it must be recorded, and title companies across Arizona follow that practice. Record it in the county where the house is, ideally before you list. Every deed recorded in Arizona also needs an Affidavit of Property Value, with a $2 fee; the state constitution otherwise bars any transfer tax on the sale of real property.

🔒 Getting a Notary Inside ADCRR and County Jails

The Arizona Department of Corrections, Rehabilitation and Reentry's Department Order 902, on legal access to the courts, lists notary services and says documents are notarized by authorized staff, with the facility paralegal able to authorize staff to notarize. A revised version of that order took effect July 13, 2026. Whether a particular unit will notarize a personal power of attorney or deed, and how quickly, varies, so ask the facility paralegal before you mail anything.

County jails set their own rules. In Maricopa County, ask the jail's inmate legal services or programs staff whether a staff notary is available or whether an outside mobile notary can come in on a professional visit. Bring a spare copy of every document in case one is rejected.

⏱️ If the Mortgage Stops Getting Paid: The 91-Day Trustee Sale

Most Arizona home loans use a deed of trust, so a lender can foreclose without going to court. Under A.R.S. 33-808, the sale can't happen sooner than the 91st day after the notice of sale is recorded; the notice must be posted on the property at least 20 days before the sale and published once a week for four weeks. To stop the sale in court, the owner needs an order under Rule 65 by 5 p.m. on the last business day before it.

That's a short runway when the owner is in custody and the mail is slow. If payments have stopped, getting the power of attorney signed and recorded is urgent, because a sale arranged by the agent can pay off the loan and save whatever equity is left. 🛡️ What the Homestead Exemption Protects

Arizona's homestead exemption, A.R.S. 33-1101, starts at $400,000 of equity and is adjusted each January 1 for inflation. It shields equity from ordinary creditors and carries over to the cash from a sale for 18 months, or until a new home is bought. It doesn't erase a mortgage or a lien that was properly recorded, and it doesn't make anyone's signature unnecessary. If the house is the family home and a sale is coming, ask an Arizona lawyer how the exemption and any restitution lien interact before you decide where the proceeds go.

🏛️ When Nobody Can Sign: Conservatorship for a Confined Owner

A.R.S. 14-5401(A)(2) allows a court to appoint a conservator when it finds, by clear and convincing evidence, that a person can't manage their property for reasons that include confinement, and that the property will be wasted or the money is needed for support. When the basis is confinement, the person doesn't have to appear in court. Incarceration alone isn't automatic grounds; the court still needs both findings. It's slower and costlier than a power of attorney, but it's the route when the owner won't or can't sign and the house is falling apart.

📜 Keeping the House Instead: The Beneficiary Deed

Not every family wants to sell. An owner facing a long sentence who wants the house to pass to a child can sign a beneficiary deed under A.R.S. 33-405. It must be signed, notarized and recorded before the owner dies, can be revoked at any time, and transfers the house subject to any mortgage and liens, restitution included. It doesn't help pay the bills now, so it fits only when someone can keep up the mortgage and taxes.

🧮 A West Phoenix House, Two Outcomes

Take a 1970s block ranch in Maryvale. Updated, nearby houses sell for about $360,000. This one needs an AC system, roof coating, flooring and a kitchen, roughly $45,000. A $20,000 restitution lien is recorded in Maricopa County.

Holding costs. Maricopa County's 2025 primary tax rates run about $3.78 to $4.07 per $100 of assessed value plus secondary levies, and assessed value is a fraction of the Limited Property Value, which can rise only 5% a year. A modest Maryvale house commonly pays $1,500–$2,200 a year, or $125–$185 a month. Electricity to keep the AC running through a Phoenix summer can top $250 a month in July, vacant-house insurance runs $100–$200, and yard and pool care add more. Budget $500–$800 a month.

Cash sale. Cash buyers pay about 60–80% of after-repair value. Here an offer would likely run $215,000 to $285,000; call it $260,000, about 72%. Arizona has no transfer tax. After the $20,000 lien and about $2,500 in title and escrow costs, the owner nets about $237,500.

Listing. As-is, the house might sell for $310,000. Subtract about 5.5% commission ($17,000), $3,500 in closing costs, the $20,000 lien and four months of holding ($2,800). The owner nets about $266,700.

That's about $29,000 more by listing, if someone local can handle showings and repair requests.

📋 What To Do This Week in Arizona

  1. Order a title report or search the county recorder for restitution liens, judgments and any notice of trustee sale.
  2. Confirm whether the owner is married and whether the house is community or separate property.
  3. Have an Arizona attorney prepare a power of attorney that meets A.R.S. 14-5501, with real property authority.
  4. Ask the facility paralegal or jail staff how notary service works and who can act as the witness.
  5. Record the signed power of attorney with the recorder in the county where the house is.
  6. Call the mortgage servicer, with the recorded power of attorney, to stop late fees from compounding.
  7. Keep the AC set high enough to prevent damage, and switch the insurance to a vacant-dwelling policy.

✅ The Arizona Takeaway

An Arizona house can be sold while the owner is incarcerated, but check the money before the mechanics. Recorded restitution comes off the top at closing and never expires on its own. Then line up the signatures: both spouses for community property, through a power of attorney that has a qualified witness and a notary and is recorded with the county. If a trustee sale is coming, move fast. If it isn't, and the house is in decent shape, list it; you'll likely net more than a cash buyer, including us, would pay.

❓ Arizona Restitution, Spouse and Prison Sale FAQ

Is restitution a lien on a house in Arizona?

It can be. Under A.R.S. 13-806 the state or a victim can file a restitution lien with no fee; against real estate it is perfected by filing with the county recorder where the property is, and it attaches to property the defendant owns now or acquires later in that county.

Does an Arizona criminal restitution order ever expire?

No. Under A.R.S. 13-805 a criminal restitution order can be recorded and enforced like a civil judgment, doesn't need renewal and doesn't expire until it's paid in full. Interest runs at 10% a year when a victim enforces it and 4% when the state does.

Can my wife sell our Arizona house while I'm in prison?

Not alone if it's community property. A.R.S. 25-214 and 33-452 require both spouses to sign a conveyance of community real property. Under A.R.S. 33-454 you can give her a power of attorney so she can sign for you.

What are the signing requirements for an Arizona power of attorney?

A.R.S. 14-5501 requires the principal's signature, one witness who isn't the agent, the agent's spouse or children, or the notary, and acknowledgment before a notary with a sworn witness affidavit. It must say it survives incapacity to be durable.

Does an Arizona power of attorney have to be recorded to sell a house?

Title companies require it, and a Maricopa County Superior Court FAQ says a power of attorney directing the agent to transfer real property must be recorded. Record it in the county where the house is.

Do Arizona prisons provide notaries for inmates?

ADCRR Department Order 902 lists notary services and says documents are notarized by authorized staff, with the facility paralegal able to authorize staff to notarize. Whether a unit will notarize a personal deed or power of attorney varies, so ask the paralegal first.

How fast can an Arizona lender foreclose if the owner is incarcerated?

Most Arizona loans use a deed of trust. Under A.R.S. 33-808 the trustee sale can be held on the 91st day after the notice of sale is recorded, so a family may have about three months once that notice appears.

Can a conservator be appointed because an Arizona owner is in prison?

Possibly. A.R.S. 14-5401 lists confinement among the reasons a person may be unable to manage property, but the court must also find by clear and convincing evidence that property will be wasted or funds are needed for support. A confined person doesn't have to appear.

Is there a transfer tax when selling a house in Arizona?

No. The Arizona Constitution bars new taxes on the sale or transfer of real property. A deed does need an Affidavit of Property Value, with a $2 filing fee.

How much is the Arizona homestead exemption?

A.R.S. 33-1101 sets a base of $400,000 of equity, adjusted each January 1 for inflation. It also protects the cash from a sale for 18 months or until a new home is bought, but it doesn't remove recorded liens or mortgages.
