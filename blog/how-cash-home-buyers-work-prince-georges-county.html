How Cash Home Buyers Work in Prince George's County, MD

What they pay, how the offer is calculated, how to check one out, and when you would net more by listing with an agent.

Home › Blog › Cash Buyers in PG County

By Dustin Ray, founder of Pages of Purpose LLC. Updated November 2, 2026

Every week someone in Prince George's County gets a postcard, a text or a yard sign that says "cash for your house." Some of those buyers are real. Some are middlemen who never planned to buy. This guide explains how the business works from the inside, so you can tell the difference and decide if a cash sale even makes sense for you.

Short answer: A cash home buyer starts with what your house would sell for fixed up, then subtracts repairs, holding and closing costs, and their profit. On PG County houses that need real work, that usually lands around 60% to 80% of fixed-up value. In return you skip repairs, showings and lender approval, and can often close in two to four weeks.

How does a cash home buyer work?

A cash buyer pays with their own money or a private line of credit, so there is no bank appraisal or loan approval to wait on. The process is short, but every legitimate one runs through a title company or settlement attorney.

  1. You describe the house. Address, condition, who is on the deed, and anything unusual: back taxes, an estate, a tenant, a co-owner who won't answer.
  2. The buyer runs the numbers. They pull recent sales near you (Bowie, Clinton, Hyattsville and Fort Washington all price very differently), estimate repairs and check the land records for liens.
  3. You get a written offer. It should name the price, who pays which closing costs, the closing date, the earnest money deposit and any inspection period.
  4. A title company does the title search. It finds mortgages, judgments, tax sale certificates and WSSC charges, and pays them off from the sale at closing.
  5. You sign and get paid. Money is wired from the title company's escrow account, not handed over by the buyer.

How much do cash home buyers pay?

Less than a retail buyer would pay for a fixed-up house, and the gap grows with the amount of work needed. Most investors work backward from the resale price, so you can check their math yourself.

Here is how a typical offer is built. The numbers are an illustration, not a quote:

Line itemExample
What the house would sell for after updates (after-repair value)$350,000
Repairs: roof, kitchen, baths, HVAC, paint, flooring− $50,000
Costs to resell it later: agent, transfer taxes, closing− $25,000

OfferAbout $208,000 to $223,000

That example comes out near 60% of fixed-up value because the house needs a lot. A house that needs paint and carpet will usually draw an offer much closer to market. If a buyer won't show you how they got to their number, ask why.

Watch for the opposite trick too. A very high verbal offer that drops after an "inspection" two weeks before closing is a common way to lock a seller up and renegotiate later.

When does listing with an agent net more money?

Most of the time, if the house is livable and the title is clean. Be honest with yourself about these situations:

  • The house is updated or only needs cosmetic work.
  • Nobody is in a hurry, and you can carry the mortgage, taxes and utilities for a few months.
  • Everyone on the deed (or every heir) agrees and can sign.
  • There are no tax sale certificates, judgments or code violations holding things up.

If all four are true, interview two or three agents before you take any cash offer. Cash makes sense when one of those is false: the house needs major work, there is a deadline like a tax sale foreclosure, or the title has a problem that would scare off a retail buyer and their lender.

How do you vet a cash home buyer in Maryland?

Ask for proof, in writing, before you sign anything. A real buyer won't mind.

Cash buyer checklist

  • ☐ Proof of funds dated in the last 30 days, in the name of the company signing the contract.
  • ☐ A real Maryland business. Look up the company in Maryland Business Express (run by the State Department of Assessments and Taxation) and confirm it's in good standing.
  • ☐ A title company or settlement attorney you can call. You can also ask to use your own.
  • ☐ Earnest money deposited with the title company, not held by the buyer.
  • ☐ A written assignment disclosure, or a clear statement that they will close in their own name.
  • ☐ A short, defined inspection period, not an open-ended "subject to partner approval" clause.
  • ☐ No fees from you. You should never pay a buyer to look at your house.
  • ☐ No quitclaim deed and no pressure to sign the same day.
  • ☐ Their real name and a local phone number. Search the company name with "complaint" and check the Maryland Judiciary Case Search for lawsuits.

A note on licenses: a company buying a house for itself doesn't need a real estate license in Maryland. Anyone acting as your agent does. If someone says they are a licensed agent, ask for the license number and check it with the Maryland Real Estate Commission.

What is a wholesaler, and what does Maryland require?

A wholesaler signs a contract to buy your house, then sells that contract to another investor for a fee. Since October 1, 2025, Maryland law requires them to tell you in writing, before you sign, that they may assign the contract.

The law is Chapter 508 of 2025 (House Bill 124), which added Section 10-715 to the Real Property Article. If a wholesale buyer skips that disclosure and then assigns your contract, you can cancel it without penalty any time before closing.

Assignment isn't automatically bad. Some sellers are fine with it. The risk is that the person who signed with you has no money, and your closing depends on them finding someone who does. Ask directly: "Will you close with your own funds, or might you assign this?" We answer that question in writing at the start, and sometimes offer a partnership instead of a straight cash price when that nets the owner more.

What does it cost to sell for cash in Prince George's County?

Usually no commission, but there are still transfer and recordation taxes. Who pays them is negotiable, so get it in the contract.

According to the Maryland Department of Legislative Services 2026 county tax rate table, Prince George's County charges a 1.4% county transfer tax and recordation tax of $2.75 per $500 of price. The state transfer tax is 0.5% under Tax-Property § 13-203. That section cuts the state rate to 0.25% for a first-time Maryland homebuyer and makes the seller pay all of it.

On a $300,000 sale, that's $4,200 in county transfer tax, $1,500 in state transfer tax and $1,650 in recordation tax, or $7,350 total before any split. Many cash buyers offer to pay all of it. Make sure the contract says so.

Other costs that come out of your proceeds: your mortgage payoff, any unpaid property taxes, any tax sale redemption amount, judgments, and the settlement fee if you agreed to pay it.

What PG County problems can a cash buyer handle?

A good one can buy around most title and tax problems, because the payoff happens at closing. These come up most often in Prince George's County.

The house was sold at the county tax sale

You still own it until a court forecloses your right of redemption. Prince George's County's tax sale redemption page says the certificate carries 20% interest per year. A principal residence can be redeemed without the buyer's legal fees for the first 7 months, and the certificate holder can file to foreclose after 9 months. For other properties those windows are 4 and 6 months. Sell before the foreclosure is final and the redemption is paid from your proceeds. Our guide to selling a Maryland house before tax sale walks through the timing.

WSSC charges

WSSC Water bills water and sewer in Prince George's County. Some properties also carry front foot benefit charges for the water and sewer lines. WSSC's own brochure says those charges appear on the annual county tax bill and can run for the life of the bond, which it lists as 23 years. The title company will find unpaid water bills and benefit charges. Ask the buyer up front whether they are paying them or taking them out of your price.

The owner died and the house is still in their name

In most cases someone has to open an estate with the Register of Wills for Prince George's County and be appointed personal representative before the house can be sold. Maryland allows a simpler small estate when probate assets are $50,000 or less, or $100,000 or less if the surviving spouse is the only heir, per the Maryland Register of Wills. Most PG County houses are worth more than that, so plan on a regular estate. See our probate house page, our guide to selling inherited property in Maryland, and what to do when heirs can't agree.

You are behind on the mortgage

Maryland gives extra protection here. Under the Protection of Homeowners in Foreclosure Act, a homeowner who signs a contract to sell a "residence in default" can cancel it within 5 days, the sale can't use a quitclaim deed, and no deed can be recorded during those 5 days (Real Property § 7-310). Be very careful with anyone who offers to "save" your house by taking title while you keep living there. That is the setup the law was written to stop. Talk to a Maryland attorney or a HUD-approved housing counselor before you sign.

How long does a cash sale take in PG County?

About two to four weeks when title is clean, mostly for the title search and payoff letters. Estates, heir disputes and tax sale cases take longer because a court or the Register of Wills has to act first.

A buyer who promises seven days on a house with an open estate is either guessing or planning to assign. A realistic buyer will tell you which step is holding up closing and roughly how long it takes.

Frequently asked questions

Are cash home buyers in Prince George's County legit?

Many are. Verify proof of funds, a registered Maryland business, an independent title company holding the deposit, and a written answer on whether they will assign the contract. Walk away from anyone who asks you for a fee or a quitclaim deed.

Is a cash offer always lower than market value?

Usually, yes. The buyer prices in repairs, holding costs, resale costs and profit. On houses that need major work, offers often land around 60% to 80% of fixed-up value. On a house in good shape, listing with an agent usually nets more.

Who pays closing costs when you sell for cash in PG County?

It's negotiable. Prince George's County charges a 1.4% transfer tax and $2.75 per $500 in recordation tax, and the state adds a 0.5% transfer tax. Many cash buyers pay these, but only the written contract counts.

Can I sell my house after it went to tax sale in Prince George's County?

Yes, until a court forecloses the right of redemption. The redemption amount, including 20% annual interest on the certificate, is paid from the sale proceeds at closing.

Can I sell an inherited house in PG County before probate is done?

You can sign a contract once a personal representative is appointed, and close when the estate allows. If no estate has been opened, the first step is filing with the Register of Wills for Prince George's County.

What happens if a cash buyer assigns my contract?

Since October 1, 2025, Maryland requires a wholesale buyer to tell you in writing before you sign that they may assign the contract. If they didn't and then assign it, you can cancel without penalty before closing.

Do I need a lawyer to sell to a cash buyer?

Not always. A title company handles most settlements. Hire a Maryland attorney if there's a foreclosure, an estate dispute, a divorce, or anything in the contract you don't understand.
