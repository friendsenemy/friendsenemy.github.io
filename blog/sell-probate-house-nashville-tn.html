Selling a Nashville Probate House: Why the Six-Month Mark Matters More Than the Court Date

In Tennessee the house passes to the heirs the moment the owner dies, but a buyer who purchases from those heirs in the first six months takes the house subject to the estate's creditors. That one rule shapes when a Nashville probate house can sell, who signs, and whether opening probate in Davidson County is worth it. Here are the routes, the TennCare step, the 2025 tax numbers, and when listing beats a cash offer. Or ask us a question, free.

Families who inherit a house in Nashville usually ask how long probate takes. It's a reasonable question with an unhelpful answer, because in Tennessee the house may never go through probate at all. The more useful question is how long ago the owner died. A sale to a buyer inside the first six months carries a risk that a sale after six months does not, and that difference drives most of the choices this guide walks through.

Below: the six-month rule, the three ways a Davidson County house actually gets sold, the TennCare release that can hold an estate open, creditor deadlines, what a surviving spouse can claim, what the house costs to hold after the 2025 reappraisal, and an honest comparison of listing versus a cash sale.

⏳ Six Months After Death: The Date Nashville Buyers Watch

Tennessee Code section 30-2-408 sets out what happens when heirs sell inherited land directly. It splits time into two periods.

Inside the first six months

A buyer or lender who takes the property from the heirs within six months of the owner's death takes it subject to the rights of the estate's creditors. If the owner died owing a hospital, a credit card company or a contractor, those creditors can still reach the land in the buyer's hands. Few buyers, and fewer title insurers, want that exposure. So a sale signed in month two or three usually means either opening probate first or waiting.

After six months, with no personal representative appointed

Once six months have passed and nobody has qualified as personal representative, a buyer who pays value takes the house free of the owner's ordinary (non-lien) debts, unless the buyer actually knows about a debt. The same section protects that buyer against a will nobody has probated, again unless the buyer knows about it. Recorded liens such as a mortgage or a tax lien still follow the house, as they always do.

The heirs don't escape the debts entirely. They stay answerable to the owner's creditors, up to the value of the land they sold. But the buyer gets clean title, which is what makes a straightforward heir-to-buyer sale possible in Tennessee.

⚠️ The rule cuts both ways: the six-month protection only applies if no personal representative has been appointed. Once someone qualifies in Davidson County, the estate is open, creditors file claims there, and the sale has to fit the estate's process. Decide whether you need probate before anyone files, not after.

🏡 The House Passes to the Heirs, Not to the Executor

Under section 31-2-103, a Tennessee owner's real estate vests in the heirs or the people named in the will at the moment of death. The personal representative takes the personal property, such as bank accounts, cars and furniture, but not the house, unless the will specifically directs that the real estate be administered as part of the estate.

That's why many Nashville houses sell without the court ever touching them: the people who own the house are the people who sign the deed. It's also why opening probate doesn't, by itself, give an executor control of the house. Our guide to who signs the deed on an inherited Nashville house covers those signature questions in more detail.

🧭 Three Ways a Davidson County Probate House Gets Sold

1. The heirs sign, usually after six months

Every heir signs the deed. If there's no will, a recorded affidavit of heirship identifies who the heirs are. Section 30-2-712 lets these affidavits be recorded with the Register of Deeds; once recorded, they are prima facie evidence of the facts they state, and anyone harmed has six years from recording to challenge them. A knowingly false heirship affidavit is a Class E felony, so it should be signed by someone who actually knew the family. This route works best when there are few heirs, everyone agrees, and no large debts are lurking.

2. The executor sells under a power of sale in the will

A will can give the executor power to sell real estate. Some Tennessee wills do it by incorporating the list of fiduciary powers in section 35-50-110, which includes selling estate real property by public or private sale. With that power, the executor signs the deed alone after letters testamentary issue, and individual heirs don't have to sign.

3. The court orders a sale

Under section 30-2-402, the personal representative or a creditor can petition the court to sell land when the personal property isn't enough to pay the debts and expenses of the estate. The surviving spouse, heirs, devisees and lienholders must be made parties, and the court can order the sale subject to later confirmation, including confirming a private contract. The Davidson County clerk's fee for a petition to sell real property is $200. This is the route for an estate with real debts and not enough cash, not a tool for overriding a sibling who simply wants to keep the house. 🩺 The TennCare Release Can Keep an Estate Open

If you do open probate, plan for TennCare. Section 71-5-116 requires the personal representative, within 60 days after letters issue, to notify TennCare of the death of any decedent who was 55 or older and request a release. An affidavit that the notice went out is filed with the court.

If the owner was enrolled in TennCare, the estate cannot close until the personal representative files a release showing the claim was paid, waived, or that nothing is owed. Long nursing-home stays can produce large claims, and the sale money from the house may be the only asset available to pay them. Before you distribute any proceeds, get that release in hand.

📬 Creditor Deadlines: Four Months, or Twelve

Once letters issue, the clerk publishes a notice to creditors. A creditor's claim is due by the earlier of two dates:

  • 📰 Four months from the first publication of the notice, or 60 days from actual notice if the creditor was mailed a notice less than 60 days before the four-month date; or
  • 📆 Twelve months from the date of death, which is the outer limit whether or not anyone publishes.

Practically, a probated Davidson County estate with no disputes often wraps up in six months to a year. Contested estates take longer. If the house sells during that time, the money waits in the estate account until claims are resolved.

💍 What a Surviving Spouse Can Claim Before Anyone Sells

A spouse who survives can change who gets what from the sale, even when the will leaves the house to the children.

  • 🍞 Year's support (section 30-2-102). A surviving spouse of someone who died without a will, or who elects against the will, can ask for a reasonable allowance for one year based on their standard of living and the size of the estate.
  • ⚖️ Elective share (section 31-4-101). A spouse can take a percentage of the net estate instead of what the will left: 10% after less than three years of marriage, rising in steps to 40% after nine years or more.
  • 🏠 Homestead (section 30-2-201). A surviving spouse or minor children have a homestead right in the decedent's land. When it can't be set apart, section 30-2-209 lets the court sell the property and set aside $35,000 of the proceeds for a replacement homestead, or pay it outright.

If there's a surviving spouse, assume they will need to sign or release their rights, and get a Tennessee lawyer's read before you price the house.

🏙️ What the House Costs to Hold After the 2025 Reappraisal

Davidson County reappraised every property in 2025, and the Metro Council set the tax year 2025 rate at $2.782 per $100 of assessed value in the General Services District and $2.814 in the Urban Services District. Residential property is assessed at 25% of appraised value. On a house appraised at $330,000, that's $82,500 assessed and roughly $2,300 a year, about $190–$195 a month.

Typical monthly costs on a vacant Nashville house:

  • 🧾 Property tax: $150–$350 for most houses in Antioch, Madison or Hermitage
  • 🛡️ Vacant-dwelling insurance: $120–$250, since a standard homeowner policy often limits coverage once a house sits empty
  • 💡 Utilities: $90–$160 to keep heat on in winter and the air running in July
  • 🌿 Lawn, gutters and checks after storms: $100–$200
  • ⚖️ If you open probate: $334.50 to file the petition and get letters, plus newspaper publication and attorney fees, commonly a few thousand dollars for a simple estate

Most families spend $500–$900 a month while a modest Nashville house sits. That's real money, but it's lower than in many cities, which is one reason waiting for a retail sale often pays here.

🔀 Which Route Fits Your Family

List the house with an agent

Usually the best price

Nashville's market has stayed strong. A house in fair condition, with heirs who agree and can sign after six months, will usually sell for noticeably more on the open market than any cash buyer will pay, even after commission. If that's your family, list it.

Sell to a cash buyer

Best for speed and condition

A cash sale makes sense when the house needs a roof, foundation work or a full cleanout, when an heir lives out of state and wants one signing, or when the estate needs cash to pay TennCare or other debts. A cash buyer can also sign during the six-month window and close the day after it ends.

Buy out the holdout, or partition

When one heir won't sign

Without a will that grants a power of sale, one heir can stop a sale. A buyout of that heir's share, or a partition case, is the usual answer. Some buyers, including us, will buy a single heir's interest.

📋 What To Do This Week in Davidson County

  1. Write down the date of death and count forward six months. That date decides whether the heirs can sell without probate.
  2. Look for a will and read it for a power of sale or a reference to section 35-50-110.
  3. Pull the deed and any mortgage from the Davidson County Register of Deeds.
  4. List every heir and any surviving spouse, with contact information.
  5. Find out whether the owner was ever on TennCare and whether there are large unpaid bills.
  6. Pay the Metro Trustee tax bill if it's due, and switch the insurance to a vacant-dwelling policy.
  7. Ask a Tennessee probate attorney one question: do we need to open an estate at all?

✅ The Nashville Bottom Line

In Tennessee, the calendar matters as much as the courthouse. Before the six-month mark, a buyer from the heirs inherits the creditors' rights along with the house, so most sales either wait or go through probate. After six months, with no personal representative appointed, heirs who agree can sign and close cleanly. Open probate when the will gives the executor a power of sale you want to use, when debts or TennCare must be dealt with, or when heirs can't agree. And in a market like Nashville's, if the house is sound and the family agrees, list it; you'll likely net more than any cash buyer, including us, will pay.

❓ Nashville Probate Questions

What is Tennessee's six-month rule for selling an inherited house?

Under Tennessee Code section 30-2-408, a buyer who purchases from the heirs within six months of the owner's death takes the land subject to the estate creditors' rights. After six months, if no personal representative has been appointed, a buyer for value takes free of the owner's non-lien debts and any unprobated will unless the buyer knows about them.

Do the heirs or the executor own a Nashville house after the owner dies?

The heirs, or the people named in the will. Section 31-2-103 vests real estate in them at death; the personal representative takes only personal property unless the will directs that the real estate be administered by the estate.

Can an executor in Tennessee sell the house without the heirs signing?

Yes, if the will gives the executor a power of sale, including by incorporating the fiduciary powers in section 35-50-110. Without that power, the heirs sign, or the court orders a sale.

When can a Tennessee court order a probate house sold?

Under section 30-2-402, the personal representative or a creditor can petition to sell land when the personal property isn't enough to pay the estate's debts and expenses. The spouse, heirs and lienholders must be parties, and the court can confirm a private sale contract.

What is an affidavit of heirship in Tennessee?

A sworn statement identifying a deceased owner's heirs, recordable with the Register of Deeds under section 30-2-712. Once recorded it is prima facie evidence of the facts it states, can be challenged for six years, and a knowingly false affidavit is a Class E felony.

Which court handles probate in Nashville?

The Seventh Circuit Court, Probate Division, commonly called the Davidson County Probate Court. Filings go through the Probate Court Clerk's Office, part of the Circuit Court Clerk's office.

What does TennCare have to do with selling a probate house?

If the owner was 55 or older, section 71-5-116 requires the personal representative to notify TennCare and request a release within 60 days of letters. If the owner was enrolled in TennCare, the estate can't close until a release shows the claim was paid, waived or that nothing is owed.

How long do creditors have to file claims against a Tennessee estate?

The earlier of four months from first publication of the notice to creditors (or 60 days from actual notice in some cases) and twelve months from the date of death.

How much are property taxes on an inherited house in Nashville?

For tax year 2025 the rate is $2.782 per $100 of assessed value in the General Services District and $2.814 in the Urban Services District, with homes assessed at 25% of appraised value. A $330,000 house pays about $2,300 a year.

Should we list a Nashville probate house or sell it for cash?

If the house is in fair shape and the heirs agree, listing usually nets more in Nashville's market, often tens of thousands of dollars more. A cash sale makes more sense for houses needing major repairs, estates that need money to pay debts, or heirs who can't agree on an agent.
