Selling a Colorado House From Jail or Prison: The Public Trustee, the Rule 120 Hearing and a Recorded Power of Attorney

Colorado runs foreclosures through an office most states don't have: a county public trustee, who sets the auction about 110 to 125 days after the lender's notice is recorded, while a district judge holds a short Rule 120 hearing on the side. An owner in a county jail or a CDOC prison gets the same deadlines as everyone else. Here's how to read that calendar and sell within it.

By Dustin Ray, Founder of Pages of Purpose LLC. This page is general information, not legal advice. Laws differ by state, Colorado statutes, court rules and CDOC procedures change, and title companies set their own requirements. Talk to an attorney licensed in Colorado, where the property is, before signing a power of attorney, a deed or anything filed with the court.

🏛️ A County Official Runs the Foreclosure

In most states, a foreclosure is either a court case or a private sale run by a trustee the lender picks. Colorado uses a third model. Each county has a public trustee, a government office, that acts as the neutral trustee on every deed of trust in that county. When a borrower defaults, the lender files a Notice of Election and Demand with the public trustee, which records it and starts the clock.

The public trustee then mails the owner a combined notice with the sale date. For most residential property, the sale is set about 110 to 125 days after the notice of election is recorded. Most counties post their foreclosure records online, so the family can look up the case by the owner's name or address and see the exact date.

⚖️ What the Rule 120 hearing does, and doesn't do

Before the sale, the lender also has to get an order authorizing the sale from the district court under Colorado Rule of Civil Procedure 120. The hearing is narrow. The court asks whether there is a reasonable probability of a default and whether the borrower is protected by the Servicemembers Civil Relief Act. It doesn't decide whether the foreclosure is fair, and it isn't a forum for selling the house.

An owner who believes he isn't in default can file a written response and be heard, and an incarcerated owner may be able to file one by mail or appear remotely if the court allows. But for most families, the Rule 120 notice simply confirms the sale is coming.

⚠️ Most important warning: a Colorado foreclosure doesn't pause because the owner is in jail. The public trustee's sale date holds unless the lender postpones it, the loan is cured, or the house is sold or paid off first. If you find a public trustee notice in an incarcerated relative's mail, count the days to the sale before doing anything else.

⏳ Cure, Postpone or Sell: The Three Ways Out

  • 💰 Cure. Under C.R.S. § 38-38-104, the owner can cure the default by filing a written notice of intent to cure with the public trustee no later than 15 calendar days before the sale, then paying the amount past due plus fees by the deadline. That requires money the family may not have.
  • 📆 Postponement. Lenders often agree to postpone a sale when a signed purchase contract shows a payoff is coming. That's up to the lender, so get the contract in front of the servicer early.
  • 🏠 Sale. Selling the house and paying off the loan at closing ends the foreclosure, and the owner keeps the remaining equity. This is the option that depends on a valid power of attorney or the owner's own signature.

Once the sale happens, it is close to final for the owner. Colorado gives junior lienholders limited redemption rights, but an owner generally has no right to buy the house back after the public trustee's sale. If the winning bid is more than the debt, the overbid goes first to junior lienholders and then to the owner, and somebody has to claim it. Ask the public trustee how claims are filed and what the deadlines are.

✍️ The Power of Attorney Under Colorado's Uniform Act

Colorado adopted the Uniform Power of Attorney Act, codified at C.R.S. §§ 15-14-701 and following. Under § 15-14-705, a power of attorney must be signed by the principal, or by someone else in his conscious presence at his direction, and a signature acknowledged before a notary is presumed genuine. A notary isn't strictly required for validity. For a house it is, in practice: the deed the agent signs will be recorded with the county clerk and recorder, and title companies will want the power of attorney acknowledged and recorded with it.

A Colorado power of attorney for a sale should expressly grant authority over real property, and ideally name the property. It should let the agent sign the deed, the Real Property Transfer Declaration (form TD-1000), closing documents and payoff authorizations. The TD-1000 is required with most deeds in Colorado, and the clerk collects a small documentary fee of one cent per $100 of the sale price. On a $280,000 sale, that's $28.

Colorado has a statutory form power of attorney. A lawyer-drafted document is usually better for a sale, because it can name the property and address what the title company will ask for.

📬 Ask the title company before anything goes in

Title companies, not statutes, are where Colorado sales usually stall. Before the document goes into a facility, send a draft to the title company that will close and ask: Will you insure a deed signed under this? Do you need the original, or a certified copy after recording? Do you need confirmation the principal was alive on the closing date? A ten-minute call can save a month of mail.

🔐 Signing Inside CDOC, County Jails and Federal Facilities

Where the owner is held affects how fast the notary can happen:

  • 🏢 County jails. Pretrial detainees and short sentences stay in county facilities, such as the Denver Downtown Detention Center or the El Paso County Criminal Justice Center. Each jail sets its own rules for notaries and professional visits.
  • 🏛️ CDOC. Sentenced people are processed at the Denver Reception and Diagnostic Center, then sent to facilities that include the Cañon City complex (Fremont, Centennial and Colorado Territorial), Sterling, Limon, Buena Vista, Arkansas Valley and Denver Women's. Legal paperwork generally goes through the person's case manager, who can tell you how notary services work there.
  • ⛓️ Federal. FCI Englewood and the Florence complex hold federal prisoners. See our federal prison guide.

Intake at the Denver reception center can take weeks with restricted contact. If a foreclosure is already running, get the power of attorney signed in county jail before the transfer if at all possible. Our guide to getting a notary in jail or prison has the steps.

👫 Spouses and Co-Owners in Colorado

Colorado isn't a community property state and doesn't have dower. A spouse who isn't on the deed generally doesn't have to sign the sale. Title companies sometimes ask anyway when the house was the family residence, so check the title commitment. If the house is held in joint tenancy with the person inside, his signature or his agent's is required. If he refuses, a co-owner can ask a district court to partition it; see how partition lawsuits work and whether a co-owner can sell without the other.

🛡️ The $250,000 homestead exemption

Under C.R.S. § 38-41-201, as amended in 2022, a homestead occupied by the owner or the owner's family is exempt from execution and attachment up to $250,000, or $350,000 if the owner or spouse is elderly or disabled. It protects equity from judgment creditors. It doesn't stop a public trustee foreclosure by the mortgage lender.

💵 Colorado Springs Math

Consider a split-level house in southeast Colorado Springs worth about $400,000 after updates. It needs a roof after hail, a furnace and interior work, about $50,000. Monthly holding costs while it sits:

  • 🧾 Property tax: about $130 to $180 a month. Colorado's residential property taxes are among the lowest in the country as a share of value, and El Paso County is lower than most of the Denver area.
  • 🛡️ Vacant-dwelling insurance: $150 to $325 a month, and hail-prone areas carry high wind and hail deductibles.
  • 💡 Utilities, HOA and upkeep: $175 to $350 a month, more if there's an HOA.
  • ⚖️ Lawyer: $300 to $800 for a power of attorney built for a sale, more if someone has to respond to a Rule 120 motion.

The bigger cost is the foreclosure itself. Interest, late fees and the lender's attorney and public trustee fees add to the payoff every month the case stays open.

A cash buyer typically pays 60% to 80% of repaired value, so $240,000 to $320,000 here. With $50,000 of work and a short timeline, $255,000 to $290,000 is realistic.

🧭 Picking a Colorado Route by the Calendar

No foreclosure notice yet: sign a POA, then list

Usually the best net

If the loan is current or only a payment or two behind, there's time for a full listing. Record the power of attorney, list with an agent, and keep the payments going if you can.

Notice recorded, sale more than about 60 days out

A quick listing may still work

A clean, priced-to-sell listing can work if the house is in good shape, the power of attorney is already signed and recorded, and the lender will postpone for a contract. If it needs repairs, a cash closing is safer.

Sale within about 45 days

Cash buyer or cure

There usually isn't time for showings, inspections and a financed buyer's appraisal. Either the family cures with the notice of intent to cure, or a cash buyer closes and pays off the loan before the auction.

Owner signs the deed at the facility

For one closing

If a notary can see the owner on a set date, he can sign the deed and the TD-1000 directly, with no power of attorney. It works best for a cash closing scheduled around the visit.

🗣️ When a Listing Is the Better Answer

Colorado houses in good condition sell well. If no foreclosure notice has been recorded, the house needs only cosmetic work, and someone outside can manage a listing, list it. You'll very likely net more than our offer. A cash sale earns its discount when the sale date is close, when hail or water damage would scare off financed buyers, or when nobody can handle repairs and showings from a distance.

📋 Your Colorado Week

  1. Search the county public trustee's online records by the owner's name or address to see if a notice of election has been recorded, and write down the sale date.
  2. Read any Rule 120 notice, and if the owner disputes the default, talk to a Colorado attorney about a written response.
  3. Have a power of attorney drafted for the sale and run it past the title company before it goes into the facility.
  4. Book a notary through the jail or the CDOC case manager, before any transfer to the Denver reception center if possible.
  5. Ask the servicer for a payoff quote and its policy on postponing a sale for a signed contract.
  6. Get a listing agent's net and a cash offer, and compare both against the days left on the calendar.

✅ Bottom Line in Colorado

Colorado's foreclosure system is predictable, and that helps if you act early. The county public trustee sets a sale roughly 110 to 125 days after the lender records its notice, the Rule 120 hearing only confirms the default, and the owner can't redeem after the sale. A power of attorney under the Uniform Act, acknowledged before a notary and approved by the title company, lets the family sell within that window. Check the public trustee's records first, then choose between a listing and a cash sale based on the time left.

❓ Colorado Foreclosure and Jail Sale Questions

What is a public trustee in a Colorado foreclosure?

Each Colorado county has a public trustee, a government office that serves as trustee on deeds of trust. The lender files a Notice of Election and Demand with that office, which records it, sends notices and conducts the foreclosure sale.

How long after the notice does the Colorado foreclosure sale happen?

For most residential property, the public trustee sets the sale about 110 to 125 days after the Notice of Election and Demand is recorded. Lenders can postpone it.

Can my brother stop the foreclosure at the Rule 120 hearing?

Usually not. The Rule 120 hearing only decides whether there's a reasonable probability of a default and whether the Servicemembers Civil Relief Act applies. An owner who disputes the default can respond, but it isn't a way to sell or delay the house.

How late can a Colorado foreclosure be cured?

Under C.R.S. 38-38-104, the owner must file a written notice of intent to cure with the public trustee no later than 15 calendar days before the sale, then pay the past-due amount and fees by the deadline.

Can the owner get the house back after a Colorado public trustee sale?

Generally no. Colorado gives certain junior lienholders redemption rights, but the owner normally has none after the sale. Any overbid above the debts must be claimed from the public trustee.

Does a Colorado power of attorney have to be notarized?

Not for basic validity, but a signature acknowledged before a notary is presumed genuine under C.R.S. 15-14-705, and title companies will expect a notarized, recorded power of attorney for a deed.

What is the TD-1000 and who signs it?

It's Colorado's Real Property Transfer Declaration, filed with most deeds. The seller's agent under a proper power of attorney can sign it, along with the deed and the closing documents.

Does my wife have to sign if the Colorado house is only in my name?

Generally not. Colorado has no community property and no dower, so a spouse off the deed usually doesn't need to sign, though the title company may ask when the house was the family residence.

Does the Colorado homestead exemption protect the house from foreclosure?

No. The exemption, $250,000 or $350,000 for elderly or disabled owners, protects equity from judgment creditors but doesn't stop the mortgage lender's public trustee foreclosure.

Should we list the house or sell for cash?

If no foreclosure notice has been recorded and the house is in good shape, list it; you'll likely net more. If the public trustee sale is a few weeks away or the house needs major repairs, a cash closing that pays off the loan protects more of the equity.
