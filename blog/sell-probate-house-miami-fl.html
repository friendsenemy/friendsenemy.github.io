Selling a Miami Probate House When the Owner or the Heirs Live Outside the U.S.

A large share of Miami estates cross a border. The owner kept a house in Kendall but lived in Caracas, or the heirs are in Bogotá, Madrid and Toronto. Florida probate handles that, but it adds an ancillary case, a residency rule for the personal representative, federal withholding at closing and sometimes a federal estate-tax filing. Here is how those pieces fit, what they cost in Miami-Dade, and when listing beats a cash sale. Or ask us a question, free.

By Dustin Ray, Founder of Pages of Purpose LLC. Not legal or tax advice — Florida probate law, Miami-Dade court practice and federal tax rules change, and every estate is different. Before you sign or file anything, talk to a probate attorney licensed in Florida and, if anyone involved lives abroad, a tax professional who handles cross-border estates.

Probate guides usually assume everyone involved lives in the same county as the house. Miami doesn't work that way. Plenty of houses in Doral, Kendall, Westchester and Hialeah belong to people who split their lives between South Florida and Latin America, Europe or Canada. When one of those owners dies, the family often learns about Florida probate from abroad, by email, in a second language, on a deadline set by a tax bill they never see.

This guide is for that family. It covers the parts of Florida probate that change when the owner was domiciled outside Florida or when the heirs live outside the United States, and it puts real Miami-Dade numbers on the costs. If the owner lived in Florida and every heir lives here too, the Florida basics are in our guide to inheriting a Miami house, which spends most of its time on homestead.

🌎 Where the Owner Lived Decides Which Florida Case You Open

The first question isn't "was there a will?" It's "where was the owner legally domiciled on the day they died?" Domicile is the place a person treats as their permanent home: where they voted, filed taxes, kept their main residence and intended to return. A Miami house can belong to someone domiciled in Florida, in another state, or in another country, and the answer picks the case.

Domicile in Florida

If the owner was a Florida domiciliary, the family opens an ordinary Florida probate in the Probate Division of the Eleventh Judicial Circuit. Filings go to the Miami-Dade Clerk at the Osvaldo N. Soto Miami-Dade Justice Center, 20 N.W. 1st Avenue, downtown. The Clerk's current filing fee is $401 for formal administration and $346 for summary administration of an estate worth $1,000 or more.

Domicile outside Florida: ancillary administration

If the owner lived somewhere else, the main estate is handled where they lived, and Florida opens a secondary case just for the Florida property. Florida calls this ancillary administration (section 734.102). The ancillary personal representative posts bond, publishes a notice to creditors in Florida and, once appointed, has the same power as any Florida personal representative to sell local real estate. The Clerk charges the same $401 for an ancillary case as for formal administration.

The short-form ancillary route

Section 734.1025 offers a lighter version for a nonresident who left a will and whose Florida property is worth $50,000 or less. That ceiling is low for Miami real estate. It can fit a small condo interest or a lot in the southern end of the county, but almost never a house. If any creditor files a claim, the court has to appoint a personal representative anyway.

⚠️ The most expensive mistake we see: a foreign court order, a notarized letter from a consulate, or a will probated in another country does not by itself let anyone sign a deed to a Miami house. A Florida court has to recognize someone first. Title companies will not insure a sale signed under foreign papers alone, so a buyer who "closes anyway" is buying a lawsuit.

🖋️ Who Is Allowed to Serve as Personal Representative

Florida limits who can act for an estate if they don't live in Florida. Under section 733.304, a nonresident can serve only if they are related to the person who died in one of the ways the statute lists, such as a child, parent, sibling, spouse, or certain other relatives and their spouses. That rule applies just as much to a son in Buenos Aires as to a son in Ohio. A close family friend abroad, or a business partner, cannot be appointed, even if the will names them.

Two more practical rules shape the process for families abroad:

  • 🧑‍⚖️ A Florida lawyer is required. Florida Probate Rule 5.030 says a personal representative must be represented by a lawyer admitted in Florida unless that person is the only one with an interest in the estate. Expect to hire Miami counsel even if you're the sole heir abroad and comfortable with paperwork.
  • 🛂 Signatures from abroad need the right form. Oaths, acceptances of service and deeds signed outside the U.S. are usually done before a U.S. consular officer, or before a local notary with an apostille if the country is in the Hague Apostille Convention. Your Florida lawyer will say which; get it right the first time because a rejected signature from overseas can cost a month.

One thing the personal representative doesn't control: a Florida protected homestead. If the owner lived in the house as a Florida resident and left a spouse or minor child, the house generally passes outside the estate, and the personal representative has no power to sell it as an estate asset (sections 733.607 and 733.608). A house owned by someone who lived abroad generally isn't a Florida homestead, so in the cross-border cases this guide is about, the house is usually an ordinary estate asset the personal representative can sell.

🔐 Will the Sale Need a Judge's Order?

Once someone holds letters of administration, whether a Miami-Dade judge has to approve the sale depends on the will. Section 733.613 requires court authorization when the owner died without a will, when the will gives the personal representative no power to sell, or when the power it gives is too limited to use. In those cases the statute says no title passes until the court authorizes or confirms the sale.

That matters more for foreign estates than people expect. Wills written in another country often say nothing about selling Florida land, or they appoint an executor who can't serve here. Either way the Florida personal representative usually ends up asking the court. In practice the order is routine: the lawyer files a petition with the signed contract and a value estimate, and if no heir objects the judge signs. Build four to eight extra weeks into the timeline and make the contract contingent on the order.

If the will does grant a power of sale and the Florida personal representative is the person it names, title companies will usually close without an order. Ask your lawyer to confirm this before you sign a listing agreement with a 30-day closing.

🧾 FIRPTA: The Money Held Back at Closing

The Foreign Investment in Real Property Tax Act makes the buyer responsible for withholding tax when a foreign person sells U.S. real estate. Whoever is actually selling, the estate or an heir who has already received the house, is tested for foreign status. The IRS treats a foreign estate as a foreign person. Whether a particular estate administered in Florida counts as foreign is a tax question; your tax adviser should answer it before you price the house.

The basic numbers:

  • 💲 15% of the gross price is the general withholding rate, collected by the closing agent and sent to the IRS.
  • 🏠 No withholding if the price is $300,000 or less and the buyer will live in the house.
  • 🏡 10% if the buyer will live in it and the price is over $300,000 and up to $1,000,000.

A cash investor isn't buying to live there, so a sale to us, or to any investor, gets the full 15%. On a $380,000 sale that's $57,000 held at closing. The tax actually owed is often far lower, because heirs generally take a basis equal to the value at death, so a sale a year later may show little or no gain. The seller can apply for a withholding certificate on IRS Form 8288-B before closing to reduce the amount, or file a U.S. return afterward to get the excess back. Either way, plan for the delay. Families who need cash quickly should start the 8288-B as soon as there is a contract.

🏛️ The $60,000 Federal Estate-Tax Line for Nonresidents

Here is the number that surprises families most. A U.S. citizen's estate doesn't owe federal estate tax unless it's worth many millions. A nonresident who wasn't a U.S. citizen gets a far smaller allowance. The IRS requires Form 706-NA when the owner's U.S.-situs assets, plus certain gifts, exceed $60,000. A Miami house nearly always does.

Whether tax is actually owed depends on the house's value, its mortgage and any tax treaty between the U.S. and the owner's country. That's a question for a cross-border tax professional, not for us. Two practical points for the sale itself:

  • 📜 The IRS says a federal transfer certificate is not required for property administered by an executor or administrator appointed and acting within the United States. Opening the Florida case and selling through the Florida personal representative keeps the sale on that track.
  • ⏱️ The estate-tax question doesn't usually block the closing, but it can decide how much of the sale money the personal representative should hold back before distributing anything to heirs abroad.

📅 Creditors, AHCA and the Three-Month Window

Every Florida administration, ancillary or not, publishes a notice to creditors in a Miami-Dade newspaper. Under section 733.702, claims are due by the later of three months after first publication or 30 days after a known creditor is served. Section 733.710 bars nearly every claim two years after death, whether or not anyone published.

If the owner was 55 or older, section 733.2121 requires the personal representative to serve the notice and a copy of the death certificate on the Agency for Health Care Administration, which runs Florida Medicaid, within three months of first publication. Owners who lived mostly abroad rarely received Florida Medicaid, but the notice is still required, and skipping it can hold up the closing of the estate.

You can usually sell during the creditor period. The sale money simply sits in the estate account until the claims window closes and the court allows distribution. For heirs overseas, that's often better than holding a vacant house through hurricane season.

💸 What the House Costs While the Estate Is Open

A house owned by someone who lived abroad was never a Florida homestead, so there's no Save Our Homes cap to lose; it has probably been taxed on near-full market value all along. The 2025 adopted millage is 19.9878 in the City of Miami and 16.9317 in unincorporated Miami-Dade. On a house with a $450,000 taxable value inside city limits, that's about $9,000 a year, or $750 a month. The November tax bill offers a 4% discount for paying early, and taxes left unpaid become a tax certificate sold at the county's sale the following spring.

Typical monthly carrying costs on a vacant Miami house:

  • 🧾 Property tax: $600–$900 for most single-family houses in the $350,000–$550,000 range
  • 🌀 Insurance: a vacant-dwelling or windstorm policy often runs $300–$600 a month, more east of I-95 or for an older roof
  • 💡 Electricity and water: $120–$250, mostly to keep the air conditioning running so mold doesn't start
  • 🌴 Lawn, pool, pest and someone to check the house after storms: $150–$300
  • ⚖️ Probate legal fees: Florida's statutory schedule presumes about $1,500 for a small estate and scales with value; personal representative commissions under section 733.617 are 3% of the first $1 million

Realistically, $1,200–$2,000 a month leaves the estate while the house sits, before any repairs. Six months of ancillary probate plus a listing period can cost $10,000–$20,000.

🔀 The Paths, Rated Honestly

Option 1: Open the Florida case, then list with an agent

Best for sound houses

If the roof is recent, the house will pass a four-point inspection and the heirs agree, a retail sale nets the most, even after FIRPTA paperwork and commission. A financed buyer who plans to live there may also qualify for the reduced 10% withholding rate or none at all. This is the path we'd point most families toward, and it doesn't involve us.

Option 2: Sign a cash contract that waits for letters and the order

Best for distance and repairs

When the house needs work a retail buyer's insurer won't accept, or when the heirs can make one consulate appointment but not five, a cash buyer can sign early, wait for letters and the court order, and close on the date the judge allows. You give up some price for certainty and fewer signatures.

Option 3: Settle disputes first, then sell

Necessary when heirs disagree

If an heir abroad objects to the sale petition, the judge has to hear it, and that can add months. Some families end up with a buyout or a partition case after distribution. Our guide on what to do when heirs can't agree covers the choices.

💵 A Miami Gardens House, Worked Through

Say the owner lived in Venezuela and owned a 1970s three-bedroom block house in Miami Gardens. The heirs are two daughters, one in Madrid and one in Caracas. Fully renovated, nearby houses sell for about $480,000. This one needs a roof, an electrical panel and a kitchen, roughly $70,000 of work.

Cash route. Cash buyers pay about 60–80% of repaired value. On this house an offer would likely land between $315,000 and $360,000. Take $340,000, about 71%. The closing agent withholds 15% for FIRPTA, $51,000, unless a withholding certificate is in place. Florida documentary stamp tax in Miami-Dade is 60 cents per $100 on a single-family house, about $2,040. With ancillary legal fees and court costs of perhaps $6,000–$9,000 and four months of carrying costs at about $1,400, the estate nets roughly $323,000 before the FIRPTA refund comes back.

Retail route. Listed as-is, the house might bring $410,000 from a buyer who can insure it, though an old roof shrinks the pool. Subtract 5–6% commission (about $23,000), doc stamps (about $2,460), the same legal fees and eight months of carrying costs (about $11,000), and the estate nets roughly $365,000, again before withholding is settled.

On those numbers, listing wins by about $40,000 if the heirs can wait and the house can be insured. The cash route wins when the roof makes the house uninsurable, when the sisters can't keep paying the bills from abroad, or when a hurricane claim is already open on the property.

📋 What To Do This Week in Miami

  1. Write down where the owner was domiciled: country, address, where they voted and filed taxes. That decides whether you open a regular case or an ancillary one.
  2. Search the Miami-Dade Clerk's online records for the deed and any recorded mortgage, lien or prior probate case.
  3. Find the original will, and any foreign probate order, and ask whether either needs a certified translation.
  4. Decide which relative will serve as personal representative and confirm they qualify under section 733.304.
  5. Hire a Florida probate lawyer and ask two questions up front: will we need a court order to sell, and what will the consulate or apostille step look like for our signers?
  6. Talk to a cross-border tax professional about FIRPTA, Form 8288-B and Form 706-NA before you sign any contract.
  7. Pay or calendar the November property tax bill and confirm the house is insured as vacant.

✅ Bottom Line

A Miami estate with a foreign owner or foreign heirs is slower and more paperwork-heavy than a local one, but it's predictable. Pin down domicile, open the right Florida case, appoint a relative who qualifies, expect a court order if the will is silent, and plan for 15% withholding at closing. If the house is in good shape and nobody is in a hurry, list it. If it needs a roof, the heirs are scattered across three countries, or the bills are piling up, a cash sale that waits for the court can be the cleaner choice. Either way, ask us; we'll tell you which one you're in.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What is ancillary probate in Florida?

Ancillary administration is a secondary probate case Florida opens for Florida property when the owner was domiciled somewhere else. Under section 734.102 the ancillary personal representative posts bond, publishes notice to creditors and then has the same power to sell the Miami house as any Florida personal representative. The Miami-Dade Clerk charges $401 to open one.

Can a foreign will or foreign court order be used to sell a Miami house?

Not by itself. A Florida court has to recognize the will or appoint a personal representative before anyone can sign a deed that a title company will insure. The foreign will can be admitted in the Florida case, often with a certified translation.

Can an heir who lives outside the United States be personal representative in Florida?

Yes, if they are related to the person who died in one of the ways section 733.304 lists, such as a child, parent, sibling or spouse. A nonresident friend or business partner cannot serve, even if the will names them.

Do I need a Florida lawyer to probate a Miami house from abroad?

Almost always. Florida Probate Rule 5.030 requires a personal representative to be represented by a lawyer admitted in Florida unless the personal representative is the only person interested in the estate.

Does a Florida personal representative need court approval to sell the house?

Under section 733.613, yes if the owner died without a will or the will gives no usable power to sell; title doesn't pass until the court authorizes or confirms the sale. If the will grants a power of sale, title companies usually close without an order.

How much FIRPTA tax is withheld when a foreign estate or heir sells a Miami house?

The general rate is 15% of the gross price. There is no withholding if the price is $300,000 or less and the buyer will live in the house, and 10% if the buyer will live in it and the price is up to $1,000,000. A cash investor's purchase gets the full 15% unless a withholding certificate on Form 8288-B reduces it.

Will FIRPTA withholding be refunded?

Often a large part of it is. Heirs generally take a basis equal to the value at death, so the actual tax on the gain may be small. The seller can apply on Form 8288-B before closing or file a U.S. tax return afterward to recover the excess, but either way it takes time.

Does a nonresident owner's estate owe U.S. estate tax on a Miami house?

It may. The IRS requires Form 706-NA when a nonresident non-citizen's U.S.-situs assets, plus certain gifts, exceed $60,000. Whether tax is owed depends on value, debt and any tax treaty, so ask a cross-border tax professional.

How long does an ancillary probate sale take in Miami-Dade?

For an uncontested estate, plan on two to four months to get letters and any court order, then a few weeks to close with a cash buyer, or a few months more to list and sell retail. Translation, consulate appointments and objections from heirs are the usual causes of delay.

Is it better to list a Miami probate house or sell it for cash?

If the house is insurable, the heirs agree and nobody needs money quickly, listing usually nets more even after commission. A cash sale tends to win when the roof or electrical system makes the house hard to insure, when the heirs are spread across countries, or when carrying costs are draining the estate.
