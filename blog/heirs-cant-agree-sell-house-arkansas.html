Heirs Can't Agree on an Arkansas House? First Find Out Whether a Surviving Spouse Still Holds Dower or Homestead Rights

Arkansas is one of the few states that still gives a surviving spouse dower or curtesy, a life estate in one-third of the land, and its constitution gives a widow and minor children rights in the homestead. So the children who inherit an Arkansas house can agree perfectly and still be unable to sell it clean. And when an administrator sells, Arkansas usually needs a court order. Someone in your family died owning a house in Arkansas, and the heirs don't agree on what to do with it. Usually the fight looks simple from the outside: one sibling wants to sell, another wants to keep it, a third is out of state and silent. But in Arkansas there's often a fourth party in the room whether anyone mentions them or not: a surviving spouse, sometimes a stepparent, with rights that the children can't vote away.

This guide starts with those rights, because they decide what can be sold and by whom. Then it covers how Arkansas probate courts handle a sale when the heirs disagree, the levers each side can pull, and what the numbers look like on a typical Little Rock-area house.

👵 Dower, Curtesy and Homestead: Rights the Children Can't Vote Away

Most states folded the old common-law protections for widows and widowers into an "elective share" decades ago. Arkansas kept them. Three rules matter for a family house:

  • 📜 Dower and curtesy when there are children. Under Ark. Code § 28-11-301(a), if a person dies leaving a surviving spouse and a child or children, the spouse "shall be endowed of the third part of all the lands for life" that the deceased spouse owned during the marriage, unless the spouse relinquished that right in legal form. Subsection (b) says the right reaches even land sold during the marriage without the spouse's proper consent. That's why Arkansas title companies insist that a married seller's spouse sign to release dower or curtesy.
  • 🏡 The constitutional homestead. Article 9, Section 6 of the Arkansas Constitution says that when the owner of a homestead dies leaving a widow, the homestead is exempt and its rents and profits vest in her for life if she has no homestead of her own. Minor children share half the rents and profits until each turns 21, and the widow and children "may reside on the homestead or not."
  • ⚖️ When there are no children. Under § 28-11-307, a spouse with no children of the marriage takes one-half of newly acquired real estate outright as against collateral heirs, and a life estate in one-half of ancestral land, with smaller fractions against creditors.

The practical effect is that the children may own the house, subject to a parent's or stepparent's life rights in it. A buyer, and a buyer's title insurer, will want those rights released or resolved. If the surviving spouse won't sign, the children's "agreement to sell" doesn't produce a clean deed.

⚠️ The warning that matters most: before the heirs argue about price or timing, find out whether a spouse survived, how long they were married, whether minor children survived, and whether any of them ever signed away dower, curtesy or homestead rights. In Arkansas, a surviving spouse's life rights can keep a house off the market no matter how the children vote, and resolving them usually means a negotiated release, often paid for out of the sale.

🧭 Who Inherits in Arkansas When There's No Will

Arkansas's table of descents, Ark. Code § 28-9-214, sends the estate first to the children and the descendants of any child who died earlier. The surviving spouse inherits ahead of parents and siblings only if there are no descendants, and only takes the whole estate if the couple had been continuously married at least three years before the death; married less than three years, the spouse takes 50 percent and the rest goes to the decedent's parents or, if none, siblings.

Put that next to the dower and homestead rules and the typical second-marriage conflict becomes clear. Dad dies without a will, survived by his second wife and his children from a first marriage. Under the table of descents the children inherit the house. Under dower and the homestead clause, the widow may hold life rights in it and can live there. Neither side can sell a clean title without the other.

A will changes some of this, and a recorded beneficiary deed under Ark. Code § 18-12-608 sends the house straight to the named beneficiary at death, but spousal rights can still come into play. If you think the deceased signed either document, find it before anyone files anything.

🏛️ Arkansas Administrators Usually Need a Court Order to Sell

When probate is opened, the circuit court in the county where the deceased lived appoints a personal representative, an executor under a will or an administrator without one. Unlike the Uniform Probate Code states, where a personal representative can often sell a house without asking a judge, Arkansas's general rule is in Ark. Code § 28-51-103: estate real property "may be sold ... under court order" when necessary to pay claims, pay a legacy, preserve estate assets, make distribution, or for "any other purpose in the best interest of the estate." A will can grant broader power, and an Arkansas probate lawyer will check whether yours does.

That court-order requirement gives disagreeing heirs two specific levers you won't find in many states:

🛡️ The heir who wants to keep the house: post a bond

Blocks a sale to pay estate debts

Under § 28-51-103(d), an order to sell or mortgage property to pay the estate's obligations "shall not be granted" if an interested person files a bond the court approves, conditioned to protect everyone's rights. In plain terms, an heir who wants the house kept can guarantee the debts instead of letting the house be sold to pay them. It doesn't stop a sale for other purposes, and the bond has real cost.

📣 The heir who wants a sale: petition for a show-cause order

Forces the administrator to act

Under § 28-51-103(e), a creditor or other interested person can petition, and the court "shall require" the personal representative to show cause why a specific property shouldn't be sold. If the court finds the sale advantageous to the estate or necessary to protect the interested persons, it orders the representative to proceed. That's the tool for an administrator who's stalling, or who's the sibling living in the house.

🧑‍⚖️ Everyone: ask the court to pick a neutral administrator

Use when the appointee is the problem

When the family can't agree who should serve, or the person serving has a conflict, the circuit court can appoint someone else, including a professional or attorney administrator. It costs more, but a neutral fiduciary with a court order can sell when the family can't.

🪓 If Probate Isn't Opened: Partition and Arkansas's Heirs Property Act

Many Arkansas families never open probate. The house sits in a dead parent's name for years while the heirs, as tenants in common, own it together. When one of them finally wants out, the remedy is a partition case in circuit court under Ark. Code § 18-60-401 and the sections that follow.

Arkansas was an early adopter of the Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act, passing it in 2015 (Ark. Code § 18-60-1001 and following). For inherited family land it adds a court-determined value, a chance for the relatives who didn't ask for a sale to buy out those who did, a hard look at dividing the land before selling it, and a preference for a listing over a courthouse auction. For a sibling who wants to keep the family place, that is real protection. For a sibling who wants out, it means filing may simply get you bought out at the appraised price.

Dower and homestead rights don't vanish in a partition, either. The court will have to account for any spouse's life interest, which is one more reason to resolve it by agreement first.

💬 Getting a Surviving Spouse and the Children to One Deal

Most Arkansas standoffs over a house with a surviving spouse end in one of four deals:

  1. Spouse stays, sale later. The spouse lives in the house for life or until a set date, pays the taxes and insurance, and the children agree in writing that the house sells afterward. Low cost, but the children wait.
  2. Release for a share of the sale. The spouse signs a release of dower and homestead rights in exchange for an agreed slice of the proceeds, often calculated from the value of a life estate at the spouse's age, and everyone sells now.
  3. Children buy out the spouse's life rights. One or more children pay the spouse to release, then keep or sell the house themselves.
  4. Spouse buys the children out. If the spouse wants to stay permanently and can finance it, they purchase the children's remainder interests.

Valuing a life estate is a math problem, not a feelings problem. Using standard life-expectancy tables, an older spouse's life rights in a third of a house are worth much less than a younger spouse's. Putting a number on it, in writing, usually moves a stuck family faster than another family meeting.

🧮 What Waiting Costs on a Little Rock House

Arkansas taxes homes on an assessed value of 20 percent of appraised value, multiplied by the local millage, and the homestead credit stops once no owner lives there. In Little Rock, the combined millage commonly puts the annual bill at roughly 1.2 to 1.4 percent of market value; check the Pulaski County collector for your parcel's actual figure.

Take a 1960s brick ranch in west Little Rock worth about $240,000 updated, needing a roof, HVAC and cosmetic work, with nobody living there:

  • 🧾 Property tax: about $2,900 to $3,400 a year, roughly $240 to $280 a month.
  • 🏠 Vacant-dwelling insurance: about $100 to $200 a month.
  • 💡 Utilities and lawn care: $120 to $250 a month, more in the August heat.
  • 🛠️ Small repairs and securing the house: $50 to $150 a month on average.

That's roughly $510 to $880 a month, $6,000 to $10,500 a year. A contested probate sale or partition can add $5,000 to $25,000 or more in legal fees, depending on how hard the family fights.

If the family cooperates and lists: spend about $30,000 on the roof, HVAC and paint, sell for $235,000, pay roughly $15,000 in commission and closing costs and $4,000 in carrying costs. Net: about $186,000, before any payment to release a spouse's life rights.

If the family sells as-is for cash: cash buyers usually pay around 60 to 80 percent of after-repair value, so $144,000 to $192,000, and for this condition realistically about $160,000 to $175,000. No repairs or commission, closing within a few weeks once every heir and the spouse sign. Net: about $158,000 to $173,000.

The listing route nets more, often $15,000 to $25,000 more, when the family can agree, front the repair money and wait. The cash route closes that gap when the house needs more than cosmetic work, when nobody will pay for a roof on a house they don't fully control, or when a release deal with a surviving spouse needs a firm number and a fast closing to hold together.

  1. 👪 Write down who survived: spouse (and how long married), children, grandchildren of any child who died first, and any minor children.

✅ Bottom Line

In Arkansas, the first question in an heirs dispute isn't what the children want. It's whether a surviving spouse holds dower, curtesy or homestead rights, because those life rights can keep the house from selling cleanly no matter how the children vote. After that, Arkansas gives each side a specific lever: estate sales usually need a court order, an heir can block a debt sale with a bond, and an heir who wants action can force the administrator to show cause. If probate was never opened, the 2015 heirs property act shapes any partition. Most families end up with a negotiated release and a sale, and putting real numbers in writing is what gets them there.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Does Arkansas still have dower and curtesy?

Yes. Under Ark. Code 28-11-301, when a person dies leaving a spouse and children, the surviving spouse is endowed for life with one-third of the lands the deceased owned during the marriage, unless the spouse relinquished that right in legal form.

Can the children sell Mom or Dad's Arkansas house if a stepparent is still living there?

Not with a clean title unless the stepparent's rights are resolved. A surviving spouse may hold dower or curtesy and constitutional homestead rights for life, so a buyer will want those released, usually by a signed agreement that often pays the spouse a share of the sale.

What homestead rights does a widow have in Arkansas?

Article 9, Section 6 of the Arkansas Constitution says that if a homestead owner dies leaving a widow with no homestead of her own, the rents and profits of the homestead vest in her for life. Minor children share half the rents and profits until each turns 21, and they may live there or not.

Who inherits an Arkansas house when there is no will?

Under Ark. Code 28-9-214, the children and the descendants of any child who died earlier inherit first. A surviving spouse inherits ahead of parents only if there are no descendants, and takes the whole estate only after at least three years of continuous marriage.

Does an Arkansas administrator need court approval to sell the house?

Generally yes. Ark. Code 28-51-103 allows estate real property to be sold under court order for purposes such as paying claims, distribution, preserving assets, or any other purpose in the estate's best interest. A will may grant broader power, so have an Arkansas lawyer read it.

Can one heir stop an Arkansas probate sale of the family house?

Sometimes. Under Ark. Code 28-51-103(d), a court won't order property sold to pay the estate's obligations if an interested person files a court-approved bond protecting everyone's rights. That doesn't block a sale for other purposes, and the bond has a real cost.

How can an Arkansas heir force a slow administrator to sell?

Ark. Code 28-51-103(e) lets an interested person petition, and the court must require the personal representative to show cause why specific property shouldn't be sold. If a sale is advantageous or necessary to protect the heirs, the court orders it.

Has Arkansas adopted the Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act?

Yes, in 2015, at Ark. Code 18-60-1001 and following. In a partition of qualifying inherited land it provides a court valuation, a buyout opportunity for relatives who didn't ask for a sale, consideration of dividing the land, and a preference for a listing over an auction.

How much does a surviving spouse get in Arkansas if there are no children?

Under Ark. Code 28-11-307, against collateral heirs the spouse takes one-half of newly acquired real estate outright and a life estate in one-half of ancestral real estate; against creditors the fractions are one-third. Intestacy under 28-9-214 can give the spouse more.

Is it better for Arkansas heirs to list the house or sell it for cash?

If the house is in good shape and every heir and any surviving spouse will sign, a listing usually nets more. A cash sale tends to make sense when the house needs major repairs, the family needs one firm number to settle a spouse's release, or a holdout has the estate stuck.
