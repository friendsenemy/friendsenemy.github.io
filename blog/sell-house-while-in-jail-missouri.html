Selling a Missouri House From Jail or Prison: Record the Power of Attorney Before the Trustee's Notice Arrives

Missouri treats a power of attorney to sell land as a land record in its own right: it has to be acknowledged and recorded the way a deed is. Meanwhile Missouri lenders can sell a house at the courthouse door on about twenty days' notice. This guide explains how to get the paperwork recorded in time, what entireties ownership means for married couples, and when a cash sale is or isn't worth it.

By Dustin Ray, Founder of Pages of Purpose LLC. Not legal advice. Laws differ by state, Missouri statutes and Department of Corrections procedures change, and each title company sets its own standards. Have a lawyer licensed in Missouri, the state where the property is, review any power of attorney or deed before it is signed.

The calls we get about Missouri houses tend to start the same way. Someone is in the Missouri Department of Corrections or in a county jail, the mortgage is slipping, and a relative has a power of attorney form from an office-supply store or a website. The question is whether that form is enough to sell the house. In Missouri, the answer turns less on what the form says and more on whether it was acknowledged like a deed and whether it is sitting in the county's land records.

If you need the basics of selling from inside first (who can sign, what a power of attorney is, how prisons handle legal mail), read our national guide to selling a house while incarcerated. This page is about what Missouri adds.

📜 In Missouri, the Power of Attorney Becomes Part of the Land Records

Section 442.360 of the Revised Statutes of Missouri is short and old. It says that every letter of attorney or other instrument containing a power to convey real estate as the owner's agent must be acknowledged or proved, certified and recorded in the same way as instruments conveying real estate. In practice, that means three things for a family:

  1. 🖊️ The owner's signature on the power of attorney has to be acknowledged before a notary using a certificate that would be good enough for a deed.
  2. 🗂️ The power of attorney gets recorded with the Recorder of Deeds in the county where the house sits (the City of St. Louis has its own Recorder separate from St. Louis County).
  3. 🔗 The deed the agent later signs refers back to that recorded power, so anyone searching the title can see who authorized the sale.

Missouri's general durable power of attorney statute points the same way. Section 404.705 says a durable power does not have to be recorded to be valid between the owner, the agent and third parties, "except to the extent that recording may be required for transactions affecting real estate" under sections 442.360 and 442.370. A form that a bank happily accepted for a checking account may still be useless at a real estate closing if it was never acknowledged properly or can't be recorded.

The practical consequence: in Missouri, the signing inside the facility has to produce a recordable document on the first try. A notary who uses the wrong certificate, leaves the commission expiration off, or lets the owner sign without a proper acknowledgment can cost the family a second trip and two or three weeks.

🔤 The Durable Wording Missouri Asks For, Word for Word

Missouri is unusual in spelling out the language that makes a power of attorney durable. Under section 404.705, the document must contain wording such as: "THIS IS A DURABLE POWER OF ATTORNEY AND THE AUTHORITY OF MY ATTORNEY IN FACT SHALL NOT TERMINATE IF I BECOME DISABLED OR INCAPACITATED OR IN THE EVENT OF LATER UNCERTAINTY AS TO WHETHER I AM DEAD OR ALIVE," or the statute's alternative phrasing. It also must be subscribed by the owner, dated, and acknowledged in the manner prescribed for conveyances of real estate.

Being in prison is not a disability or incapacity, so a non-durable power of attorney does not end simply because someone is locked up. But sentences are long, health changes, and the people using these documents want certainty. Use the statutory durable language anyway. It costs nothing and removes one question from the title examiner's list.

Other drafting choices that matter in a Missouri prison sale:

  • 🏠 Describe the house. Include the street address and the legal description from the recorded deed, and say expressly that the agent may sell, convey and sign closing documents for it.
  • 👤 Pick an agent who can show up. The agent will sign the sale contract, the deed, the settlement statement and the seller's affidavits. Someone living out of state can do this, but it adds mail time.
  • 🧾 Leave room for the payoff. If there's a mortgage or a judgment, the agent needs authority to order payoffs and direct where the proceeds go, including into the owner's own account.

🏛️ Recorder of Deeds First, Then Closing

Once the signed and acknowledged power of attorney is back from the facility, the order of operations is simple. The title company or closing attorney reviews it, records it with the county Recorder of Deeds (often the same day the deed is recorded, sometimes earlier), and the agent signs the deed. Recording fees are modest, typically a few dozen dollars depending on page count.

While you're looking at the county records, check for anything recorded against the owner: judgments from the criminal case (court costs, restitution), child-support liens, or a prior power of attorney the owner gave someone else. An old recorded power that was never revoked can confuse the title search; if there is one, the new document should revoke it, and the revocation should be recorded too.

💑 Tenancy by the Entirety: Both Spouses or Nothing

In Missouri, when a married couple takes title together, the law presumes they hold it as tenants by the entirety. Neither spouse owns a separate half that can be sold alone; the couple owns the whole house as a unit. Two consequences follow for families with someone incarcerated.

  • ✍️ Both spouses must sign the deed. The spouse at home cannot sell entireties property alone, no matter how long the sentence. The incarcerated spouse signs either the deed itself or a recorded power of attorney, often naming the spouse at home as agent.
  • 🛡️ A judgment against only one spouse generally can't reach the house. Under Missouri law, a creditor of just one spouse usually can't force a sale of entireties property. A state-court restitution or cost judgment against the incarcerated spouse alone may not attach to the house, although federal restitution orders and federal tax liens follow different rules. Have the title company confirm before closing, because it decides whether that judgment gets paid from your proceeds.

If the couple is divorcing, entireties ownership ends with the decree and the house is divided like other marital property. Our guide to selling a house during a divorce covers how courts handle a sale while the case is pending.

If the incarcerated person co-owns with someone other than a spouse (a sibling, a parent, an ex), they're usually tenants in common, and each owner's share can be sold separately. That's where a buyer for a partial interest, or a partition case, comes in. See whether a co-owner can sell without the other.

⚡ Twenty Days' Notice: How Fast a Missouri Trustee's Sale Moves

Most Missouri home loans are secured by a deed of trust with a power of sale, which lets a trustee sell the house without a court case. Once the servicer has waited out the federal 120-day delinquency period and referred the loan to a foreclosure trustee, Missouri's own clock is short. The trustee must mail notice to the owner at least 20 days before the sale (RSMo 443.325) and publish the sale in a local newspaper (RSMo 443.320). Sales happen at the courthouse in the county where the house is located.

Put together, a Missouri sale can land roughly five to six months after the first missed payment, and only about a month after the trustee's notice shows up. For a family dealing with prison mail, that month is short. A power of attorney mailed into a facility, signed, notarized, mailed back and recorded can easily take three to five weeks on its own.

⚠️ Don't wait for the trustee's letter. In Missouri, if the owner is behind on payments and going to be inside for a while, get the recordable power of attorney signed now. Once the 20-day notice arrives, there may not be time to get a signature out of the facility, find a buyer and close before the sale date.

🔁 The One-Year Redemption Right That Has to Be Claimed Before the Sale

Missouri does have a post-sale redemption right, but it's narrow and front-loaded. Under section 443.410, if the lender (or someone buying for it) is the purchaser at the trustee's sale, the former owner can redeem within one year. The catch is in the proviso: the person who wants to redeem must give the trustee written notice of the intent to redeem at the sale or within ten days before the advertised sale date, and then meet a bond requirement under section 443.420 and pay the debt, interest, taxes and costs within the year.

If a third party outbids the lender at the sale, there is no redemption right at all. For someone in prison, the notice requirement is the problem: the notice has to be given before the sale, by someone with authority, while the family may not even know the date. If the house has meaningful equity and a sale date is set, a Missouri foreclosure attorney should look at this right away. Better still, close a sale before the trustee's sale and keep the equity.

🏢 Signing at a Missouri DOC Prison or a County Jail

Missouri's Department of Corrections runs about twenty adult institutions, from Jefferson City to Bonne Terre to Potosi, and many people serve shorter sentences or wait for trial in county jails such as those in St. Louis, Jackson and Greene counties. The logistics differ:

  • 🏛️ DOC institutions usually route legal paperwork through the caseworker or the institutional legal or records office. Ask whether the facility has staff notaries who will notarize a power of attorney for real estate, or whether an outside notary must schedule a visit.
  • 🚓 County jails often allow a mobile notary during professional visiting hours, sometimes through glass. Paper may have to go in and out through the jail's mail or property room rather than hand to hand.
  • 🪪 Identification is the most common failure point. The owner's driver's license is usually in stored property. Ask the facility in advance what ID it can provide for the notary.

Because Missouri needs a deed-grade acknowledgment, send the notary the exact certificate the title company wants to see, and ask the title company to review a scan before the original is mailed back. For more detail on the notary problem itself, see our guide on getting a notary into a jail or prison.

🪪 A Beneficiary Deed Solves a Different Problem

Some families aren't trying to sell now. They want to make sure the house doesn't end up in probate if the owner dies in custody. Missouri's nonprobate transfer law (RSMo 461.025) lets an owner sign a beneficiary deed naming who receives the house at death. It must be signed, acknowledged and recorded before the owner dies, and it doesn't transfer anything or limit the owner's rights while alive. It won't help sell the house today and won't stop a foreclosure, but for a sound, paid-off house and a long sentence, it may be the right document to sign during the same notary visit.

💵 What an Empty Missouri House Costs and What It Brings

Missouri assesses homes at 19% of market value, and local levies vary widely, so taxes on the same price house differ a lot between rural counties and the St. Louis or Kansas City areas. On a $200,000 house in St. Louis County, property tax is often around $220 to $285 a month. Add vacant-home insurance ($100 to $250), utilities to keep pipes from freezing ($100 to $200), and lawn and upkeep ($75 to $150), and an empty house costs roughly $500 to $900 a month before any mortgage payment.

Recorded power of attorney, then list with an agent

The most money when time allows

Best for a sound house with payments current. One signing inside, one recording, and the agent handles everything else over the next few months.

Spouse at home as agent for entireties property

Simplest for married owners

The incarcerated spouse names the spouse at home as agent. Once the power is recorded, she signs for both of them. Ask the title company in advance whether it wants anything extra when one spouse signs in both capacities.

As-is cash sale

When a trustee's notice is coming

When the loan is far behind, the house needs heavy work, or no one can run a listing, a cash buyer can close within weeks of the power being recorded. You give up price for certainty.

✅ Bottom Line

Selling a Missouri house from prison or jail is mostly a paperwork race. The power of attorney has to carry the statutory durable wording, be acknowledged like a deed and be recorded with the Recorder of Deeds, and for entireties property both spouses have to sign. A trustee's sale can follow its notice by about three weeks, and the one-year redemption right only exists if someone claims it before the sale. Get the recordable power signed before the trustee's letter comes, then decide between listing and a cash sale on the actual numbers.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Does a power of attorney to sell a house have to be recorded in Missouri?

Yes. RSMo 442.360 requires a power to convey real estate to be acknowledged or proved, certified and recorded the same way deeds are. The title company records it with the county Recorder of Deeds before or with the deed the agent signs.

What words make a Missouri power of attorney durable?

Section 404.705 requires language such as "This is a durable power of attorney and the authority of my attorney in fact shall not terminate if I become disabled or incapacitated," plus the owner's signature, a date, and an acknowledgment in the form used for real estate conveyances.

Does a Missouri power of attorney stop working when the owner goes to prison?

No. Incarceration is not a disability or incapacity, so it does not end the agent's authority. Using the statutory durable wording is still wise because sentences are long and it removes a question for the title company.

Can my spouse sell our Missouri house without me while I'm in jail?

Not if you own it together as husband and wife. Missouri presumes that ownership is a tenancy by the entirety, and both spouses must sign. You can sign the deed yourself or give your spouse a recorded power of attorney to sign for you.

Can a restitution judgment against my husband take our jointly owned Missouri house?

Usually a state-court judgment against only one spouse cannot be collected from entireties property in Missouri. Federal restitution and federal tax liens follow different rules, so have the title company and an attorney review any judgment before closing.

How much notice does a Missouri lender give before a foreclosure sale?

For a deed of trust with a power of sale, the trustee must mail notice at least 20 days before the sale under RSMo 443.325 and publish it in a local newspaper under 443.320. Sales are usually held at the county courthouse.

Can I get my Missouri house back after a foreclosure sale?

Only in a narrow case. Under RSMo 443.410, if the lender buys the house at the sale, the owner can redeem within one year, but only if written notice of intent to redeem was given at the sale or within ten days before it, and a bond is posted.

Can an inmate sign a beneficiary deed in Missouri?

Yes, if the inmate can sign before a notary. A beneficiary deed under RSMo 461.025 names who gets the house at death and must be recorded before the owner dies. It does not sell the house or stop a foreclosure.

What does it cost to keep an empty house in St. Louis County while the owner is in prison?

On a $200,000 house, property tax often runs about $220 to $285 a month. With vacant-home insurance, utilities and upkeep, expect roughly $500 to $900 a month before any mortgage payment.

How much will a cash buyer pay for a Missouri house when the owner is incarcerated?

Typically 60% to 80% of what the house would sell for fully repaired, depending on condition. On a $210,000 Kansas City house needing $35,000 of work, that is roughly $130,000 to $155,000. Listing often nets more if time allows.
