Partition Lawsuits in Kentucky: A Sale Below Two-Thirds of the Appraisal Can Be Undone for a Year

Kentucky attaches an unusual string to every court-ordered sale of real estate. Before the auction, two disinterested residents of the county appraise the property. If the winning bid comes in under two-thirds of that appraisal, KRS 426.530 lets the defendant redeem the property within a year by repaying the price plus 10% a year. Add a statute that presumes a co-owned house cannot be divided, and you have a partition system that pushes hard toward a sale — and then makes cheap bids risky. Or ask us a question, free.

Most partition guides end at the auction: the property is sold, the money is split, the case is over. A Kentucky partition can have one more chapter. KRS 426.530 says that if real property sold under a judgment or order of a court, other than an execution, "does not bring two-thirds (2/3) of its appraised value, the defendant and his representatives may redeem it within a year from the day of sale, by paying the original purchase money and ten percent (10%) per annum interest thereon."

That rule shapes who bids, what they bid, and what the co-owners can do after the hammer falls.

⏳ The Year After the Auction: Kentucky's Redemption Right

Here is how the statute works when it applies:

  • 💰 Redemption price: the original purchase money plus 10% per year interest, paid to the clerk of the court that ordered the sale.
  • 🗝️ Who can redeem: "the defendant and his representatives." In a partition case the defendants are usually the co-owners who did not file.
  • 🏠 What the buyer gets meanwhile: an immediate writ of possession and a deed that contains a lien in favor of the defendant, reflecting the right to redeem during the statutory period. When the defendant pays, the master commissioner conveys the property back.

For a co-owner who did not want the sale, that is real protection. If the house sells for $90,000 against a $150,000 appraisal, the bid is below two-thirds ($100,000), and a defendant co-owner who can raise $90,000 plus interest within the year can take the property back.

For bidders, it is a deterrent. Investors at Kentucky commissioner's sales know that a bargain bid under the two-thirds line buys a house they may have to give back a year later at a 10% return. So bids tend to stretch toward two-thirds of the appraisal or stop short entirely. Whether and how 426.530 applies in a particular partition case is a question for your Kentucky attorney — but every bidder in the room will be thinking about it.

⚠️ What this means for you: in Kentucky the pre-sale appraisal is not paperwork. It sets the two-thirds line that decides whether a cheap sale can be undone. If the appraisal is too low, the line is too low. Watch for the appraisal filing and challenge it before the sale, not after.

🏘️ Two Residents of the County Appraise the Property First

KRS 426.520 requires that before any real property is sold under a court order or judgment other than an execution, the commissioner or other officer selling it must have it appraised under oath "by two (2) disinterested, intelligent housekeepers of the county." If they disagree, the officer acts as umpire. The appraisal must be written, signed, returned to the court and filed in the case record before the sale.

The language is from another century, and in practice the appraisers are usually local people with real estate experience chosen by the master commissioner's office. But the statute does not require a licensed appraiser, and two people walking through a house for an hour can miss a new roof, a flooded basement, or what renovated homes on the block are selling for. Co-owners who care about the result send the master commissioner's office recent comparable sales and repair information early, and check the filed appraisal as soon as it hits the record.

📐 Kentucky Starts From "This House Cannot Be Split"

The partition-by-sale statute, KRS 389A.030, lets any co-owner who shares title "in such manner that a conveyance by them jointly would pass a fee simple title" sue in the circuit court of the county where the land, or most of it, lies. Its subsection (3) sets the default: "indivisibility of the real estate shall be presumed" unless a party raises the issue in the pleadings. Only if the court is satisfied the property can be divided without materially impairing the value of any interest does it order a physical division instead.

Many states start from the opposite end, presuming land should be divided and making the party who wants a sale prove otherwise. Kentucky's presumption means a co-owner who wants the land split — typically farm ground — has to say so in the pleadings and prove it. For a house on a city lot, the presumption simply confirms what everyone already knew: it will be sold.

Some exclusions and details from the same statute:

  • 💑 Tenants by the entirety in residential property they actually occupy as a principal residence are outside the statute.
  • 🧑‍⚖️ The case is tried without a jury, and a defendant under a disability with no fiduciary is represented by a guardian ad litem, with the court holding that person's proceeds until a bonded fiduciary claims them.
  • 🪦 A party who dies while the case is pending does not stop it; the court directs their share to their successors.
  • 👵 A life tenant's interest is valued and paid from the proceeds, unless an objection shows that would defeat the purpose of the will or deed that created it — then the life tenant's portion of the proceeds goes to a trustee.

🏛️ The Master Commissioner's Auction

When the court orders a sale, KRS 389A.030(4) refers it to the master commissioner (or a specially appointed commissioner) to conduct a public sale on the terms the court sets. Kentucky's master commissioners run judicial sales on a recurring schedule, often at or near the courthouse, after published notice. Terms frequently allow the buyer to pay a percentage down and the balance within a short period or on bond, and the sale is reported to the court for confirmation.

Commissioner's sales draw experienced local investors. Retail buyers who need mortgage financing, inspections and appraisals rarely compete, which is why these auctions tend to produce lower prices than a listing would. The two-thirds redemption line raises the practical floor; it does not produce market value.

🌾 The Other Statute: Commissioners Who Divide Land

Kentucky has a second partition path. KRS 381.135 lets a person who holds land jointly, or who owns 20% or more of a closely held farm corporation or partnership, petition the circuit court for a division. The court appoints three commissioners to determine the allotment; a registered land surveyor performs the survey; the commissioners report, and the court confirms, sets aside or remands. Costs are apportioned by interest, except that costs of a contest go against the unsuccessful party.

This is the route for land that can really be split — a 200-acre farm with road frontage on two sides — and it is the reason a Kentucky family farm case can look very different from a city house case.

⌛ Time, Money and Kentucky Carrying Costs

  • 🗓️ Timeline: service, answers and judgment usually take three to six months; appraisal, notice and the commissioner's sale another two to four; confirmation and distribution another one to three. Eight to fifteen months is common, longer with contests or unknown heirs.
  • 💵 Lawyers: each owner generally pays their own lawyer; costs of the sale come out of the proceeds. Expect a few thousand dollars per side for a simple case and much more for a contested one.
  • 🏚️ Carrying the property: Kentucky effective property tax rates commonly run about 0.8–1.2% of value a year. On a $160,000 house that is roughly $110–$160 a month, plus $100–$200 insurance, $120–$250 utilities and yard care — $4,000–$7,000 a year of holding cost that someone fronts.
  • 🧾 Transfer tax: Kentucky's real estate transfer tax, fifty cents per $500 of value, is collected when the deed is recorded.

💵 A Louisville Shotgun House, Worked Through

Four cousins each own a quarter of their grandmother's shotgun house in Louisville. Repaired, it would sell for about $180,000; it needs $40,000 of work. The court-ordered appraisal comes in at $135,000, so the two-thirds line is $90,000.

  • 🔨 Auction at $100,000: above the line, so the sale is confirmed. After roughly $8,000–$12,000 of costs and fees, each quarter is worth about $22,000–$23,000.
  • ⏳ Auction at $80,000: below the line. A defendant cousin could redeem within a year for $80,000 plus 10% a year — a bargain if they want the house and can raise the money.
  • 🤝 Agreed listing: as-is perhaps $140,000; minus 6% commission ($8,400) and closing costs, about $129,000, or roughly $32,000 each.
  • 💵 Cash offer for the whole house: 60–80% of $180,000 is $108,000–$144,000; on this condition a realistic offer is about $115,000–$125,000, closing in weeks with no auction or redemption period.

If the cousins can sign one listing agreement, that is the best result. Our offer only beats the courthouse, not the open market.

✅ Bottom Line

A Kentucky partition is built to end in a sale: the statute presumes a house cannot be divided and hands it to the master commissioner. But the sale carries two safeguards most states lack — a sworn pre-sale appraisal by two county residents, and a one-year redemption right when the price falls under two-thirds of that appraisal. Make the appraisal honest, use the redemption right if you want the property back, and settle before the sale date if you can. Kentucky has not enacted the Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act, so families with inherited property should not count on its buyout procedures; a heirs-property bill draft has been discussed for the 2027 session, and your attorney can tell you whether anything has changed.

❓ Kentucky Partition, Ten Questions Answered

Can a Kentucky partition sale be undone after the auction?

Sometimes. KRS 426.530 provides that if real property sold under a court judgment or order, other than an execution, does not bring two-thirds of its appraised value, the defendant and his representatives may redeem it within a year by paying the purchase price plus 10% per year interest. Ask your attorney whether it applies to your sale.

Who appraises property before a Kentucky court-ordered sale?

Under KRS 426.520 the commissioner or officer selling the property must have it appraised under oath by two disinterested, intelligent housekeepers of the county, with the officer acting as umpire if they disagree. The written appraisal is filed in the case record before the sale.

Does a Kentucky court have to divide the land instead of selling it?

No. KRS 389A.030 presumes the real estate is indivisible unless a party raises the issue in the pleadings and the court is satisfied it can be divided without materially impairing the value of any interest. If divisible, division proceeds under KRS 381.135.

Who conducts a partition sale in Kentucky?

The court refers the sale to the master commissioner or appoints a commissioner to hold a public sale and convey the property on the terms and with the distribution of proceeds the court sets. The sale is reported back to the court.

What does a buyer at a below-two-thirds Kentucky sale receive?

An immediate writ of possession and a deed containing a lien in favor of the defendant that reflects the right to redeem during the one-year period. If the defendant pays the redemption money to the clerk, the master commissioner conveys the property back.

Can a married couple's home be partitioned under KRS 389A.030?

The statute excludes tenants by the entirety in residential property actually occupied by them as a principal residence. Spouses in that situation generally resolve the property through agreement or divorce proceedings rather than a partition sale.

What happens if a co-owner dies during a Kentucky partition case?

The case continues. Under KRS 389A.030(5) the death of a party before distribution does not affect the action, and the court may direct that party's share to their successors in interest on application.

How are life estates handled in a Kentucky partition sale?

The court values the life or term interest and pays that party a portion of the net proceeds in satisfaction of it. If a party objects and the court finds this would defeat the purpose of the will or deed that created the interest, the life tenant's share goes to a court-appointed trustee instead.

Can Kentucky farmland be divided rather than sold?

Yes, when it can be divided fairly. KRS 381.135 lets a joint owner, or a holder of 20% or more of a closely held farm corporation or partnership, petition for division by three court-appointed commissioners, with a registered land surveyor preparing the survey.

Does Kentucky have the Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act?

Kentucky has not enacted the Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act, so inherited property is partitioned under the general statutes. A heirs-property bill draft has been discussed for the 2027 legislative session; check with a Kentucky attorney for any change.
