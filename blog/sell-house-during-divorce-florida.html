In a Florida Divorce, the House Has a Second Asset Attached: Its Save Our Homes Tax Cap

A Florida home owned for years can carry an assessed value far below its market value, and that gap can be worth thousands of dollars a year. Who keeps it, who loses it and whether it can be split depends on whether you sell, deed the house to one spouse or stay on title together. Most settlements never mention it. Here's how it works, what the deadlines are, and how it changes the choice to sell or keep.

Two neighbors in Pinellas County could own the same house and pay wildly different property taxes. One bought in 2009 and has lived there since. The other bought in 2023. Florida's Save Our Homes amendment limits how much a homestead's assessed value can rise each year, to 3% or the change in the Consumer Price Index, whichever is less. Over a long ownership, the distance between market value and assessed value can become very large.

When a long-married Florida couple divorces, that distance is worth real money. A spouse who stays keeps paying the low tax bill. A buyer pays the full one. And under the portability rules, some or all of that benefit may move to a new Florida home, but only in the right circumstances. A settlement that ignores this could hand one spouse a benefit nobody priced.

🌴 What the Save Our Homes Differential Is Worth in a Divorce

Look up your home on the county property appraiser's website. You want two figures: just (market) value and assessed value. The gap is your differential. On a home with a $430,000 just value and a $215,000 assessed value, it's $215,000. With a combined tax rate of about 2%, common in many Florida urban counties, that gap saves around $4,300 a year compared with what a new owner would pay. Rates vary widely by county and city.

Then consider what each path does to it:

  • One spouse keeps the home and stays. The Florida Department of Revenue's guidance says a change or transfer between spouses doesn't trigger reassessment, and practitioners generally say a homestead doesn't reset just because one spouse leaves title and the other stays. Confirm that for your facts with the appraiser.
  • The spouse who leaves deeds away their interest. As the Sarasota County Property Appraiser explains it, a spouse who deeds out has no portability from that home.
  • Both stay on title as tenants in common. That same office says the spouse who left can transfer 50% of the cap. If you hold as joint tenants with survivorship, it says the resident spouse keeps portability. How title reads can matter, so ask your own county.
  • You sell. The home is abandoned, so each spouse who sets up a new Florida homestead can take a share. A Florida Bar Journal review described each taking half, and the Department's rule at the time treated the shares as not transferable to anyone else.
The trap: a quitclaim deed signed in a hurry can cost the departing spouse a portable benefit worth thousands of dollars a year on their next home. Before anyone signs a deed in a Florida divorce, ask what it does to portability, and price that in. Florida courts have to consider tax consequences of a distribution, so raise it with your lawyer while the case is open.

🚚 How portability works if you move

Portability lets you transfer up to $500,000 of differential to a new Florida homestead. If the new home is worth as much as or more than the old one, you can move the whole amount you're entitled to. If it's worth less, you take a proportional share. You file Form DR-501T with your homestead application, generally by March 1 of the year you claim it. The new homestead must be established within three years of January 1 of the year you abandoned the old one. That's a county-level process, and the appraiser's staff will walk you through it.

🛡️ Homestead Joinder: The Name on the Deed Isn't the Last Word

Article X, Section 4(c) of the Florida Constitution lets an owner of homestead sell it only if joined by the spouse. So even if your husband or wife isn't on the deed, a title company will want that person's signature while you're married and the house is your homestead. Trying to sell around a spouse doesn't produce marketable title. The rule ends when the marriage does, but by then a judge has usually dealt with the house. Most Florida married couples also hold as tenants by the entirety, and under Florida Statutes § 689.15, dissolution of marriage turns that into a tenancy in common.

Florida's procedure sets the other limits. One spouse needs six months of Florida residency before filing under § 61.021, and a final judgment can't generally be entered until 20 days after the petition under § 61.19. An agreed, simplified case can finish quickly, and a contested one can last much longer.

⚖️ How a Florida Judge Treats the House

Under § 61.075, equitable distribution starts from an equal split of marital assets and debts, and a judge can depart from that for reasons listed in the statute. Marital property is generally what was acquired during the marriage. The 2024 amendment to § 61.075 requires a gift of real property between spouses to be in a signed written instrument, so verbal promises about "our house" won't turn separate property into marital property.

Three Florida-specific items often come up:

  • The child factor. Section 61.075(1)(h) lets a court consider whether it's desirable for a dependent child to stay in the marital home, when that's equitable, in the child's best interest and financially feasible. It's often framed as exclusive use and possession until a set date.
  • Credits and setoffs. Section 61.077 says that, unless the settlement or judgment provides otherwise, nobody gets credits or setoffs from the sale of the marital home. Without a settlement, the court weighs eight factors, including who has exclusive use, whether alimony or child support already covers home costs, the value of that use, and capital gains. In plain English, if you've paid the mortgage since separating and expect credit, write that into the agreement.
  • Partition afterward. If the house isn't resolved, either ex can file a partition case under Chapter 64, usually ending in a court-supervised sale, often at a lower price than a private sale. See our Florida partition guide.

🔀 Which Way to Go

Keep the home with a buyout

Strong if the home's costs still fit

The stayer keeps a low assessment that a replacement home wouldn't have. Price that fairly. Then be honest about the refinance rate and the insurance renewal, because today's premium may make the "cheap" house less cheap than it looks.

Sell and each start over

Fairest treatment of the tax cap

Both spouses can establish new homesteads and port a share. For a couple with a big differential, that can be the way to make sure neither one walks away with nothing. Mark the three-year window on your calendar.

Delay under exclusive use

Good for kids, needs firm terms

Set the sale date, who pays taxes and insurance, how repairs get approved and how credits are counted. Section 61.077 makes that language valuable. Vague terms invite a second lawsuit.

Sell as-is for cash

When the house can't get to a clean listing

Older roofs can make a Florida home hard to insure, and that can scare off buyers using mortgages. A condo may have assessments or inspection issues that complicate a sale. When a cash close solves that problem, or a long fight over a listing, it can earn its discount. Otherwise, list.

💵 The Tampa Example, Tax Cap Included

Suppose a home with a $430,000 just value, a $215,000 assessed value and $180,000 owed. The equity is $250,000. At about 2%, tax on a roughly $165,000 taxable value after the homestead exemption is about $3,300 a year, or $275 a month. Insurance might be $400 a month in a coastal county, and often more for older roofs. Add $150 for upkeep and you're at about $825 a month, before the mortgage.

  • Listing: around $430,000 less about 6% in commissions, plus documentary stamps of about $0.70 per $100 (Miami-Dade's rate differs), leaves about $221,000 after the mortgage, or $110,500 each. Each could port a share of the $215,000 differential.
  • Buyout: the stayer owes $125,000 and refinances to about $305,000 at today's rates. They keep a tax bill of perhaps $3,300, where a new buyer would pay more than twice that. If the departing spouse gives up a portable $107,500 by deeding out, that should be part of the price.
  • Cash sale at 70%: $301,000 less the $180,000 mortgage leaves $121,000, or $60,500 each. Compared with a listing, that's about $100,000 less for the couple. On a sound house, don't do it. On a home with a failing roof, no insurer and a lender demanding coverage, the listing number may not exist.

Worth noting: the mortgage rate matters more than the tax saving in many buyouts. If the old loan is at 3% and the new one is near today's rates, the refinance can cost more each year than the tax cap saves. Ask a lender whether a loan assumption is possible, and have your attorney check how it would work with a release.

📋 What To Do This Week

  1. Pull your property appraiser record, and write down just value, assessed value and the exemptions.
  2. Call the appraiser's office and ask how your title and plan would affect the Save Our Homes benefit and portability.
  3. Read the deed to see whether you hold by the entireties and whether both names appear.
  4. Get your mortgage payoff, your insurance renewal date and the roof's age.
  5. Ask your attorney about credits under § 61.077 and how the cap should appear in the agreement.
  6. If either of you plans to buy, get preapproved before agreeing to keep or deed the house.
  7. Keep the mortgage, the insurance and the tax bill current while you decide.

✅ Bottom Line

A long-owned Florida home comes with a tax benefit that belongs in the settlement. Find out your differential, and learn what a deed, shared title or a sale does to it before you sign anything. Remember that homestead rules give a spouse a say while you're married and that § 61.077 rewards clear writing on credits. If one of you can truly carry the house, a buyout may make sense. If both of you need a fresh start, a sale may be fairer. Use a cash buyer only when a condition or insurance problem blocks a normal sale, or the conflict has gone on too long.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What happens to Save Our Homes in a Florida divorce?

The cap generally stays with a spouse who keeps living in the homestead. A transfer between spouses doesn't by itself trigger reassessment under state guidance. A spouse who deeds out generally gives up portability from that home, while selling lets each spouse who sets up a new homestead take a share.

Can both spouses port the Save Our Homes benefit after selling?

Yes, generally each spouse who establishes a new Florida homestead can transfer a share of the differential, up to the $500,000 limit. A home of lower value takes a proportional amount. File Form DR-501T with the homestead application and confirm the numbers with the county property appraiser.

What is the deadline to transfer Save Our Homes after a divorce sale?

The new homestead must be established within three years of January 1 of the year you abandoned the old one. That isn't three years from the sale date, so it can be shorter than expected. Applications are generally due by March 1.

My spouse isn't on the deed. Do they still need to sign to sell our Florida home?

If it's your homestead and you're married, generally yes. Article X, Section 4(c) of the Florida Constitution requires joinder of the spouse. Title companies will ask for it. Once the divorce is final, a former spouse who isn't on title normally has no homestead joinder right, but check any judgment.

What happens to a tenancy by the entirety when a Florida divorce is final?

Under Florida Statutes section 689.15, spouses holding by the entirety become tenants in common at dissolution of marriage. Survivorship ends, and each owns a share that can pass by will. Either can file for partition, so the final judgment should cover the house.

Can I get credit for paying the mortgage after I moved out in Florida?

Only if your agreement or the judgment provides it, or a court awards it after weighing section 61.077 factors. Those factors include whether exclusive use was granted and whether alimony or child support already covers the costs. Put credits in writing.

How long must we wait to divorce in Florida?

One spouse needs six months of Florida residency before filing. A final judgment generally can't be entered until 20 days after filing, unless a court shortens that for injustice. An agreed case can move quickly. A contested dispute about a house can take far longer.

Can a Florida court order the marital home sold?

Yes, as part of equitable distribution or through partition after the divorce. A court may instead delay a sale and grant exclusive use where that serves a dependent child and is financially feasible. Court-supervised partition sales often bring less than a private sale.

Will a buyer pay more property tax than we do?

Usually yes on a long-held home, because the assessed value resets to just value the January 1 after a sale. List prices should reflect that, and buyers' lenders budget on the higher tax. The county appraiser's website often has a tax estimator for new owners.

Should we sell our Florida house for cash during divorce?

Rarely, if the home is sound and insurable, since a listing usually pays much more. Cash can make sense if roof age, storm damage or lack of insurance blocks financed buyers, or if a sale must finish quickly. Compare net proceeds and portability together.
