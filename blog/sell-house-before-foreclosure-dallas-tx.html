In Dallas, Foreclosure Runs on a Calendar: Count Back From the First Tuesday

Texas can take a house in about six weeks from the cure letter, with no judge and no right to buy it back after the sale. But every sale must land on the first Tuesday of a month, and that gives you a date to plan around. Here's how to work backward from it in Dallas County, when a home equity loan changes the rules, and when listing beats selling to a buyer like us.

By Dustin Ray, Founder of Pages of Purpose LLC. Not legal advice — Texas foreclosure law and Dallas County procedures change, and laws differ by state. Before you sign or skip anything, talk to a Texas-licensed attorney or a HUD-approved housing counselor.

Most foreclosure guides talk about months and stages. In Dallas, the thing to find is a date. Texas Property Code § 51.002 requires most foreclosure sales to happen on the first Tuesday of a month, between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m., at the county courthouse or a spot the commissioners have designated. There's one exception: when that Tuesday is January 1 or July 4, the sale moves to the first Wednesday.

That rule is the reason a Dallas foreclosure can feel as if it came out of nowhere. In a deed-of-trust state like Texas, there's usually no lawsuit, no hearing and no judge. A substitute trustee posts a notice and sells the house. It also means you can build a real plan, because a lender who misses one posting deadline has to wait a whole month for the next sale day. Your entire strategy comes down to counting backward from a Tuesday.

🗓️ Find Your Tuesday: The Dates That Control Everything

The law requires the notice of sale to be given at least 21 days before the sale. It's posted at the courthouse, filed with the county clerk and sent by certified mail. In Dallas County, the clerk's office says the same thing in plain terms: notices must be posted 21 days before the first Tuesday.

You can check whether your house is posted. Dallas County's clerk has a foreclosure notices page, sorted by month and city. For notices filed on or after February 24, 2026, the county's public search site at dallas.tx.publicsearch.us has a foreclosure filter you can sort by sale date or city. Notices are also posted in the lobby of the George Allen Courts Building. If your address is on a list, the sale is real, even if nobody's called you.

The most expensive mistake in Dallas: assuming a call with your servicer stopped the sale. Unless you've got written confirmation that the sale is cancelled, it's on. Loan modification paperwork, a promised callback and a pending short sale do not by themselves take your house off the first-Tuesday list.

⏳ The 41-Day Minimum, and Why It's Usually Longer

Before a notice of sale can go out on a residence, § 51.002(d) requires the servicer to send a certified-mail default notice giving you at least 20 days to cure. Add the 21-day sale notice, and the statute's floor comes to 41 days. That's a good number to keep in mind. It isn't really how it goes, for two reasons.

Federal servicing rules generally keep a servicer from starting until you're over 120 days late (12 C.F.R. § 1024.41), and a complete loss mitigation application can hold it off longer. And the calendar forces sales onto one day. So if you miss your first payment in January, a common real-world path is a cure letter in spring, a posting in late spring or summer, and a first-Tuesday sale around five to nine months in. Lenders vary. Nothing in Texas law makes them hurry, but nothing makes them wait beyond those notices once the federal period ends either.

🏦 Home Equity Loans Run on a Different Track

Before you do anything else, find out what kind of loan you've got. Texas treats home equity loans unlike almost any other state, and it matters in two big ways.

A court order is required. The Texas Constitution, article XVI, § 50(a)(6)(D), says a home equity lien may be foreclosed only by court order. Lenders use a fast process under Rule 736 of the Texas Rules of Civil Procedure. After the citation goes out, your response is due the first Monday after the 38th day. If you respond, a hearing is held 20 to 30 days after a party asks for one. If you don't, the lender can take a default order. It's quick, narrow and not a money judgment, and the order then allows the sale to go ahead under the usual Chapter 51 notices. That usually adds a couple of months.

You generally can't be pursued for the shortfall. Section 50(a)(6)(C) makes a home equity loan nonrecourse against the owner and spouse, unless it was obtained by actual fraud. If the loan on your house is a true Texas home equity loan, the risk of owing money after the sale largely goes away. That changes how much you should pay to avoid the sale, particularly if you're underwater.

Reverse mortgages, certain tax-lien transfers and some HOA liens also use the Rule 736 route. Read your deed of trust, or ask a lawyer. A refinance that only looks like home equity may not be one.

🔨 What Happens Under the Overhang on Commerce Street

According to the Dallas County Clerk, sales take place on the north side of the George Allen Courts Building, facing Commerce Street, below the overhang, at 600 Commerce Street, unless the commissioners designate another spot. Sales begin no earlier than 10 a.m., and each notice shows the earliest time for that property. A sale must begin within three hours after that time.

Bidding is a live auction by a substitute trustee, for cash or its close equivalent. The lender usually enters a credit bid, and investors decide on the spot whether there's margin. It's a tough place to get full value for a house. If nobody beats the lender, the property goes back to the bank, and you become an occupant who'll likely get an eviction notice soon after.

A last-minute lawsuit and restraining order can sometimes stop a sale. In a Rule 736 case, a separate lawsuit filed by 5 p.m. on the Monday before the sale automatically stays the proceeding. Lawyers do this when there's a real defect, such as a bad notice or a lender that ignored its own promises. It isn't a free pass. Filing without grounds costs money and buys only a month or two.

🚫 After the Sale: Texas Doesn't Give It Back

This is where Texas is harshest. After a mortgage foreclosure, there's generally no right of redemption. The sale ends your ownership, and there isn't a 10-day upset window or any court confirmation.

Two kinds of sales are different, and people often mix them up:

  • 🏛️ Property tax sales. Under Tax Code § 34.21, the former owner of a homestead can redeem within two years of the deed being filed, by paying the buyer's price plus a 25% premium in the first year or 50% in the second. Commercial property gets far less time.
  • 🏘️ HOA foreclosures. Under Property Code § 209.011, the owner can redeem within 180 days after the association mails notice of the sale.

If you've been told you've got a redemption right after a bank sale in Dallas, get that claim checked immediately. It's almost certainly about one of these other sales.

💸 Can the Lender Come After You?

On an ordinary purchase mortgage or refinance, yes. Texas allows deficiency suits, and Property Code § 51.003 gives the lender two years after the sale to file. You've got one key protection: you can ask the court to determine the home's fair market value on the sale date, and if that's higher than the sale price, you get an offset. If no one asks, the sale price controls, so you must raise it.

Your homestead does protect you from a lot in Texas, but not from the lender with a valid lien on that same house. It can, however, protect a replacement homestead from a deficiency judgment later. That's a question for your attorney. A sale that pays the loan in full ends the debt, and it's far cleaner than any of those arguments.

🧭 Your Options, in Honest Order

🔁 Reinstate or modify

Best if the problem is temporary

The cure letter tells you what you need to pay. Ask for a reinstatement figure in writing, and if you need a modification, submit a complete application as early as possible. Ask a free HUD-approved housing counselor to review it.

🪧 List the house

Best with 60+ days left

In much of Dallas, a sound house can sell quickly. Price it to sell, and tell your agent the date you must close by. Your first Tuesday goes on the listing plan. With time on your side, listing usually beats us by a wide margin, and we'd rather tell you that.

💵 Sell for cash

Best inside 30 days or on a rough house

When the notice is posted and a financed buyer can't close before the Tuesday, cash is often the only sale left. Foundation trouble, a failing roof or an occupant who won't cooperate can also rule out financed buyers. You'll pay for that speed in price.

⚖️ Chapter 13

Strong if you have regular income

A filing generally halts the sale, and you can cure arrears through a plan. If you're going this route, file well before the Tuesday, with a Dallas bankruptcy attorney's advice. Same-day filings work, but they leave no margin for error.

💵 Worked Example: A House in Oak Cliff

Suppose a house would bring $300,000 fixed up, needs $30,000 of foundation and roof work, and would list as-is at about $265,000. The payoff, with arrears and fees, is $175,000. Monthly holding cost is about $2,350 (below).

  • 🪧 Listing as-is, two months. $265,000, minus 6% commission ($15,900), $2,500 of title and closing costs, and two months of holding ($4,700). After payoff, about $67,000.
  • 💵 Cash sale in three weeks. Cash buyers typically pay 60-80% of repaired value. At 70%, that's $210,000. Minus $1,500 of closing costs and one month of holding. After payoff, about $31,000. Texas has no state transfer tax on deeds, so that cost doesn't come off either number.
  • 🔨 Auction. Say the lender credit-bids around the debt, and no investor tops it. You lose the $90,000 gap between the debt and the as-is value, and you walk away with nothing.

The listing wins by $36,000 if there's time. If your Tuesday is 18 days away, that listing isn't real, and $31,000 beats nothing. The calendar decides between those lines, not the size of the check.

🏠 Monthly Cost of Waiting in Dallas County

Property tax is the big Texas number. The City of Dallas rate has been near 70 cents per $100, Dallas County's adopted 2026 rate is about 25 cents, and school, hospital and college district rates sit on top of those. All-in, plan on something around 2% to 2.4% of taxable value without exemptions. Check your own bill with the Dallas Central Appraisal District. On $265,000, 2.2% works out to about $485 a month.

  • 💳 Mortgage interest, late fees and foreclosure fees accruing: about $1,250
  • 🏛️ Property tax: $485
  • 🛡️ Insurance: about $250, with hail-exposed Dallas roofs often higher, and a vacancy policy may run more
  • 💡 Utilities and upkeep: about $365, and a dead lawn draws a code notice

That's about $2,350 a month. Your homestead exemption also ends if you move out and claim another home, so a vacant house can see a higher bill next year.

📋 What To Do This Week

  1. Search your address. Check the Dallas County foreclosure notices and find your first Tuesday.
  2. Read your loan type. Check the deed of trust. Is it a home equity loan? If so, watch for a Rule 736 citation and its response date.
  3. Get two numbers in writing. Ask for reinstatement and payoff through the end of the month.
  4. Call a HUD-approved counselor. It's free or close to it, and they know the servicers.
  5. Get an as-is value. Ask a local agent how fast your house would sell and whether it would finance.
  6. Make the call on list or cash. Use the table above. If you're inside 30 days, a cash buyer may be the only sale left.
  7. Talk to a lawyer if the sale is close. Ask about notice defects, Chapter 13 and your deficiency exposure.

✅ Bottom Line

In Dallas, a foreclosure comes down to a date. Find your first Tuesday, count back, and let that number decide. With 60 days or more, list the house. With three weeks, a cash sale can still save your equity. After the sale, there's no redemption from a mortgage foreclosure in Texas, and on a regular loan the lender has two years to sue for the balance. If your loan is a true home equity loan, you've got a court step and strong protection from the shortfall. Don't wait for someone from the bank to tell you what day it is. Look it up.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

When are foreclosure sales held in Dallas County?

On the first Tuesday of the month, between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m., under Texas Property Code § 51.002. If the first Tuesday is January 1 or July 4, the sale moves to the first Wednesday.

Where are Dallas County foreclosure sales held?

The County Clerk says sales are held on the north side of the George Allen Courts Building, facing Commerce Street, below the overhang, unless the commissioners designate another spot.

How much notice do I get before a Texas foreclosure sale?

For a residence, at least 20 days to cure a default, then at least 21 days' notice of sale. That's a floor of 41 days, after federal rules that generally keep foreclosure from starting until you're more than 120 days late.

Can I get my Dallas house back after a foreclosure sale?

Generally not. Texas has no general right of redemption after a mortgage foreclosure. Redemption exists after property tax sales and HOA foreclosures, but those are different kinds of sales.

Does a Texas home equity loan foreclosure need a court order?

Yes. The Texas Constitution says a home equity lien may be foreclosed only by court order, usually through an expedited Rule 736 application. Your response is due the first Monday after the 38th day from citation.

Can a Texas lender sue me after foreclosure?

Usually yes on a regular mortgage, within two years under Property Code § 51.003, and you can ask for a fair-market-value offset. A true home equity loan is generally without personal liability unless there was actual fraud.

Can I sell my Dallas house after the notice of sale is posted?

Yes, until the sale. Closing must pay off the lender before the first Tuesday. Get a written payoff, and have the title company confirm the funds and get the sale cancelled in writing.

Does calling my servicer stop a Dallas foreclosure sale?

Not unless you get written confirmation that the sale is cancelled. Federal rules can protect a complete loss mitigation application filed in time, but a phone call doesn't stop a posted sale.

How can I tell if my house is posted for foreclosure in Dallas County?

Search the County Clerk's foreclosure notices, or the county's public search site for notices filed on or after February 24, 2026. Notices also go up in the George Allen Courts Building lobby.

Should I list or sell for cash before a Dallas foreclosure?

With 60 days or more and a house a lender will finance, listing usually nets more. Inside about 30 days, or on a house with foundation or roof problems, a cash sale may be the only one that can close in time.
