Selling a Co-Owned House in Iowa: A Sale Is the Default, and the Protections Only Apply If Someone Asks

Iowa Code § 651.2 says a co-owned property shall be partitioned by sale unless an owner files a request for partition in kind — and § 651.27 withholds the heirs-property appraisal and buyout rights from anyone who does not make that same request. Meanwhile Iowa's homestead rules can let a surviving spouse occupy the whole house for life. Here is how to use both. Or ask us a question, free.

Iowa rewrote its entire partition law in 2018. The old court rules were rescinded, the old chapter was repealed, and what replaced them — Iowa Code chapter 651 — starts with a sentence that decides most co-ownership disputes in this state before anyone argues about anything:

"A property subject to partition shall be partitioned by sale and the proceeds from the sale divided by the owners of the property unless one or more of the property owners files a request for partition in kind and the court determines partition in kind is equitable and practicable." (Iowa Code § 651.2)

Read that backwards and you have the whole Iowa strategy. The default is a sale. Keeping the land, dividing it, or getting the family protections written for inherited property are all things that happen only if an owner files a request. In Iowa, silence is a vote to sell the house.

🖊️ Nobody Protects the House Unless Someone Asks in Writing

Two separate protections in chapter 651 are opt-in, and both are missed constantly.

Partition in kind. Section 651.2 makes sale the rule and division the exception, and the exception requires a filed request. Even then the court has to find that dividing the property would be equitable and practicable — a realistic outcome for 80 acres in Hardin County, a very unlikely one for a house on a city lot in Cedar Rapids.

The heirs property subchapter. Iowa adopted its version of the heirs-property reforms as subchapter III of chapter 651, and section 651.27 is explicit about the trigger: "If a cotenant requests a partition in kind in an action to partition heirs property, the partition action shall proceed under the special provisions for partition of heirs property under this subchapter." No request for partition in kind, no subchapter III — which means no court-ordered appraisal under § 651.28, no 45-day cotenant buyout under § 651.29, and none of the great-prejudice factors in § 651.31 that weigh how long the family has owned the land and who has been paying for it.

That is a drafting choice with real consequences. In most heirs-property states the court must determine on its own whether the land is heirs property. In Iowa, an heir who answers a partition petition by saying only "I don't want to sell" has not asked for anything the statute recognizes, and the case proceeds as an ordinary sale.

⚠️ If you want to keep an inherited Iowa house, the words matter. File a request for partition in kind. That single filing is what opens the door to an appraisal, a buyout right at the appraised value, and a judge weighing your family's history with the property. Iowa counsel can file it with an answer; a phone call to the other side's lawyer does nothing.

💍 A Surviving Spouse Can Occupy the Whole House for Life

Before you treat an Iowa house as co-owned at all, check who has homestead rights, because Iowa's are unusually strong and they override the arithmetic of shares.

Under Iowa Code § 561.11, on the death of either spouse the survivor "may continue to possess and occupy the whole homestead until it is otherwise disposed of according to law." Section 561.12 lets the survivor elect to retain the homestead for life in lieu of their distributive share in the deceased spouse's real estate. And § 561.16 exempts the homestead from judicial sale absent a specific statutory exception.

So three adult children can inherit the remainder interest in a farmhouse while their stepmother elects to keep the homestead for life — and their "ownership" buys them no possession, no rent, and no practical ability to sell anything but a remainder. Section 561.18 adds that if there is no survivor, the homestead descends to the issue of either spouse under the rules of descent unless a will says otherwise, and § 561.19 keeps that inherited homestead exempt from the parents' antecedent debts and the heirs' antecedent debts, with an exception for Medicaid estate recovery under § 249A.53.

The practical move: get the election in writing from the probate file before anyone makes an offer on anything. A life occupancy changes what a share is worth by more than any repair estimate will.

✍️ Both Spouses Have to Sign to Sell an Iowa Homestead

Iowa Code § 561.13 is the signature rule that stops Iowa closings. A conveyance or encumbrance of, or a contract to convey or encumber, the homestead "is not valid, unless and until the spouse of the owner executes the same or a like instrument, or a power of attorney for the execution of" it. It does not matter that only one spouse is on the deed. If the property is the homestead and the owner is married, the non-owning spouse signs or the deed fails.

The statute's own exceptions are narrow: the non-signing spouse's interest was terminated by a dissolution decree or other court order; the right of recovery is barred by § 614.15; the encumbrance is a purchase-money mortgage as defined in § 654.12B; or a court sitting in equity finds that invalidating the conveyance would unjustly enrich the non-signing spouse. Subsection 2 allows a spouse holding only homestead rights and the surviving spouse's statutory share to relinquish those rights in an instrument, including a power of attorney under § 597.5, without joining the granting clause.

Two practical notes. First, Iowa defines the homestead by area, not value: under § 561.2 it cannot exceed one-half acre inside a city plat, and otherwise cannot exceed forty acres in the aggregate. That matters on an acreage, where part of the parcel may be homestead and part may not. Second, if a co-owner is unavailable — deployed, hospitalized, in custody — the recorded power of attorney is the fix, not a workaround at the closing table. Our guide on selling when an owner is in jail or prison covers how those documents actually get signed and notarized.

⏸️ You Cannot Partition While the Estate Is Doing Its Work

Iowa puts a hard pause on partition when a deceased owner's estate is open. Section 651.3 provides that if an entire interest in real estate was owned by a decedent whose estate is in probate or administration, a partition action shall not be brought until four months after the second publication of the notice of the appointment of the personal representative — and shall not be brought at any time while an application for authority to sell that real estate is pending in the probate proceeding.

That is a scheduling fact worth knowing in both directions. If you are the heir who wants out, you cannot shortcut the estate by filing a partition case the week after the funeral. If you are the heir who wants to keep the house, that four-month window after the second publication is your time to arrange financing, because nobody can file against you during it. And if the personal representative has applied for authority to sell the house, the partition court is closed to everyone until that application is resolved.

For how the probate side of an Iowa house works when there is no will at all, see what happens to a house when there's no will; for the family-dynamics version, when heirs can't agree on selling.

📑 What an Iowa Partition Case Can and Cannot Contain

Chapter 651 keeps these cases narrow on purpose. Section 651.4 requires the petition to describe the property and the plaintiff's interest and to name all indispensable parties under § 651.5 — which includes every owner of an undivided interest and every holder of a lien on all or part of the property. A defendant's answer must state the amount and nature of their interest, and may deny the interest of the plaintiff or, by supplemental pleading, of any other defendant (§ 651.6).

Then the unusual part. Section 651.7 allows a party to perfect or quiet title to the property, or to have any right connected to the property adjudicated, including a lien between the parties — and then says that apart from that, joinder of any other claim is not permitted and a counterclaim to a partition petition is not permitted. You cannot bolt a dispute about Dad's bank accounts, a loan between siblings, or a damages claim onto an Iowa partition case. Title questions, yes. Everything else, a separate lawsuit.

Iowa also reaches interests that do not exist yet: under § 651.5(3) the court has jurisdiction over an unborn person's contingent or prospective vested interest as a cotenant, must appoint a guardian ad litem for them, and the decree binds them as though they had been alive when it was entered.

👤 One Referee, Three Appraisers, and a 125 Percent Bond

Iowa runs these cases through a referee, not a commission. Section 651.12 requires the court to file an initial decree establishing everyone's shares and interests and to appoint one referee — more only if every owner agrees to a larger number. The same decree orders an appraisal or valuation and may direct a public or a private sale, and unless all owners agree on another method of valuation, it appoints three disinterested persons with knowledge of property valuation to appraise the property. The referee then files a report recommending how to complete the partition.

The referee's duties are concrete. For a partition in kind the referee takes an oath without bond, marks each proposed parcel with visible monuments, may hire a surveyor at the case's expense, and files a report with a plat and any recommended owelty payments; the court can approve, modify or reject it, or order a sale instead (§ 651.16). For a sale, the referee gives bond of 125% of the total sale price before conveying real property, recommends a public or private sale process, and conveys by a referee's deed that is recorded and valid against subsequent purchasers and all parties (§§ 651.18, 651.19). Appraisers, referees and attorneys the referee hires with court approval are paid reasonable compensation as part of the costs (§ 651.24).

📉 An Iowa Court Can Approve a Sale Below the Appraisal

This is where Iowa differs sharply from states with statutory price protections, and where sellers get hurt.

Section 651.18(8) says that after the hearing on a proposed sale the court may approve or disapprove it — and that "the court may expressly order a private sale of the property for less than the appraised value of the property." There is no two-thirds floor, no required reserve, no rule that the appraisal sets a minimum. The appraisal is information for the judge, not a shield for the owners.

The notice rules are thin, too. For a public sale, § 651.18(6) requires two publications at least six days apart in a newspaper of general circulation in the county, with the last publication at least seven days before a sale of real estate. The court can authorize additional advertising and an auctioneer, with those costs taxed to the case. Everything else — marketing, photographs, exposure — depends on what the referee proposes and the court approves. Real property is not conveyed until the court approves the sale and the price is paid in full (§ 651.18(9)), and if the court disapproves a sale, money and securities go back (§ 651.18(10)).

The lesson for an Iowa co-owner: do not assume the court will protect your number. Show up at the hearing on the referee's report with evidence of what the property is worth and what a normal listing would produce, because that hearing is the only place the price gets tested.

💸 Advanced Costs, Proportional Shares, and the Plaintiff's Attorney Fee

Section 651.22 makes the plaintiff advance all the costs of the action, then has those costs paid by all the parties in proportion to their respective interests. A cost created by a contest arising from the case is taxed against the losing contestant unless the court orders otherwise. On a partition in kind, costs are adjudged and collected as § 651.16(5)(c) provides — entered as a judgment against each owner for their apportioned share, as a lien on that owner's allotted parcel, with special execution available on demand of any interested person. On a sale, costs come out of the proceeds and are deducted from the shares of the parties taxed with them.

Then the fee rule that surprises defendants: § 651.23 provides that on partition of real property the court shall order a reasonable fee in favor of the plaintiff's attorney, taxed as costs. Not may — shall. The only relief is subsection 2: if the plaintiff is the losing contestant in a contest arising from the action, the plaintiff's attorney fees relating to that contest are not taxed as costs.

Practically, that means the co-owner who files is having their lawyer paid partly out of everyone's shares, and the co-owner who does nothing but object loudly is paying for it. Contest something real and win, or negotiate — those are the two ways to keep the fee from landing on you.

🧾 Liens, Taxes, and What Iowa No Longer Charges

Three money rules Iowa co-owners should have in front of them.

Liens. Under § 651.20, real property is partitioned by sale free of all liens except liens held against the entire property. A mortgage both owners signed survives as a claim on the whole; a judgment against one owner's interest is cut off and travels to the proceeds, where § 651.21 gives each party and each lienholder the same rights in the money they had in the property, subject to a prior charge for costs. Section 651.14 requires lien disputes to be decided before a partition in kind, though a sale and distribution to unaffected parties can go ahead first.

Inheritance tax. Iowa's inheritance tax has been repealed for deaths on or after January 1, 2025. For older deaths the phase-out rates may still matter, so an estate with a 2021–2024 date of death should be checked against the schedule in effect that year.

Transfer tax. Iowa's real estate transfer tax under § 428A.1 is 80 cents for each $500, or fraction of $500, of value above the first $500 — about $1.60 per $1,000. On a $160,000 house that is roughly $255. And where there is no consideration, or the instrument is corrective of title and says so, there is no tax at all, which is why a family deed between co-owners often costs nothing to record. A declaration of value goes to the county recorder with most transfers.

One absence to plan around: Iowa has not adopted a transfer-on-death deed for real estate. Iowa owners who want to avoid probate on a house use joint tenancy with right of survivorship or a trust, not a recorded beneficiary deed, and the practical effect is that more Iowa houses pass through probate and arrive in the hands of three or four co-owners with no instructions.

💵 A Waterloo House, Three Siblings, Four Numbers

Three siblings inherit a 1,400-square-foot house in Waterloo. Repaired value is about $160,000; it needs a roof, a furnace and a bathroom — call it $28,000 — so as-is value is near $118,000. Nobody lives there. One sibling wants to keep it and rent it out; two want cash.

The holding cost is the part families underestimate. Iowa taxes residential property on a rolled-back share of assessed value, and city levy rates in Black Hawk County are among the higher ones in the Midwest; a house in this range commonly runs $2,400 to $3,200 a year, and the homestead credit that helped the parents is gone the moment no owner occupies the house. Add a vacant-dwelling policy at $1,700 to $3,000 a year, utilities kept on through an Iowa winter so the pipes survive, lawn and snow removal, and you are burning $500 to $700 a month on an empty house.

🧭 Four Endings an Iowa Family Can Choose

🤝 A buyout at an agreed appraised value

Usually the best net

One appraiser everyone accepts, a refinance or family loan, and a deed. Often no transfer tax where the deed is a family conveyance without consideration under § 428A.1. No referee, no publication, no statutory plaintiff's fee.

🖊️ File a request for partition in kind, in time

The only way to keep land

Under §§ 651.2 and 651.27 this filing is what unlocks division, the heirs-property appraisal, and the 45-day cotenant buyout. On acreage it is realistic; on a city lot it rarely is, but it still buys you a valuation and a buyout window.

🏷️ List it with all signatures and the homestead cleared

Highest price

Confirm any surviving spouse's homestead election under § 561.12 and get spousal signatures under § 561.13 before you take listing photos. A clean title and a normal listing beat every court-run process in Iowa.

🙅 When You Should List and Ignore Us

If the house is structurally sound, all the owners will sign, and no one needs money inside six months, list it with a local agent. On the Waterloo numbers that is $10,000 or more per share better than selling to us, and we would rather tell you that than win a deal you regret.

If one sibling can qualify for a mortgage and wants the house, do the buyout. Iowa's transfer tax exemption for conveyances without consideration and the lack of any statutory fee award in a private deal make it the cheapest ending available.

And if a surviving spouse has elected a life occupancy of the homestead, do not sell a remainder interest to anybody — us included — until an Iowa attorney has told you what that interest is actually worth. 📋 Your Iowa Checklist This Week

  1. Pull the deed and the probate file. You are looking for the vesting language, any surviving spouse's homestead election under § 561.12, and whether a personal representative has applied for authority to sell.
  2. Find the second publication date of the notice of appointment. Section 651.3 blocks a partition case until four months after it, which is either your protection or your delay.
  3. Decide now whether anyone wants the property kept, and if so, have Iowa counsel file a request for partition in kind. Nothing in subchapter III happens without it.
  4. Confirm who must sign. If any owner is married and the property is or was the homestead, § 561.13 requires that spouse's signature or a power of attorney.
  5. Get the carrying numbers in writing from the county treasurer and your insurer: the current tax bill without the homestead credit, and a vacant-dwelling quote. That is the cost of another year of arguing.
  6. Make one written offer with a deadline. An agreed appraisal and a buyout price on paper is cheaper than the plaintiff's attorney fee § 651.23 will put on everyone's shares.

✅ Bottom Line

Iowa is a sale-first state. Section 651.2 sells the property unless an owner files a request for partition in kind, and section 651.27 withholds the heirs-property appraisal and buyout rights from anyone who does not make that same request. A court can approve a private sale below the appraised value, and section 651.23 orders a fee for the plaintiff's attorney out of the proceeds. None of that rewards waiting.

What Iowa gives families is on the other side of the ledger: a surviving spouse who can occupy the whole homestead for life, a homestead that cannot be conveyed without the spouse's signature, a four-month pause while an estate gets organized, and no inheritance tax on deaths from 2025 forward. Those are real protections, and they are reasons a family deal usually beats a court case here.

So get the homestead question answered, decide in writing whether anyone is keeping the house, and make one documented offer before somebody files. ❓ Iowa Co-Ownership Questions We Get

If one Iowa co-owner wants out, does the house have to be sold?

Usually, yes. Iowa Code § 651.2 provides that property subject to partition shall be partitioned by sale and the proceeds divided, unless one or more owners files a request for partition in kind and the court determines that dividing it is equitable and practicable. Sale is the statutory default in Iowa, and no owner has to prove the property cannot be divided.

Do I have to ask for partition in kind, or will an Iowa court consider it on its own?

You have to ask. Section 651.2 makes division available only where an owner files a request for partition in kind, and the court must then find it equitable and practicable. Objecting to a sale, or telling the other owners you do not want to sell, is not a request. Iowa counsel can file it with your answer.

When do Iowa's heirs property protections apply?

Only when a cotenant requests a partition in kind. Section 651.27 provides that if a cotenant requests partition in kind in an action to partition heirs property, the case proceeds under subchapter III, whose provisions control over the general rules. That subchapter is what supplies the court-ordered appraisal under § 651.28, the 45-day cotenant buyout under § 651.29, and the great-prejudice factors in § 651.31.

Can an Iowa court approve a sale for less than the appraised value?

Yes. Iowa Code § 651.18(8) allows the court, after the hearing on the referee's report, to approve or disapprove a sale and expressly permits it to order a private sale for less than the appraised value. Iowa has no statutory price floor, so the hearing on the referee's report is where an owner has to put in evidence of value.

Does my spouse have to sign to sell our Iowa house?

If the property is the homestead and the owner is married, yes. Section 561.13 makes a conveyance, encumbrance, or contract to convey or encumber the homestead invalid unless the owner's spouse executes the same or a like instrument or a power of attorney for it. Narrow exceptions exist for a dissolution decree, a purchase-money mortgage under § 654.12B, the bar in § 614.15, and unjust enrichment found by a court in equity.

Can a surviving spouse stay in an Iowa house the children inherited?

Often, for life. Section 561.11 lets the survivor continue to possess and occupy the whole homestead until it is otherwise disposed of according to law, and § 561.12 lets the survivor elect to retain the homestead for life instead of taking a distributive share of the deceased spouse's real estate. Heirs holding the remainder cannot force possession or a sale of the occupied homestead.

How soon after a death can an Iowa partition case be filed?

Not for a while. Under § 651.3, where a decedent owned the entire interest and the estate is in probate or administration, a partition action cannot be brought until four months after the second publication of the notice of appointment of the personal representative, and cannot be brought at all while an application for authority to sell that real estate is pending in the probate case.

Who pays the attorney fees in an Iowa partition case?

Everyone contributes to the plaintiff's. Section 651.23 requires the court, on partition of real property, to order a reasonable fee in favor of the plaintiff's attorney, taxed as costs; § 651.22 then has costs advanced by the plaintiff and paid by all parties in proportion to their interests. If the plaintiff is the losing contestant in a contest, their fees for that contest are not taxed as costs.

Are liens wiped out by an Iowa partition sale?

Partly. Section 651.20 provides that real property is partitioned by sale free of all liens except liens held against the entire property, so a mortgage both owners signed survives against the whole while a judgment against one owner's interest is cut off and follows the money. Section 651.21 gives parties and lienholders the same rights in the proceeds they had in the property, subject to a prior charge for costs.

Does Iowa charge inheritance tax or transfer tax when co-owners sell?

Iowa's inheritance tax is repealed for deaths on or after January 1, 2025, though earlier deaths can still fall under the phase-out schedule for their year. The real estate transfer tax under § 428A.1 is 80 cents per $500 of value above the first $500, roughly $1.60 per $1,000, and there is no tax where there is no consideration or where the instrument is corrective of title and says so.
