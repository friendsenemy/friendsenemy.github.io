Selling Your Share of a Co-Owned Colorado House: No Entireties Protection, a One-Page Severance Deed, and the Homestead Exception

Colorado stopped recognizing tenancy by the entirety in 2006, and its joint tenancy statute lets any joint tenant cut off survivorship by recording a deed to themselves. That gives a single co-owner in Colorado more room to act alone than in many states, with one notable exception for a recorded homestead. Here is what you can do without the other owner, and what still takes a signature or a judge. Or ask us a question, free.

You own part of a house in Colorado with someone you no longer agree with. It might be an ex-partner, a sibling you inherited it with, or a friend you bought with years ago. The other owner won't sell, won't buy you out, or won't answer. The first question in any state is what you can do on your own. Colorado's answer is more generous than most, and the reason is a pair of statutes most co-owners have never read.

🧾 Colorado Has No Tenancy by the Entirety, So Every Co-Owner Holds a Share

About half of U.S. states let married couples own a home as tenants by the entirety, a form of ownership that treats the couple as a single owner. In those states one spouse generally can't sell, mortgage or sever anything while the marriage lasts. Colorado isn't one of them. Under C.R.S. § 38-31-201, no conveyance of Colorado real estate, whenever it was signed, creates a tenancy by the entirety. A deed that tried to create one before July 1, 2006 is presumed to be a joint tenancy, and one signed after that date simply is a joint tenancy.

That matters in three practical ways:

  • 🔓 Married co-owners hold real, separable interests. A husband and wife on a Colorado deed are usually joint tenants or tenants in common, and either spouse's interest can be severed or conveyed under the same rules that apply to anyone else, subject to the homestead rule below and to any divorce court orders.
  • 🧑‍🤝‍🧑 There's no "wait for the divorce" lock on title. In entireties states a separated spouse often has to wait for a decree before the property can be divided. In Colorado, the title itself doesn't impose that wait, although a pending divorce case usually should, for reasons we explain later.
  • 🏦 Creditors can reach a spouse's interest. Without entireties protection, a judgment against one owner can attach to that owner's share. If your co-owner has judgments or tax liens, they follow the share and can complicate any sale.

⚠️ The warning that matters most: before you record anything or sign a contract to sell your interest, pull the full recorded history of the property from the county clerk and recorder, not just the vesting deed. A homestead declaration, a divorce decree, a lis pendens or a judgment recorded against one owner can each change what you're allowed to do alone, and none of them shows up on the deed you have in your drawer.

✂️ The Self-Deed: Ending Survivorship Without Asking Anyone

If your deed says "in joint tenancy," "as joint tenants" or uses the abbreviation JTWROS, the owners hold with a right of survivorship: when one dies, that owner's interest ends and the survivors keep the house. That can be the wrong result after a breakup or a family falling-out. Colorado gives each joint tenant a clean, unilateral fix. Under C.R.S. § 38-31-101(5)(a), a joint tenant can sever the joint tenancy by executing and recording a deed conveying their own interest to themselves as a tenant in common. The severance happens when that deed is recorded.

A few details make this more useful than it sounds:

  • 📝 No consent and no notice are required by the statute. The other owner doesn't sign. Many lawyers still recommend telling them, because a surprise severance tends to harden a dispute.
  • 👥 With three or more joint tenants, the others stay joint tenants among themselves. Only the severing owner's interest becomes a tenancy in common.
  • ⚖️ Filing for bankruptcy doesn't sever. Subsection (5)(b) says so expressly, which surprises people who assume a bankruptcy estate breaks survivorship.
  • 📌 It changes inheritance, not possession. After severance you can leave your interest in a will or sell it, but you don't gain any new right to move in, collect rent from the other owner, or force a sale. Those still run through agreement or a partition case.

Severance is often the first move for someone who has decided to sell their interest, because a buyer of a share wants a clear tenancy in common rather than an interest that might evaporate if the seller dies before closing. It's also a move to make with a lawyer when a divorce is pending, because a district court handling the divorce can enter orders about marital property, and acting around those orders can backfire.

🏠 The Exception: A Recorded Homestead Declaration Means Two Signatures

The severance statute has carve-outs, and the one that matters most for families is homestead. Colorado's homestead exemption arises automatically for an owner-occupied home, but an owner or the owner's spouse can also record a written homestead declaration with the county clerk and recorder describing the property and stating that it is being homesteaded. When that has been done, C.R.S. § 38-41-202(4) requires the signature of both spouses to convey or encumber the property, and § 38-31-101(5)(a) expressly cross-references that subsection.

In practice this comes up most often when one co-owner is married to someone who isn't on the title, or when a married couple is separating and one spouse recorded a homestead declaration to protect the house. If a declaration is on record, plan on needing the spouse's signature for a deed of the homestead interest, or a court order. A title company will find it; it's better if you find it first.

⚖️ Equal or Unequal? How Colorado Reads Your Fraction

Colorado's statute answers several questions that other states leave to case law, and the answers decide how much you actually own.

What the deed saysWhat Colorado treats it asWhat you can do alone
Two or more people named, no joint tenancy languageTenancy in common (§ 38-31-101(4))Sell, give or will your share
"In joint tenancy," "as joint tenants" or "JTWROS"Joint tenancy with survivorship (§ 38-31-101(1))Record a self-deed to sever, then sell your share
"As tenants by the entirety" (any date)Joint tenancy (§ 38-31-201)Same as joint tenancy
Two personal representatives or trusteesPresumed joint tenancy among the fiduciaries (§ 38-31-101(3))Act under the will, trust or probate court, not individually
Joint tenancy with stated unequal sharesValid, unequal joint tenancy (§ 38-31-101(6))Sever or sell the share the deed states

Two of these rows deserve a closer look. First, Colorado allows joint tenants to hold unequal shares. If the deed is silent, the interests are presumed equal, and that presumption is conclusive for outsiders who relied on the recorded deed without notice of anything different. Between the owners themselves it can be rebutted with evidence, and the statute keeps claims for partition and accounting open. So if you paid the whole down payment and the deed says nothing, you may be able to prove a bigger share against your co-owner, but a buyer of the property is entitled to treat the shares as equal.

Second, if the house came to you and your siblings through an estate, the way it was distributed matters. A personal representative's deed of distribution to several heirs, with no joint tenancy language, creates a tenancy in common. If the estate was never opened, the heirs may own the house but have no recorded deed showing it, which most buyers won't accept. 🤝 Sell the whole house together

Best price

If both owners will sign, a normal listing with a Colorado agent is almost always the best financial result. Spring is the strongest season along the Front Range, and a house that only needs paint and carpet does not need a cash buyer. Our honest advice: if you can get to "yes" on a listing, take that path, even if it means giving a little on how the proceeds are split.

💵 Buyout by the owner who wants to stay

Often the fairest

The owner living in the house frequently wants to keep it. The obstacle is usually financing: they need to qualify alone to refinance the existing loan and pay you. Get a written appraisal or broker opinion so the price starts from a shared number, and put a deadline on the refinance in writing.

🧩 Sell only your interest

Fastest exit

A tenant in common can sell an undivided interest to anyone. The buyer steps into your position, including the right to ask a court for partition. Because the buyer takes on a co-owner, carrying costs and possibly a lawsuit, you'll be paid a discount to your share of market value. This is a tool for a stuck owner, not a way to maximize price.

⚖️ Ask the court to partition

The backstop

Any co-owner can file a partition action in the district court for the county where the property sits under C.R.S. § 38-28-101. The court appoints commissioners, and if the land can't be divided without manifest prejudice, it can order a sale and pay commissioners, costs and attorney's fees from the proceeds. Our partition lawsuit explainer walks through how these cases usually run, and most settle before a sale date.

💵 What a Shared Colorado Springs House Costs to Hold, and What It Sells For

Take a three-bedroom ranch in Colorado Springs owned 50/50 by two former partners. Updated, it would sell for about $450,000. With a 20-year-old roof, original baths and some hail damage, a retail buyer might pay around $405,000 as it stands. Neither owner lives there now.

  • 🧾 Property tax. Colorado taxes homes on a small assessed percentage of actual value, so bills are low by national standards, roughly half a percent of value a year in much of El Paso County. Budget about $1,800 to $2,300 a year, or $150 to $190 a month.
  • 🛡️ Vacant-home insurance. A standard homeowner's policy may lapse coverage after a house sits empty. A vacancy policy on the Front Range, where hail claims drive pricing, often runs $150 to $300 a month.
  • 🔥 Utilities and upkeep. Heat has to stay on through the winter to protect the pipes. Plan on $150 to $275 a month for gas, power, water, snow removal and yard care.
  • 🏦 Mortgage. If there's still a loan, the payment is due every month no matter who is arguing with whom, and a missed payment damages both owners' credit.

Without a mortgage, the empty house costs the two owners roughly $450 to $765 a month together. Over a year of stalemate, that's $5,400 to $9,200 gone before anyone has paid a lawyer.

Now the three exits on that same house:

  • 📈 Listed together as-is: $405,000 less about 7 percent in commission and closing costs leaves around $376,000, or $188,000 each.
  • 💵 Sold together to a cash buyer: cash buyers usually pay 60 to 80 percent of the after-repair value, which here is $270,000 to $360,000. For this house we'd likely land around $315,000 to $335,000, closed in two to four weeks with no repairs, showings or hail claim to manage, or about $157,500 to $167,500 each.
  • 🧩 Only your half, sold while the other owner refuses: a buyer of a half interest is pricing in a negotiation or a partition case. Offers might run $110,000 to $145,000. That's far below $188,000, and it only makes sense when the other path is another year of carrying costs, legal fees and silence.

The point of the math: if a joint listing is possible, it wins. A cash sale of the whole house trades some price for speed and certainty. A sale of just your share is a last resort that ends your exposure, not a way to get top dollar.

📋 What To Do This Week as a Colorado Co-Owner

  1. 📄 Order the recorded deed and a full document search from the county clerk and recorder, and read exactly how the owners are named.
  2. 🏠 Check specifically for a recorded homestead declaration, a lis pendens, and any judgment or tax lien against either owner.
  3. ✂️ If you're a joint tenant and want your share to go to your own heirs, ask a Colorado attorney about recording a severance deed now, especially if you're in poor health or the relationship has ended.
  4. 🧾 Gather proof of who paid the down payment, mortgage, taxes, insurance and repairs. Colorado lets owners prove unequal contributions against each other.
  5. ✉️ Send the other owner a short written proposal with three choices: list together, buy me out at a stated price by a stated date, or I'll consider selling my interest.
  6. 🔎 Get a broker's opinion of value or an appraisal so every number starts from the same place.

✅ Bottom Line

Colorado gives a single co-owner unusual room to act. There's no tenancy by the entirety to lock married owners together, a joint tenant can end survivorship by recording a deed to themselves, and a tenant in common can sell an undivided interest without anyone's permission. The exceptions are a recorded homestead declaration, which brings both spouses' signatures back into the picture, and a pending divorce, where the court's orders come first. Everything else is negotiation, and when negotiation fails, a partition case in district court.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Does Colorado recognize tenancy by the entirety?

No. Under C.R.S. 38-31-201, no conveyance of Colorado real estate creates a tenancy by the entirety. A deed that tried to create one is treated as a joint tenancy, so married co-owners hold separable interests like anyone else.

How do I sever a joint tenancy in Colorado without the other owner?

Execute and record a deed conveying your interest to yourself as a tenant in common. Under C.R.S. 38-31-101(5)(a), the joint tenancy is severed when that deed is recorded with the county clerk and recorder. The other owner doesn't have to sign.

When do both spouses have to sign to sell a Colorado home?

When an owner or the owner's spouse has recorded a homestead declaration with the county clerk and recorder. C.R.S. 38-41-202(4) then requires both spouses' signatures to convey or encumber the property, and the severance statute cross-references that rule.

Does filing bankruptcy sever a Colorado joint tenancy?

No. C.R.S. 38-31-101(5)(b) says a joint tenant's bankruptcy petition does not sever the joint tenancy. Survivorship stays in place unless the tenancy is severed some other way.

Can Colorado joint tenants own unequal shares?

Yes. Colorado allows joint tenancy interests to be equal or unequal. If the deed doesn't say, they're presumed equal. That presumption binds outsiders who relied on the recorded deed, but co-owners can try to prove different shares against each other.

What does a Colorado deed to two people with no other wording create?

A tenancy in common. Under C.R.S. 38-31-101, a conveyance to two or more people creates a joint tenancy only if the deed declares it, and anything else is a tenancy in common that each owner can sell or leave by will.

Can I sell my share of a Colorado house if my co-owner refuses?

Yes, if you hold as a tenant in common, or after you sever a joint tenancy. The buyer becomes the other owner's new co-owner and can ask the district court for partition. Expect an offer below your share of market value.

Where is a partition case filed in Colorado?

In the district court for the county where the property is located, under C.R.S. 38-28-101. The court appoints commissioners and can order a sale if the property can't be divided without manifest prejudice, paying fees and costs from the proceeds.

Should I sell my interest during a Colorado divorce?

Usually not without your divorce lawyer's advice. The district court handling the divorce divides marital property and can enter orders about the house. Selling your interest to an outsider before the decree can conflict with those orders.

Should Colorado co-owners list the house or take a cash offer?

A joint listing wins on price whenever both owners will sign a listing agreement and the house shows well. A cash sale of the whole house makes sense when it needs major work or speed matters, and selling only one share is a last resort when the other owner won't cooperate.
