Detroit Homes Facing Tax Foreclosure, Probate or Repairs

Wayne County forecloses for unpaid taxes after about two years

For many inherited and vacant Detroit houses, the biggest threat is not a bank. It is the Wayne County Treasurer. Take the County's own timeline for 2022 taxes: the unpaid bill went to the County as delinquent on March 1, 2023, with a 4 percent administration fee and 1 percent monthly interest. On March 1, 2024 the property was forfeited, and interest rose to 1.5 percent a month, back-dated to the delinquency. The Circuit Court entered foreclosure judgments in March 2025, the last day to redeem was March 31, title passed to the Treasurer on April 1, 2025, and auctions followed in September and October. The same cycle repeats every year.

A house sold before that April 1 pays the back taxes out of the sale, and the owner or heirs keep the rest. After foreclosure, a former owner can still ask for leftover auction money, but only by filing a Notice of Intent to Claim Remaining Foreclosure Proceeds by July 1 of the foreclosure year, and what comes back is often much less than a normal sale would have produced.

SEV, taxable value and the uncapping after a sale

Michigan taxes property on its taxable value, not its market price. State equalized value (SEV) is half of true cash value. While the same owner keeps the house, taxable value can rise each year only by the lesser of inflation or 5 percent; for 2026 that multiplier is 1.027. After a sale, taxable value "uncaps" and resets to SEV the following year.

Worked example: a house with a true cash value of $110,000 has an SEV of $55,000. If its 2025 taxable value was $38,000, the 2026 figure is $38,000 × 1.027 = $39,026. If it sells to a new owner in 2026, the 2027 taxable value jumps to that year's SEV, roughly $55,000 here. Multiply each number by your millage to see the change. Opening the estate downtown

Estates are filed with the Wayne County Probate Court in the Coleman A. Young Municipal Center, 2 Woodward Avenue. Michigan's small estate affidavit is capped at $53,000 for deaths in 2026, and it cannot be used when the estate includes real property. A house therefore usually needs a probate case so a personal representative can sign the deed; see how unsupervised probate works for a Detroit sale.

When the lender, not the county, is foreclosing

Michigan mortgage lenders usually foreclose by advertisement, without a lawsuit. The notice runs weekly for four weeks, the sheriff's sale is a public auction, and afterward the owner typically has six months to redeem, which can be cut to 30 days if the house is abandoned. Selling during the redemption period is possible, but interest keeps adding up and the auction buyer may demand inspection access.
