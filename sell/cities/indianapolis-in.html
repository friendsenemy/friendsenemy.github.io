Indianapolis Property Problems: Probate, Foreclosure, Tax Caps

Indiana lets heirs move a modest house with a recorded affidavit

Unlike many states, Indiana lets real estate from a small estate change hands without a full probate case. When the gross probate estate, less liens and encumbrances, is no more than $100,000 for deaths after June 30, 2022, plus administration costs and reasonable funeral expenses, an affidavit can be recorded with the recorder of the county where the house sits. In Indianapolis that is the Marion County Recorder. The affidavit must give the legal description, name each person who inherits, state each share and explain how the shares were figured. Bank accounts and other personal property use a separate affidavit, available 45 days after death. Before insuring a sale, a title company will normally want the real estate affidavit on record and will check whether any mortgage, judgment or tax lien still has to be paid from the proceeds.

Larger estates go to the Marion Superior Court, Probate Division, which holds hearings at the Community Justice Campus, 675 Justice Way. A personal representative appointed there can sign a deed.

Indiana's circuit breaker caps put a ceiling on the bill

Indiana caps property taxes as a share of gross assessed value: 1 percent for a homestead, 2 percent for other residential property, including rentals, and 3 percent for nonresidential property. Levies approved by voters in a referendum can sit outside the caps, and the county auditor credits back anything above the ceiling.

Worked example: a homestead assessed at $210,000 can be billed no more than $210,000 × 1% = $2,100, before any referendum levies. If the owner dies and the house becomes a rental or stops qualifying as a homestead, its cap category can move to 2 percent, so the same house could be billed up to $4,200. That shift is one reason holding an empty inherited Indianapolis house costs more than families expect.

Foreclosure in Marion County takes a court and a sheriff

Indiana forecloses through the courts. For mortgages signed after mid-1975, a judgment cannot be enforced by sale until three months after the complaint is filed, unless the court finds the property abandoned. After the clerk certifies the judgment, the sheriff's sale has to be scheduled within 120 days in most cases. That stretch between filing and sale is when a listing or a direct sale can still pay off the loan. If the owner has died during the case, the heirs or the estate's representative usually have to be brought in before anything can be signed. More in our guide to selling before foreclosure.

When heirs disagree or one can't sign

The affidavit route works only when the heirs agree on their shares. When one refuses, the choices narrow to a probate case or a partition action, discussed in whether every heir has to agree to sell.
