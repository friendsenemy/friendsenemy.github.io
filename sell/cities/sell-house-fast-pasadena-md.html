Pasadena, MD: Selling an Inherited or Problem House in North County

Ask whether a cash buyer is even the right call before anything else. For a lot of Pasadena sellers it isn't. If the house is livable, the owners agree and there's no clock running, an agent listing it on the open market in northern Anne Arundel County will usually net more. This page is for the other cases.

Pasadena houses that end up needing a different kind of buyer

Pasadena is unincorporated, spread across the peninsula between the Patapsco River and the Magothy River, with Bodkin Creek, Rock Creek and a long list of smaller coves running into it. Much of the housing went up as summer cottages and modest postwar homes that were later winterized and added onto, sometimes without permits. Those layers are what tend to stall a conventional sale:

  • Additions or enclosed porches that were never permitted.
  • Older septic systems on small lots, or a pending public sewer connection.
  • Bulkheads and piers that need work and permits of their own.
  • Waterfront lots inside the Critical Area, where Maryland law limits building and clearing within 1,000 feet of tidal water.

None of that makes a house unsellable. It narrows the list of buyers who will close without a long list of repair requests.

Running the tax numbers for FY2027

There is no Pasadena town government, so no town tax. The FY2027 bill (July 2026 through June 2027) is the Anne Arundel County rate of $0.968 plus the state's $0.112, or $1.080 per $100 of assessed value.

The homestead credit after an owner dies

Anne Arundel limits yearly increases in an owner-occupant's taxable assessment to 2%. A parent who lived in a Pasadena house for thirty years may have been taxed on a number far below its market value. An heir who does not move in generally does not keep that credit, so the next bills can rise faster.

The estate side, briefly

Probate for a Pasadena owner is handled by the Register of Wills in Annapolis, with the Orphans' Court resolving disputes. A personal representative appointed there has the authority to sign for the estate. Maryland's small-estate process applies when the estate is $50,000 or less, or $100,000 if a surviving spouse is the only heir. If you're still sorting out who has the right to sell, start with our page on selling an inherited or probate house.
