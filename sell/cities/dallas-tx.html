Selling a Dallas House Through Probate, Debt or Divorce

Dallas County has three courts that hear nothing but probate

In many Texas counties, estates go to a county court that juggles other case types too. Dallas County has three statutory probate courts: the Probate Court (No. 1), Probate Court No. 2 and Probate Court No. 3, all on the 7th floor of the George Allen Courts Building at 600 Commerce Street. A Dallas estate is assigned to one of them, and each has its own presiding judge and associate judge, so the court number on your case decides whose calendar and procedures you follow.

Texas also allows a small estate affidavit when the deceased person's assets, not counting the homestead and exempt property, total $75,000 or less, and a judge approves it. The only real estate it can transfer is the homestead. A rental house or a vacant lot needs another route, such as probate or a muniment of title.

The 2025 homestead vote and a Dallas tax bill

Texas has no state income tax, so local governments rely heavily on property tax. A Dallas bill stacks separate rates from the county, the city, the school district and other local units, each charged per $100 of taxable value. In November 2025 voters raised the school homestead exemption from $100,000 to $140,000, and the total for homeowners who are 65 or older or disabled to $200,000.

Worked example, school portion: suppose the Dallas Central Appraisal District values a homestead at $360,000. School taxable value is $360,000 − $140,000 = $220,000. Every $1.00 of school rate per $100 equals $220,000 ÷ 100 × $1.00 = $2,200, so multiply by your district's actual rate from the bill. For heirs, the issue is what happens after the year of death, when no qualifying owner lives there and the full value can be taxed. Our page on the January 1 tax reset for inherited Dallas houses explains the timing.

Community property and the surviving spouse's homestead right

Texas is a community property state. A house bought during a marriage is often owned by both spouses even when only one name is on the deed, and the Texas homestead protects a surviving spouse's right to keep living there. In a divorce or after a death, that can mean a spouse has to sign who did not expect to. Read selling a Texas house during a divorce for the owelty and homestead pieces.

First Tuesday at the county

Texas foreclosures are usually non-judicial. For a residence, the servicer must send a notice of default giving at least 20 days to cure, then at least 21 days' notice of the sale. Sales take place on the first Tuesday of the month between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m. Once a sale notice arrives, any sale of the house has to close before that Tuesday.
