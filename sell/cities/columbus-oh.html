Columbus Houses With Estate, Lien or Sheriff Sale Problems

A Columbus house can leave an estate through a "real estate only" release

Franklin County Probate Court, on the 22nd floor at 373 South High Street, offers a route many heirs never hear about. When the only thing left in the deceased owner's name is an interest in real estate, that interest passes under the will or Ohio's intestacy statute, more than six months have gone by since the death and no creditor has filed a claim, the family can use the court's release from administration for real estate only. The court then issues a certificate of transfer, which moves title to the heirs so they can sell.

The general release limits are a gross estate under $35,000 when anyone other than a surviving spouse inherits, or under $100,000 when a spouse takes everything. A summary release also exists for very small estates where a relative paid the funeral bill. Anything larger needs a full administration with an executor or administrator.

Franklin County's 2026 value update and the 35 percent rule

Ohio taxes real estate on 35 percent of its appraised value. The Franklin County Auditor does a full reappraisal every six years, most recently in 2023, with an update at the midpoint; the 2026 update relies on arm's-length sales from 2023 through 2025 instead of a full exterior inspection of every parcel. The bill applies the effective rate from voted levies in your taxing district.

Worked example: a Columbus house appraised at $240,000 has a taxable value of $240,000 × 0.35 = $84,000. The Treasurer's bill multiplies $84,000 by the effective rate for that district. If the update lifts the appraisal to $270,000, taxable value rises to $94,500 and the bill follows, so heirs carrying a vacant house should budget for that change.

What happens at a Friday sheriff sale

Ohio forecloses through the courts. Once a judgment is entered, the Franklin County Sheriff sells the property online through Realauction, normally on Fridays at 9:00 a.m. Eastern. Bidding opens at two-thirds of the appraised value unless the court orders otherwise, and the official ad runs in the Columbus Dispatch for three consecutive weeks, starting five weeks before the sale. Those five weeks are often the last practical chance to sell and pay the lender yourself. Our page on selling to stop a Franklin County foreclosure covers the earlier steps.

Rentals and out-of-town heirs

Many Columbus estates include a rented house and heirs who live in other states. A tenant's lease generally survives the owner's death, so the rent, deposit and lease terms belong in the conversation from the start. Heirs can usually sign closing documents by mail before a notary instead of traveling.

Times to list instead

If the certificate of transfer is recorded, the house is in good repair and there is no sheriff sale date, an agent in Columbus will usually get a higher net price from a buyer with a mortgage.
