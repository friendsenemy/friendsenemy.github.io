Selling a Federal Hill Rowhouse: Ground Rent, Vacant Notices and Baltimore City Taxes

Old rowhouses south of the Inner Harbor come with old paperwork

Federal Hill is a Baltimore City neighborhood, built mostly as attached brick rowhouses on narrow lots just south of the Inner Harbor. Many of these houses are well over a century old, and the age shows up in more than the plaster. Two items that rarely come up outside Baltimore show up here often enough that every seller should check for them before signing anything: ground rent and Vacant Building Notices.

Ground rent

Some Baltimore houses sit on land that is technically leased from a ground rent holder, and the owner of the house pays a small yearly rent for the lot. It is usually a modest amount, but it has to be identified, paid up, and either carried forward or redeemed at closing. A title search will find it; a seller who didn't know about it can lose weeks sorting it out. Our article on inheriting a Baltimore house with ground rent or a vacant notice explains how each is handled.

Vacant Building Notices

If a house sits empty and unsecured, the city can issue a Vacant Building Notice. A notice can make the house harder to sell to a buyer using regular financing, and it can come with requirements and fines. Even in a sought-after neighborhood, a rowhouse that has been empty since the owner went into care or passed away can end up with one.

The city tax rate is the number that changes the math

Baltimore City has the highest property tax rate in Maryland. There is no separate county on top of it, but the city rate alone is high. On a rowhouse assessed at $285,000, the FY2027 rates (city $2.248 + state $0.112 = $2.360 per $100) come to about $6,726 a year, or about $560 a month. For an heir paying that on an empty house while the estate moves slowly, it adds up quickly.

The city's Homestead Tax Credit caps yearly increases in an owner-occupant's taxable assessment at 4%. Someone who lived in the house for decades may have a bill well below what a new owner will pay. A child who inherits it and does not live there generally does not keep that credit.

Probate in Baltimore City

Estates for city property are handled by the Register of Wills for Baltimore City, downtown. The personal representative named there has authority to sell, sometimes with Orphans' Court approval depending on the will and the estate. Maryland's small-estate threshold is $50,000, or $100,000 if a surviving spouse is the only heir, but a Federal Hill rowhouse alone will usually be worth more than that. See selling a probate house in Baltimore for how the PR's powers work, and talk to a Maryland estate attorney about your specific estate.

Listing it may be the better move

Federal Hill draws buyers who want to walk to the harbor, the ballparks and the restaurants along Light Street and Cross Street. If the rowhouse is in decent shape, the title is clean, and the heirs agree, an agent who knows the neighborhood will probably get you a better price than a cash buyer. That is the honest answer, and we would rather you hear it from us.

A cash or partnership offer is more useful when the house has a ground rent or vacant notice problem, needs a full gut, has tenants, or belongs to siblings who can't agree on a plan.
