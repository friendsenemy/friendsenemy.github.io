Lexington Park Houses: Navy Orders, Worn-Out Rentals and Estates

The Base Sets the Rhythm Here

Lexington Park grew up around Naval Air Station Patuxent River, and the base still drives the housing market. Sailors, Marines, civilian engineers and contractors move in for a tour or a contract, and many move out again when orders or the work change. That creates a steady supply of houses that someone needs to sell quickly, from a distance, or after several years as a rental.

Lexington Park is not an incorporated town; it is an unincorporated community in St. Mary's County, so there is no town tax and no town government. Permits, code, taxes and tax sale all run through the county.

Leaving on Orders

When orders come, the choice is usually between listing, renting the house out and selling direct. Listing gets the highest price if you have time and the house is ready. Renting can work if you are prepared to manage from afar. A direct sale trades some price for certainty and speed, with a simple sale closing in as few as 7 days. Our article on PCS orders and selling a Maryland house compares the options.

The rental that wore out

Plenty of Lexington Park houses were kept as rentals when an owner left for another duty station. Ten years later the owner is dealing with turnover, repairs ordered over the phone, and a property manager who rarely visits. We buy houses with tenants still in them and handle the lease ourselves. See selling a rental you no longer want.

Running the Numbers on a St. Mary's County Tax Bill

For FY2027, St. Mary's County's rate is $0.8478 per $100 of assessed value, plus the state's $0.112, for a total of $0.9598. A Lexington Park house assessed at $260,000: $260,000 / 100 × $0.9598 = $2,495 a year, around $208 a month. That is modest compared with much of Maryland, but it adds up on a house that stays empty between tenants.

The local homestead credit cap is 3%, limiting how fast an owner-occupant's taxable assessment can rise each year. A house that becomes a rental, or passes to an heir living elsewhere, generally loses the credit.

Inherited Homes and the Leonardtown Courthouse

When a Lexington Park owner dies, the estate is opened with the St. Mary's County Register of Wills in Leonardtown, the county seat. Before the house can be sold, a personal representative has to be appointed, unless the estate qualifies for Maryland's small-estate process ($50,000 or less, or $100,000 if a surviving spouse is the only heir). Because so many local families have relatives spread across the country, heirs often have to sign from out of state. A Maryland estate attorney can sort out who needs to sign. We regularly buy from estates with heirs in several states.

When You Do Not Need Us

If the house is in good condition, you are not on a tight timeline, and everyone on the deed agrees, a local agent will usually get you a better price. Lexington Park has a deep pool of financed buyers, many using VA loans, and they pay retail for move-in-ready homes.
