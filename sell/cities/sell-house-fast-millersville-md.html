Selling a House in Millersville When Repairs or an Estate Stand in the Way

Unincorporated Millersville: County and State Tax Only

Millersville is not an incorporated town. It is a community in central Anne Arundel County, so there is no separate municipal tax bill and no town government to deal with. Every property here pays Anne Arundel County's rate plus the state rate, and county agencies handle permits, code complaints and the tax account. For a seller, that keeps the paperwork simpler than in Annapolis or another taxing municipality.

The FY2027 numbers are $0.968 per $100 for the county and $0.112 for the state, a combined $1.080. Take a Millersville house assessed at $420,000: the annual bill comes to about $4,536, or $378 a month. If the house is vacant after a move or a death, that money still goes out every month along with insurance and utilities.

The 2% homestead cap and what happens after a death

Anne Arundel County caps yearly increases in the taxable assessment of an owner-occupied home at 2% through the Homestead Tax Credit. That is one of the tighter caps in Maryland, so a long-time owner may be paying tax on a figure well below the current assessment. An heir who inherits the house and does not live in it generally loses that credit, and the gap between the capped figure and the full assessment can show up on the next bills.

Houses Needing More Work Than Anyone Planned

Many Millersville houses are split-levels, ramblers and colonials that have been lived in by the same family for decades. They are solid, but the original kitchens, panels, well pumps on some older lots, and roofs tend to come due at the same time. A buyer using an FHA or VA loan may not be able to close on a house with failing systems. We wrote about this in more detail in selling an Anne Arundel County home that needs major repairs.

Pages of Purpose LLC, run by Dustin Ray, looks at those houses as they stand. You can get a written cash offer, or a partnership offer if that leaves you with more. Which one makes sense depends on how much time and risk you want to carry.

Estates Are Handled in Annapolis

When a Millersville owner dies, the estate is opened with the Anne Arundel County Register of Wills in Annapolis. The personal representative named by the Orphans' Court is the person who can sign a contract to sell. If there is no will, Maryland law decides who the heirs are, and every heir's position matters. Maryland's small-estate process is available when probate assets are $50,000 or less, or $100,000 or less if the surviving spouse is the sole heir; a house in Millersville is usually worth more than that, so most estates here go through regular administration.

A Maryland estate attorney can explain the timing for your family. We can work alongside the personal representative and the attorney, and we can sign a contract that closes once the court has approved what it needs to approve.

Should You Sell This House to a Cash Buyer at All?

Often, no. If the house is in decent shape, the owners agree, and there is no deadline, a listing with a local agent will very likely bring more money than a cash buyer will pay. Millersville sits between Annapolis and Baltimore, and well-kept houses draw commuter buyers.

A direct sale is worth comparing when:

  • the house needs repairs you cannot or do not want to fund;
  • an estate, divorce or co-owner dispute makes a long listing risky;
  • tax, mortgage or lien deadlines are getting close;
  • you live far away and cannot manage showings and contractors.
