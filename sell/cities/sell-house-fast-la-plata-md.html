Selling a House Inside the Town of La Plata, Charles County's Seat

Two Governments, One House: La Plata's Town Tax

La Plata is an incorporated town and the seat of Charles County, so a house inside town limits answers to both the Town of La Plata and the county. The practical difference shows up on the tax bill. For FY2027 the rates are county $1.023, town $0.350 and state $0.112, a combined $1.485 per $100 of assessed value. A house just outside town limits skips the $0.35.

Worked through for a La Plata house assessed at $340,000: $340,000 / 100 × $1.485 = $5,049 a year, about $421 a month. The town's share alone is $1,190 of that. If the house is empty while an estate is settled or a divorce plays out, that money keeps leaving someone's account every month.

The homestead cap and who keeps it

The Homestead Tax Credit cap that applies here is 7% a year on growth in a homeowner's taxable assessment. It applies only while an owner lives in the house as a primary residence. Heirs who live elsewhere, or a spouse who has moved out, generally lose the credit, and the taxable value can rise sharply in later years.

The Register of Wills Is Down the Street

Because La Plata is the county seat, the Charles County Register of Wills and Orphans' Court are in town. Any estate with Charles County real estate is opened here, from Waldorf to Indian Head to Cobb Island. For La Plata families that saves a trip, but it does not shorten the process: a personal representative still has to be appointed before anyone can sign a deed. Maryland's small-estate route covers estates of $50,000 or less, or $100,000 when a surviving spouse is the sole heir.

We work with estates at every stage, including before the personal representative is appointed, so the family knows the numbers in advance. See our inherited and probate house page. For legal questions, talk with a Maryland estate attorney.

The Kinds of La Plata Houses We Usually See

Many of the calls we get from La Plata involve older homes near the town center or houses on larger lots on the edge of town that were owned by the same person for decades. Common threads:

  • An elderly parent moved to assisted living and the house sat
  • Siblings inherited and one wants to keep it, the others do not
  • A divorce where neither spouse can afford to buy the other out
  • Deferred maintenance: roof, HVAC, a septic system on lots outside the sewer area
  • Back taxes or a lien that has been building quietly

Our article on signs it is time to sell a Charles County home goes deeper into how owners decide.

Should a La Plata Owner Sell to a Cash Buyer at All?

Not always. A La Plata house in good condition, with owners who agree and no clock running, will usually net more by listing with a local agent. Buyers commuting toward Washington and families working at the county and the hospital keep the town's market active. If that describes your situation, list it.
