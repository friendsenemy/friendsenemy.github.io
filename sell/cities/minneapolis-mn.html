Selling a Minneapolis Home: TISH Reports, Hennepin Probate and Redemption

Before a single-family house, duplex or townhouse in Minneapolis can be shown to buyers, the owner needs a full Truth in Sale of Housing (TISH) evaluation. It is a city requirement, not a lender's, and it applies to sales by owner, agent-assisted sales, contracts for deed and other transfers. For an estate or a run-down rental, the TISH report is often the first time anyone writes down what is wrong with the house.

What the TISH Report Means for a Problem House

A city-licensed evaluator inspects the property and lists conditions, some of which become required repairs with a reinspection to follow. A buyer can sign an acknowledgement taking responsibility for those required repairs, which is how many as-is sales close. The report also includes an energy disclosure. Properties brought out of condemnation with a Certificate of Code Compliance, and new construction with a Certificate of Occupancy, are handled differently.

The practical takeaway: order the evaluation early. It tells you whether a traditional buyer with a loan is realistic, or whether the list of repairs points toward an as-is buyer.

Hennepin County Estates

Probate for Minneapolis is handled by Hennepin County District Court. Minnesota's small estate affidavit works only when the whole probate estate, after liens, is $75,000 or less, and at least 30 days have passed since the death. It collects bank accounts and personal property; it does not transfer a house. Summary administration through the court is available in narrower cases, such as when the only property is homestead or exempt property, or the gross estate outside the homestead is $150,000 or less.

If you are dealing with a parent's house, a divorce or a co-owner, check with a local attorney licensed in Minnesota before signing. Our guide on whether all heirs have to agree to sell covers the general rules.

Six Weeks of Notice, Six Months to Redeem

Most Minnesota foreclosures are by advertisement, which means outside court. The sheriff's sale date is published for six consecutive weeks, and the occupant is served notice about four weeks before the sale. The sale is the deadline to bring the loan current. After it, the usual redemption period is six months, during which the owner can stay in the house but must pay off the full amount or move out at the end.

Those six months are a real opportunity. A sale during the redemption period can pay off the sheriff's sale buyer and leave the remaining equity with you, instead of losing the house at the end of the period.

How Minneapolis Taxes Are Figured

Minnesota does not apply a rate straight to market value. The county first converts value into tax capacity using class rates, then applies the combined local rate. A homestead pays a 1.00% class rate on up to $500,000 of taxable value and qualifies for a market value exclusion if worth less than $517,200. A non-homestead house, such as a rental or an inherited home no one lives in, uses 1.25%.

Worked example: an inherited Minneapolis house valued at $300,000 and no longer homesteaded has a tax capacity of 300,000 × 1.25% = $3,750. That number is then multiplied by the combined city, county, school and district tax-capacity rate printed on the Hennepin County statement. Losing the homestead class after a death is one reason the bill can jump the next year.

A Fair Comparison: Listing vs. an As-Is Offer

If the TISH report shows routine items, the house is clean and you are not in a redemption period, a Minneapolis listing agent will usually get you more money than we will. A cash or partnership offer makes sense when the report lists major required repairs, the house is vacant and losing its homestead class, heirs disagree, or the redemption clock is running.
