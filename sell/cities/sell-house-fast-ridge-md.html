Selling a House in Ridge, Near the Tip of St. Mary's County

Ridge is a small unincorporated community near the southern tip of St. Mary's County, a few miles above Point Lookout State Park, with the Potomac River on one side and the Chesapeake Bay on the other. It's quiet, rural and mostly made up of single-family homes on wells and septic, some on creeks and coves. That mix of water and distance shapes who buys here and what can slow a sale.

Selling from a distance

A lot of Ridge houses change hands because an owner has died, moved to be near family, or been transferred. Families who grew up here often live far away now, and an empty house in southern St. Mary's is hard to manage from out of state: the lawn grows, a pipe freezes, someone has to check the place after a storm. Some owners are military families tied to NAS Patuxent River in Lexington Park, up the road, who receive orders and need to go.

Leonardtown handles the estate

When a Ridge owner dies, the estate is opened with the St. Mary's County Register of Wills in Leonardtown, the county seat, and the Orphans' Court there settles disputes. The personal representative it appoints is the one who signs to sell. Maryland's small-estate process applies when the estate is $50,000 or less, or $100,000 if the surviving spouse is the only heir. If heirs don't agree on selling, our page on what Maryland heirs can do when they disagree explains the choices before a partition case.

The FY2027 tax bill

Leonardtown is the only incorporated town in St. Mary's, so Ridge owners pay just the county and state rates. For July 2026 through June 2027 that's $0.8478 county plus $0.112 state, or $0.9598 per $100 of assessed value.

A Ridge house assessed at $310,000 owes $310,000 / 100 x $0.9598 = about $2,975 a year, or about $248 a month while it sits.

St. Mary's caps yearly increases in an owner-occupant's taxable assessment at 3% under the Homestead Tax Credit. An heir who doesn't live there generally can't keep that cap.

Water and land rules

Because so much of Ridge is near tidal creeks, the Potomac or the Bay, many lots are inside the Critical Area, where Maryland law limits clearing, additions and new impervious surface within 1,000 feet of tidal water. Septic systems close to the water may face tighter rules if they need to be replaced. Buyers using a loan will usually want well and septic inspections.

What tends to come up

  • Older homes that need roofs, HVAC or updating.
  • Septic systems that fail inspection.
  • Additions or sheds built without permits.
  • Back taxes on a vacant or inherited house.

Listing versus a cash offer

If the house is in good condition, the owners are on the same page and you have time, list it with a St. Mary's County agent; the open market will usually pay more. If the house needs serious work, the septic or well has failed, there are liens or back taxes, the heirs disagree, or a co-owner, divorce or incarceration makes signing hard, a cash offer or partnership offer from Pages of Purpose may fit better.
