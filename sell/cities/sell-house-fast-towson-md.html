Towson Houses in Probate: Selling From the County Seat Outward

The Estate File Is Already in Towson

Towson is the seat of Baltimore County, which means the Register of Wills and the Orphans' Court for the entire county are right here. If you inherited a house in Towson, the estate will be opened and supervised a short drive from the property itself. That is a real convenience compared with families who have to deal with a courthouse an hour away, and it matters, because nothing on an inherited house can be sold until someone has the legal authority to sign the deed.

Baltimore County also has no incorporated towns at all. Towson, Parkville, Catonsville and every other community in the county is governed by the county itself, so there is no extra municipal tax layered on top.

From Death Certificate to Deed: The Probate Sequence

  1. Find the will, if there is one, and file it with the Register of Wills in Towson.
  2. Get a personal representative appointed. The Register issues Letters of Administration, the document that lets that person act for the estate.
  3. Check the size of the estate. Maryland treats estates of $50,000 or less (or $100,000 when the surviving spouse is the only heir) as small estates with a shorter process.
  4. Keep the house insured, secured and taxed while the estate is open.
  5. Sell, with the personal representative signing, and distribute the proceeds as the will or Maryland intestacy law directs.

When there is no will, Maryland law decides who inherits, and it can produce a long list of heirs. Our article on what happens to a house when someone dies without a will explains that order. A Maryland estate attorney should answer any question about your specific family.

Heirs who will not sign off

Towson families are often spread out: one sibling in the old house, one in Pennsylvania, one in California. When they cannot agree on price, timing or whether to sell at all, the estate stalls. We buy in those situations and sometimes purchase one heir's share. See do all heirs have to agree to sell a Maryland house.

Carrying Costs While the Estate Is Open

Baltimore County's FY2027 rate is $1.100 per $100 of assessed value, plus the state's $0.112, for a combined $1.212. A Towson house assessed at $410,000 works out to $410,000 / 100 × $1.212 = about $4,969 a year, close to $414 a month. Probate in Maryland commonly runs many months, so that figure adds up before anyone sees a dollar from the sale.

The county's Homestead Tax Credit limits yearly increases in a homeowner's taxable assessment to 4%. It is tied to an owner who occupies the house. Heirs who do not live in it generally lose that protection, and the taxable assessment can climb to catch up with the full value.

Cash Offer, Partnership Offer, or a Towson Listing Agent

Plenty of Towson houses sell well on the open market. The neighborhoods near Towson University and the county offices draw steady buyers, and if the house is in good condition, the heirs agree, and the estate has time, a listing agent will almost always put more money in the family's pocket than a cash buyer. That is the honest answer, and we will say it on the phone.

Where we fit is the house that cannot easily go that route: a dated home a lender will not finance, an estate with a deadline, liens or back taxes, a relative living in the property, or heirs who will not cooperate. We put a cash offer in writing, and a partnership offer alongside it when that nets the family more. A simple sale can close in as few as 7 days after the personal representative has authority.
