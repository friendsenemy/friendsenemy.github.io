Selling a Canton Rowhouse: Ground Rent, City Taxes and Inherited Homes

Canton is part of Baltimore City, and that sets the rules

Canton is a waterfront neighborhood of Baltimore City, not a separate town, so every rule that applies to city property applies here. Its blocks of brick rowhouses run down toward the harbor, and many have been renovated while others still sit much as a long-time owner left them. Selling one usually comes down to three city-specific issues: the highest property tax rate in Maryland, ground rent on older deeds, and what happens if a house sits empty long enough to draw a Vacant Building Notice.

The tax rate is the first number to check

Baltimore City is the only local taxing authority here, so there is no separate neighborhood tax. For FY2027 the rate is city $2.248 + state $0.112 = $2.360 per $100 of assessed value.

On a Canton rowhouse assessed at $340,000, that is about $8,024 a year, roughly $669 every month. That is the carrying cost before water bills, insurance or any repair, and it keeps running while an estate is open.

The city's Homestead Tax Credit caps yearly growth in a resident owner's taxable assessment at 4%. A long-time owner may have been taxed on much less than full value. An heir who does not move in generally cannot keep that credit, which can push the bill up sharply after the house changes hands.

Ground rent and city notices

Many older Baltimore deeds are subject to a ground rent, a small yearly payment owed to whoever holds the ground lease. Ground rents have to be registered with the state, and an unpaid one has to be dealt with before a clean sale. A title search will show whether a Canton house has one.

If a house stays empty and falls into disrepair, the city can issue a Vacant Building Notice. That notice follows the property, adds requirements for the next owner, and makes many lenders walk away. Open housing code violations work the same way. Our article on inherited Baltimore houses, ground rent and vacant notices goes deeper.

Estates handled downtown

Baltimore City's Register of Wills and Orphans' Court sit in the Clarence M. Mitchell Jr. Courthouse. Maryland's small-estate process is available when probate assets are $50,000 or less, or $100,000 if the surviving spouse is the sole heir; a Canton house will nearly always push an estate past that, so expect a personal representative under regular administration. Read how a Baltimore probate sale works for the steps. For legal advice on a specific estate, use a Maryland attorney.

When you should not sell to a cash buyer

A renovated Canton rowhouse with a clean title draws plenty of buyers on the open market. If that describes your house, the owners agree, and you have time, list it with a good agent. You will very likely net more.

A direct sale is a better fit for houses with problems like these:

  • A Vacant Building Notice or open code violations.
  • A roof, rear wall or basement that a lender's appraiser will flag.
  • An unregistered or unpaid ground rent, or other title defects.
  • Heirs who disagree, or one who has stopped answering.
  • Unpaid city water bills or taxes that are piling up.
