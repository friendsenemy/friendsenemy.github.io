Selling a Portland Home That Comes With an Estate or Debt Problem

Oregon's $1 Million Estate Tax Line Reaches Ordinary Portland Families

Most states have no estate tax, and the federal exemption is far above what a typical family leaves behind. Oregon is different. The Oregon Department of Revenue requires an estate transfer tax return when the total value of everything a person owned at death is $1 million or more. A paid-off house in Portland plus a retirement account and a life insurance policy can reach that figure without anyone thinking of the family as wealthy.

For a seller, this changes the order of operations. The personal representative may need a date-of-death value for the house, may need to file Form OR-706, and may want the sale to close so there is cash to pay the tax. Get an appraisal or a broker's opinion of value early, and talk with an attorney licensed in Oregon or a CPA before distributing anything to heirs.

Multnomah County Circuit Court's Probate Department

In Portland, estates go through the Probate Department of Multnomah County Circuit Court, which also handles guardianships, conservatorships and trust cases. Oregon offers a simpler path, the small estate affidavit, when the estate is modest:

  • Personal property worth no more than $75,000
  • Real property worth no more than $200,000 in fair market value

Very few Portland houses fit under $200,000, so most families selling a parent's home will open a full probate and have a personal representative appointed before a deed can be signed. Our guide to selling an inherited house in Portland covers that process in more detail.

Why the Tax Bill Doesn't Track the Sale Price

Oregon taxes homes on assessed value, not market value. Under Measure 50, a home's maximum assessed value can grow only 3% a year, and the Multnomah County assessor notes that this is the only place a 3% limit applies. The bill itself can still rise faster because of new levies, bonds or exception events such as remodeling.

The county calculates the bill two ways and charges the lower result:

  1. Assessed value multiplied by the tax rate for your code area, plus special assessments.
  2. Real market value multiplied by the Measure 5 limits: $10 per $1,000 for general government and $5 per $1,000 for schools, plus items outside those limits such as bonds.

A worked example: a house with a real market value of $500,000 has a Measure 5 ceiling of $5,000 for general government ($500,000 × 0.010) and $2,500 for education ($500,000 × 0.005). If its assessed value is $260,000, next year's maximum assessed value is $267,800 ($260,000 × 1.03). When the rate-based figure for a category goes over its ceiling, the difference is removed. The county calls that compression.

120 Days Between Notice and a Trustee's Sale

Most Portland mortgages are trust deeds, which lenders can foreclose without a lawsuit. After a notice of default is recorded, ORS 86.764 requires the trustee's notice of sale to be served or mailed at least 120 days before the sale. Those four months are usually the best window for a homeowner to sell, pay off the loan and keep any remaining equity rather than letting the auction decide the price.

Co-Owners and Heirs Who Want Different Things

When siblings inherit together, or two unmarried partners own a house, one person cannot sell the whole property alone. Options include buying out the other owner, selling a share, or asking the court for a partition. Read selling a co-owned house in Portland before anyone files anything.
