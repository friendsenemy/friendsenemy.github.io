Selling a Hampden Rowhouse in Baltimore City

Hampden grew up as a mill village along the Jones Falls, and its housing still shows it: rows of small brick and formstone houses built for workers, many now a century old. Because Hampden is a Baltimore City neighborhood, owners deal with two issues most Maryland counties don't have, ground rent and the city's Vacant Building Notices, along with the highest property tax rate in the state.

Ground rent: check the deed before you price the house

Some Baltimore rowhouses are still subject to a ground lease, meaning the homeowner owns the building but pays a small yearly rent to whoever holds the ground. Maryland law requires ground leases to be registered with the State Department of Assessments and Taxation. An unpaid or unregistered ground rent can come up during the title search and hold up a closing. If you inherited a Hampden house and find a ground rent you didn't know about, it can usually be paid off or redeemed, but it should be dealt with before you sign a contract, not after.

When the house is empty

Baltimore City can issue a Vacant Building Notice on a house that is unoccupied and unfit, and a property under a notice is harder to sell to anyone using a regular mortgage. Fines can accrue, and a buyer generally has to bring the house up to code. Heirs sometimes don't learn a notice exists until a buyer's title company finds it. Our article on inherited Baltimore houses with ground rent and vacant notices goes into more detail.

What Baltimore City taxes add up to

Baltimore City is its own jurisdiction, so there is no separate county bill. For FY2027 (July 2026 through June 2027) the city rate is $2.248 and the state rate is $0.112, for $2.360 per $100 of assessed value.

On a Hampden rowhouse assessed at $240,000, that is $240,000 / 100 x $2.360 = $5,664 a year, or $472 a month. A vacant house sitting through a long estate can easily run up several thousand dollars in taxes before anything is sold.

The city's Homestead Tax Credit limits yearly increases in an owner-occupant's taxable assessment to 4%. An heir or landlord who doesn't live in the house generally doesn't qualify, so a bill based on a capped assessment can jump once ownership changes.

Probate in Baltimore City

Estates of Baltimore City residents go through the Register of Wills for Baltimore City, with the Orphans' Court handling contested matters. Maryland's small-estate procedure covers estates of $50,000 or less, or $100,000 when the surviving spouse is the sole heir. A modest Hampden rowhouse can sometimes fall close to that line, which makes the process faster. See our guide to selling a probate house in Baltimore.

Should you sell to a cash buyer at all?

Hampden has steady demand from retail buyers. If the house has been kept up, has no open notices and the family agrees, list it with a local agent; you will probably come out ahead. A cash buyer makes sense when the house needs a full rehab, has a Vacant Building Notice, has ground rent or lien problems, or when heirs, co-owners or divorcing spouses can't agree.

You can get a written cash offer, or a partnership offer if that leaves you with more. We don't have a Hampden office. A clean sale can close in as few as 7 days; one with title problems takes as long as the fixes take.

  • Bring the deed and any ground rent bills you have.
  • Note any notices or code letters from the city.
  • Know who the heirs are, even if they're not talking.
