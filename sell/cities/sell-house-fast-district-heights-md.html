Selling a House in District Heights: The City Tax, Estates in Upper Marlboro, and Older Homes

District Heights has its own city tax

District Heights is an incorporated city in Prince George's County, just outside the D.C. line off Pennsylvania Avenue. Being incorporated means the city levies its own property tax on top of the county rate, and that combined rate is noticeably higher than in the unincorporated areas around it.

Here's what that looks like on a house assessed at $260,000. FY2027 rates of county $0.923 + city $0.692 + state $0.112 = $1.727 per $100 come to about $4,490 a year, or about $374 a month. On a house sitting empty while an estate is sorted out, the city portion alone is roughly $1,799 a year.

The state tax table lists a 10% Homestead Tax Credit cap for District Heights, which limits how fast an owner-occupant's taxable assessment can rise each year. Heirs who inherit the house and don't live in it generally don't receive the credit.

Small brick houses from the postwar years

Much of District Heights was built as modest brick homes after World War II, often on slabs or with small basements. Many are now on their second or third owner, and some have been owned by the same person since the 1960s or 70s. Common issues when they come up for sale: original electrical panels, galvanized or old cast-iron plumbing, basement water, and roofs past their life. None of these make a house unsellable, but they decide whether a buyer with FHA or VA financing can close on it as-is.

If the house has structural movement or a cracked foundation wall, our article on selling a Maryland house with foundation problems explains how buyers price that in.

Estates are opened in Upper Marlboro

Prince George's County's Register of Wills and Orphans' Court sit in Upper Marlboro, the county seat. When an owner dies, the estate is opened there and a personal representative is appointed. The PR is the person who signs the contract and deed. Smaller estates, under $50,000 or $100,000 when a surviving spouse is the only heir, may qualify for Maryland's small-estate procedure. A Maryland estate attorney can tell you which applies.

Other situations we hear about here

  • A house in a deceased parent's name for years because no estate was ever opened.
  • An owner who is incarcerated and needs a power of attorney to sell.
  • Unpaid city or county taxes that put the house on the path to Prince George's tax sale.
  • A rental with a tenant who has stopped paying.

Where listing beats a cash offer

If your District Heights house is updated or close to it, all owners agree, and you have a few months, a local agent will likely get you a higher net price than a cash buyer. Buyers who want to be close to D.C. at a lower price than closer-in suburbs do look here.
