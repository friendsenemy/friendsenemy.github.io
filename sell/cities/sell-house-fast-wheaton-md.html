Selling a Wheaton House: Older Homes, Estates and Rockville Probate

When a Wheaton homeowner dies, the estate does not stay in Wheaton. It goes to the Montgomery County Register of Wills in Rockville, the county seat, and any dispute among heirs is heard by the Orphans' Court there. Wheaton itself is an unincorporated community, centered on the Wheaton Metro station on the Red Line and the Westfield Wheaton shopping center, with Brookside Gardens and Wheaton Regional Park on its edge. There is no Wheaton city government and no city tax.

Settling a Wheaton Estate Step by Step

  1. Find the will, if there is one, and file it with the Register of Wills in Rockville.
  2. Get a personal representative appointed. That person, not the heirs as a group, signs the deed.
  3. Check whether the small-estate process applies. Maryland allows it when the probate property is $50,000 or less, or $100,000 if the surviving spouse is the only heir. A Wheaton house almost always exceeds that.
  4. Keep the house insured and the taxes paid while the estate is open.
  5. Decide how to sell: listed, as-is to a cash buyer, or through a partnership arrangement.

If a parent is still living but can no longer manage the sale, a valid power of attorney may let a child sell without probate. Our guide on selling a parent's house with power of attorney in Maryland explains what the title company will check.

What Wheaton Owners Pay in Property Tax

Because Wheaton is unincorporated, the FY2027 rate is the Montgomery County rate of $0.6706 plus the state rate of $0.112, for $0.7826 per $100 of assessed value. A house assessed at $455,000 owes about $3,561 a year, close to $297 a month. Montgomery County bills can also carry other charges besides the property tax rate, so look at the actual bill before budgeting.

The Homestead Tax Credit in Montgomery County caps yearly increases in an owner-occupant's taxable assessment at 10%. It protects the homeowner who lives there. Once the house passes to an heir who lives somewhere else, or becomes a rental, the credit generally ends.

The Houses Themselves

Much of Wheaton's single-family housing went up in the years after World War II: brick ramblers, Cape Cods and split-levels on modest lots. Many have had one owner for decades. When they come to market after a death or a move to assisted living, they often need a new roof, updated electrical service, a new furnace, or work on a damp basement. Some have been rented for years and show the wear.

Divorce and co-owned houses

A house owned jointly by spouses who are separating needs both signatures, or a court order, to sell. Our article on selling a house during a divorce in Maryland covers the timing questions.

Listing Will Often Win Here

Wheaton is close to the Metro and to jobs in Silver Spring, Bethesda and Washington, and a house in decent shape usually draws strong interest from regular buyers. If yours is updated, the owners agree and there is no deadline, a listing agent will most likely get you more than any cash offer. We would rather say that than waste your time.
