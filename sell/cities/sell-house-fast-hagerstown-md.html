Hagerstown Houses With Back Taxes, Liens or an Estate to Settle

Hagerstown's City Tax Is Higher Than the County's

Hagerstown is an incorporated city and the seat of Washington County, and inside city limits the city's property tax rate is actually larger than the county's. For FY2027 the rates are city $1.057, county $0.803 and state $0.112, for a combined $1.972 per $100 of assessed value. That changes the math on holding an empty house.

Example: a Hagerstown house assessed at $190,000 owes $190,000 / 100 × $1.972 = $3,747 a year, about $312 a month. The city's share is $2,008, more than the county's $1,526. A family carrying a vacant house through a long estate or a repair project feels that every month.

The homestead cap

The homestead credit cap that applies here is 5% a year on increases in a homeowner's taxable assessment. Only an owner living in the house gets it; heirs and landlords generally do not.

When Taxes and Liens Pile Up

Unpaid property taxes in Hagerstown can eventually lead to a tax lien sale, after which the lien buyer may file to foreclose the owner's right of redemption and take the property. City water and sewer charges, code-enforcement fines and old judgments can add more liens. Selling before the process runs its course usually lets the owner keep the equity left over after paying them off.

We buy houses with these problems and pay the liens from the sale proceeds at closing. Read selling a Maryland house before tax sale and selling with liens or back taxes for how that works.

Older Housing in the City's Core

Hagerstown has a large stock of older homes, especially in and around downtown: brick rowhouses, twins and large older houses, some converted to apartments. Those houses often need work that is hard to finance: roofs, masonry, knob-and-tube wiring, lead paint, old boilers, basements that take on water. A retail buyer with an FHA or conventional loan may not get past the appraisal or inspection. You can get a written cash offer, or a partnership offer if that leaves you with more.

Estates Are Handled Right Here

As the county seat, Hagerstown is home to the Washington County Register of Wills and Orphans' Court. Estates for houses anywhere in the county, from Williamsport to Boonsboro to the farms near the Antietam battlefield around Sharpsburg, are opened here. The court appoints a personal representative, who then has authority to sell. Maryland's small-estate route applies to estates of $50,000 or less, or $100,000 when the surviving spouse is the only heir. Consult a Maryland estate attorney about your family's specific situation.

If your Hagerstown house is in solid condition, the owners agree and you have time, a listing agent will generally get you more than a cash buyer. The city's spot where I-70 and I-81 meet keeps buyers and investors coming through, and a clean house can attract a financed buyer.
