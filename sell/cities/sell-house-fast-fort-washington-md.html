Inherited a house in Fort Washington and don't know where to start?

If a parent or relative passed away owning a house in Fort Washington, the deed does not simply move to you. Someone has to open an estate with the Prince George's County Register of Wills in Upper Marlboro, get appointed personal representative, and only then can the house be sold. If there was no will, Maryland's intestacy rules decide who inherits, which often means several siblings now own one house together. We buy inherited houses directly from the estate or from the heirs, we can wait for letters of administration to issue, and we don't care whether the house is full of belongings or hasn't been touched in years. Our guide on selling inherited property in Maryland walks through the paperwork.

Behind on property taxes in Prince George's County?

Prince George's County sells tax liens on delinquent properties at its annual tax sale each spring. Once a lien is sold, the clock starts on the owner's right to redeem, and the costs stack up with interest and attorney fees. If you own a house in Fort Washington with unpaid taxes, the cleanest exit is usually a cash sale before the redemption window closes: we pay the taxes at settlement out of the purchase price, so you never write the check yourself. Our guide to selling a Maryland house before tax sale explains the timeline.

Going through a divorce?

When a Fort Washington house has to be sold as part of a divorce, listing it with an agent means months of showings, repairs, and two people who don't want to talk to each other negotiating every offer. A cash sale gives both sides a clean number, a firm closing date, and no contingencies. We work with both spouses or with either attorney, and we can close before or after the decree.
