Memphis Houses With Back Taxes, Probate or Foreclosure in Shelby County

A house inside Memphis city limits carries two separate property tax bills: one from the City of Memphis Treasury and one from the Shelby County Trustee. Falling behind on either one starts its own collection process, and sellers are often surprised at closing to find a city balance they thought was part of the county bill, or the reverse.

Two Tax Bills, One House

Tennessee assesses residential property at 25% of appraised value, and each government applies its own rate per $100 of that assessed value. The City set its 2025 rate at $2.58081 per $100. City bills become delinquent after August 31, and the City adds 1.5% interest on the first of each month after that.

Worked example for the city portion only: a Memphis house appraised at $120,000 is assessed at 120,000 × 0.25 = $30,000. Divide by 100 and multiply by 2.58081: 300 × 2.58081 = $774.24 owed to the City. The Shelby County Trustee bill is calculated the same way at the county's rate and is added on top. Check both balances before you list or sign a contract.

When Back Taxes Turn Into a Tax Sale

Unpaid property taxes in Tennessee are collected through a court lawsuit that ends in a public auction. The owner's right to redeem afterward depends on how long the taxes were behind:

  • One year for delinquency of five years or less.
  • 180 days for more than five but less than eight years.
  • 90 days for eight years or more.
  • 30 days when the home is vacant or abandoned.

That last line matters in Memphis, where many tax-delinquent houses are empty rentals or inherited homes nobody lives in. Redeeming means paying the taxes, penalties, costs and 12% interest on the buyer's purchase price, through a motion filed with the court. Selling before the auction is almost always cheaper than redeeming after it.

Shelby County Has Its Own Probate Court

Estates in Memphis go to the Shelby County Probate Court, a court with its own judges and its own clerk's office in downtown Memphis. Tennessee's small estate process covers personal property of $50,000 or less, but the house itself normally passes either by every heir signing the deed or through a personal representative with authority from a will or the court. If a parent's Memphis house has been left in a deceased owner's name for years, expect the title company to ask for proof of who the heirs are.

For estate or divorce questions, speak with a local attorney licensed in Tennessee. Our guide to what happens to a house when there is no will explains the general rules.

Tennessee's Short Foreclosure Notice

Most Tennessee mortgages are foreclosed outside court by a trustee. The notice of sale must be published at least three times in a local newspaper, with the first publication at least 20 days before the sale, and the borrower must be sent notice by certified mail on or before that first publication date. In practice, the first ad in the paper can be your only real warning, so a sale has to come together quickly. See our page on selling before a foreclosure auction.

Is a Cash Sale the Right Move for a Memphis House?

Not always. If the house is occupied, in fair condition, current on both tax bills and you can wait for a buyer with a loan, listing with a Memphis agent will usually net more. A cash or partnership offer is the better fit for a vacant house with tax-sale risk, a rental with tenants and deferred repairs, or an estate where some heirs live out of state.
