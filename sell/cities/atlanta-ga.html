Selling a Problem House in Atlanta: Fulton Probate to First Tuesday

Inherited an Atlanta house? Georgia may let the heirs skip a full estate

Georgia has no dollar-capped small estate affidavit that can move a house. What it offers instead is unusual: when the owner died without a will, the heirs can petition the probate court for an order that no administration is necessary. There is no value ceiling on that petition. The judge can grant it when every heir consents and the estate either owes nothing or its creditors agree. For most Atlanta homes the filing goes to the Fulton County Probate Court, whose Estates Division is at 136 Pryor Street SW. Part of the city lies in DeKalb County, and a house on that side is handled by DeKalb's probate court instead.

The weak point is the word "every." One heir who refuses to sign, or who cannot be located, sinks the petition, and the family then needs a full administration with a personal representative who has authority to sell. Georgia's narrow bank-deposit affidavit, capped at $15,000 per account, does nothing for real estate.

Lenders here sell on the first Tuesday, without a judge

Georgia mortgage lenders usually foreclose under the power of sale written into the security deed, with no lawsuit. The sale is held on the first Tuesday of the month at the courthouse, after the notice runs in the county's legal newspaper once a week for four consecutive weeks. At least 30 days before that date, the lender must mail the owner a notice naming the person or company with full authority to negotiate, amend or modify the loan. Call that contact right away. A signed sale contract plus a payoff letter ordered from the servicer before the auction is often the cleanest exit, because nothing slows the process once bidding starts on the courthouse steps. Our step-by-step on selling before a foreclosure auction lays out the order.

How Fulton County turns market value into a tax bill

Georgia taxes real estate on 40 percent of its fair market value as of January 1. That 40 percent figure is the assessed value, and it is multiplied by the millage of each taxing body on the bill: Fulton County, the school system and the City of Atlanta. One mill equals $1 for every $1,000 of assessed value.

A worked example with a placeholder rate: a house appraised at $400,000 has an assessed value of $400,000 × 0.40 = $160,000. Every 10 mills of combined millage costs $160,000 × 0.010 = $1,600 a year before exemptions. Use the real millage from your bill, not the estimate on the assessment notice, which is built on the prior year's rates. Homestead status matters as well. Fulton County has limited its own yearly increase on homesteads to the lesser of 3 percent or inflation since 2005, and later voter-approved floating homestead exemptions do something similar for many city and school levies. When a parent dies or moves out and the house stops qualifying as a homestead, heirs often see a jump on the next bill. Homestead applications are due April 1.

Relatives who end up owning it together

When several heirs take title and one wants out, Georgia partition law can give the others a chance to buy that share before any court-ordered sale; our page on co-owned Georgia houses and the 90-day buyout explains it. If an owner is incarcerated, the deed still has to be signed before a notary, which is covered in selling a Georgia house from jail.

When an Atlanta agent will net you more

If the house is in decent shape, the title is clean, the estate is settled and nobody faces a deadline, list it with a licensed Atlanta agent. A buyer using a mortgage will usually pay more than a cash buyer, and the gap often exceeds the commission.
