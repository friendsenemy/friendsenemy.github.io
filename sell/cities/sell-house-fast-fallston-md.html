Fallston Home Sellers: When an Agent Wins and When a Cash Offer Does

The Honest Starting Point for a Fallston Seller

Fallston is one of the quieter, more spread-out parts of Harford County, with larger lots and plenty of single-family houses. Houses like that, when they are in decent shape, tend to sell well through a local listing agent. So the first thing to say on this page is this: if your Fallston house is in good condition, everyone on the title agrees, and you are not up against a deadline, list it. You will almost certainly net more than with any cash buyer, including us.

The rest of this page is for the houses where that is not the case.

Where Fallston Sales Get Complicated

Wells and septic

Much of Fallston is outside public water and sewer service, so many homes rely on a private well and septic system. A financed buyer's lender may require water testing and a septic inspection. A failed drain field, a contaminated well or a system that was never properly permitted can derail a sale or force a costly replacement before closing. We price that into an as-is offer instead.

Foundations, additions and older systems

Houses built in the 1960s through the 1980s, often expanded over the years, can have settling, water in the basement, or additions done without permits. If an inspector has already flagged structural trouble, see selling a Maryland house with foundation problems.

Owners who cannot agree

Inherited houses in Fallston often go to several adult children. One may want to move in, one wants cash, one does not answer the phone. Maryland law gives co-owners options, including a partition action, but it is slow and expensive. Our guide to heirs who cannot agree on a Maryland house lays them out.

Estates Go to Bel Air, Not Fallston

Fallston is unincorporated; there is no town government. Harford County's Register of Wills and Orphans' Court sit in Bel Air, the county seat, and that is where an estate is opened. Estates of $50,000 or less, or $100,000 when the surviving spouse is the only heir, can use Maryland's small-estate procedure. Larger ones go through regular administration. Either way, the court-appointed personal representative is the person who signs the deed. A Maryland estate attorney can tell you which route fits your family.

Harford County Taxes on a Fallston House

Because Fallston has no town tax, the FY2027 bill has two parts: Harford County at $0.9779 per $100 of assessed value and the state at $0.112, for a total of $1.0899. On a Fallston house assessed at $480,000, that is $480,000 / 100 × $1.0899 = $5,232 a year, or about $436 a month while the house waits.

Harford's Homestead Tax Credit limits yearly increases in an owner-occupant's taxable assessment to 5%. It does not carry over to an heir who keeps the house as a second property or rental, so the bill can climb after the original owner is gone.
