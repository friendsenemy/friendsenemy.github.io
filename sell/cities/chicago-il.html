Selling a Chicago House Stuck in Probate, Foreclosure or Tax Debt

A Chicago house in a dead owner's name means the Daley Center

Illinois raised its small estate affidavit limit to $150,000 for people who die on or after August 15, 2025, and vehicles no longer count toward that total. That sounds generous until you reach the exclusion: if the person owned Illinois real estate in their own name, at any value, the affidavit cannot be used. Unless the house passed by joint tenancy or a transfer-on-death instrument, a Chicago home left in the deceased owner's name means a case in the Probate Division of the Circuit Court of Cook County, on the 18th floor of the Richard J. Daley Center, 50 W. Washington Street. The court appoints an executor or administrator, and that person is the one who can sign the deed.

The assessor's three-year cycle, and the city's turn

Cook County reassesses on a three-year rotation, and the City of Chicago was reassessed in 2024; its next turn is 2027. Residential property is assessed at 10 percent of the Assessor's market value. The Illinois Department of Revenue then applies a single countywide equalizer, which was 3.0355 for the 2024 tax year. For an owner-occupied home, the homeowner exemption removes $10,000 of equalized value.

Worked example: market value $300,000 × 10% = $30,000 assessed. $30,000 × 3.0355 = $91,065 equalized. Subtract the $10,000 homeowner exemption and $81,065 remains. Multiply that by the tax rate printed on your second-installment bill, which depends on where in the city the property sits. When the owner has died and nobody lives there, the exemption can fall off later bills, which raises the cost of carrying an empty inherited house.

Illinois foreclosure moves through a judge, on set windows

Illinois forecloses mortgages in court. After being served, a homeowner has 90 days to reinstate by paying what is past due plus costs. The redemption period, the time to pay off the full balance, lasts until the later of seven months after service or three months after the judgment, and the judicial sale cannot take place until it ends. Notice of the sale must be published for at least three consecutive weeks. That schedule usually gives a Chicago owner months to sell on the open market or to a direct buyer, as long as the contract closes before the sale date. Two-flats, siblings and a co-owner who won't sign

Chicago's two-flats and three-flats mean an inherited building often comes with tenants and several heirs at once. Rents, security deposits and leases all have to be sorted out alongside the estate. If one co-owner refuses to sell, read what to do when the other owner of a Chicago house won't sell.

Times a listing is the better call

When the house is in good condition, the estate has an administrator with authority to sell and you can wait for a buyer with financing, a Chicago listing agent will usually net more than a cash sale. We would rather tell you that than buy a property you regret selling. Cash and partnership offers fit best when a redemption deadline, heavy repairs or a family stalemate stands in the way.
