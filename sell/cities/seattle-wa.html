Selling a Complicated Seattle House: From Excise Tax to Probate

In Washington, the Seller Pays the Real Estate Excise Tax

The closing cost that surprises most Seattle sellers is the real estate excise tax. Washington's rule, WAC 458-61A-301, says the tax is imposed on the seller, and the state portion is graduated. From January 1, 2023 through December 31, 2026, the state rates are:

Portion of the sale priceState rate
$525,000 or less1.10%
$525,000.01 to $1,525,0001.28%
$1,525,000.01 to $3,025,0002.75%
Over $3,025,0003.00%

Seattle adds its own local tax: 0.25% that it calls REET I and a second 0.25% called REET II, or 0.5% in total. A worked example on an $800,000 sale:

  • First $525,000 × 1.10% = $5,775
  • Next $275,000 × 1.28% = $3,520
  • Seattle's 0.5% on $800,000 = $4,000
  • Total excise tax: $13,295, paid out of the seller's proceeds

Starting January 1, 2027 the rates stay the same but the tiers move up to $551,000, $1,551,000 and $3,051,000. Whatever offer you compare, ask whether it is quoted before or after this tax.

Both Spouses Sign: Washington Community Property

Washington is a community property state. RCW 26.16.030 says neither spouse or registered domestic partner may sell, convey or encumber community real property unless the other joins in signing the deed, and both must acknowledge it. A separated spouse, a spouse in a care facility, or one who is incarcerated still has to be accounted for, often through a power of attorney or a court order. Our guide to selling during a divorce covers the usual paths.

King County Superior Court's Ex Parte and Probate Department

Seattle estates are handled in King County Superior Court, where the Ex Parte and Probate department hears probate along with guardianships, trusts and similar matters. Washington's small estate affidavit is limited:

  • The estate must be worth $100,000 or less after liens.
  • More than 40 days must have passed since the death.
  • It is for personal property; the state's form says not to use it to transfer real estate.

That means nearly every inherited Seattle house needs a probate and a personal representative before it can be sold. Our probate and inherited home page explains how a sale fits into that.

Ninety Days From Notice to Trustee's Sale

Washington lenders usually foreclose deeds of trust without going to court. Under RCW 61.24.040, the notice of trustee's sale must be recorded and sent at least 90 days before the sale, or 120 days when the pre-foreclosure letter under RCW 61.24.031 was required. A borrower who received that letter has until 90 calendar days before the sale to be referred to the state's foreclosure mediation program. Selling within that window is how many owners keep their equity.

How King County Property Taxes Are Set

Washington uses a budget-based system. Each taxing district's regular levy can grow only 1% a year plus new construction, and the rate is that levy divided by the district's total assessed value. Your bill is your assessed value times the combined rate. King County reports about $8.4 billion in 2026 property taxes on roughly $920 billion of value, which works out to an average near $9.13 per $1,000. On an $800,000 assessment that average would be about $7,304 a year; your actual rate depends on your levy code.

When Listing With a Seattle Agent Pays More

If the house is in good condition, everyone who must sign agrees, and there is no trustee's sale date, a local listing agent will usually net you more than a cash buyer. We say so when that fits.
