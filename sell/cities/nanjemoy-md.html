Selling a Rural Nanjemoy Property: Wells, Septic, Waterfront Rules and Estates

Out past the end of the sewer line

Nanjemoy is a rural, unincorporated area in western Charles County, bordered by the Potomac River and cut by Nanjemoy Creek, home to a well-known great blue heron sanctuary. Most properties here are on large lots or acreage, and nearly all of them rely on a private well and a septic system rather than public water and sewer. For a seller, those two systems often decide the sale. A buyer with a mortgage usually needs a passing well water test and a working septic system, and a failed perc or a collapsed drain field can stop a financed deal entirely.

Land near the river and creeks adds another layer. Anything within 1,000 feet of tidal water is in Maryland's Chesapeake Bay Critical Area, where clearing, lot coverage and new construction are restricted. An old cottage on a waterfront parcel may not be replaceable with a bigger house, and buyers will price that in.

Family land and estates

A lot of Nanjemoy property has been in the same family for generations, sometimes passed down without a will or with the deed still in a grandparent's name. When that happens, every heir under Maryland law may hold a share, and some may be hard to find. Sorting that out goes through the Charles County Register of Wills and Orphans' Court in La Plata.

Maryland's small-estate process applies when probate assets are $50,000 or less ($100,000 if a surviving spouse is the sole heir). A modest house or a few acres can sometimes fall near that line, so check before assuming full administration. Our pages on selling vacant land and on vacant or distressed property in Southern Maryland cover common situations. For questions about heirship or a will, a Maryland attorney is the right resource.

Taxes on a rural Charles County parcel

With no town government in Nanjemoy, owners pay only the Charles County rate plus the state rate. For FY2027: county $1.141 + state $0.112 = $1.253 per $100 of assessed value.

For a home assessed at $235,000, the yearly tax is about $2,945, or about $245 a month. Taxes on unimproved land are lower, but they still need to be paid, and unpaid county taxes can lead to the property being sold at tax sale.

The Charles County Homestead Tax Credit caps yearly growth in an owner-occupant's taxable assessment at 7%. Heirs who do not live on the property generally do not receive it.

Listing or selling direct

If your Nanjemoy home has a sound well and septic, a clean title and owners who agree, a local agent can market it to buyers looking for space and quiet, and that will often bring the better price. Selling direct makes more sense when:

  • The septic has failed or the well does not test clean.
  • The title has gaps from generations of informal inheritance.
  • The house is a mobile home or has been empty for years.
  • Back taxes have built up on the parcel.
