Selling a Home or Land in Mechanicsville, Northern St. Mary's County

Farm Country in Northern St. Mary's County

Mechanicsville is an unincorporated area in the northern part of St. Mary's County, along the Route 5 and Route 235 corridors. It is farm country: tobacco barns, open fields, and a well-known Amish and Mennonite community nearby. Houses here usually sit on private wells and septic systems, often on a few acres, and some properties include outbuildings, old equipment or a second dwelling. Selling that kind of property is different from selling a house in a subdivision. Buyers' lenders may require septic and water testing, and rural parcels can have boundary, access or survey questions that a title company will want answered.

Pages of Purpose LLC, run by Dustin Ray, regularly looks at rural houses and land in Southern Maryland. Expect a written cash figure, and a partnership option when it would put more in your pocket.

St. Mary's County Taxes on a Mechanicsville Property

There is no town government in Mechanicsville, so only the St. Mary's County and state rates apply. For FY2027, the county rate is $0.8478 and the state rate is $0.112, for a combined $0.9598 per $100 of assessed value. On a house assessed at $335,000, the yearly tax comes to about $3,215, or $268 a month. Land with farm use may be assessed differently, so check the property's assessment record before you estimate.

The county's Homestead Tax Credit cap is 3%. That cap keeps an owner-occupant's taxable assessment from rising more than 3% a year. When the home goes to an heir who does not live there, the credit generally stops, and the taxable amount can rise toward the full assessment.

Who Sells Here and Why

  • Families settling an estate. Estates are opened with the St. Mary's County Register of Wills in Leonardtown, and the personal representative appointed by the Orphans' Court signs the sale.
  • Navy and contractor households. NAS Patuxent River in Lexington Park draws many residents to the county, and orders can force a sale on a short schedule.
  • Owners with a failing septic or well. Repairs may need county health department approval and can be expensive.
  • Heirs who want to sell land, not just a house. Our land page covers lots and acreage.

Small estate or regular estate

Maryland's small-estate process applies when probate assets are $50,000 or less, or $100,000 or less if the surviving spouse is the only heir. With acreage involved, most Mechanicsville estates end up in regular administration. A Maryland estate attorney can help with that, particularly when heirs are scattered or there is no will.
