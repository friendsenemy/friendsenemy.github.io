Philadelphia Houses With Tangled Titles, Back Taxes or Foreclosure

In Philadelphia, a common reason a house cannot be sold has nothing to do with its condition. The family has lived there for years, but the deed is still in the name of a grandparent or parent who died, and no estate was ever opened. The city calls this a tangled title, and reporting based on Pew research has put the number of affected Philadelphia properties at more than 10,000, worth over $1.1 billion combined.

Untangling a Title Through the Register of Wills

Estates in Philadelphia start at the Register of Wills, which probates wills and grants letters to an executor or administrator. Disputes, objections and court approvals go to the Orphans' Court Division of the Court of Common Pleas. Once someone has authority to act for the estate, a new deed can be prepared and recorded with the Department of Records in City Hall.

When several generations have passed, the title company needs every link in the chain: each owner who died, each estate, and each heir's share. The City's tangled-title information sheet points residents to Community Legal Services, Philadelphia Legal Assistance and the Register of Wills' Probate Deferment Initiative, which can defer probate fees and waive deed recording fees for low- and middle-income residents.

Pennsylvania's small estates petition is limited to personal property worth $50,000 or less; real estate is excluded from that process entirely, so a house still needs a proper estate or every heir's signature. For estate or divorce questions, talk with a local attorney licensed in Pennsylvania. If one heir wants to sell and others do not, read Pennsylvania partition and co-owner options.

The 4.578% Transfer Tax

Since July 1, 2025, Philadelphia's realty transfer tax is 4.578% of the price: 3.578% for the City and 1% for the Commonwealth. On a $150,000 sale that is $6,867 in total. Transfers to a spouse, children or grandchildren are exempt with supporting documents, and so is a transfer under a will. But a sale from an estate to an outside buyer is taxed. Which side pays is negotiated in the agreement of sale, so ask any buyer how they are handling it.

Real Estate Tax at 1.3998%

Philadelphia's real estate tax rate is 1.3998% of the Office of Property Assessment's value, split between the City (0.6159%) and the School District (0.7839%). The rate has not changed since 2016.

Worked example: a rowhouse assessed at $200,000 with no exemptions owes 200,000 × 0.013998 = $2,799.60 a year. An owner who lives there can apply for the Homestead Exemption, and income-qualified owners may also qualify for LOOP or a tax freeze. Owners behind on taxes can ask the Revenue Department about an Owner-Occupied Payment Agreement, which spreads past-due taxes over monthly payments with no down payment.

Foreclosure Diversion Before a Sheriff's Sale

Pennsylvania foreclosure goes through court, and Philadelphia adds a step. When a lender files on an owner-occupied home, the court schedules a conciliation conference under the Residential Mortgage Foreclosure Diversion Program, and the lender cannot take judgment until those conferences end. Homeowners are told to call the Save Your Home Philly Hotline first for a free housing counselor. The program can buy time, and that time can be used to refinance, modify or sell before a sheriff's sale.
