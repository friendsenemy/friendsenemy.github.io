San Antonio Homes With Estate, Tax or Lien Problems

When a Veteran's Total Tax Exemption Ends With the Owner

Veterans who own homes in San Antonio, the home of Joint Base San Antonio, may qualify for one of the most valuable property tax breaks in Texas. Under Tax Code § 11.131, a veteran rated 100% disabled by the VA, or paid at that rate for individual unemployability, pays no property tax at all on the residence homestead. The exemption covers the total appraised value.

What happens next depends on who inherits. A surviving spouse keeps the exemption if the spouse has not remarried and the house stays the spouse's homestead. Children or other heirs who do not live there do not. The first full tax bill can arrive while the family is still deciding what to do. A worked example using only the City of San Antonio's portion:

  • Appraised value: $280,000
  • City rate for tax year 2026: $0.56288 per $100
  • City tax: $280,000 ÷ 100 × 0.56288 = $1,576.06

Bexar County, the school district and other taxing units add their own rates on top of that, so the total bill is several times the city figure. If no one plans to move in, factor that cost into how long the heirs can hold the house.

Bexar County Has Two Statutory Probate Courts

Estates here are heard by Bexar County Probate Court No. 1 or Probate Court No. 2. Under Government Code § 25.0173, the county clerk sends odd-numbered cases to Court No. 1 and even-numbered cases to Court No. 2, and both courts have power to decide title to real property. That matters when heirs dispute who owns what.

The small estate affidavit and the homestead

Texas Estates Code Chapter 205 allows a small estate affidavit when at least 30 days have passed since the death and the estate, not counting the homestead and exempt property, is worth $75,000 or less. If the homestead is the only real property, title can pass under the affidavit once it is recorded in the deed records of the county. A buyer can rely on that recorded affidavit, but an heir left off it can still bring a claim against the heirs who received the sale money. Accurate heirship facts protect everyone.

Home Equity and Reverse Mortgage Foreclosures Need a Judge First

Most Texas foreclosures are nonjudicial. A residence owner gets a notice of default with at least 20 days to cure, then a notice of sale at least 21 days before a courthouse auction held on the first Tuesday of the month. But some loans follow a different track. Texas Rules of Civil Procedure 735 and 736 require the lender to get a court order before it can sell when the lien secures:

  • A home equity loan or home equity line of credit
  • A reverse mortgage
  • Certain property owners' association assessments

That court step adds time, and that time is often when an owner or estate can sell and keep the equity. Heirs of an owner who had a reverse mortgage are often on exactly this track.
