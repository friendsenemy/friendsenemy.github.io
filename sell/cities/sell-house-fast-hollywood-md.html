Complicated situations we handle in Hollywood

Most "we buy houses" companies want an easy house with one owner and a clean title. We specialize in the ones they walk away from. Here is what that looks like in Hollywood and the rest of St. Mary's County.

Owner in jail or prison?

You can sell a house in Hollywood while the owner is incarcerated. It takes a properly drafted Maryland power of attorney, a notary who can reach the facility (or a facility notary), and a buyer who has done it before. We handle the paperwork, coordinate with the facility and the family, and can direct proceeds wherever the owner instructs. See selling a house while incarcerated in Maryland.

House needs work you can't afford?

Roof, foundation, mold, a kitchen from 1978, a basement that floods when it rains — none of it stops us. We buy Hollywood houses as-is and price the repairs into the offer, so you skip contractors, permits, and inspection renegotiations. If the house has been sitting empty, we can also handle a hoarded or vacant property without you clearing it out first.

Co-owner or sibling won't agree to sell?

A surprising number of the Hollywood houses we buy are owned by two or more people who can't agree. One sibling wants to sell, one wants to keep it, one has gone quiet. Maryland law lets any co-owner sell their own share without the others' permission, and we are one of the few buyers who will purchase a partial interest. That often breaks the deadlock faster and cheaper than a partition lawsuit in St. Mary's County Circuit Court, which can take a year and eat a large chunk of the proceeds in legal fees. Read can a co-owner sell a house in Maryland for the details.

How a cash sale works in St. Mary's County

Tell us about the house using the short form below — address optional. We research the title, the estate status if there is one, and any liens on record with the county. Within two business days you get a written cash offer with no fees, no commission, and no repairs required. If you accept, we open settlement with a Maryland title company; simple sales close in as few as seven days, and probate or heir sales close as soon as the estate paperwork allows. If you don't accept, there is no charge and no follow-up pressure. Not sure the house is worth selling? Ask us anyway — we answer questions for free, and sometimes the right answer is to keep it.
