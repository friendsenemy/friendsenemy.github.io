Denver Property Problems: Probate Court, Public Trustee, Taxes

Denver estates go to a court that exists for nothing else

Across most of Colorado, district courts hear probate. Denver is the exception: Article VI of the Colorado Constitution created a separate Denver Probate Court with exclusive jurisdiction over estates, guardianships and conservatorships in the City and County of Denver. It sits at 1437 Bannock Street. If someone dies with a Denver house in their own name, the person who can sell it is the personal representative this court appoints.

Colorado's small estate affidavit, form JDF 999, has a limit that is adjusted every year ($82,000 for a 2024 death), but the form states plainly that it is not valid for transferring real estate. A Denver house will need a probate case unless it passed by joint tenancy or a beneficiary deed.

Foreclosure runs through the Public Trustee, not a judge

Colorado uses public trustees, government officials who hold deeds of trust and conduct foreclosure sales. In Denver the Public Trustee is part of the Clerk and Recorder's office, and its auctions are held online on Thursdays at 10 a.m. For a home, the sale must be set no fewer than 110 and no more than 125 days after the Notice of Election and Demand is recorded. An owner can file an Intent to Cure with the Public Trustee at no charge; filing at least 15 days before the auction date ensures the lender's attorney has to supply the cure figures in time. A sale that closes before the auction is the other exit; see how to sell before a foreclosure sale.

The two-year value cycle, with real Denver numbers

Denver reappraises every two years, in odd-numbered years. For tax year 2024 (paid in 2025), the Assessor's math went like this: start with actual value, subtract the $55,000 residential value reduction, multiply by the 6.7 percent residential assessment rate, then multiply by Denver's combined general mill levy of 79.202 mills.

Worked example on a $550,000 home: $550,000 − $55,000 = $495,000. $495,000 × 0.067 = $33,165 assessed value. $33,165 × 0.079202 = about $2,627 in tax. The legislature has changed the reduction and the assessment rate from year to year, so check the current figures on your notice of value before estimating.

One government wearing several hats

Because Denver is a combined city and county, the same Clerk and Recorder's office records your deed, keeps the foreclosure file and runs the Public Trustee auction. That makes it easier to pull the documents a buyer or title company needs, such as the recorded Notice of Election and Demand. The Public Trustee's office moved to 200 W. 14th Avenue at the end of 2025.
