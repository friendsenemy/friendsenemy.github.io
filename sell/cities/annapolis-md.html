Selling an Annapolis House: City Tax, Historic District and Waterfront Rules

Annapolis is Maryland's capital, the seat of Anne Arundel County and home of the U.S. Naval Academy, bordered by the Severn River and its creeks. For someone selling a house here, three local facts matter more than anything else: Annapolis is an incorporated city with its own property tax, the county's Register of Wills is right here in town, and much of the city lies within 1,000 feet of tidal water, which brings Maryland's Critical Area law into play.

City, county and state: how the Annapolis bill adds up

That is about $618 a month for a house assessed at $520,000. Because Anne Arundel applies a lower county rate to property inside the city, the city portion is actually the larger share here. On an empty house waiting on an estate, those payments are the cost of delay, along with insurance and utilities.

The Homestead Tax Credit cap that applies in Annapolis is 10%, limiting how much the taxable assessment on an owner-occupied principal residence can rise in a year. An heir who does not live in the house generally does not keep that credit.

Probate in the county seat

Anne Arundel County's Register of Wills and Orphans' Court are in Annapolis. For an estate worth $50,000 or less, or $100,000 or less when the surviving spouse is the only heir, Maryland's small estate process is usually available. Larger estates go through regular administration, and the appointed personal representative has the power to sell. Where heirs disagree, our page on whether all heirs must agree in Maryland covers partition and buyouts. A Maryland estates attorney can advise on any specific estate.

Historic district and waterfront limits

The older core of Annapolis is a historic district, and exterior changes to properties there go through the city's Historic Preservation Commission. Outside the core, many lots along the Severn and the creeks fall within the Critical Area, where impervious surface, shoreline buffers and tree clearing are regulated. For a buyer, that can mean a renovation takes longer to approve or an addition is smaller than hoped. For a seller with a dated house, it affects what an investor will pay.

Problems that make a standard listing hard

  • A house in the historic district that needs major exterior repairs.
  • Flood or water damage on a low-lying lot.
  • Liens, back taxes or an estate that has not been opened.
  • Co-owners or divorcing spouses who cannot agree on price.
