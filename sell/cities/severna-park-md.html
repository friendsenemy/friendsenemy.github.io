Selling a Severna Park House: Waterfront Rules, Estates and Honest Options

A peninsula between two rivers changes what a buyer will pay

Severna Park is an unincorporated community in Anne Arundel County, wrapped by the Severn River on one side and the Magothy River on the other, with countless coves and creeks cutting in between. That geography is the first thing a seller here should think about. Land within 1,000 feet of tidal water falls under Maryland's Chesapeake Bay Critical Area law, which can restrict how much of a lot can be covered, where an addition can go, and whether an old house can simply be torn down and replaced with something larger. A dated rancher on a creek lot can be worth a lot or a little depending on what the county will let the next owner build.

If you are holding a family house near the water that needs a full renovation, the question is less about the kitchen and more about the permits. Builders and cash buyers price that uncertainty in. A retail buyer with a mortgage often cannot wait for it.

No town tax here, but the county bill still adds up

Because Severna Park is not an incorporated town, owners pay only the Anne Arundel County rate plus the state rate. For FY2027 (July 2026 through June 2027) that is county $0.968 + state $0.112 = $1.080 per $100 of assessed value.

On a house assessed at $520,000, that works out to about $5,616 a year, or roughly $468 a month. That bill keeps arriving whether anyone lives in the house or not, along with insurance, utilities and lawn care.

Anne Arundel's Homestead Tax Credit caps the yearly rise in taxable assessment at 2% for an owner who lives in the home. That is one of the tightest caps in Maryland, so a long-time owner may have been paying tax on far less than the full assessment. An heir or a landlord who does not live there generally does not get that credit, and the bill can jump once the credit drops off.

When the owner has passed away

Estates for Severna Park homes go through the Register of Wills and Orphans' Court in Annapolis. Maryland allows a simplified small-estate process when the probate assets are $50,000 or less ($100,000 if the surviving spouse is the only heir), but almost any house here will be above that line, so expect regular administration with a personal representative who has authority to sign a deed.

  • Get the personal representative appointed before you sign any contract.
  • Find the deed and any mortgage or home equity line still recorded on the property.
  • Keep the house insured as vacant if nobody is living there; many standard policies change after a period of vacancy.

Our guide to selling an inherited house in Maryland walks through the steps in more detail. For anything involving a will contest or a divorce, talk with a Maryland attorney.

Should you list instead? Often, yes

Plenty of Severna Park homes sell well on the open market. If the house is in decent condition, everyone on the title agrees, and nobody is up against a deadline, a local listing agent will usually net you more than any cash offer. We would rather tell you that up front.

A direct sale tends to make sense when one or more of these is true:

  • The house needs major work that a lender will not finance, such as a failing roof, a cracked foundation or an old bulkhead.
  • Heirs live in different states and want one simple closing.
  • There are liens, back taxes or a tax sale certificate on the property.
  • Someone on the deed cannot or will not cooperate.
