Williamsport Homes: Probate in Hagerstown, Town Taxes and Your Options

Probate Starts in Hagerstown

Williamsport is a small incorporated town on the Potomac River in Washington County, where Conococheague Creek meets the river and the C&O Canal passes through. When someone who owned a house here dies, the estate is opened with the Washington County Register of Wills in Hagerstown, the county seat, and the Orphans' Court there supervises it. Nothing about the house can be sold for the estate until someone has authority to sign.

What the personal representative needs to sort out

  • Letters of administration from the Register of Wills naming who represents the estate.
  • Whether the will allows the sale of real estate, or whether every heir will need to sign off.
  • Whether the small-estate process applies: Maryland permits it when probate assets are $50,000 or less, or $100,000 or less if a surviving spouse is the sole heir.
  • A list of debts tied to the property, such as a mortgage, a home equity loan, unpaid water and sewer, or back taxes.

Town Tax Plus County Tax

Because Williamsport has its own town government, property owners pay a town tax in addition to Washington County's. The FY2027 rates (July 2026 to June 2027) are $0.803 per $100 for the county, $0.4742 for the town and $0.112 for the state, for a combined $1.3892 per $100 of assessed value.

Take a house assessed at $210,000. The bill works out to about $2,917 a year, or roughly $243 a month. During a probate that runs many months, that adds up, and it is on top of insurance and utilities for a house nobody lives in.

For an owner who lives in the home, the Homestead Tax Credit caps the yearly rise in the taxable assessment at 5% in Williamsport. Once the house belongs to an heir who doesn't occupy it, that cap usually no longer applies.

River Town Houses and What Buyers Ask About

Williamsport's housing leans older, and some lots near the river and the creek can be affected by flooding. Buyers and their lenders may want to know about flood zone status, flood insurance cost, foundation condition and past water in the basement. An older house that has been closed up for a season can also have mold or plumbing problems. Those issues don't stop a sale, but they shape who will buy and on what terms.

Where a Listing Agent Wins

If the house is livable, the heirs or owners agree, and no one is pressed for time, a local agent who knows Washington County will usually get you the highest price. That's true for most houses in decent shape.

A direct sale is more useful when there is no time or money for repairs, the estate has several heirs in different places, there is a tax sale or foreclosure threat, or a co-owner is unreachable.
