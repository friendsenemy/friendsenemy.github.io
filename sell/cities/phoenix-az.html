Phoenix Houses With Spouse, Estate or Foreclosure Problems

In Arizona, a Married Owner Usually Cannot Sell Alone

The first thing that surprises many Phoenix sellers is that Arizona is a community property state. Under A.R.S. § 25-214(C), both spouses must join in any transaction that disposes of an interest in real property. If you bought the house during the marriage, or your spouse has a community claim to it, expect the deed to need two signatures even when only one name appears on title. That matters when a couple is separated, when a divorce is pending, or when one spouse is in jail or will not answer the phone.

None of that makes a sale impossible. It means the paperwork has to account for the spouse early: a signed deed, a disclaimer, a power of attorney, or a court order in a divorce case. Sorting this out before you sign a contract avoids the stalled closing that happens when the title company asks about a spouse nobody mentioned. Our guide to selling a house during a divorce covers the common paths.

Maricopa County Probate and the New $300,000 Affidavit

Estates in Phoenix are handled by the Maricopa County Superior Court, through its Probate and Mental Health Department. Arizona raised its small estate limits in an amendment to A.R.S. § 14-3971 that took effect September 26, 2025:

  • Real property: up to $300,000 in value, after subtracting liens (it was $100,000).
  • Personal property: up to $200,000 (it was $75,000).
  • Timing: the court's self-help instructions for the real property affidavit require that at least six months have passed since the death.

Above those limits, or when relatives disagree about who inherits, a personal representative has to be appointed before anyone can sign a deed. We explain how the affidavit works for a Phoenix house in selling an inherited Phoenix house with Arizona's $300K affidavit.

The Trustee's Sale Clock Starts at Recording

Most Arizona home loans are secured by a deed of trust, so foreclosure normally happens outside of court. A.R.S. § 33-808 sets the timing:

  1. The sale date can be no sooner than the 91st day after the notice of sale is recorded.
  2. The notice must be posted on the property at least 20 days before the sale.
  3. It must be published once a week for four weeks, with the last publication at least 10 days before the sale.
  4. To stop the sale through the courts, an owner has to obtain a court order by 5:00 p.m. Mountain Standard Time on the last business day before the sale.

Selling during that 90-day window lets you pay the loan off and keep whatever equity remains. Our Phoenix trustee's sale guide walks through it week by week.

How a Maricopa Tax Bill Is Built: Limited Value Times 10%

Since Proposition 117 passed in 2012, Arizona property taxes are levied on a property's Limited Property Value, not its full cash value. The limited value can rise no more than 5% a year and can never exceed full cash value. A residence is then assessed at 10% of that value under A.R.S. § 42-15003. Here is a worked example:

  • Last year's limited value: $280,000
  • Maximum limited value this year: $280,000 × 1.05 = $294,000, even if full cash value jumped to $360,000
  • Assessed value: $294,000 × 10% = $29,400
  • Tax: $29,400 × the combined rate for your tax area, per $100 of assessed value

Rates differ by school district and city, so check the Treasurer's bill for the exact figure. The point for sellers is that a long-held Phoenix home often carries a limited value well below what it would sell for.

When a Phoenix Listing Will Net You More

If the house is in decent shape, both spouses agree, title is clean and you are not facing a deadline, a local listing agent will usually get you a higher price than any cash buyer.
