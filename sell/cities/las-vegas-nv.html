Las Vegas Houses With HOA Liens, Estates or Liens: What Nevada Law Means

In much of the Las Vegas Valley, the biggest threat to a struggling owner is not the bank. It is the homeowners association. Nevada gives an HOA lien a super-priority slice under NRS 116.3116, and the association can foreclose on its own, without going to court. A few months of unpaid dues can turn into a trustee's sale long before the mortgage company acts.

The HOA Super-Priority Lien

Unpaid HOA assessments, late fees and collection costs become a lien on the home automatically. Most of that lien ranks behind the first mortgage, but a portion covering up to nine months of regular assessments before the notice of default sits ahead of it. The Nevada Supreme Court held in 2014 (SFR Investments) that a proper foreclosure of that portion can wipe out the first deed of trust. The process runs outside court: a notice of delinquent assessment, a recorded notice of default and election to sell, a notice of sale, then the sale itself.

For an owner, the lesson is practical. Get a current ledger from the HOA's collection company, because the fees grow quickly. If you are selling, the title company will demand that balance at closing, and an HOA that has already recorded a notice of default is on a timetable.

Bank Foreclosure in Clark County

Most Nevada mortgage foreclosures are also nonjudicial. The trustee records a notice of default, then has to wait three months before recording the notice of sale. Owner-occupants receive information about the state's Foreclosure Mediation Program with that notice. You can bring the loan current until five days before the sale date. Those three months are usually the best window to sell and keep your equity.

Community Property and Clark County Estates

Nevada is a community property state. Property bought during a marriage is generally owned half by each spouse, no matter whose name is on the paycheck, and that affects both divorce sales and inherited houses. Talk with a local attorney licensed in Nevada before signing anything in a divorce or estate.

Estates are handled by the Eighth Judicial District Court in Clark County, where probate commissioners hear many matters. According to the court's own 2025 guide, an estate can be set aside without administration when its net value is $150,000 or less, and summary administration is available up to $500,000 after encumbrances. The affidavit route, $25,000 or less, cannot be used when the estate includes Nevada real estate. Those higher limits mean many Las Vegas houses with a mortgage can go through the shorter path.

How the Clark County Tax Bill Works

Nevada taxes 35% of a home's taxable value, and the rate is stated per $100 of that assessed value. The Clark County Treasurer lists 3.2782 for district 200, the City of Las Vegas, for fiscal 2026-2027.

Worked example: a home with a taxable value of $300,000 has an assessed value of 300,000 × 0.35 = $105,000. At 3.2782 per $100, the full tax is 1,050 × 3.2782 = $3,442.11 before any abatement. A primary residence can claim a partial abatement that limits the yearly increase to 3%, which often keeps an owner's real bill below that figure. The Assessor notes that a newly recorded ownership document removes the owner-occupied abatement, so heirs or buyers should expect the bill to be recalculated.

Listing vs. Selling As-Is in Las Vegas

If the house is clean, the HOA is paid and you are not facing a deadline, a Las Vegas listing agent will usually net you more than a cash buyer. A cash or partnership offer fits when time or condition is the issue: an HOA or trustee sale date, a vacant estate home, sun-damaged roofs and failing AC units that hold up financing, or co-owners in a dispute.
