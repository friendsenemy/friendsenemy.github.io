Silver Spring Sellers: Estates, Condos and Older Homes Near the DC Line

Silver Spring is not a city in the legal sense. It is a large unincorporated area of Montgomery County along the District of Columbia line, so there's no Silver Spring town government and no town property tax. Owners pay Montgomery County and the state, and estates go through the county's Register of Wills in Rockville.

Start with the probate question

Many Silver Spring sales begin with a death in the family. The estate is opened with the Montgomery County Register of Wills in Rockville, and the Orphans' Court resolves disputes. The personal representative appointed by the court signs the deed. Maryland's small-estate option is limited to estates of $50,000 or less, or $100,000 when the surviving spouse is the only heir; most Silver Spring houses exceed that, though a condo with a mortgage sometimes doesn't.

Common snags in Silver Spring estates:

  • Heirs spread across several states or countries who must sign or consent.
  • No will, so Maryland's intestacy rules decide who inherits.
  • A parent who added a child to the deed years ago, creating a co-ownership question.
  • A reverse mortgage that comes due after the owner dies.

If the heirs aren't on the same page, our guide on Maryland heirs who can't agree lays out the paths.

What it costs to hold a Silver Spring house in FY2027

For July 2026 through June 2027, Montgomery County's rate is $0.6706 and the state rate is $0.112, a total of $0.7826 per $100 of assessed value. With a house assessed at $480,000: $480,000 / 100 x $0.7826 = about $3,756 a year, or $313 a month, on top of utilities, insurance and any condo or HOA fees.

Montgomery's Homestead Tax Credit allows an owner-occupant's taxable assessment to rise no more than 10% a year. An heir who doesn't move in generally doesn't get it.

Housing stock and what holds up a sale

Silver Spring mixes older brick colonials and Cape Cods, garden apartments, high-rise condos near the Metro Red Line, and newer townhouses. Different problems show up in each:

Older detached houses

Original electrical, oil heat, basement water and outdated plumbing are typical. Buyers with loans often ask for repairs or credits.

Condos

Unpaid condo fees, special assessments or a building with pending repairs can make lenders hesitate. The association's documents matter as much as the unit.

Who should list instead

If your Silver Spring property is in reasonable condition, the owners agree, and there's no foreclosure or other deadline, list it with a local agent. The area's demand from Washington-area buyers means a ready house will usually sell for more on the open market than a cash buyer will offer.
