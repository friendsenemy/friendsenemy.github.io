Selling a Rural Pomfret House in Charles County: What to Know First

Wells, Septic and Acreage Change How Pomfret Houses Sell

Pomfret is an unincorporated community in Charles County, between the county seat at La Plata and the Indian Head peninsula. Much of the housing sits on larger lots than in Waldorf, and many properties rely on a private well and septic system rather than public water and sewer. For a seller, that has real consequences. Lenders and buyers often ask for a water test and a septic inspection, and a failed system can hold up or kill a financed sale. Replacing a septic field is a significant expense, and it usually needs county health department approval first.

Pages of Purpose LLC, run by Dustin Ray, buys rural houses where the well, septic, roof or foundation needs work. We put a cash offer in writing, and a partnership offer alongside it when that nets the family more.

Tax Math for an Unincorporated Address

Because Pomfret has no town government, only Charles County's and the state's rates apply. For FY2027 those are $1.141 and $0.112 per $100 of assessed value, together $1.253. A Pomfret house assessed at $365,000 carries a yearly bill of about $4,573, which is about $381 a month. If an estate is open and the house is vacant, those payments, plus insurance and electricity for the well pump, come out of the heirs' pockets or the estate account.

Charles County's Homestead Tax Credit cap is 7%, which limits how fast the taxable assessment on an owner-occupied home can rise each year. Once the house passes to an heir who does not live there, that credit generally stops. Missed payments can eventually lead to the county's tax sale, so it is worth keeping the account current while you decide what to do.

Military Moves and Long-Distance Owners

Pomfret is within commuting range of Naval Support Facility Indian Head, and some owners here are service members or civilian employees who receive orders and have to leave on a schedule. Others inherited a parent's house and live out of state. In both cases, managing a rural property from far away is difficult. Our guide to selling a Maryland house on PCS orders covers the timing questions.

Estates go through La Plata

When a Pomfret owner dies, the estate is opened with the Charles County Register of Wills in La Plata. The personal representative appointed by the Orphans' Court signs the sale. Maryland's small-estate process applies only when probate assets are $50,000 or less, or $100,000 or less if the surviving spouse is the sole heir, so most estates that include a house here go through regular administration. A Maryland estate attorney can explain your family's options, especially if there is no will.

Is a Direct Sale Better Than Listing?

For a well-kept house with a passing septic inspection, a clean water test and owners who agree, a local agent will usually bring a better price than any cash buyer. If that describes your property, list it.

A direct sale is worth comparing when:

  • the septic or well has failed or is likely to fail inspection;
  • outbuildings, old vehicles or debris need to be cleared from the land;
  • heirs disagree, or one owner cannot be reached;
  • a tax sale, foreclosure or relocation date is set.
