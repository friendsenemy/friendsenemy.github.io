Raleigh Homeowners: Selling a Problem House in Wake County

Wake County Revalues Property Again on January 1, 2027

Raleigh owners who held onto a house after the 2024 revaluation should know the next one is close. Wake County has moved from revaluing property every four years to a three-year cycle and then a two-year cycle, with new values effective January 1, 2027 and the following round on January 1, 2029. The county says property values rose 51% from 2020 to 2024, and that big jumps after long gaps caused large swings in tax bills.

For a family deciding whether to keep a vacant inherited house, rent it out, or sell it, more frequent revaluations mean the carrying cost will track the market more closely each year. That is worth putting into the math before the heirs agree to hold.

Reading a 2026 Raleigh Tax Bill

Wake County bills and collects taxes for the City of Raleigh. For the 2026 bills, the county rate is 53.71 cents per $100 of value after a 2-cent increase in the fiscal 2027 budget, and Raleigh adopted 37.2 cents per $100. A worked example for a house inside city limits with no special district:

ItemCalculationAmount
Assessed valueFrom the 2024 revaluation$400,000
Wake County$400,000 ÷ 100 × 0.5371$2,148.40
City of Raleigh$400,000 ÷ 100 × 0.3720$1,488.00
Total before feesCombined 0.9091 per $100$3,636.40

Unpaid bills become a lien on the property, and they have to be paid at closing from the seller's proceeds.

Estates Are Opened With the Clerk, Not a Separate Probate Judge

North Carolina does not have stand-alone probate courts. In Raleigh, estates are opened before the Wake County Clerk of Superior Court, whose estates staff appoint the executor or administrator and review accountings. The state's shortcut, collection by affidavit under G.S. 28A-25-1, is narrow:

  • It covers personal property only, up to $20,000 after liens, or $30,000 when the surviving spouse is the sole heir.
  • It can be used only after 30 days have passed since the death.
  • It does not transfer the house, although the affidavit must list each tract of real estate the person owned.

So a Raleigh house usually needs either a full estate with a personal representative, or a deed signed by every heir. When the heirs do not agree, read heirs who can't agree to sell a North Carolina house.

The Same Clerk Hears Foreclosures

Most North Carolina deeds of trust carry a power of sale, but the lender still has to go through a hearing before the Clerk of Superior Court in the county where the house sits. Under G.S. 45-21.16, notice of that hearing must be served at least 10 days ahead, or 20 days ahead if it is served by posting on the property. To authorize a sale, the clerk must find:

  1. A valid debt held by the party foreclosing
  2. A default and a right to foreclose under the deed of trust
  3. Proper notice to everyone entitled to it
  4. For home loans, that the pre-foreclosure notice and waiting periods were met
  5. That the sale is not barred because the owner is on military duty

A sale that closes before the hearing pays off the loan and keeps the foreclosure off the record.
