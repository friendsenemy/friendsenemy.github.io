Selling a Complicated House in Iowa: Estates, Heirs and Deadlines

The first thing many Iowa families learn after a parent dies is that the shortcut they heard about does not reach the house. In April 2026 Iowa approved House File 2660, which raises the small estate affidavit limit for a decedent's personal property from $50,000 to $100,000. Under Iowa's usual rule, new acts take effect on July 1, so the higher figure applies from July 1, 2026. But the affidavit still requires that the person who died owned no real estate. If the home was in Mom's or Dad's name alone, someone will almost always need to open an estate before it can be sold.

Iowa's $100,000 affidavit and the house it can't move

The affidavit is useful for bank accounts, vehicles and other personal property once 40 days have passed since the death. It is not a deed. A title company or buyer will want to see a court appointment, an order or a recorded chain that shows who has the right to sign.

Iowa also has no transfer-on-death deed law for real estate. Homes avoid probate here mainly through joint tenancy with right of survivorship, a revocable trust or a life estate deed set up while the owner was alive. If none of those happened, the estate route is the normal one.

The court that handles Iowa estates

Iowa does not have a separate probate court. Estates are filed with the district court sitting in probate (Iowa Code 633.10), in the county where the person lived. For smaller estates, Iowa Code chapter 635 offers small estate administration when the gross value of the probate assets is $200,000 or less. That route still goes through the court, but with fewer steps, and a home can be part of it.

  • Find the original will, if there is one, and the most recent deed.
  • Check whether the deed says joint tenancy with full rights of survivorship. If it does, the survivor may already own the house.
  • List every heir by name, including children of a sibling who has died.
  • Ask an Iowa probate attorney whether chapter 635 fits before you sign a listing or purchase agreement.

Brothers and sisters who disagree about the Iowa farmhouse

When several relatives inherit a house together, they become co-owners, and any one of them can ask a court to divide or sell it. Iowa adopted the Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act in 2018, and the heirs property rules now sit in Iowa Code chapter 651, subchapter III. They apply when the property passed among relatives and there is no recorded agreement among the co-owners about partition.

Under those rules the court has the property appraised and gives everyone 30 days to object to the value. Co-owners who did not ask for a sale then get 45 days to elect to buy out the one who did, at the court-set value times that person's share. If no one buys, the court must divide the land physically unless that would cause great prejudice to the owners as a group, weighing factors such as how long the family has owned it and whether it has sentimental value. A forced sale is the last stop, not the first.

That process takes months and lawyer time. Many families settle earlier: one heir buys the others out, or everyone sells to one buyer and splits the proceeds. Our pages on buying inherited houses when heirs disagree and how a partition lawsuit works walk through both paths.

An owner in an Iowa jail or prison

A person who is locked up still owns their house and can still sell it, usually through a power of attorney. Iowa Code 633B.105 says the power of attorney must be signed by the owner, or by someone else in the owner's conscious presence at the owner's direction, and it must be acknowledged before a notary public or another person allowed to take acknowledgments. The agent named in the document cannot be the notary.

Access to a notary inside a county jail or state prison depends on the facility, so the first call is to the facility's records or counselor office to ask how legal documents get notarized. Our guide on getting a notary in jail or prison lists the questions to ask.

Divorce: Iowa divides fairly, not automatically in half

Iowa is an equitable division state. Under Iowa Code 598.21 the court divides marital property in a way it considers fair, looking at the length of the marriage, what each spouse contributed and their earning capacity. Property one spouse inherited or received as a gift is generally that spouse's alone, unless keeping it out would be unfair to the other spouse or the children. If the house came from one spouse's parents, that matters.

Foreclosure: Iowa's one-year redemption after sheriff's sale

Iowa foreclosures run through the courts and end in a sheriff's sale. Iowa Code 628.3 gives the owner one year from the day of sale to redeem, with the first six months exclusive to the owner. Redeeming means paying the full amount, which few people can do after losing the house. Selling before the sheriff's sale is usually the better way to keep whatever equity is left.

Back taxes and the Iowa tax sale certificate

When property taxes go unpaid, the county treasurer sells a tax sale certificate to an investor. The owner generally has one year and nine months from the sale to redeem by paying the amount owed plus interest of 2% per month. After that, the certificate holder sends a notice that gives 90 more days. If no one redeems, the holder can get a tax deed. An heir who finds an old certificate on a parent's house should get the redemption figure from the county treasurer right away.

When an Iowa listing will put more money in your pocket

If the house is in decent shape, the owners agree, the estate is open or not needed, and nobody faces a deadline, list it with a local Iowa agent. Buyers paying full price will usually net you more than a cash or partnership sale. We are the better fit when time, family conflict, title problems or repairs make a normal listing hard to finish.

Quick answers for Iowa

Can I use Iowa's small estate affidavit to sell my parent's house?

No. Iowa raised the affidavit limit to $100,000 of personal property starting July 1, 2026, but the affidavit still requires that the person who died owned no real estate, so a house in their sole name usually needs an estate opened.

Which court handles probate in Iowa?

Iowa estates are filed with the district court sitting in probate in the county where the person lived. Estates with probate assets of $200,000 or less can use small estate administration under Iowa Code chapter 635.

Has Iowa adopted the Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act?

Yes. Iowa adopted it in 2018, and its rules in Iowa Code chapter 651 give co-owners 45 days to buy out a relative who asked for a sale at a court-set value.

How long do I have to redeem after an Iowa foreclosure sale?

Iowa Code 628.3 gives the owner one year from the day of the sheriff's sale to redeem, and the first six months belong only to the owner.
