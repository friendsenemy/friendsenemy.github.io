Selling a Complicated Texas House When the Paperwork Never Got Done

In Texas, the clock that matters most for an inherited house is four years. Under Texas Estates Code section 256.003, a will generally may not be admitted to probate after the fourth anniversary of the death unless the person offering it shows they were not in default. Families who put the will in a drawer and kept paying the taxes often find out about this rule only when a buyer's title company asks for it.

The Four-Year Will Rule and What Happens After It

If the will is filed within four years, a Texas court can usually admit it, and many simple estates use a muniment of title, where the will itself is recorded as the transfer document without naming an executor. After four years, the same statute says letters testamentary generally cannot be issued if the application was filed late. A late will may still be admitted as a muniment of title in some cases, but only with proof of a good reason for the delay.

The statute also protects buyers. A person who buys in good faith from the heirs after the four-year mark, without knowing a will exists, holds good title to what the heirs would have inherited without a will. In practice, that means the title company may treat the house as passing by intestacy, so every legal heir has to sign.

What to do this week

  • Find the original will, not a copy, and note the date of death.
  • Write down every child, grandchild of a deceased child and spouse, with phone numbers.
  • Pull the latest tax statement from the county appraisal district so you know what is owed.

The Homestead-Only Small Estate Affidavit

Texas has a small estate affidavit in Estates Code chapter 205, but it is narrow for houses. The estate, not counting homestead and exempt property, must be worth $75,000 or less, at least 30 days must have passed, and the judge has to approve the affidavit. Section 205.006 allows title to transfer under the affidavit only when the homestead is the only real property in the estate, and the approved affidavit must then be recorded in the county deed records.

A rental house, a vacant lot or a second home cannot pass this way. When the person who died owned the family home plus a piece of land in another county, the family usually needs an heirship proceeding or an administration in the county court or statutory probate court that has jurisdiction.

Texas also allows transfer on death deeds under Estates Code chapter 114, effective since September 1, 2015. If a parent signed and recorded one, the named beneficiary may be able to sell without probate. The statute says a transfer on death deed cannot be created through a power of attorney.

Heirs Who Will Not Sign, and Texas Partition

When several heirs own a house together and one refuses to sell, any co-owner can ask a court for partition. Texas adopted the Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act in 2017 as Property Code chapter 23A. When it applies, it adds an appraisal, a chance for the other owners to buy out the person who filed, and a preference for an open-market sale over a courthouse auction.

Many disputes settle before that point. One heir sells a share, the others buy it, or everyone agrees on a price once someone puts real numbers on paper. Our page on selling when heirs disagree explains how a buyer can work alongside the family's attorney, and our guide to heirship and partition when Texas heirs can't agree goes deeper on the court steps.

Community Property and the Spouse's Signature

Texas is a community property state, so property acquired during the marriage is generally presumed to belong to both spouses. During a divorce, a sale usually needs both signatures or a court order, and the decree should say who signs the deed and how the proceeds are split. When one spouse has died, the surviving spouse may already own half, which changes who the heirs are for the other half.

An Owner in a Texas Jail or Prison

A durable power of attorney in Texas must be acknowledged before an officer authorized to take acknowledgments, usually a notary (Estates Code section 751.0021). If the agent will sign a deed, section 751.151 requires the power of attorney to be recorded in the county clerk's office where the property is located, no later than 30 days after the deed is filed. Ask the facility how it arranges notary visits before mailing documents in; practices vary by jail and unit. Our guide to selling a house while in jail in Texas walks through the paperwork.

Two Texas Deadlines That Move Fast

First-Tuesday foreclosure sales

Most Texas home loans are foreclosed without a lawsuit. Property Code section 51.002 requires the sale to be a public auction on the first Tuesday of a month, between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m. For a home used as a residence, the servicer must first send a default notice by certified mail giving at least 20 days to cure, and the notice of sale must be posted, filed with the county clerk and mailed at least 21 days before the sale.

Tax deeds and the right of redemption

Texas sells property for unpaid taxes through a tax suit and sale, and the buyer receives a deed. Under Tax Code section 34.21, an owner whose residence homestead or agricultural land was sold to a private buyer can redeem within two years after the buyer's deed is recorded, paying a 25 percent premium in the first year or 50 percent in the second. For most other property the period is 180 days. Selling before the tax sale keeps that equity with the family.

When a Texas Listing Agent Will Net More

If the house is in good condition, the heirs or spouses all agree, title is clear and nobody faces a first-Tuesday sale or tax suit, list it. A local agent will usually get a higher price than any cash buyer, and we will tell you that. A cash or partnership offer makes more sense when the paperwork is the problem: a missing heir, a late will, a vacant house costing money every month or a sale date already posted.

Quick answers for Texas

Can a will be probated in Texas after four years?

Generally not. Texas Estates Code section 256.003 bars admitting a will after the fourth anniversary of death unless the applicant shows they were not in default, and letters testamentary generally cannot be issued on a late application.

Can a Texas small estate affidavit transfer a house?

Only the homestead, and only when it is the only real property in the estate. The estate must be worth $75,000 or less excluding homestead and exempt property, a judge must approve the affidavit, and it must be recorded in the county deed records.

Has Texas adopted the Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act?

Yes. Texas adopted it in 2017 as Property Code chapter 23A, which adds an appraisal, a buyout opportunity for the other co-owners and a preference for open-market sale.

How long do I have to redeem a Texas homestead sold for taxes?

Under Tax Code section 34.21, two years after the buyer's deed is recorded, with a 25 percent premium in the first year or 50 percent in the second. Most non-homestead, non-agricultural property has 180 days.
