Selling an Arizona House That Is Stuck: Estates, Trustee Sales, Liens and Heirs

A house worth up to $300,000 can pass by affidavit

Arizona has one of the more generous probate shortcuts for real estate. Under A.R.S. § 14-3971(E), if the deceased owner's Arizona real property is worth no more than $300,000 after subtracting liens, the heirs can use an affidavit instead of a full probate case. The catch is timing: the affidavit cannot be filed sooner than six months after the death.

The value is measured net of liens, so a mortgage helps you qualify. A $380,000 house with a $120,000 loan balance is under the line. A separate affidavit lets heirs collect personal property worth up to $200,000 after 30 days.

When the affidavit will not work

  • The equity is over $300,000.
  • Six months have not passed and a foreclosure or tax deadline will not wait.
  • The heirs disagree, or someone has already asked the court to appoint a personal representative.

In those cases most families use informal probate. A probate registrar, not a judge, issues the statement of informal probate, and the registrar can act once at least 120 hours have passed since the death. Our guides on using Arizona's $300,000 affidavit on an inherited Phoenix house and a Phoenix probate sale when the owner lived out of state go deeper.

Five questions to answer first

  1. What is the exact date of death? It controls both the 30-day and six-month waits.
  2. Whose names are on the recorded deed, and is there a beneficiary deed on file?
  3. Roughly how much is owed on the house, so you can estimate equity net of liens?
  4. Has anyone received a notice of trustee's sale or a tax lien letter?
  5. Do all the heirs agree on selling?

Beneficiary deeds

Arizona allows beneficiary deeds under A.R.S. § 33-405. The owner names who gets the property at death, and the deed has no effect unless it is signed and recorded with the county recorder before the owner dies. If you find a beneficiary deed in the county records, the house may not need probate at all.

Community property and divorce

Arizona is a community property state. In a divorce, A.R.S. § 25-318 tells the court to divide community and jointly held property equitably, though not necessarily in kind, and marital misconduct is not considered. A house bought during the marriage usually needs both spouses to sign a sale until a decree or written agreement says otherwise. Selling during the case can work well when the agreement spells out how the proceeds are divided.

Heirs who disagree: Arizona joined the heirs' property act in 2024

Arizona enacted the Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act in 2024. When inherited land qualifies, the court sets a value, gives the relatives who want to keep it a chance to buy out the one who wants to sell, and prefers a fair-market sale over a quick auction. That protects families, but it does not make a partition case short. If one heir wants out, buying that share can end the standoff sooner. See our page on what to do when heirs disagree.

Behind on the mortgage: the 91-day trustee's sale

Most Arizona homes are financed with deeds of trust, and foreclosures run through a trustee outside court. Under A.R.S. § 33-808, the sale date can be no sooner than the 91st day after the notice of sale is recorded. That is roughly three months to sell, refinance or reinstate. Read selling a Phoenix house before the trustee's sale for a step-by-step plan.

Back taxes: liens with a three-year wait

Arizona counties sell tax liens, not houses. Bidders compete by offering the lowest interest rate. The owner can redeem by paying the taxes, interest and fees. Under A.R.S. § 42-18201, the lien holder cannot file a lawsuit to foreclose the right to redeem until three years after the lien sale, and the lien lapses if no action is brought within ten years. Heirs who inherit a house with old liens still have time, but the balance grows every month.

An owner in jail or prison: one witness and a notary

Arizona has stricter signing rules for a financial power of attorney than many states. Under A.R.S. § 14-5501, the principal must sign and acknowledge it before a notary, and one adult witness must also sign and swear before the notary. The witness cannot be the agent, the agent's spouse or child, or the notary. Inside a jail or prison that means arranging two people, not one, so ask the facility how legal visits and notaries are scheduled before you send papers.

When a local listing will net more

If the house shows well, every owner agrees, and no trustee's sale or lien foreclosure is coming, an Arizona real estate agent will usually get you a higher price than a cash or partnership offer. Quick answers for Arizona

Can an Arizona house transfer without probate?

Often, yes. Under A.R.S. 14-3971, if the deceased owner's Arizona real property is worth $300,000 or less after liens, heirs can use an affidavit, but not sooner than six months after the death.

Does Arizona allow beneficiary deeds?

Yes. Under A.R.S. 33-405 an owner can name who receives the property at death, but the beneficiary deed must be signed and recorded with the county recorder before the owner dies.

How long does an Arizona trustee's sale take?

The sale date can be no sooner than the 91st day after the notice of sale is recorded, which gives an owner roughly three months to sell, refinance or reinstate.

What does an Arizona power of attorney need to be valid?

Under A.R.S. 14-5501, the principal must acknowledge it before a notary and one adult witness must also sign; the witness cannot be the agent, the agent's spouse or child, or the notary.
