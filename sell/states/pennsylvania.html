Selling a Pennsylvania Home Through an Estate, a Split Family or a Sheriff Sale

Pennsylvania is one of the few states that still taxes inheritances, and that tax puts a clock on almost every estate house. According to the Pennsylvania Department of Revenue, children and other lineal heirs pay 4.5 percent, siblings pay 12 percent, and most other heirs pay 15 percent. A surviving spouse pays nothing, and so does a parent inheriting from a child aged 21 or younger. The tax is due at death, becomes delinquent nine months later, and is discounted 5 percent if paid within three months.

That means a family that inherits a house often needs cash before the house has sold. This page covers that tax, the Pennsylvania courts that control an estate sale, and what to do when an heir, a spouse, a lender or a jail stands in the way. The Nine-Month Inheritance Tax Clock

Here is the math in plain terms. If three children inherit a house worth $240,000, the inheritance tax on the house alone is about $10,800 at 4.5 percent. Paying within three months knocks 5 percent off. Waiting past nine months adds interest. Families with no savings sometimes sell the house, or sell their shares, so they can pay on time.

A niece, nephew or friend who inherits pays 15 percent, which is a much bigger bill. If the will leaves the house to someone outside the immediate family, plan for that early.

Register of Wills and the Orphans' Court Division

A Pennsylvania estate starts at the county Register of Wills, which probates the will and issues letters to an executor or administrator. Disputes, objections and some court approvals go to the Orphans' Court Division of the Court of Common Pleas.

The $50,000 small estate petition leaves the house out

Under 20 Pa.C.S. § 3102, the Orphans' Court can settle a small estate on petition when the personal property is worth $50,000 or less. Real estate is excluded from that calculation and that process. So a house still needs a personal representative with letters, or every heir signing the deed.

When Heirs or Co-Owners Are Split

Pennsylvania runs partition through its Rules of Civil Procedure, Rules 1551 to 1574. One distinctive feature is that a court may order a private sale where bidding is limited to the co-owners themselves, so the person who wants to keep the house may have to outbid a sibling. Our guide to co-owned Pennsylvania houses, partition and bidding walks through it step by step.

Pennsylvania has not yet adopted the Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act. House Bill 1498 passed the state House on June 23, 2025, by a vote of 181 to 22 and was sent to the Senate Judiciary Committee the next day, with no later action listed. Until something passes, heirs follow the existing rules.

Partition is a last resort. Often one heir buys out another, or the heirs who want out sell their shares. Our page on buying inherited houses when heirs disagree explains how that works.

Divorce and Married Owners

Pennsylvania divides marital property by equitable distribution. Under 23 Pa.C.S. § 3502, the court divides marital property in the percentages it finds just, without regard to marital misconduct. Married couples in Pennsylvania commonly hold title as tenants by the entirety, which means neither spouse can sell the house alone. A sale during a divorce normally needs both signatures or a court order.

A Strict Power of Attorney for an Owner Behind Bars

If an owner or an heir is in jail or prison, the usual answer is a power of attorney. Pennsylvania's formalities are strict. Under 20 Pa.C.S. § 5601(b), the document must be dated and signed, acknowledged before a notary who is not the agent, and witnessed by two adults who are neither the agent nor the notary. That is four people at one signing, which takes planning inside a facility.

Ask the facility how a resident requests a notary and whether two outside witnesses can be present. Our step-by-step guide to getting the POA signed in a Pennsylvania jail covers the questions to ask, and our page on buying houses when the owner is incarcerated explains how we handle closing.

Foreclosure Starts With a 33-Day Window

Pennsylvania mortgage foreclosures go through the courts and end in a sheriff's sale. Before that, most homeowners receive an Act 91 Notice. According to the Pennsylvania Housing Finance Agency, to put the foreclosure on hold you must have a face-to-face meeting with a designated counseling agency within 33 days of the date on that notice. The agency can then send an application for the state's Homeowners' Emergency Mortgage Assistance Program to PHFA.

Use that window. Meet the counselor, and at the same time find out what the house would sell for. If keeping it is not realistic, a sale before the sheriff's sale protects the equity that an auction usually wipes out.

Back Taxes

Unpaid county and school taxes in Pennsylvania can end in a county tax sale. Rules differ in Philadelphia and in the rest of the state, so call the county tax office as soon as you know taxes are behind. Delinquent taxes on an estate house are paid from the sale proceeds at closing.

When a Pennsylvania Agent Will Net More

If the house is in good shape, the executor has letters, the heirs agree and there is no deadline, list it with a local real estate agent. On the open market you will usually net more than from a cash buyer, including us.

First Moves for a Pennsylvania Family

  1. Note the date of death and count three and nine months forward for the inheritance tax.
  2. Find out whether anyone has opened the estate at the Register of Wills.
  3. Gather the deed, the mortgage statement and the tax bills.
  4. Speak with an attorney licensed in Pennsylvania about the estate, a divorce or a criminal case. We cannot give legal advice.

Quick answers for Pennsylvania

How much is Pennsylvania inheritance tax on a house left to children?

Children and other lineal heirs pay 4.5 percent, siblings pay 12 percent and most other heirs pay 15 percent, while a surviving spouse pays nothing. The tax becomes delinquent nine months after death, and paying within three months earns a 5 percent discount.

Can a Pennsylvania small estate petition transfer a house?

No. The small estate petition under 20 Pa.C.S. 3102 covers personal property of $50,000 or less, and real estate is excluded. A house needs a personal representative with letters or every heir's signature.

How must a Pennsylvania power of attorney be signed?

It must be dated and signed, acknowledged before a notary who is not the agent, and witnessed by two adults who are not the agent or the notary. That makes four people at a signing inside a jail or prison.

Has Pennsylvania adopted the Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act?

Not yet. House Bill 1498 passed the Pennsylvania House on June 23, 2025, and went to the Senate Judiciary Committee with no later action listed, so partition still follows Rules 1551 to 1574.
