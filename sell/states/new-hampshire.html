New Hampshire House Stuck in an Estate, a Dispute or a Tax Lien

Two New Hampshire rules shape almost every complicated sale here. First, the state's real estate transfer tax is charged to both the buyer and the seller, at 75 cents per $100 of the price each. Second, the probate shortcut that families use most, called waiver of administration, is not tied to the size of the estate at all. It turns on who the heirs are and whether they cooperate. If you have inherited a house in Concord, Nashua, the Lakes Region or the North Country and nobody has opened an estate, those two facts tell you a lot about what comes next.

The Waiver of Administration and Your Seller's Share of Transfer Tax

Estates in New Hampshire go to the Circuit Court Probate Division. Under RSA 553:32, the administrator can skip the inventory, bond and accounting when, for example, a single person is the sole heir or sole beneficiary and serves as administrator, or when all of the heirs serve together as co-administrators. The administrator later files a sworn statement, no sooner than six months and no later than one year after appointment, listing the real estate the person owned and confirming no known debts remain.

Because there is no dollar ceiling, a three-bedroom house can move through this simpler track as long as the family situation fits. If one heir objects, any interested person can ask the court for full administration instead, which adds inventory and accounting steps.

When the house sells, the seller's half of the transfer tax comes out of the estate's proceeds. On a $300,000 sale, that is $2,250 from the seller's side, with the buyer paying the same again. Budget for it before you agree on a price with siblings.

Transfer-on-Death Deeds Are New Here

New Hampshire adopted the Uniform Real Property Transfer on Death Act as RSA chapter 563-D, effective July 1, 2024. A parent can now record a deed titled "Transfer on Death Deed" naming who receives the house at death, without probate. The deed must be signed and acknowledged, and recorded at the county registry of deeds within 60 days after signing and before the owner dies, or it is void.

If your parent died after mid-2024, check the registry of deeds before anything else. A recorded TOD deed may mean the house already belongs to the named beneficiary, subject to any mortgage and liens.

When Heirs or Co-Owners Cannot Agree

New Hampshire lets a co-owner file a partition petition in either the superior court or the probate court for the county where the property sits (RSA 547-C:2). If a related case is already pending, such as the estate itself, the partition goes to the court handling that case. If any party claims a right to a jury, it goes to superior court.

For heirs, that means a sibling who refuses to sell can sometimes be brought into the same probate division that is already handling Mom's estate. Partition is still slow and costly. Many families settle once they see the timeline, either by one heir buying out the others or by selling the whole house together. Our page on selling your share when a co-owner won't sell explains how that settlement usually works, and how a partition lawsuit runs from filing to sale covers the court side.

Divorce and the Equal-Division Starting Point

New Hampshire divides marital property by equitable distribution. Under RSA 458:16-a, the court presumes an equal division is equitable unless the facts make that unfair. If the house is the main asset and neither spouse can refinance, selling and splitting the net proceeds is often the cleanest path.

Liens for Unpaid Property Taxes

Most New Hampshire towns use the tax lien process. Delinquent taxes carry 8 percent interest (for taxes assessed since April 2019). Once the collector executes the lien, the recorded amount carries 14 percent per year under RSA 80:69. The owner can redeem at any time before the collector issues a deed, and the collector cannot deed the property to the town until two years after the lien was executed, with at least 30 days' certified-mail notice first.

If the town does take the property and later sells it, RSA 80:88 requires the town to keep only what it is owed and pay the excess into superior court for the former owner and lienholders. Selling before the deed issues keeps the family in control of the price and the timing.

Foreclosure Moves Fast Without a Court

Most New Hampshire mortgages are foreclosed under a power of sale, outside court. RSA 479:25 requires the lender to notify a residential owner at least 45 days before the sale and to publish notice once a week for three weeks. The notice must tell the owner that they can petition the superior court to stop the sale, and an owner who does not file and serve that petition before the sale loses the right to challenge it later on that basis.

That 45-day window is short. If you are behind and the house has equity, a sale before the auction can pay off the loan and leave money in your pocket. See selling before a foreclosure auction for the steps.

An Owner Who Is Incarcerated

A person in jail or prison can still sell through an agent. New Hampshire's power of attorney law (RSA 564-E:105) requires the principal to sign, and the signature must be acknowledged before a notary public or other person authorized to take acknowledgments. Electronic signatures are not accepted for this. Ask the facility how it schedules notary visits; practices vary, so we confirm with the facility and the family's attorney before a closing is set.

What to Do This Week

  • Search the county registry of deeds for a recorded TOD deed or recent liens.

  • Write down every heir or co-owner and whether they want to sell, keep or buy out.

Quick answers for New Hampshire

Is there a dollar limit on New Hampshire's waiver of administration?

No. Waiver of administration under RSA 553:32 depends on the family situation, such as a sole heir serving as administrator or all heirs serving together, not on the estate's value.

Who pays the real estate transfer tax when a New Hampshire house sells?

Both parties. The tax is 75 cents per $100 of the price, charged to the buyer and to the seller.

Can a New Hampshire partition case be filed in probate court?

Yes. RSA 547-C:2 allows a partition petition in the superior or probate court for the county where the property is, and a jury demand sends it to superior court.

How long before a New Hampshire town can take a house for unpaid taxes?

The collector cannot deed the property to the town until two years after the tax lien was executed, and must send certified notice at least 30 days before. The owner can redeem any time before the deed issues.
