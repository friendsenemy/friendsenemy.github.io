Selling a Louisiana House After a Death: Successions, Co-Heirs, Community

Louisiana does not use the word probate the way other states do. When someone dies, their estate is a succession, the heirs are successors, and co-owned property is held in indivision. The most useful tool for many families is the small succession affidavit. When the person died in Louisiana without a will and the property was worth $125,000 or less, heirs can transfer a house without opening a court succession, as long as the affidavit describes it and is recorded in the parish where it sits.

The small succession affidavit and a Louisiana house

Under Code of Civil Procedure Article 3421, a small succession is one where the property in Louisiana had a gross value of $125,000 or less at death. A succession also counts as small, regardless of value, when the death was at least twenty years before the affidavit, which helps with family houses that were never transferred.

For a person who died without a will, Article 3432 lets the family list the property, including immovable property such as a house, in a sworn affidavit. The description of the house has to be good enough to identify it for transfer, and the affidavit must say whether it was community or separate property and give its value.

Who has to sign the affidavit

  • At least two people must sign, including the surviving spouse if there is one, and one or more heirs.
  • With no surviving spouse, at least two heirs sign. If there is only one heir, a second person who knows the facts signs too.
  • An heir who does not sign must either be impossible to locate after reasonable effort, or have been mailed 30 days' notice and not objected.

Under Article 3434, the affidavit is then recorded in the conveyance records of the parish where the house is. Once recorded, a person who claims to be an heir but is left out has two years from the recording to assert a claim against a buyer who paid for the property.

When there is a will

If the person left a will, the affidavit route is narrower. Under Article 3431, a testate small succession can skip court only if the person left no immovable property in Louisiana. A house left by will means a judicial succession. That case is opened in the district court of the parish where the person lived, or, if they lived out of state, the parish where the house is.

Heirs who don't agree: partition of property held in indivision

Civil Code Article 807 says no one can be forced to hold a thing in indivision with another, and any co-owner has a right to demand partition. Co-owners can agree in writing to block partition, but only for up to fifteen years unless another law allows longer. In practice, one heir who wants out can ask a court to divide or sell the house.

Louisiana has not adopted the Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act, which grew out of common-law states. The Louisiana State Law Institute has studied heirs property at the Legislature's request. Until something changes, the Civil Code partition rules apply. Many families avoid court by having one heir buy the others out, or by selling the whole house together. Community property and the surviving spouse

Louisiana is a community property state. Under Civil Code Article 2336, each spouse owns a present undivided one-half interest in community property. When a spouse dies with no descendants, the surviving spouse inherits the deceased spouse's share of the community under Article 889. When there are children, their rights and the spouse's rights both come into play, which is why the affidavit must label every item community or separate.

In a divorce, the community ends and must be partitioned between the spouses. A house bought during the marriage is usually community, so both spouses, or a court judgment, will be needed to sell it. See our page on selling a house during a divorce.

An owner in a Louisiana jail or prison

Louisiana calls a power of attorney a mandate. A person who is incarcerated can name an agent in a mandate to sell their house. Because the deed has to be recorded, the title company or closing notary will want the mandate signed before a notary in a form they can accept. Ask the facility how legal documents are notarized, and send the closing notary a draft before the owner signs.

Foreclosure and unpaid taxes in Louisiana

Louisiana mortgage foreclosures go through the district court and end with a sheriff's sale. Once a sale date is set, there is little room to sell, so contact a buyer or attorney as early as possible.

Unpaid property taxes can lead to a tax sale, after which the owner has a period set by the Louisiana Constitution to redeem. Louisiana has rewritten its tax sale statutes, and redemptions for sales are handled under the law in effect on December 31, 2025 (R.S. 47:2241.1). If a parent's house went to tax sale, get the redemption amount and date from the parish tax collector in writing before doing anything else.

When a Louisiana listing agent will net you more

If the succession is settled or the affidavit is recorded, the heirs agree, the house is in reasonable shape and there is no sheriff's sale coming, list with a Louisiana real estate agent. Retail buyers pay more. A cash or partnership sale makes sense when heirs are scattered, someone will not sign, the house needs work, or the taxes are behind.

Quick answers for Louisiana

Can a Louisiana small succession affidavit transfer a house?

Yes, when the person died without a will and the Louisiana property was worth $125,000 or less. The affidavit must describe the house and be recorded in the parish conveyance records.

Which court opens a Louisiana succession?

A succession is opened in the district court of the parish where the person lived, or in the parish where the house is if they lived out of state.

Can one Louisiana heir force the sale of an inherited house?

Civil Code Article 807 says no one can be forced to hold property in indivision, so any co-owner can demand partition unless the owners agreed in writing to block it.

Is Louisiana a community property state?

Yes. Under Civil Code Article 2336, each spouse owns a present undivided one-half interest in community property.
