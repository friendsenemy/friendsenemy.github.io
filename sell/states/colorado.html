Colorado Homes With Legal Knots: Public Trustee Sales, Estates and Tax Liens

Colorado foreclosures run through a Public Trustee

Colorado handles most home foreclosures differently from most states. A county official called the Public Trustee holds the deed of trust and runs the sale. The process is out of court, but a short court step called a Rule 120 hearing is part of it. At that hearing the judge only looks at a few issues: whether you are in default, whether you are protected by the Servicemembers Civil Relief Act, whether the lender is the real party in interest, and whether a pending loan modification request should stop the sale.

The 110-to-125-day window

Once the lender records its Notice of Election and Demand, the Public Trustee sets a sale date no fewer than 110 and no more than 125 calendar days later. To cure the default, file a notice of intent to cure with the Public Trustee no later than 15 calendar days before the sale; you then have until noon the day before the sale to pay the cure amount. After a sale, the homeowner has no redemption right, except when a homeowners' association foreclosed, which carries a 180-day period. That makes the time before the sale the time to act, and a sale of the house is one way out. Our guide to selling before a foreclosure auction walks through it.

Steps to take this week

  1. Find the date the Notice of Election and Demand was recorded and count forward 110 days.
  2. Mark the intent-to-cure deadline, 15 calendar days before the sale date.
  3. Ask the lender's attorney for a payoff figure and a cure figure.
  4. Decide whether you can cure, refinance or sell before the Public Trustee's auction.

Back taxes: tax lien certificates and a 2024 change

Colorado counties sell tax liens. The winning bidder gets a certificate of purchase, and the owner has three years after the sale to redeem. Since House Bill 24-1056 took effect on July 1, 2024, a certificate holder who wants title applies for a public auction of the property, and any amount bid above what is owed goes to the former owner, who has to ask for it. That law was Colorado's answer to the U.S. Supreme Court's 2023 decision in Tyler v. Hennepin County. If you inherited a house with a tax lien on it, the surplus rule matters, but selling before an auction usually still leaves more in your pocket.

Estates: district courts, and a separate Denver Probate Court

In most of Colorado, district courts handle estates. Denver has its own Denver Probate Court. Either way, a house titled only in the deceased owner's name generally needs a personal representative to sign the deed.

Colorado's small estate affidavit is adjusted every year (about $88,000 for a 2026 death) and can be used 10 days after the death, but the official form says it is not valid to transfer real estate. If the house did not pass by joint tenancy or a beneficiary deed, plan on opening an estate case.

Colorado does allow beneficiary deeds (C.R.S. § 15-15-401 and following). The deed must be recorded before the owner dies. When one is on record, the named beneficiary can often sell without probate.

Our page on Denver probate court and Public Trustee rules covers the Denver details.

Divorce: an equitable share and the family home

Colorado divides marital property equitably under C.R.S. § 14-10-113, which often lands near half but does not have to. One factor the court considers is whether to award the family home to the spouse who has the children most of the time. Increases in value during the marriage on a home one spouse owned beforehand can be marital property. Both spouses usually sign a sale until a decree or written separation agreement says otherwise.

Family members who share title

Colorado has not adopted the Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act. When relatives inherit a house together and cannot agree, the remedy is an ordinary partition case in court, which can end in a forced sale. A buyout of one owner's share is often simpler. Read what to do when heirs disagree about selling.

An owner who is incarcerated

An owner in jail or prison still owns the house and can sign a deed or a power of attorney. For a sale, the title company will want the power of attorney signed in front of a notary so it can be recorded. Each facility arranges notaries its own way, so call before mailing documents. See our page on selling when the owner is incarcerated.

When a Colorado agent is the better choice

If the house is in good condition, all the owners agree, and there is no Public Trustee sale or tax lien auction coming, list with a local Colorado agent. You will usually net more than with a cash or partnership sale. Quick answers for Colorado

How long does a Colorado Public Trustee foreclosure take?

The Public Trustee sets the sale no fewer than 110 and no more than 125 calendar days after the Notice of Election and Demand is recorded. To cure, file a notice of intent to cure at least 15 calendar days before the sale.

Can I redeem my Colorado house after a foreclosure sale?

Generally no. A Colorado homeowner has no redemption right after a Public Trustee sale, except when a homeowners' association foreclosed, which carries a 180-day period.

Can a Colorado small estate affidavit transfer a house?

No. The affidavit limit is adjusted every year, about $88,000 for a 2026 death, but the official form says it is not valid to transfer real estate, so a house usually needs an estate case or a beneficiary deed.

What changed for Colorado tax liens in 2024?

House Bill 24-1056, effective July 1, 2024, requires a public auction before a certificate holder gets title, and any amount bid above what is owed goes to the former owner, who has to ask for it.
