Selling a Shared, Inherited or Tax-Delinquent Illinois House

Illinois co-owners have heirs property protections since 2019

When relatives inherit a house together in Illinois and one wants to sell while another wants to stay, the law gives the family more structure than most people expect. Illinois enacted the Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act as Public Act 101-0520, signed and effective August 23, 2019. For inherited property held by relatives as tenants in common, a partition case now starts with a court-set value, then gives the other owners a chance to buy out the person who filed, and only then moves toward a sale, usually on the open market.

That means a relative who refuses to sell cannot simply be forced to the courthouse steps overnight, and the relative who wants out cannot be ignored either. Most families do better negotiating within that framework than litigating it. Probate in the Illinois circuit courts

Illinois estates are opened in the circuit court of the county where the person lived. Cook County runs a separate Probate Division. Most Illinois estates use independent administration, which lets the executor or administrator sell real estate without asking the judge to approve each step, as long as no one objects.

The small estate affidavit does not reach the house

Illinois' small estate affidavit now covers personal property up to $150,000, not counting registered vehicles. But Illinois Legal Aid Online explains that it cannot be used if the person owned real estate that would otherwise go through probate. A house only stays out of the way if it already passed at death through a transfer on death instrument (TODI) or a joint tenancy with right of survivorship.

So the first question for an inherited Illinois house is: how was it titled the day the owner died? If there was no TODI and no survivorship deed, plan on opening an estate.

Back taxes: Illinois sells the lien, and the clock is 2.5 years

Illinois counties hold an annual tax sale where investors buy the right to collect unpaid property taxes plus penalties. Under 35 ILCS 200/21-350, most homes can be redeemed within 2.5 years of the sale date. Vacant non-farm land, buildings with 7 or more units, and commercial or industrial property get only 1 year.

If you are not sure whether taxes were sold, the county clerk can tell you which tax year was sold, to whom, and the current amount needed to redeem. Families often learn about a sold tax lien only when a "take notice" letter arrives late in that window. Redemption costs rise with time, and if it is not paid, the tax buyer can ask the court for a tax deed and take the house and every dollar of equity in it. If an inherited house has been sitting with taxes unpaid, check the county clerk's redemption records now.

Foreclosure is judicial, with a built-in redemption window

Illinois forecloses through the courts. For residential property, 735 ILCS 5/15-1603 gives the owner a redemption period that ends at the later of 7 months after the owner was served (or otherwise came under the court's jurisdiction) or 3 months after the judgment of foreclosure. In practice, most owners have many months between being served and the judicial sale. That is usually enough time to sell, even when title needs work. See our page on selling a house before foreclosure.

An owner in an Illinois jail or prison

Illinois' statutory short form power of attorney for property must be signed in front of a notary and at least one witness. The witness cannot be the agent, the agent's relatives, or the person's own close family, and the notary cannot double as the witness. Without both, title companies will reject it. Plan the signing with the facility before the paperwork goes in.

Divorce

Illinois is not a community property state. Under 750 ILCS 5/503, the court first separates marital from non-marital property, then divides marital property "in just proportions," which is not always equal. A house bought during the marriage is presumed marital. An inherited house kept in one spouse's name usually stays non-marital, unless it was mixed with marital money or retitled.

When an agent will net you more

If every owner agrees, the estate is open or not needed, taxes are current, and the house is in decent condition, an Illinois listing agent will usually get a better price than an investor. Chicago and its suburbs have deep buyer pools for move-in-ready homes.

Quick answers for Illinois

Has Illinois adopted the Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act?

Yes. Illinois enacted it as Public Act 101-0520, effective August 23, 2019, giving relatives who inherit together a court-set value and a chance to buy out a co-owner who files for partition.

How long do Illinois owners have to redeem after a tax sale?

Under 35 ILCS 200/21-350, most homes can be redeemed within 2.5 years of the sale date. Vacant non-farm land, buildings with 7 or more units, and commercial property have 1 year.

Can an Illinois small estate affidavit transfer a house?

No. The affidavit covers personal property up to $150,000, not counting registered vehicles, and cannot be used if the person owned real estate that would otherwise go through probate.

What is the redemption period in an Illinois residential foreclosure?

Under 735 ILCS 5/15-1603, it ends at the later of 7 months after the owner was served or 3 months after the foreclosure judgment.
