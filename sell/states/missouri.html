Selling a Missouri House After a Death, a Split, or a Missed Tax Bill

Before you assume a Missouri house has to go through a long probate, check the county recorder of deeds. Missouri has allowed beneficiary deeds for decades, and its small estate affidavit, unlike the version in many states, can be used to establish who inherits real estate. Between those two tools, a surprising number of Missouri houses can be sold without a full estate administration.

Start with the beneficiary deed

Under RSMo 461.025, a Missouri owner can sign a beneficiary deed naming who receives the house at death. It does not need to be delivered to the beneficiary or paid for. The catch is timing: it must be signed and filed with the recorder of deeds in the county where the property sits before the owner dies. A beneficiary deed found in a drawer after the funeral does not work.

If a recorded beneficiary deed exists, the named beneficiary may be able to sell soon after the death without opening an estate. If it names several children, every one of them generally signs the sale.

The Missouri small estate affidavit covers real estate

Missouri estates are handled by the probate division of the circuit court in each county. For smaller estates, RSMo 473.097 allows a small estate affidavit when the whole estate, less liens, debts and encumbrances, is $40,000 or less. A few features make it unusual:

  • It can cover personal property, real property, or both.
  • It cannot be made until 30 days after the death, and no letters can be pending or granted.
  • The affidavit and the clerk's certificate are filed with the probate division, then recorded with the recorder of deeds in each county where land is located.
  • A bond is normally required, though the court may waive it.

Because the limit is figured after mortgages and other liens, a modest house with a loan on it can sometimes qualify even if its sale price is well above $40,000. A Missouri probate attorney can confirm the math for your estate.

Independent administration for larger estates

When a full estate is needed, RSMo 473.780 allows independent administration, meaning the personal representative can act without court orders for each step. It is available when the will authorizes it, or when all the heirs or devisees consent and the will does not prohibit it. With it, selling the house is far simpler than in a supervised estate. Our page on selling an inherited or probate home explains how a sale fits around the estate timeline.

Co-owners and heirs who will not sell

When two or more people own a Missouri house and one refuses to sell, the others can ask the circuit court to partition it. Missouri adopted the Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act in 2019 (Senate Bill 83). For inherited family property that qualifies, the court sets a value and gives the relatives who did not ask for a sale the chance to buy out the ones who did.

Partition takes months and lawyers' fees come out of the proceeds. If you are the co-owner who wants out, selling your share directly is often faster. See selling your share when a co-owner won't sell and our guide to a co-owner's rights to force a sale.

Tax sale on the fourth Monday in August

Missouri county collectors hold the annual delinquent land tax sale on the fourth Monday in August (RSMo 140.150). The buyer gets a certificate of purchase, not the house. Under RSMo 140.340, the owner, a lienholder or an occupant can redeem at any time during the following year, and the right to redeem continues, in weaker form, until the tax sale buyer actually acquires the deed. Selling during that window lets the redemption be paid off at closing.

Foreclosure under a deed of trust

Most Missouri home loans use a deed of trust with a power of sale, so foreclosure normally happens without a lawsuit. RSMo 443.320 sets the notice: in counties with a city of 50,000 or more, the sale notice must run at least 20 times in a daily newspaper up to the sale day; elsewhere it can run in four successive weekly issues.

Missouri also has a limited right of redemption. Under RSMo 443.410, if the lender itself buys the house at the sale, the owner can redeem within one year, but only if written notice of intent to redeem was given at the sale or within ten days before the advertised sale date. Most owners miss that step, so treat the sale date as the real deadline.

Divorce and owners who are away

Missouri is not a community property state. In a divorce, the court divides marital property "in such proportions as the court deems just" under RSMo 452.330, and it may consider awarding the family home, or the right to live in it for a time, to the parent with custody. When both spouses are on the deed, both usually sign a sale until the decree says otherwise.

If an owner is in jail or living far away, a power of attorney used to convey Missouri real estate must be acknowledged and recorded like a deed (RSMo 442.360). For estate, divorce or criminal-case questions, consult an attorney licensed in Missouri; Pages of Purpose LLC is not a law firm.

Quick answers for Missouri

Does Missouri allow beneficiary deeds?

Yes. Under RSMo 461.025 a beneficiary deed passes the house at death, but it must be signed and filed with the county recorder of deeds before the owner dies.

Can a Missouri small estate affidavit transfer real estate?

Yes. Under RSMo 473.097 it can cover real property when the estate, less liens and debts, is $40,000 or less, and it is recorded with the recorder of deeds in each county where the land is located.

When is the Missouri tax sale held?

County collectors hold the annual delinquent land tax sale on the fourth Monday in August, and the owner can redeem at any time during the following year.

Is there a right of redemption after a Missouri foreclosure?

Only in limited cases: if the lender buys the house, the owner can redeem within one year if written notice of intent to redeem was given at the sale or within ten days before it.
