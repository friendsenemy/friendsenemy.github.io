Selling a Hard-to-Sell Maryland House: Our Home State, County by County

Maryland is where Pages of Purpose started, and it is the one state where we work from home ground in Southern Maryland. It is also a state with its own vocabulary. Estates are handled by the Orphans' Court, with paperwork filed through each county's Register of Wills, not a general probate court. Tax bills that go unpaid are sold at tax sale as liens. And co-owner disputes run under a partition law Maryland rebuilt in 2022. Knowing which office and which clock applies is most of the work.

Orphans' Court, the Register of Wills and the small estate line

When a Maryland homeowner dies, the family opens an estate with the Register of Wills in the county where the person lived, and the Orphans' Court oversees it. The court appoints a personal representative, who is the person who can sign a deed for the estate.

Under Estates and Trusts §5-601, an estate can use the simpler small estate process when the Maryland property is worth $50,000 or less. The limit is $100,000 when the surviving spouse is the sole legatee or heir. Value means fair market value minus debts secured by the property, such as a mortgage, so a house with a large loan balance can sometimes fit.

  • Pull the deed from Maryland Land Records to see exactly how the title was held.
  • Check the Register of Wills estate search to see whether anyone already opened an estate.
  • If the house had a mortgage, get the payoff. It changes whether the small estate limit is met.

Co-owners and heirs: Maryland's 2022 partition rules

Maryland adopted the Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act in 2022. The partition subtitle of the Real Property Article (Title 14, Subtitle 7) now defines a court-set determination of value and lets a partition sale happen by open-market sale, sealed bids or auction. The open-market option matters. A house sold through a listing usually brings more than a courthouse auction, and that protects the heir who did not want to sell.

For siblings who are stuck, our Maryland guides cover whether all heirs have to agree to sell and whether a co-owner can force a sale. The national page on buying when heirs disagree explains how a buyout offer to one heir works.

Spouses and tenancy by the entirety

Maryland recognizes tenancy by the entirety for married couples. One spouse cannot sell or force a partition of an entireties house alone. That matters in a divorce and when one spouse is incarcerated, because both signatures, or a court order, are needed. Maryland divides marital property equitably in a divorce and is not a community property state.

Maryland tax sale: the 6-month and 9-month marks

Maryland counties and Baltimore City sell tax sale certificates on delinquent property. Under Tax-Property §14-833, the certificate holder can file to foreclose the owner's right of redemption 6 months after the sale. For owner-occupied homes, that wait is 9 months, and the first required notice cannot go out until 7 months after the sale. Holders must also send notices before filing, and a certificate becomes void if no foreclosure is filed within 2 years.

An inherited house that sits vacant is often not owner-occupied anymore, so the shorter 6-month clock can apply. Our guide to selling before a Maryland tax sale covers redemption amounts.

Maryland foreclosure: the 45-day notice and mediation

Under Real Property §7-105.1, a lender must send a notice of intent to foreclose at least 45 days before filing. It cannot file until the later of 90 days after the default or 45 days after that notice. Owner-occupants can ask for prefile mediation within 25 days after the notice of intent is mailed, or postfile mediation within 25 days after receiving the final loss mitigation affidavit. Mediation must generally be held within 60 days of the request. Each of those deadlines is a window where a sale can still close and protect equity.

When the owner is in a Maryland jail or prison

An incarcerated owner usually sells through a power of attorney signed before a notary. If the house is owned with a spouse as tenants by the entirety, the spouse cannot sell alone. Our Maryland incarcerated-owner guide walks through it.

A Maryland agent may net you more

If the house is in good shape, the heirs or owners agree, and no tax sale foreclosure or auction date is coming, list it with a Maryland real estate agent. Retail buyers will usually pay more than we can. We are a better fit for estates nobody has opened, split families, back taxes, liens, or houses that need major work.

From our base in Southern Maryland, we pull Land Records, the Register of Wills docket, SDAT records and tax sale status ourselves, then sort out every heir and lien. Quick answers for Maryland

Which court handles estates in Maryland?

Maryland estates are overseen by the Orphans' Court, and the paperwork is filed through the Register of Wills in the county where the person lived.

What is Maryland's small estate limit?

Under Estates and Trusts §5-601, an estate qualifies when the Maryland property is worth $50,000 or less, or $100,000 or less if the surviving spouse is the sole legatee or heir.

When can a Maryland tax sale buyer foreclose on a house?

A certificate holder can file to foreclose the right of redemption 6 months after the tax sale, or 9 months for owner-occupied homes, under Tax-Property §14-833.

Has Maryland adopted the Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act?

Yes. Maryland adopted it in 2022, and its partition law allows a court-ordered sale by open-market sale, sealed bids or auction.
