Massachusetts Houses Caught in a Divorce, a Co-Owner Dispute or an Estate

In Massachusetts, the court that splits up a family is often the same court that splits up the house. A petition to partition land can be filed in the Probate and Family Court for any county where part of the land lies, or in the Land Court for land anywhere in the state (General Laws chapter 241, section 2). That same Probate and Family Court hears divorces and estates. For couples, ex-couples and siblings who share a deed, it often means one judge sees the whole picture.

Who can force a partition in Massachusetts, and who can't

Under chapter 241, section 1, a person who holds a present undivided legal interest in land can ask the court to partition it. A fee owner can seek partition of the whole. A life tenant or tenant for years can usually seek partition only for the length of their own interest.

One group is left out: tenants by the entirety. If a married couple owns their house as tenants by the entirety, neither spouse can bring a partition case to force a sale. The way out is either both signing a sale, or a divorce, after which the court can deal with the house.

For unmarried co-owners and heirs, a partition case is a real threat to a holdout, but it takes time and lawyer fees that come out of everyone's share. Our pages on selling your share when a co-owner won't sell and how partition lawsuits work show the buyout and sale options.

Divorce: the judge's reach over the house

Massachusetts divides property under General Laws chapter 208, section 34. The key words are that the court may assign to either spouse "all or any part of the estate of the other." The court weighs factors such as the length of the marriage, each spouse's conduct, age, health, income and employability, the needs of the children, and contributions as a homemaker. Because the statute speaks of the whole estate, a house one spouse owned before the marriage is not automatically off the table.

Tenancy by the entirety also protects the home during the marriage. Under chapter 209, section 1, a creditor of one spouse generally cannot seize that spouse's interest in an entireties home while it is the other spouse's principal residence, though both spouses can be liable for necessaries. That protection often decides whether a sale is voluntary or forced. For more, see our page on selling a house during a divorce.

Before you sign anything in a divorce

  • Get a copy of the deed and check how title is held: tenants by the entirety, joint tenants or tenants in common.
  • Ask your attorney whether any court order limits a sale while the case is open.
  • Get the mortgage payoff and any tax bills, so both sides see the same numbers.

Estates: voluntary administration does not reach the house

Massachusetts has a short form for small estates called voluntary administration. Under chapter 190B, section 3-1201, it can be filed 30 days after the death if no one has asked to be appointed personal representative, and the estate can include a motor vehicle plus other personal property up to $25,000. It cannot transfer real estate.

For a house, the usual route is to have a personal representative appointed through the Probate and Family Court, often through informal probate. Informal probate is generally available only within three years of the death. After that, the family has to show circumstances that allow a late filing, which can slow a sale. Our page on selling an inherited or probate house explains the personal representative's role.

Tax title and the new excess equity rules

When property taxes go unpaid in Massachusetts, the town can take the property or sell its tax title. Under chapter 60, section 65, the holder can petition the Land Court to foreclose the owner's right of redemption 12 months after the sale or taking, or sooner in some cases, such as abandoned buildings or with the owner's written consent.

Massachusetts changed what happens after that foreclosure. Under chapter 60, section 64A, the holder must either keep the property or sell it, and former owners can claim the excess equity: the value above what was owed. A sale must be listed with a licensed real estate broker, and if it does not sell within 12 months it goes to auction, with bids of at least two-thirds of the appraised value. Starting August 5, 2025, the holder has 30 days after the judgment becomes final to choose whether to keep or sell, up from 14.

That rule protects some value, but a sale by the owner or heirs before the Land Court judgment keeps control in the family's hands.

Mortgage trouble: the 90-day right to cure

Under chapter 244, section 35A, a homeowner who misses a required payment has a 90-day right to cure the default, usable once in any five-year period. The lender cannot accelerate the loan or enforce the mortgage for a payment default until at least 90 days after written notice. Those 90 days are often the best time to sell with the most options.

An owner in a Massachusetts jail or prison

An incarcerated owner can sell through an agent named in a power of attorney. Because the deed will be recorded, a title examiner will expect the power of attorney to be acknowledged before a notary. Ask the facility how inmates get documents notarized. If the house is owned as tenants by the entirety, the other spouse cannot sell alone.

When a Massachusetts listing makes more sense

If the owners agree, the house is in good condition and there is no Land Court petition or foreclosure notice pending, list with a Massachusetts real estate agent. Buyers in a normal market will usually pay more than a cash or partnership buyer. We fit when a divorce, an estate or a tax title makes a normal listing hard to finish.

Quick answers for Massachusetts

Which Massachusetts court handles a partition case?

A partition petition can be filed in the Probate and Family Court for the county where the land lies, or in the Land Court for land anywhere in Massachusetts.

Can one spouse force the sale of a Massachusetts house owned as tenants by the entirety?

No. Chapter 241 excludes tenants by the entirety from bringing partition, so the house is usually sold only with both spouses' signatures or after a divorce.

Can voluntary administration transfer a house in Massachusetts?

No. Voluntary administration covers a motor vehicle plus up to $25,000 of other personal property and cannot transfer real estate.

What is the Massachusetts right to cure a mortgage default?

Under chapter 244, section 35A, a homeowner has a 90-day right to cure a missed payment, usable once every five years, before the lender can accelerate or enforce the mortgage.
