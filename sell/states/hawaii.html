Selling an Inherited or Shared Home in Hawaii From the Mainland

Mainland heirs face a 7.25% withholding on a Hawaii sale

Many people who inherit a house in Hawaii no longer live in the islands. That creates a cost most families never see coming. Under Hawaii's real property tax withholding law, known as HARPTA (Hawaii Revised Statutes section 235-68), the buyer must withhold 7.25% of the amount realized when a nonresident sells Hawaii real property and send it to the state Department of Taxation.

That money is a prepayment of Hawaii income tax, not a final tax. If the estate or the heirs owe less, they file a Hawaii return to get the difference back, and in some cases a waiver or reduced withholding can be requested before closing. On an inherited house, where the tax basis is usually stepped up to value at death, the real tax owed may be far less than 7.25%. Plan for the cash flow gap and talk to a Hawaii tax preparer before you sign a contract.

Two recording systems: Land Court and the Regular System

Hawaii is one of the few places that still runs two parallel title systems. Some parcels are registered in Land Court under HRS chapter 501, a Torrens-style registration where the state issues a certificate of title. Others are recorded in the Regular System at the Bureau of Conveyances. A house can also sit on leased land, where the family owns the building but not the ground.

Before anyone promises a closing date, find out which system the property is in and whether it is fee simple or leasehold. Land Court parcels usually need the paperwork to match the certificate exactly, including the names of heirs, before a new certificate issues.

Probate in Hawaii's circuit courts

Estates in Hawaii are handled by the circuit court for the island where the person lived or left property. For very small estates, HRS 560:3-1205 lets an interested person or the court clerk petition when the estate's total value in Hawaii is $100,000 or less and no personal representative has been appointed; the court can then authorize the clerk to collect and administer the property. With island home values, few houses fit under that cap, so most inherited homes need a personal representative.

Hawaii also adopted the Uniform Real Property Transfer on Death Act, HRS chapter 527. If the owner recorded a TOD deed, the house can pass to the named beneficiary without probate. Check both the Bureau of Conveyances and Land Court records for one.

Family members who share title and disagree

Hawaii adopted the Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act through Act 260 of 2016, effective January 1, 2017. When inherited property is owned by relatives as tenants in common, the court sets a fair market value, lets the other family members buy out the one who filed, and prefers an open-market sale to an auction if a sale is ordered. For families who want to keep the 'ohana house, the buyout right matters.

When nobody can afford a buyout and one heir needs out, buying that heir's share is often faster than a lawsuit. See our page on heirs who disagree about selling and the general guide to partition lawsuits.

Divorce, incarceration, foreclosure and taxes

Divorce

Hawaii's family court divides property under HRS 580-47 after weighing the parties' merits, their abilities, the condition each will be left in, and other circumstances. Hawaii courts use a partnership model that starts from an equal split and allows deviations for valid reasons. Hawaii also recognizes tenancy by the entirety for married owners, so both spouses usually sign.

An owner who is incarcerated

An owner in custody keeps title. To sell, they sign the deed or a power of attorney, and anything that will be recorded at the Bureau of Conveyances or filed in Land Court needs a notarized acknowledgment. Arrange the notary through the facility well in advance, especially if the owner is housed in a mainland facility.

Foreclosure

Hawaii lenders can foreclose through a lawsuit in circuit court or, where the mortgage allows, through a non-judicial power of sale, and Hawaii law adds protections for owner-occupants. Ask your servicer which process it is using; the deadlines are very different.

Property taxes

Real property taxes in Hawaii are set and collected by each county, not the state. Unpaid taxes can lead to a county tax sale, so contact the county treasury early if taxes are behind.

Quick answers for Hawaii

What is HARPTA withholding on a Hawaii home sale?

When a nonresident sells Hawaii real property, HRS 235-68 requires the buyer to withhold 7.25% of the amount realized and send it to the state. It is a prepayment of Hawaii income tax, and any excess can be recovered by filing a return.

What is the difference between Land Court and the Regular System in Hawaii?

Hawaii has two title systems. Land Court parcels are registered under HRS chapter 501 with a state-issued certificate of title, while Regular System documents are recorded at the Bureau of Conveyances.

Does Hawaii have a small estate procedure?

Yes. Under HRS 560:3-1205, an interested person or the court clerk can petition when the estate's total value in Hawaii is $100,000 or less and no personal representative has been appointed.

Has Hawaii adopted the Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act?

Yes. Act 260 of 2016 adopted it effective January 1, 2017, giving family co-owners a court-set value and a chance to buy out a relative who files for partition.
