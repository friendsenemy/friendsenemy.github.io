North Dakota Co-Owner Buyouts, Estates and Tight Deadlines

North Dakota has a partition rule built for inherited houses. When one co-owner asks a court to sell, and at least one owner holds an inherited-type interest, section 32-16-49 of the North Dakota Century Code lets another co-owner buy out the person who asked for the sale at the appraised value of that person's share. That means a brother who wants cash and a sister who wants to keep Dad's house in Bismarck or Minot both have a path, without a public auction.

Section 32-16-49: The Buyout Before Any Sale

In a partition action filed in district court, when a sale is requested or needed to avoid an unfair division, the court appoints a referee to get an appraisal of fair market value. The court then tells the parties the value. A co-owner may buy the shares of those who asked for the sale at the appraised value of their fractional interest, and if more than one wants to buy, the court divides the interests fairly among the buyers.

The statute does not set deadlines, so the court controls timing. If nobody buys, the general rules apply: the court can order a sale when dividing the land would cause great prejudice to the owners (section 32-16-12), and court-ordered sales are made at public auction after published notice.

Partition still costs money for an appraisal, a referee and lawyers. If one heir simply wants out, selling that share privately is usually quicker. We buy partial interests and whole houses, and our page on selling your share when a co-owner won't sell explains how. For the broader process, read what a partition lawsuit involves.

Foreclosure: A Court Case and a Short Redemption

A mortgage on real estate in North Dakota is foreclosed by an action in district court. Before filing, the lender must serve a notice at least 30 days and not more than 90 days ahead (section 32-19-20). After the foreclosure sale, the owner of residential property has only 60 days to redeem (section 32-19-18). Agricultural land and abandoned property follow different rules.

Sixty days is short. The better window to sell is between that pre-filing notice and the sale itself. See how to sell before the foreclosure sale for the steps.

Unpaid Taxes and the October 1 Foreclosure Date

North Dakota counties foreclose their own tax liens. By June 1 each year, the county auditor sends notice of foreclosure on property where two or more years have passed since the tax became due (section 57-28-01). The tax lien foreclosure date is the October 1 after that notice is served (57-28-02). If the taxes, interest, penalties and costs are not paid by then, the auditor issues a tax deed to the county.

The former owner, or the owner's executor, parent, spouse or child, may still repurchase while the county holds title (57-28-19). Once the county sells at its annual auction, which begins the third Tuesday of November, that chance is gone.

Estates: The County Clerk of Court and Informal Probate

North Dakota follows the Uniform Probate Code. Most estates use informal probate, which the North Dakota courts describe as available when less than three years have passed since the death, the will is not disputed and the people with priority agree on who should serve. Questions about an existing case go to the county clerk of court.

The small-estate affidavit has a $100,000 limit and can be used 30 days after death, but it covers personal property only (section 30.1-23-01). A house needs a personal representative, unless title passes another way.

Transfer-on-Death Deeds

North Dakota allows transfer-on-death deeds under chapter 30.1-32.1. To work, the deed must be recorded with the county recorder before the owner's death. Search the recorder's records before opening an estate; a recorded TOD deed may mean the house already belongs to the named beneficiary.

Divorce

When a divorce is granted, section 14-05-24 directs the court to make an equitable distribution of the parties' property and debts. If the spouses do not agree on a valuation date, the court uses the date 60 days before the first scheduled trial. Selling the house before trial can remove the hardest asset to value.

A Co-Owner in Jail or Prison

A person who is incarcerated can sign a power of attorney so an agent can handle the sale. Title companies expect the signature to be acknowledged before a notary, and the facility controls when a notary can visit, so we confirm the process with the facility and the family's lawyer. See getting a notary in jail or prison.

Quick answers for North Dakota

Can a North Dakota co-owner stop a partition sale by buying out the other owner?

Often, yes. Section 32-16-49 lets a co-owner buy the shares of those who asked for a sale at the appraised value of their fractional interest when at least one owner holds an inherited-type interest.

How long do I have to redeem a North Dakota home after a foreclosure sale?

For residential property, 60 days after the sale under section 32-19-18.

When does a North Dakota county take a house for unpaid taxes?

The county auditor sends notice by June 1 when taxes are two or more years past due, and the tax lien foreclosure date is the October 1 after that notice.

Can a North Dakota small estate affidavit transfer a house?

No. The $100,000 affidavit under section 30.1-23-01 covers personal property only.
