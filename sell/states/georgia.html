Selling a Georgia House Before the First Tuesday or After a Death

Georgia foreclosures move fast and happen outside a courtroom

If a Georgia house is behind on its mortgage, the calendar is the most important thing on this page. Georgia lenders usually foreclose under a power of sale in the security deed, with no judge involved. Sales happen at the county courthouse on the first Tuesday of the month between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m., the same schedule Georgia law sets for sheriff's sales. The lender only has to send the borrower written notice at least 30 days before the proposed sale date under O.C.G.A. 44-14-162.2.

Thirty days is not much time to settle an estate, find a missing heir or get a signature from someone in prison. That is why families who call us in Georgia usually call late. If you have a notice in hand, count back from the first Tuesday on it. A closed sale before that morning pays off the loan; after it, the equity is usually gone.

Inherited houses go through the Probate Court

Each Georgia county has a Probate Court that handles wills and estates. Families have three common paths:

  • Full administration. The court appoints an executor or administrator who can sell the house.
  • No administration necessary. Under O.C.G.A. 53-2-40, heirs can ask the court for an order that no administration is needed. Every heir must sign the agreement dividing the estate, known creditors must consent or be served, and a lender holding a security deed on the house must consent or not object. When the order covers real estate, a certified copy is recorded in the county deed records.
  • Year's support. A surviving spouse and minor children can claim property, including the house, for their support during the 12 months after the death. Year's support ranks ahead of most other debts.

The no-administration route works well when the family agrees. It fails the moment one heir refuses to sign, which leads to the next problem.

Heirs and co-owners who disagree in Georgia

Georgia was an early adopter of the Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act, effective January 1, 2013, now found at O.C.G.A. 44-6-180 through 44-6-189.1. For inherited land held by relatives as tenants in common, the court sets a value by appraisal or another method, gives the other cotenants a chance to buy out the person who filed, and favors an open-market sale through a broker over an auction.

Partition is still slow and costly. Many families settle first: the heir who wants out sells their share, and the rest decide what to do. Our guide on selling a co-owned Georgia house covers the buyout window in detail.

When the owner is in jail or prison

A Georgia owner who is incarcerated keeps title and can still sell, but someone has to sign. Georgia's power of attorney law, O.C.G.A. 10-6B-5, requires the document to be signed by the principal and attested in their presence by a witness who is not named as an agent, plus a second attesting officer under the deed-recording rules, which in practice is usually a notary. Missing either signature can make the POA useless for a real estate closing.

If foreclosure is also running, both clocks matter at once. Our page on buying when the owner is in jail or prison explains how we coordinate signatures, and the Georgia jail guide ties it to the first-Tuesday deadline.

Back taxes: Georgia tax sales and the redemption premium

Georgia counties can sell a house at a tax sale, but the owner can still redeem. Under O.C.G.A. 48-4-42, redeeming costs the sale price plus taxes paid and a premium of 20% for the first year or fraction of a year, and 10% for each year after. After 12 months, the buyer can serve notice under 48-4-45 to end the right to redeem. Waiting makes this more expensive every year, and a sale that pays the redemption amount can keep the rest of the equity for the family.

Divorce

Georgia superior courts divide marital property by equitable division, which aims at fairness rather than an automatic half. Where the house is titled to both spouses, both usually sign the deed, and a settlement agreement should say who controls the sale.

Is listing with an agent the smarter move?

Sometimes yes. If there is no foreclosure date, the owners agree, the title is clean, and the house is in good shape, a Georgia listing agent will usually get a higher price than any investor. Metro Atlanta in particular has strong retail demand for updated homes.

We fit the harder cases: a first-Tuesday date on the calendar, an heir who will not sign the no-administration agreement, an owner behind bars, or a tax buyer about to cut off redemption.

Quick answers for Georgia

When are Georgia foreclosure sales held?

Georgia non-judicial foreclosure sales are held at the county courthouse on the first Tuesday of the month between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m. The lender must send written notice at least 30 days before the sale.

Can Georgia heirs skip full probate?

Yes, if everyone agrees. Under O.C.G.A. 53-2-40, heirs can petition the Probate Court for an order that no administration is necessary, but every heir must sign the division agreement and known creditors must consent or be served.

Has Georgia adopted the Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act?

Yes. Georgia's version took effect January 1, 2013, at O.C.G.A. 44-6-180 through 44-6-189.1, and it gives cotenants a chance to buy out a relative who files for partition.

How much does it cost to redeem a Georgia house after a tax sale?

Under O.C.G.A. 48-4-42, the owner pays the sale price plus taxes paid and a premium of 20% for the first year and 10% for each later year. After 12 months, the buyer can serve notice to cut off redemption.
