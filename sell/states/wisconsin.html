Wisconsin Homes in Foreclosure, Divorce or an Estate: What Applies

Wisconsin builds a reward for trying to sell into its foreclosure law. Every residential mortgage foreclosure goes through circuit court, and under Wisconsin Statutes section 846.102, if the owner shows before judgment that they have signed a listing agreement with a licensed real estate broker and are trying in good faith to sell, the court can lengthen the waiting period before the sheriff's sale. Few owners hear about this until it is too late to use.

The Foreclosure Timeline and the Listing Extension

For a mortgage signed on or after April 27, 2016, the house generally cannot be sold until 3 months after judgment if the lender waives a deficiency judgment, or 6 months if it does not. Mortgages signed before that date use 6 and 12 months. If the owner files the motion before judgment and the court finds a good-faith listing with a licensed broker, the 3-month period can become 5 months and the 6-month period 8 months.

What that means in practice

  • Talk to a listing agent or a buyer before the judgment hearing, not after.
  • Keep copies of the listing agreement and any offers; the court will want to see a real effort.
  • Even after the sheriff's sale, the court has to confirm it, so stay in contact with your attorney.

If the house needs too much work to list, a direct sale before judgment can still pay off the loan and leave the rest of the equity with you. Our page on selling before a foreclosure auction lists the documents to gather.

Marital Property: Wisconsin's Version of Community Property

Wisconsin is a marital property state, which works much like the community property systems out West. Property acquired during the marriage is generally owned by both spouses equally, regardless of whose name is on the deed. That affects three common situations:

  1. Divorce. The court divides the marital estate, and the house often has to be sold or bought out at an agreed value.
  2. A spouse who died. The survivor may already own half, which changes what passes to the children.
  3. A spouse who will not or cannot sign. A title company may still ask for that spouse's signature even if the deed shows one name.

Our page on selling a house during a divorce covers how a sale and a divorce judgment fit together.

Small Estates: The $50,000 Transfer by Affidavit

Wisconsin Statutes section 867.03 allows a transfer by affidavit when the decedent's property subject to administration in Wisconsin is $50,000 or less in gross value. Unlike the small estate shortcuts in many states, the Wisconsin affidavit can cover an interest in real property, and a good-faith buyer who relies on it takes title free of claims of the estate. Notice must go to the other heirs before it is used. Larger estates go to circuit court, where the Register in Probate handles informal administration for most uncontested estates.

Wisconsin also allows a transfer on death designation for real property under section 705.15, but it only works if the document naming the beneficiary was submitted for recording with the county register of deeds before the owner died. A form found in a drawer after the funeral does not count.

When Heirs or Co-Owners Disagree

Co-owners in Wisconsin can ask the circuit court for partition, and when a house cannot be fairly divided the court can order a sale. Wisconsin has not adopted the Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act, so the general partition statute applies. In many families, the faster answer is for one owner to sell their share or for the others to buy them out at an appraised value.

Back Taxes and In Rem Foreclosure

Wisconsin counties and some cities collect long-delinquent property taxes by in rem foreclosure, a court case against the property itself. Under section 75.521, the treasurer may file the list of tax liens two years after the date of the tax certificate (one year where razing costs are included). The published notice then sets a last day for redemption at least 8 weeks out. After a judgment, the owner's interest is gone, so families should pay or sell long before that notice runs.

An Owner Who Is Incarcerated

For a deed, title companies in Wisconsin generally require a notarized power of attorney. Jails and Department of Corrections facilities each have their own rules for notary access and legal mail, so ask the facility first. If the incarcerated owner is married, remember the marital property rules above; the spouse may need to sign too. Our page on selling when an owner is in jail or prison explains how a buyer works with the agent named in the power of attorney.

If Listing Will Net More, List

A Wisconsin house in good repair, with owners who agree and no judgment or tax notice pending, will usually sell for more with a local agent than to a direct buyer. Remember that a listing can also extend a foreclosure redemption period. A cash or partnership sale fits better for vacant homes with code orders, estates where heirs are scattered, or houses already facing an in rem case.

Quick answers for Wisconsin

Can listing my house extend a Wisconsin foreclosure redemption period?

Yes. Under section 846.102, if before judgment the court finds a good-faith listing with a licensed broker, a 3-month period can become 5 months and a 6-month period 8 months.

Can a Wisconsin transfer by affidavit be used for a house?

Yes, if the property subject to administration in Wisconsin is $50,000 or less in gross value; section 867.03 allows the affidavit to cover an interest in real property.

Is Wisconsin a community property state?

Wisconsin is a marital property state, which works much like community property: property acquired during the marriage is generally owned equally by both spouses.

When can a Wisconsin county start in rem tax foreclosure?

Under section 75.521, generally two years after the date of the tax certificate, or one year where razing costs are included, with a redemption deadline at least 8 weeks after the first published notice.
