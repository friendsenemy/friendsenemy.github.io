Selling an Inherited or Stuck House in Michigan When the Family Is Divided

Michigan never adopted a transfer-on-death deed for real estate. That one gap shapes almost every inherited-house situation here: unless the owner used a trust, a joint deed, or a Lady Bird (enhanced life estate) deed, the house usually has to go through the county Probate Court before anyone can sell it. If the heirs then disagree, Michigan now has a newer set of partition rules built for exactly that fight.

No transfer-on-death deed: what Michigan families use instead

Many states let an owner record a simple deed that hands the house to a named person at death. Michigan does not. Its transfer-on-death statute covers securities, not land. What Michigan owners use instead is the Lady Bird deed, a common-law tool the owner signs and records during life. The owner keeps full control, and whatever is left passes to the named beneficiary at death without probate.

So the first thing we check on a Michigan house is the last recorded deed. If there is a Lady Bird deed, a trust, or a surviving joint owner, probate may not be needed at all. If the deed is in the dead owner's name alone, plan on Probate Court.

Michigan Probate Court: small estates and unsupervised administration

Each county has a Probate Court. For very small estates, the court can assign assets through a petition and order for assignment. The limit is adjusted every year; for deaths on or after January 1, 2026, Kent County Probate Court lists it at $53,000 (it was $51,000 for 2025 deaths). The simpler Affidavit of Decedent's Successor cannot be used at all if the estate includes real property.

Most houses are worth more than that, so a personal representative is appointed. Most Michigan estates run as unsupervised administration. Under MCL 700.3715, an unsupervised personal representative can sell land at a public or private sale without a separate court order, unless the will or a court order says otherwise. That makes a Michigan probate sale faster than people expect once the representative has letters of authority.

A Michigan tax cost families miss: uncapping

When a Michigan house changes hands, the taxable value is usually "uncapped" and resets toward market value the next year, which can raise the tax bill sharply. Since December 31, 2014, a transfer of residential property to a close relative (parent, sibling, child, adopted child or grandchild of the owner or the owner's spouse) is exempt, but only if the property is not used for any commercial purpose afterward. Renting it out counts as commercial use. The new owner must also file a Property Transfer Affidavit with the local assessor within 45 days. If you plan to keep and rent an inherited house, budget for the higher tax.

When heirs or co-owners in Michigan cannot agree

Michigan adopted the Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act through Public Act 215 of 2024, effective April 2, 2025. It applies to partition cases filed after that date when the court finds the house is "heirs property," meaning co-owned real estate that passed down through relatives.

Under these rules the court orders an appraisal (unless everyone agrees on a value), and co-owners who did not ask for a sale get the chance to buy out the ones who did, at that value. If no one buys, the court generally prefers dividing the land, and orders a sale only when division would hurt the owners as a group. A court-ordered sale is usually an open-market listing through a court-appointed broker.

That process protects families, but it takes months and costs money. Often one heir simply wants out. We can buy one heir's share, or buy the whole house from all of them at once and handle the title work. Our page on selling when heirs disagree walks through those options.

Back taxes and the Michigan forfeiture calendar

Michigan counties foreclose on unpaid property taxes on a fixed calendar. Using Wayne County's published timeline for 2022 taxes as an example:

  • March 1, 2023: unpaid taxes became delinquent and went to the county treasurer.
  • March 1, 2024: the property was forfeited, with added fees and interest at 1.5% per month.
  • March 31, 2025: last day to redeem after the court's foreclosure judgment.
  • April 1, 2025: title passed to the county treasurer.
  • July 1, 2025: deadline to file the Notice of Intent to Claim Remaining Foreclosure Proceeds.

Since the Michigan Supreme Court's 2020 Rafaeli decision and the 2020 law that followed, former owners can claim surplus sale proceeds, but only by meeting that filing deadline. Selling before March 31 of the foreclosure year keeps far more of the equity than waiting for a surplus check.

Mortgage foreclosure by advertisement

Most Michigan home foreclosures happen outside court, "by advertisement." The notice of sale runs once a week for four weeks in a county newspaper and is posted on the house within 15 days after the first publication. After the sheriff's sale, the owner usually still has a redemption period: six months in the most common case, one year if less than two-thirds of the original loan was still owed, and as little as 30 days if the house is abandoned. A sale to us can close inside that window and pay off the lender.

Owner in jail, a divorce, or a co-owner out of state

Michigan's Uniform Power of Attorney Act took effect July 1, 2024. A power of attorney can be signed in front of a notary, or in front of two witnesses who are not the named agent. For a house sale, get it notarized: a title company needs an acknowledged signature to record the deed. If the owner is incarcerated, ask the facility how notary visits are arranged before mailing documents. Our guide for owners in jail or prison covers the paperwork.

Michigan is not a community property state, and married couples can own a home as tenants by the entirety, which gives both spouses equal rights to manage it. In a divorce, both usually must sign to sell. For estate, divorce or criminal-case questions, talk with an attorney licensed in Michigan; Pages of Purpose LLC is not a law firm.

Quick answers for Michigan

Does Michigan allow transfer-on-death deeds for houses?

No. Michigan's transfer-on-death statute covers securities, not land, so owners use a Lady Bird (enhanced life estate) deed, a trust or joint ownership to avoid probate.

What is the small estate limit in Michigan for 2026?

For deaths on or after January 1, 2026, the Probate Court small estate limit is $53,000. The Affidavit of Decedent's Successor cannot be used if the estate includes real property.

Can heirs force the sale of an inherited house in Michigan?

A co-owner can file for partition, and since April 2, 2025 Michigan's Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act gives the other heirs a chance to buy out the share at a court-determined value before any sale.

When is the last day to save a Michigan house from tax foreclosure?

After the foreclosure judgment, owners can redeem until March 31 of the foreclosure year; title passes to the county treasurer on April 1.
