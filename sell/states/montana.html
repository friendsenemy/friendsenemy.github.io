Montana Homeowners Facing a Trustee's Sale, an Estate, or a Tax Lien

Montana moves fast at the end of a foreclosure. Most home loans here are trust indentures under the Small Tract Financing Act, and the trustee can sell the house at auction once a notice has been mailed at least 120 days ahead. Under MCA 71-1-319, the buyer at that sale is entitled to possession on the 10th day after it. For a Montana family behind on payments, the four months before the sale date are the window that counts.

The 120-day trustee's sale clock

MCA 71-1-315 sets the notice rules for a trustee's sale:

  • The trustee mails a copy of the recorded notice of sale by certified mail at least 120 days before the sale date.
  • The notice is published once a week for three successive weeks in a newspaper of general circulation in each county where the property sits.
  • The last publication (or posting, where no paper exists) must be at least 20 days before the sale.

Once the gavel falls, the statute's next step is possession, not a long waiting period. That is why we encourage Montana owners to call early in the 120 days, not the week before. A sale to us, or to any buyer, can pay the lender in full and leave the remaining equity with you. More on that in our page on selling a house before a foreclosure auction.

Property taxes: a lien with a three-year runway

Montana handles unpaid property taxes through a tax lien that attaches to the property. Under MCA 15-18-111, the owner can redeem by the first working day in August, three years after the lien attached. For certain vacant subdivision lots with special improvement district assessments, the period is two years. After that, the lien holder can pursue a tax deed.

A three-year window feels long, but it is easy to lose track of, especially on a cabin or rural parcel owned by someone who has died. If taxes on a family property are behind, check the county treasurer's records now.

Settling a Montana estate in District Court

Montana follows the Uniform Probate Code, and estates are handled by the District Court. Montana's small estate procedure, the affidavit under MCA 72-3-1101, can be used 30 days after the death when the probate estate, less liens and encumbrances, is $100,000 or less. It is written for tangible personal property and items like bank balances and stock, not for transferring land, so a house in the deceased person's name usually means opening a probate.

Montana's transfer-on-death deed

Montana adopted the Uniform Real Property Transfer on Death Act in 2019 (MCA 72-6-401 and following). Under MCA 72-6-408, the deed must state that the transfer happens at the owner's death and must be recorded with the county clerk and recorder before the owner dies. If you find one in the county records, the beneficiary may be able to sell without probate.

When the heirs or co-owners are split

Montana was an early adopter of the Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act, passing it in 2013 (Senate Bill 207). When a co-owner of inherited family land asks a court for a partition, the court determines the value, and the relatives who did not ask for a sale get first chance to buy out the share at that value. That matters when a family cabin or ranch parcel is co-owned by siblings scattered across several states.

If you simply want out of a co-owned Montana property, you may be able to sell your share without a lawsuit. See options when heirs disagree.

Divorce and long-distance owners

Montana is an equitable distribution state. In a divorce, MCA 40-4-202 directs the court to "finally equitably apportion" all property of either or both spouses, considering things like each spouse's income, needs, the length of the marriage and homemaking contributions. Equitable does not always mean equal.

When an owner cannot sign in person, because of distance, illness or incarceration, a power of attorney under Montana's Uniform Power of Attorney Act (MCA 72-31-305) can do the job. The signature is presumed genuine when the principal acknowledges it before a notary public, and in practice a notarized power is what a title company will accept for a deed. If the owner is in jail or prison, our page on selling when an owner is incarcerated explains how families arrange signatures. For estate, divorce or criminal-case questions, consult an attorney licensed in Montana. Pages of Purpose LLC is not a law firm.

Be honest with yourself about listing

A well-kept Montana home with clear title, owners who agree and no trustee's sale or tax deadline will almost always net more on the open market with a local agent. If that describes your situation, list it. Quick answers for Montana

How much notice is required before a Montana trustee's sale?

Under MCA 71-1-315, the notice must be mailed by certified mail at least 120 days before the sale and published once a week for three successive weeks, with the last publication at least 20 days before the sale.

When does the buyer at a Montana trustee's sale get possession?

Under MCA 71-1-319, the purchaser is entitled to possession on the 10th day after the sale.

How long is the Montana property tax lien redemption period?

Under MCA 15-18-111, redemption is due by the first working day in August three years after the tax lien attached, or two years for certain vacant subdivision lots.

Does Montana allow transfer-on-death deeds?

Yes. Montana adopted the Uniform Real Property Transfer on Death Act in 2019, and the deed must be recorded with the county clerk and recorder before the owner dies.
