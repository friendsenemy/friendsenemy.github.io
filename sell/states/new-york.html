Selling an Inherited or Contested House in New York State

The first surprise for most New York families is that the small-estate shortcut does not cover the house. Voluntary administration in Surrogate's Court is limited to $50,000 of personal property, and the law says it does not apply to any interest in real property (SCPA 1301 and 1302). So unless the house was in a trust, held jointly, or passed by a transfer-on-death deed, someone has to be appointed executor or administrator before it can be sold.

Surrogate's Court Letters Come Before the Sale

Each county has a Surrogate's Court. With a will, the named executor petitions for probate and receives letters testamentary. Without one, a family member petitions for letters of administration. Either way, the title company will want those letters before closing, along with proof that any required court authority to sell is in place.

Plan for the court calendar. Meanwhile, the house still needs insurance, heat in winter and taxes paid. If the estate has no cash, that pressure is often what pushes heirs to look at a quick sale or partnership.

The Transfer-on-Death Deed, With Two Witnesses

New York added transfer-on-death deeds in 2024 under Real Property Law section 424. They let an owner name who receives the house at death, outside probate. The requirements are stricter than in many states: the deed must be signed in front of two witnesses present at the same time, acknowledged before a notary, and recorded with the county clerk before the owner dies. A revocation also has to be recorded. If a parent signed one but it was never recorded, it does not work, and the house goes through Surrogate's Court.

Heirs Property Rules Slow Down a Forced Sale

New York adopted the Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act in 2020, in RPAPL section 993. When relatives own an inherited house together and one sues to force a sale, the court must set the value, usually by ordering an appraisal by a New York-licensed appraiser. The co-owners who did not ask for the sale then get 45 days after notice of the value to buy out the ones who did. The court must prefer dividing the property in kind unless that would cause great prejudice to the co-owners as a group, and it weighs family history and contributions to taxes and upkeep.

The result is that a sibling who wants out cannot quickly force a sale of a family home. A negotiated buyout is usually faster. We can buy one heir's share, purchase the whole house when the family agrees, or partner on a resale. Mortgage Foreclosure Is Judicial and Slow

New York requires a court action to foreclose. Before filing on a home loan, the lender must send a notice at least 90 days ahead (RPAPL 1304), unless the borrower is in bankruptcy or no longer lives there. The court process that follows is often long. For an heir carrying a parent's unpaid mortgage, that time is useful for selling while there is still equity. See selling before a foreclosure sale for practical steps.

Back Taxes and the Two-Year Redemption Period

Most counties enforce unpaid property taxes through an in-rem foreclosure under Article 11 of the Real Property Tax Law. RPTL 1110 sets the default: the redemption period expires two years after the lien date, although a tax district can change that period in some cases. After it expires, the county can start a proceeding to take title. Ask the county treasurer for the lien date and the exact payoff before you decide anything. If the house was a parent's and the estate has no cash, a sale before the period runs out lets the heirs pay the taxes from the proceeds instead of losing the property.

Spouses: Entireties Ownership and Divorce

A deed to a married couple in New York creates a tenancy by the entirety unless it says otherwise (EPTL 6-2.2). When one spouse dies, the survivor owns the whole house without probate. In divorce, marital property is distributed equitably under Domestic Relations Law section 236, which is not always 50/50. Until the court rules or the spouses agree, a sale normally needs both signatures.

Owner in Prison

New York's statutory short form power of attorney must be signed, acknowledged and witnessed by two people who are not named as agents or gift recipients (General Obligations Law 5-1501B). Getting two witnesses and a notary into a facility at the same time takes planning, so start early. Our general guide to selling a house while in jail lists what to gather.

Quick answers for New York

Can a New York small estate affidavit transfer a house?

No. Voluntary administration in Surrogate's Court covers up to $50,000 of personal property and does not apply to real property, so someone must usually be appointed executor or administrator.

What does a New York transfer-on-death deed require?

It must be signed before two witnesses present at the same time, acknowledged before a notary and recorded with the county clerk before the owner dies, under Real Property Law section 424.

Can my sibling force the sale of our inherited New York house?

Not quickly. Under RPAPL 993 the court sets the value, and co-owners who did not ask for a sale get 45 days to buy out those who did.

How long is the redemption period for unpaid New York property taxes?

Under RPTL 1110 the default redemption period expires two years after the lien date, though a tax district can change it in some cases.
