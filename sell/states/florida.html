Selling a Complicated Florida House When Homestead Law Applies

Florida homestead rules decide who can sell before anything else does

Most states let a will control who inherits a house. Florida often does not. Under Florida Statutes section 732.4015, a homestead cannot be left by will if the owner is survived by a spouse or a minor child, with one exception: it can go to the spouse when there are no minor children. A will that leaves Grandma's house to one grandson may simply not work.

When the homestead passes outside the will, section 732.401 gives the surviving spouse a life estate and the descendants the remainder. The spouse can instead elect an undivided one-half share as a tenant in common, but that election must be made within 6 months after the death and recorded. If you are a son or daughter trying to sell, find out early whether the spouse made that election, because it changes who must sign.

Probate court, summary administration and the two-year rule

Florida estates are handled in the probate division of the circuit court in the county where the person lived. Full administration means a personal representative is appointed and the court supervises the sale if needed.

Summary administration is shorter. Under section 735.201 it is available when the estate, not counting property exempt from creditors, is worth $75,000 or less, or when the person has been dead for more than two years. That second option surprises families: if a parent died years ago and nobody opened probate, the faster route may already be open.

Florida has no statutory transfer-on-death deed. Many owners instead sign an enhanced life estate deed, often called a "lady bird" deed, which can move the house at death without probate. Always pull the last recorded deed before assuming the house is in the estate.

When Florida heirs or co-owners will not agree

Florida adopted the Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act in 2020. For inherited property owned by relatives as tenants in common, the court must first set the fair market value by appraisal or agreement, the other cotenants get a chance to buy out the person asking for partition, and a forced sale generally goes to the open market through a court-appointed broker instead of the courthouse steps.

That protects families, but it also means a partition case takes time and money. Our guide on Florida heirs who cannot agree covers the homestead twist, and our heirs-disagree page explains how we buy one share or the whole house so the family can settle without a trial.

Back taxes: Florida sells tax certificates, then tax deeds

Florida counties do not sell the house when taxes go unpaid. They sell a tax certificate, which is a lien that earns the investor interest of up to 18% a year. The house is at risk later: under section 197.502, a certificate holder can apply for a tax deed any time after 2 years have passed since April 1 of the year the certificate was issued. After that, the property can go to a public tax deed sale.

The fix before that point is simple: pay the delinquent taxes and interest to the tax collector. If nobody in the family can, selling before the tax deed application protects the equity.

Foreclosure goes through court in Florida

Florida forecloses judicially. Once the judge signs a final judgment, section 45.031 sets the clerk's sale not less than 20 or more than 35 days later unless the lender agrees to a later date. Your right to redeem ends when the clerk files the certificate of sale (or a later date in the judgment). A sale that closes before then can pay off the loan and keep the remaining equity in your pocket. Our guide on selling a Miami home before foreclosure walks through that timing in South Florida.

An owner in a Florida jail or prison

Florida is strict about powers of attorney. Section 709.2105 requires the person to sign in front of two subscribing witnesses and acknowledge it before a notary. A POA missing a witness will be rejected by a title company. If the owner is incarcerated, line up both witnesses and the notary with the facility ahead of time. Our Florida jail and prison guide covers the paperwork.

Divorce and deed taxes

Florida divides marital property by equitable distribution and recognizes tenancy by the entirety for married couples, so a house titled to both spouses generally needs both signatures. On any sale, Florida charges documentary stamp tax on the deed at 70 cents per $100 of price under section 201.02, usually paid by the seller.

When a regular Florida listing is the better choice

If the house shows well, every owner agrees, probate is finished or not needed, and you have no tax or foreclosure clock running, list it with a good local agent. Florida's retail buyers will usually pay more than an investor can.

Quick answers for Florida

Can a Florida homestead be left to anyone in a will?

Not always. Under Florida Statutes 732.4015, a homestead cannot be devised if the owner is survived by a spouse or minor child, except to the spouse when there are no minor children.

How long does a Florida surviving spouse have to elect a half share of the homestead?

Under section 732.401, the spouse must elect within 6 months after the death to take an undivided one-half interest as a tenant in common instead of a life estate.

When can a Florida tax certificate holder take the house?

The holder can apply for a tax deed any time after 2 years have passed since April 1 of the year the certificate was issued, and the property can then go to a tax deed sale.

Does Florida allow summary administration for older estates?

Yes. Summary administration is available when the estate is worth $75,000 or less, not counting exempt property, or when the person has been dead for more than two years.
