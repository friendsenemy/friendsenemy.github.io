Maine Houses Facing Tax Liens, Probate or Family Disputes

Maine's property tax system has a feature that catches families off guard: the foreclosure happens automatically. Once the town files a tax lien certificate at the registry of deeds, the lien works like a mortgage in the town's favor. If the taxes, interest and costs are not paid within 18 months of filing, the lien is treated as foreclosed under 36 M.R.S. §943, and the right to redeem ends. There is no court hearing and no auction date to fight. An heir who learns about the lien late can find that the town already owns the house.

The Maine tax lien clock, step by step

  1. The town files a tax lien certificate at the registry of deeds. That filing creates the lien mortgage and serves as notice.
  2. The town cannot take possession while the 18-month redemption period runs.
  3. Between 45 and 30 days before the foreclosure date, the treasurer must send written notice to the person named on the lien and each recorded mortgage holder, giving the exact foreclosure date.
  4. If that notice was not given on time, the person who missed it can still redeem until 30 days after proper notice is given.
  5. If nobody pays by the date, the lien is foreclosed and the town holds title.

What changed for the excess value

Maine now requires towns that sell foreclosed tax property to someone other than the former owner to follow a set process under 36 M.R.S. §943-C. The town must send the former owner notice at least 90 days before listing, list the house with a licensed real estate broker at the highest reasonable price, and pay the former owner the excess proceeds after taxes, interest, fees and costs. If the town keeps the property, it pays the excess based on an independent appraisal.

That protects some equity, but a broker sale after foreclosure is not under your control. Selling before the foreclosure date usually keeps more money in the family's hands and avoids months of waiting for an accounting. If a lien is on the house, call the town office for the exact redemption amount and date today.

Inherited houses: Maine's Probate Court and the shortcuts that exist

Maine estates go through the Probate Court, with a separate court in each county. Since Maine's updated Probate Code (Title 18-C) took effect, families have several ways to handle a house.

  • Transfer-on-death deed. Title 18-C includes the Uniform Real Property Transfer on Death Act. A TOD deed passes the house to a named beneficiary at death, but only if the owner recorded it at the county registry of deeds before dying.
  • Small estate affidavit. Under 18-C §3-1201, an affidavit can collect personal property when the entire estate, less liens and encumbrances, is no more than $40,000, adjusted for inflation, and at least 30 days have passed since death. The house's value counts toward that limit, and the affidavit itself only reaches personal property.
  • A personal representative. When a house is in the estate, the usual path is to have a personal representative appointed, who can then sign the deed.

Our page on selling an inherited or probate house explains the sale from the personal representative's side.

Heirs who can't agree on the camp, the farm or Mom's house

Maine gives families a partition option inside the estate itself. Under 18-C §3-911, before the estate closes, the personal representative or any heir or devisee can ask the Probate Court to partition property that two or more of them inherited together. Not everyone has to agree. If the property cannot be divided without harm and cannot be given to one person, the court can direct the personal representative to sell it.

Outside an estate, co-owners who hold as tenants in common or joint tenants can be made to divide the property through a civil partition action under 14 M.R.S. §6501. Either way, a buyout between relatives is often faster and cheaper. Read more on selling when heirs disagree.

A Maine owner who is incarcerated

Under 18-C §5-905, a Maine power of attorney is not valid unless it is acknowledged before a notary public or another official allowed to take acknowledgments. A durable power of attorney also has to include specific notices to the principal and the agent, or it is invalid. A form from another state may lack those notices. Ask the jail or prison how legal papers are notarized, and have a Maine attorney check the form before it is mailed. See our page on selling when the owner is in jail or prison.

Divorce: dividing a Maine home in "just" proportions

Under 19-A M.R.S. §953, the court first sets aside each spouse's own property, then divides marital property in proportions it considers just. Inherited and gifted property generally stays separate. The statute lists the desirability of awarding the family home, or the right to live in it for a time, to the spouse who has custody of the children as a factor. So a sale may be delayed rather than ordered.

Mortgage foreclosure: mediation, then 90 days

Maine mortgage foreclosures go through court. For owner-occupied homes of up to four units, mediation is required when the homeowner returns the notice form, asks for it or appears in the case, and no final judgment can enter until the mediator reports. After judgment, the owner has 90 days to redeem under 14 M.R.S. §6322 before the lender proceeds to a public sale. That 90-day window is often the last practical time to sell.

When listing with a Maine agent makes more sense

If the taxes are current, the estate is open or not needed, the owners agree and the house is ready to show, a Maine real estate agent will usually get you a higher price than a direct sale. Quick answers for Maine

How long until a Maine tax lien forecloses?

If the taxes, interest and costs are not paid within 18 months after the town files the tax lien certificate, the lien is treated as foreclosed under 36 M.R.S. §943, with no court hearing.

Does a Maine town keep all the money if it sells a tax-foreclosed house?

No. Under 36 M.R.S. §943-C, a town that sells to someone other than the former owner must list it with a licensed broker and pay the former owner the excess proceeds after taxes and costs.

Does Maine allow transfer-on-death deeds?

Yes. Maine's Probate Code includes the Uniform Real Property Transfer on Death Act, and the deed must be recorded at the county registry of deeds before the owner dies.

How long is the redemption period after a Maine foreclosure judgment?

The owner has 90 days from the foreclosure judgment to redeem under 14 M.R.S. §6322 before the lender proceeds to a public sale.
