Selling an Inherited or Tangled Oregon House: What State Law Allows

Oregon does something many states do not: its small estate affidavit can reach a house. Under ORS 114.510 and 114.515, an estate can use the affidavit when it holds no more than $75,000 of personal property and no more than $200,000 of real property, measured at fair market value. If a parent's house fits under that line, the family may be able to transfer it without opening a full probate. If it does not, there are still faster and slower roads, and this page explains them.

Oregon's Affidavit Path for Modest Estates

The affidavit is filed with the clerk of the probate court in a county where a probate could be opened. In most of Oregon that court is the circuit court. Under ORS 111.075, six counties are different: Gilliam, Grant, Harney, Malheur, Sherman and Wheeler, where the county court handles probate.

The two caps are separate. An estate with a $190,000 house and $40,000 in a bank account can qualify. An estate with a $260,000 house cannot, even if there is nothing else. The value counted is fair market value, not the lower assessed value on the tax bill, so get a realistic number before deciding.

When full probate is the only road

Once a house is over the cap, a personal representative has to be appointed by the court before anyone can sign a listing or a deed. Our guide to selling an inherited Portland house through probate walks through that timeline in detail.

Deeds that skip probate

Oregon also allows transfer-on-death deeds, covered in ORS 93.948 to 93.979. If the owner recorded one, the named beneficiary may not need probate for the house at all. Check the county recorder's records before filing anything.

Oregon's Estate Tax Starts at $1 Million

Federal estate tax affects very few families, but Oregon's is different. Under ORS 118.010, Oregon's tax applies to taxable estates of $1,000,000 or more, with rates starting at 10 percent and rising to 16 percent. A paid-off house plus retirement accounts can cross that line. When an estate owes this tax, the house often has to be sold on a schedule to pay it. A tax professional or Oregon estate attorney can tell you early whether it applies.

Co-Owners and Spouses

When two or more people inherit, they usually own as tenants in common. Under ORS 105.205, any co-owner can file for partition in circuit court and ask for the house to be sold. These cases follow the partition statutes, ORS 105.205 to 105.405, and under ORS 105.405 shared costs, including some attorney fees, are split by ownership share. Our article on co-owned Portland houses, partition and buyouts covers the options short of a lawsuit.

Married owners

Under ORS 93.180, a deed to spouses married to each other creates a tenancy by the entirety unless it clearly says otherwise. One spouse cannot sell that house alone.

Divorce

Oregon is not a community property state. In a divorce, ORS 107.105 directs the court to divide property in a way that is "just and proper in all the circumstances," starting from a presumption that both spouses contributed equally to property acquired during the marriage. Inheritances and gifts from third parties are treated differently.

Trustee Sales Move Fast in Oregon

Most Oregon home loans are secured by a trust deed, and most foreclosures happen outside court through a trustee's sale. Before a residential trustee foreclosure, ORS 86.726 generally requires the lender to request a resolution conference with the borrower through a state service provider, unless the lender qualifies for an exemption.

After that, the trustee must serve or mail the notice of sale at least 120 days before the sale date. Under ORS 86.771, the owner can reinstate the loan by curing the default no later than five days before the last scheduled sale date. Once a trustee's sale happens, there is generally no right to buy the house back, so the time to sell is during that 120-day window. Read our guide on selling to stop a foreclosure for how that timing usually plays out.

Unpaid Property Taxes and the County

Oregon does not sell tax liens to investors. Instead, under ORS 312.010, the county can foreclose once three years have passed since the earliest delinquency. After the foreclosure judgment, ORS 312.120 gives former owners two years to redeem by paying what is owed plus a 5 percent penalty and a fee. Then the county takes a deed.

A 2025 law, codified at ORS 312.500 to 312.560, now entitles former owners to claim any surplus value left after the county's costs, with claims handled through the State Treasurer. That is better than before, but selling during the redemption period almost always keeps more money in the family's hands.

Signing From a Jail or Prison Cell

If an owner or heir is incarcerated, they can still sign. Most families use a power of attorney so a relative can sign the deed and closing papers. Oregon's statute requires the power of attorney to be in writing, and in practice a title company will want it signed before a notary so it can be recorded with the deed. Each facility sets its own rules for notary access, so ask the facility's records or legal office early.

Where an Oregon Family Should Start

  1. Pull the deed and check for a recorded transfer-on-death deed.
  2. Get a realistic market value and compare it with the $200,000 real property cap.
  3. Check the tax account with the county assessor and tax collector.
  4. Talk to an attorney licensed in Oregon about the estate, a divorce or a pending criminal case.

Quick answers for Oregon

Can an Oregon small estate affidavit transfer a house?

Yes, if the estate has no more than $200,000 of real property and no more than $75,000 of personal property at fair market value under ORS 114.510. Larger estates need a court-appointed personal representative.

Which court handles probate in Oregon?

The circuit court handles probate in most Oregon counties. Under ORS 111.075, the county court handles it in Gilliam, Grant, Harney, Malheur, Sherman and Wheeler counties.

How long does an Oregon homeowner have before a trustee's sale?

The notice of sale must be served or mailed at least 120 days before the sale. The owner can cure the default and reinstate the loan up to five days before the last scheduled sale date.

What happens if Oregon property taxes go unpaid?

The county can foreclose after three years of delinquency, and former owners then have two years after the judgment to redeem by paying the taxes plus a 5 percent penalty and a fee.
