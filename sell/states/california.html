Selling a Stuck California House: Co-Owners, Probate, Divorce and Tax Default

Since 2023, California partition protects nearly every co-owner

When two or more people own a California house and one wants to sell, the next step used to be a fairly blunt partition lawsuit. That changed on January 1, 2023. AB 2245 renamed the state's heirs' property law the Partition of Real Property Act and widened it to cover any real property held as tenants in common when there is no written agreement binding all the co-owners that governs partition (Code of Civil Procedure § 874.311). It applies to partition cases filed on or after that date.

In practice, siblings who inherited a house, unmarried partners and friends who bought together now get the act's process: the court determines the property's value, the co-owners who want to keep it get a chance to buy out the one asking for a sale, and the court weighs whether a sale on the open market will bring a fair price. That is good for fairness and slower for everyone.

What to check before anyone files

  • Pull the recorded deed and confirm the owners hold title as tenants in common.
  • Look for any signed co-ownership or partnership agreement. If one binds every owner and governs partition, the act may not apply.
  • Write down what each owner has paid toward the mortgage, taxes and repairs, since those payments often come up in settlement talks.
  • Ask each co-owner, in writing, whether they want to keep the house or be bought out.

A negotiated buyout of one co-owner's share often ends the dispute before a filing. Our guides on selling a co-owned California house under Prop 13 and Prop 19 and heirs who cannot agree on a California house cover the tax side too.

Inherited home worth up to $750,000: a simpler court petition

A full California probate can take a long time. Probate Code § 13151, as amended by AB 2016 effective January 1, 2025, offers a shorter path for the deceased person's primary residence. If that home's gross value is $750,000 or less (adjusted periodically), and 40 days have passed since the death, the heir can petition the superior court in the right county for an order confirming they have succeeded to the property. The petitioner must send notice to each heir and beneficiary named in the petition within five business days of filing.

The separate small estate affidavit under § 13100, with a statutory base of $166,250 adjusted periodically, is mainly used to collect personal property such as accounts and vehicles.

Revocable transfer-on-death deeds

California allows revocable transfer-on-death deeds, but the law that created them is set to be repealed on January 1, 2032 unless the Legislature extends it. If a parent recorded one, the house may pass outside probate. Check the county recorder's index before assuming you need a court case.

Divorce in a community property state

California Family Code § 2550 requires the court to divide the community estate equally unless the spouses agree otherwise. A house bought during the marriage is usually community property even if only one name is on the deed, and both spouses normally sign a sale while the case is open. Our guide to selling during a California divorce, including restraining orders and credits explains the steps.

Owner in jail or prison: notary or two witnesses

Under Probate Code § 4121, a California power of attorney must be either acknowledged before a notary public or signed by at least two qualified witnesses. For a real estate sale, the title company will want the notarized version so it can be recorded. Ask the facility how notary visits are arranged before mailing documents.

Foreclosure: at least three months after the notice of default

Most California foreclosures are handled by a trustee outside court. Civil Code § 2924 requires at least three months between recording the notice of default and the next step, the notice of sale. Those three months are usually the best time to sell, because the equity is still yours.

Back taxes: five years before the power to sell

California does not sell tax liens. Unpaid taxes stay on the county's books, and under Revenue and Taxation Code § 3691 a home becomes subject to the tax collector's power to sell once it has been tax-defaulted for five years or more. Nonresidential commercial property can reach that point in three years unless the county applies the five-year rule. Inherited houses often sit in that five-year window while the family sorts out probate.

When a California listing will net more

If the house is in good shape, all owners agree, and no foreclosure or tax sale is near, a local California agent will usually get you more than a cash or partnership offer. We are the better fit when a partition case, a probate petition, a missing heir or major repairs is holding things up.
