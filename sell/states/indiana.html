Selling an Inherited or Troubled House in Indiana

Indiana heirs can sometimes transfer a house by recorded affidavit

Here is the fact that surprises most Indiana families: in many states a small estate affidavit cannot touch real estate, but Indiana's can. Under Indiana Code 29-1-8-3, when the estate qualifies to be settled without administration, heirs can record an affidavit with the county recorder where the house sits. It lists the legal description, each person entitled to an interest, the share each one gets, and how those shares were figured.

The dollar limit depends on the date of death. For deaths after June 30, 2022, the gross probate estate, less liens and encumbrances, can be up to $100,000, plus administration costs and reasonable funeral expenses. For deaths from mid-2006 through June 30, 2022 the figure is $50,000, and before that $25,000. The general small estate affidavit under 29-1-8-1 cannot be used until 45 days after the death.

Because the limit is measured net of the mortgage, a modest Indiana house with a loan on it may qualify even if its market value is above $100,000. Run the numbers with an Indiana attorney before you assume you need full probate.

When the affidavit works, and when it does not

The affidavit works best when heirs are known and agree. Its weak points:

  • Every heir ends up on title. If there are six siblings, all six must sign the eventual deed.
  • A missing, deceased or uncooperative heir still blocks a sale.
  • Title companies review these affidavits closely, and an error in the heir list can cloud title for years.

When the affidavit will not work, Indiana probate is handled in the county's circuit or superior court. Indiana offers unsupervised administration under Indiana Code 29-1-7.5: if the estate is solvent, the personal representative is qualified, and the heirs consent (or the will authorizes it), the representative can sell the house without asking the court to approve each step.

Transfer on death deeds

Indiana's Transfer on Death Property Act (Indiana Code 32-17-14) allows TOD deeds. If the owner signed one, the house may pass to the beneficiary without probate or an affidavit at all. Search the county recorder's index under the owner's name.

Heirs who cannot agree

When co-owners in Indiana deadlock, any of them can file a partition proceeding under Indiana Code 32-17-4. The court can divide the property or order a sale, and with a single house, a sale is the usual result. Lawyers' fees and costs come out before the family sees anything. Buying out one heir's share is often the faster route. Our page on selling when heirs disagree explains how we buy a share or the whole house.

Foreclosure: a settlement conference and a three-month wait

Indiana forecloses through the courts, and the law gives owners two useful pauses. First, for a primary residence, the lender must mail a pre-suit notice at least 30 days before filing, and the summons must tell the owner about the right to a settlement conference, which the owner requests by notifying the court within 30 days after service (Indiana Code 32-30-10.5-8). Second, under Indiana Code 32-29-7-3, a sheriff's sale generally cannot be ordered until three months after the complaint is filed, then must take place within 120 days of the judgment being certified to the sheriff, with notice published at least 30 days ahead.

That combined time is often enough to sell before the sheriff's sale. See our page on selling before a foreclosure sale.

Back taxes: a one-year redemption after the tax sale

Indiana sells tax sale certificates on delinquent properties. Under Indiana Code 6-1.1-25-4, the owner generally has one year after the sale to redeem. That shrinks to 120 days in certain cases, such as when the county executive takes the lien, and property on a vacant-and-abandoned list has no redemption right. After the window closes, the buyer can seek a tax deed.

Divorce and an owner in custody

Divorce

Indiana courts start from a presumption, under Indiana Code 31-15-7-5, that an equal division of marital property is just and reasonable; either spouse can argue for something different. Indiana also recognizes tenancy by the entirety for married owners, so a house deeded to both spouses generally needs both signatures.

An owner who is incarcerated

An Indiana owner in jail or prison can sell through a power of attorney. Under Indiana Code 30-5-4-1, the document must be signed in front of a notary public or in front of qualifying witnesses. For real estate, title companies expect the notarized version. Arrange the notary with the facility ahead of time.

When a traditional Indiana sale is the better deal

If the house is in good condition, the heirs agree, the affidavit or estate is in place, and no foreclosure or tax deadline is pressing, a local agent will usually net more than an investor. Indianapolis, Fort Wayne and the suburbs all draw retail buyers for clean homes.

Quick answers for Indiana

Can an Indiana small estate affidavit transfer a house?

Yes, when the estate qualifies. Indiana Code 29-1-8-3 lets heirs record an affidavit with the county recorder listing the legal description and each heir's share.

What is the Indiana small estate limit?

For deaths after June 30, 2022, the gross probate estate less liens and encumbrances can be up to $100,000, plus administration costs and reasonable funeral expenses. The affidavit cannot be used until 45 days after death.

How soon can an Indiana sheriff's sale happen after foreclosure is filed?

Under Indiana Code 32-29-7-3, a sheriff's sale generally cannot be ordered until three months after the complaint is filed, and notice must be published at least 30 days before the sale.

How long do Indiana owners have to redeem after a tax sale?

Under Indiana Code 6-1.1-25-4, the owner generally has one year after the tax sale to redeem, shortened to 120 days in some cases such as when the county executive takes the lien.
