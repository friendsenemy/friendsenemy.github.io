South Dakota Houses in Foreclosure, Estates and Family Disputes

Losing a South Dakota house at a foreclosure sale is not always the end of the story. Under SDCL 21-52-11, the owner generally has one year from the date of the sale to redeem the property. The exception is a mortgage written as a 180-day redemption mortgage, where the window is 180 days. That window changes the math for anyone trying to save equity.

This page covers that rule and the other South Dakota laws that decide how a complicated house gets sold: inherited homes, heirs who disagree, divorce, an owner in custody and unpaid taxes. Foreclosure by Advertisement and the Year That Follows

South Dakota allows two kinds of foreclosure. A lender can sue in circuit court, or, if the mortgage contains a power of sale, it can foreclose by advertisement under SDCL 21-48-1 without a lawsuit. Either way, the redemption period after the sale is set by statute.

What redemption means in practice

During the redemption year, the owner can buy the house back by paying what the law requires. Most families cannot raise that money. But the period also gives time to sell. A buyer who pays more than the redemption amount leaves the difference with the owner rather than with the auction purchaser.

Waiting until after the sale is still risky. Interest and costs keep growing, and a 180-day redemption mortgage cuts the time in half. Read your mortgage to see which kind you have. The best time to sell is before the sale date. Our guide on selling a house to stop foreclosure and our page on selling before a foreclosure auction walk through the timing.

Inherited Houses: Two Affidavits and a Deed

South Dakota estates are handled by the circuit court; the state's probate code defines "court" that way in SDCL 29A-1-201. Before opening a full probate, look at three shortcuts.

A real property affidavit for small interests

Under SDCL 29A-3-1203, 60 days after death, the heirs can file an affidavit with the register of deeds in each county where the land is, if the person's interest in all South Dakota real property is no more than $50,000. Non-farm property can be valued using the assessment rolls for the year of death. All claiming heirs must sign, and the affidavit must state that the estate owes nothing to the Department of Social Services for nursing home or similar care.

The $100,000 personal property affidavit

Under SDCL 29A-3-1201, 30 days after death, an heir can collect personal property such as bank accounts and vehicles by affidavit when the whole estate, less liens, is worth $100,000 or less. It does not list real estate among the assets it collects, so the house usually needs the separate real property affidavit, a deed, or probate.

Transfer-on-death deeds

South Dakota has adopted the Uniform Real Property Transfer on Death Act, SDCL 29A-6-401 to 29A-6-435. Under 29A-6-408, the deed must be recorded before the owner's death. If it was, the beneficiary can confirm the transfer with an affidavit and sell without probate.

When Heirs and Co-Owners Disagree

Any co-owner can bring a partition action under SDCL chapter 21-45. The court can divide the land, allow an unequal division balanced by payments between the owners under 21-45-19, or order a sale at public auction when division is not practical. Under 21-45-34, a co-owner can bid on the property at that sale.

A public auction rarely brings full value. Often it makes more sense for the heirs who want out to sell their shares, or for one heir to buy the rest. Our page on buying houses when heirs disagree explains how a share purchase works.

Divorce

South Dakota is not a community property state. Under SDCL 25-4-44, a divorce court makes an equitable division of the property belonging to either or both spouses, whoever's name is on it. If the house is part of the case, a sale normally waits for a court order or a signed agreement.

An Owner in Jail or Prison

A person in custody keeps ownership of their house and can sell it. The usual route is a power of attorney naming a trusted relative to sign the deed. For a title company to accept it, plan on the owner signing before a notary so the document can be recorded with the register of deeds. Facilities set their own rules for notary access, so ask early. Our guide to getting a notary in jail or prison lists the questions to ask, and our page on buying when the owner is incarcerated explains how we close these sales.

Unpaid Taxes and Tax Certificates

When property taxes go unpaid in South Dakota, the county treasurer sells a tax certificate. The owner can still pay off the taxes, but under SDCL 10-25-1, the certificate holder can start a tax deed proceeding three years after the certificate sale, and may do so at any time within six years of the sale. The notice of intent to take a tax deed must be served on the owner of record and the person in possession the same way a court summons is served, under 10-25-5.

If you get that notice, act quickly. Once a tax deed issues, the family loses the house and the equity in it.

Quick answers for South Dakota

How long can I redeem my house after a South Dakota foreclosure sale?

Under SDCL 21-52-11, the owner generally has one year from the sale to redeem. If the mortgage is a 180-day redemption mortgage, the period is 180 days.

Can a South Dakota house pass by affidavit without probate?

Yes, if the person's interest in all South Dakota real property is $50,000 or less. Under SDCL 29A-3-1203, the heirs can file an affidavit with the register of deeds 60 days after death.

Does South Dakota allow transfer-on-death deeds?

Yes. South Dakota adopted the Uniform Real Property Transfer on Death Act, and the deed must be recorded before the owner's death under SDCL 29A-6-408.

When can a South Dakota tax certificate holder take the house?

The certificate holder can start a tax deed proceeding three years after the certificate sale, and within six years of it, under SDCL 10-25-1. The notice must be served on the owner and the person in possession.
