Washington State Houses: Spouses, Estates and Trustee's Sale Deadlines

Washington's community property law reaches into almost every complicated home sale in the state. RCW 26.16.030(3) says neither spouse or registered domestic partner may sell, convey or encumber community real property unless the other joins in signing the deed, and both must acknowledge it. That one rule decides what happens when a couple separates, when one spouse is incarcerated and when a spouse dies with the house in one name.

Community Property Means Two Signatures

A house bought during the marriage is usually community property, even if only one spouse is on the deed. A title company in Washington will normally ask about marital status and, if the house looks like community property, require the other spouse to sign. If they will not or cannot, the usual routes are:

  • a power of attorney from the absent spouse,
  • a court order in a pending dissolution case, or
  • a document showing the house is actually separate property, such as a recorded quitclaim or a prenuptial agreement.

Separated couples are sometimes surprised that a long separation does not by itself remove a spouse from the signature line. Our page on selling a house during a divorce explains how a sale can be timed with the case.

If the absent spouse is in jail or prison

Washington's power of attorney statute, RCW 11.125.050, lets a principal sign either before a notary or in front of two qualified witnesses who are not related to the principal or agent. For a deed, title companies generally want a notarized power of attorney they can record. Facilities set their own rules on notary visits and legal mail, so call ahead before you send documents in.

Inherited Houses and Nonintervention Powers

Washington estates are handled in the superior court of the county. Washington's small estate affidavit under RCW 11.62.010 is for personal property, with a limit of $100,000 after liens, so it does not move a house. Most inherited homes therefore need a personal representative.

The good news is that Washington lets a personal representative ask for nonintervention powers under RCW 11.68.011, whether or not there was a will. With those powers, the personal representative can usually sell the house without returning to court for approval of each step. That makes a Washington estate sale faster than in many states once the appointment is in place. If a spouse survives, community property rules also mean the spouse may already own half of the house outright.

Washington has allowed transfer on death deeds since 2014 under chapter 64.80 RCW. If one was recorded, the named beneficiary can usually sell without probate. Our page on inherited and probate houses covers how a sale fits around the court process.

Heirs and Co-Owners Who Disagree

Any co-owner of a Washington house can ask the superior court for partition. Washington adopted the Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act in 2023. For qualifying inherited property, it adds a court-ordered appraisal, a buyout right for the other owners and a preference for an open-market listing over an auction. Many cases settle when one heir sells a share to a buyer or to the others, so ask for numbers before you ask for a lawsuit.

Foreclosure Under the Deed of Trust Act

Most Washington home loans are foreclosed out of court by a trustee under chapter 61.24 RCW. A few dates that set the pace:

  1. RCW 61.24.030 requires a written notice of default to be sent at least 30 days before the notice of sale is recorded or served.
  2. The sale itself cannot take place less than 190 days from the date of default.
  3. Homeowners may be eligible for the state's foreclosure mediation program, and the notice must tell them to contact a housing counselor or a Washington attorney.

Owners with equity have time to sell during that stretch if they start early. Once the trustee's sale happens, choices narrow quickly.

Unpaid Property Taxes

Washington counties do not sell tax liens to private investors. Under RCW 84.64.050, after three years of delinquency the county treasurer issues certificates of delinquency to the county and starts a foreclosure that can end in a county tax sale. Inherited houses are often the ones that drift into year three, because nobody is opening the mail. Paying the delinquent years, or selling before the treasurer's foreclosure judgment, protects the equity.

Times a Washington Listing Is the Better Choice

If both spouses or all the heirs agree, the house is in good condition and there is no trustee's sale or tax foreclosure scheduled, list it with a Washington agent. That will usually bring more money than a direct sale. A cash or partnership offer is worth considering when a spouse cannot sign without a power of attorney or court order, the estate has no one appointed yet, the house needs major repairs or the 190-day foreclosure clock is well underway.

Quick answers for Washington

Do both spouses have to sign to sell a house in Washington?

For community real property, yes. RCW 26.16.030(3) says neither spouse or registered domestic partner may sell or encumber it unless the other joins in the deed, and both must acknowledge it.

Can a Washington small estate affidavit transfer a house?

No. The affidavit under RCW 11.62.010 is for personal property in estates worth $100,000 or less after liens, so an inherited house usually needs a personal representative.

What are nonintervention powers in a Washington estate?

Under RCW 11.68.011 a personal representative can ask the court for nonintervention powers, with or without a will, which usually lets them sell the house without returning to court for each step.

How soon can a Washington trustee's sale happen after default?

Not less than 190 days from the date of default, and a written notice of default must be sent at least 30 days before the notice of sale.
