Utah Homes Facing a Sale Date, an Estate or a Family Split

Utah is unforgiving once a sale actually happens. After a nonjudicial trustee's sale on a deed of trust, Utah law gives the former owner no period to buy the house back, and Utah County's auditor states plainly that property sold at its May tax sale may only be redeemed up to the time of the sale. Everything useful you can do in Utah happens before the auction, so the dates on the notices matter more than anything else on this page.

No Second Chance After a Utah Auction

Trust deed foreclosure, step by step

Most Utah home loans are secured by a trust deed with a power of sale, so the lender does not need to sue. The usual sequence looks like this:

  1. The servicer sends a preforeclosure notice giving at least 30 days to cure.
  2. The trustee records a notice of default with the county recorder.
  3. At least three months must pass after the notice of default before a notice of sale is given. During that time you can usually reinstate by catching up the missed payments and costs.
  4. The notice of sale is published and posted on the property at least 20 days before the auction.
  5. The trustee's sale takes place, and there is no post-sale redemption.

The three months after the notice of default are the real window. That is when a sale, a refinance or a payoff from a buyer can still protect the equity. Our guide to selling before a foreclosure auction lists the papers to gather first.

Years of unpaid property tax, then one auction

Utah does not sell tax liens to investors. Unpaid property taxes stay with the county until the property is offered at the county's annual tax sale, which Utah County holds in May. Utah County describes the properties in its sale as at least five years delinquent and says the owner can redeem before the sale by paying the full delinquent amount, but not afterward. If you inherited a house and the taxes have not been paid since the death, check the county treasurer's balance today.

A Utah House Usually Needs a Probate Case

Utah's small estate affidavit covers estates worth under $100,000, but the Utah courts' self-help guidance says it cannot be used to transfer title to land or a house, and one of its conditions is that the estate has no real property. So when a parent dies owning a Utah home in their own name, someone usually opens a probate case in the district court and is appointed personal representative. Many uncontested Utah estates use informal probate, which is handled on paper without a hearing in the typical case.

Two things can avoid that step. Utah allows transfer on death deeds, so check the county recorder's index for one signed before the death. And a house held in a living trust is sold by the trustee, not through probate. Our page on probate and inherited houses explains how a sale fits around the court timeline.

Siblings and Co-Owners Who Disagree

Any co-owner of a Utah house can ask the district court to partition it, either by dividing it or, more often for a single home, by ordering a sale. Utah adopted the Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act in 2022. When the house qualifies as heirs property, the court orders an appraisal, gives the other owners a chance to buy out the co-owner who asked for the sale, and prefers an open-market sale through an agent over an auction.

A buyer can also purchase one heir's share and then work out a buyout or a joint sale with the others. Our page for families where heirs disagree shows how that works in practice.

When the Owner Is Incarcerated

Utah's power of attorney statute requires the document to be signed before a notary public or another person authorized to take acknowledgments, and the principal must have capacity when signing. For a deed, title companies generally want that notarized power of attorney recorded with the county recorder before or with the deed. Each jail and prison sets its own rules for notary visits and legal mail, so call the facility first. If the owner is married, the spouse's interest in the home still needs to be addressed separately.

Divorce and a Utah House

Utah divides marital property equitably in a divorce rather than under community property rules. In practice the decree usually says whether one spouse keeps the house, refinances and pays the other, or the house is sold and the proceeds split. If a foreclosure notice arrives in the middle of the case, both spouses usually need to act quickly, because the three-month window keeps running while the court case is pending.

Choosing Between a Listing and a Direct Sale in Utah

If the house shows well, everyone on title agrees and there is no notice of default or tax sale coming, a Utah listing agent will usually net you more money than a direct buyer. Take that route. A cash or partnership sale earns its place when time or paperwork is the problem: a notice of sale already published, a house five years behind on taxes, an heir who cannot be reached, or repairs nobody can pay for.

Quick answers for Utah

Can I redeem my house after a trustee's sale in Utah?

No. Utah gives no redemption period after a nonjudicial trustee's sale, so selling, reinstating or paying off must happen before the auction.

Can a Utah small estate affidavit transfer a house?

No. The affidavit covers estates under $100,000, but Utah court guidance says it cannot transfer land or a house, so most inherited homes need a probate case and a personal representative.

How long after a notice of default can a Utah foreclosure sale be scheduled?

At least three months must pass after the notice of default is recorded before a notice of sale is given, and the notice of sale must be published and posted at least 20 days before the auction.

Has Utah adopted the Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act?

Yes, in 2022. For qualifying heirs property it adds an appraisal, a buyout opportunity for the other owners and a preference for an open-market sale.
