Garrett County Cabins, Farmhouses and Inherited Mountain Homes

Should you sell a Garrett County property to a cash buyer at all? Often the honest answer is no. A well-kept house or lake cabin in Maryland's westernmost county, owned by people who agree on selling and with no deadline, will usually bring more through a local listing agent who knows the Deep Creek Lake and mountain markets. Where a different kind of buyer helps is the property that is hard to sell in the normal way: a cabin nobody has opened in years, a farmhouse on an old well and septic, land split among a dozen heirs, or a house with a tax bill that has fallen behind.

Why Garrett County Properties Get Complicated

Garrett is mountain country. Winters are long and snowy, roads to some properties are steep or unpaved, and a lot of homes depend on private wells, septic systems and propane or oil heat. Many houses were built or added onto by the families who owned them, sometimes decades ago, and not every addition was permitted. Second homes and vacation cabins are common around Deep Creek Lake, and many of the owners live in other parts of Maryland or in other states, which makes it hard to keep an eye on a property through a winter.

  • Frozen or burst pipes in a house left unheated.
  • Roof and porch damage from snow load.
  • Septic systems and wells with no records.
  • Family land passed down without an estate ever being opened, leaving the title in a long-dead relative's name.

Opening an Estate in Oakland

The Garrett County Register of Wills and Orphans' Court are in Oakland, the county seat. A personal representative appointed there is who signs a deed for an estate property. When the probate property is worth $50,000 or less, or $100,000 if the surviving spouse is the only heir, Maryland's small-estate process is available, and that can matter here because some rural parcels and older houses fall under those numbers. If the deed is still in the name of a grandparent, each line of heirs may need to be traced before a sale. Our guides on selling inherited property in Maryland and what happens when heirs cannot agree cover the steps.

Garrett County Taxes for FY2027

The county rate is $1.000 per $100 of assessed value, and the state adds $0.112, for $1.112 outside town limits. On a house assessed at $240,000, the yearly bill is about $2,669, roughly $222 a month, while the property sits unused.

Garrett has an unusually long list of towns that add their own municipal tax on top: Accident, Deer Park, Friendsville, Grantsville, Kitzmiller, Loch Lynn Heights, Mt. Lake Park and Oakland. A house inside any of those limits pays more than the example.

The county's Homestead Tax Credit limits yearly increases in an owner-occupant's taxable assessment to 2%. That only helps someone using the house as a principal residence. A vacation cabin, a rental or a house inherited by someone who lives elsewhere generally does not get it.

Land without a house

Some Garrett families inherit wooded lots or farm acreage with no house at all. Those sell differently from homes, with fewer lenders willing to finance them. Our page on selling vacant land explains how we price acreage.
