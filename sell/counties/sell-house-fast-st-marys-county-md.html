Selling a St. Mary's County House: Waterfront Rules, Base Moves and Estates

A Peninsula Surrounded by Tidal Water

St. Mary's County is bordered by the Potomac River, the Patuxent River and the Chesapeake Bay, with dozens of creeks in between. A large share of homes here are on or near tidal water. Under Maryland's Critical Area law, land within 1,000 feet of tidal waters is subject to extra limits on clearing, impervious surface, additions and rebuilding, with stricter rules closest to the shoreline. For an older waterfront cottage or a house with storm damage, those rules can decide what a buyer is able to do with it, and therefore what they will pay.

Away from the water, much of the county is rural, so many homes rely on a private well and a septic system. A lender-financed buyer may ask for water testing and a septic inspection, and failing systems are an expensive repair.

Naval Air Station Patuxent River

NAS Patuxent River in Lexington Park is the county's largest employer and the source of many housing decisions. Military members, civilian staff and contractors move in and out on orders and contract cycles. Owners who leave sometimes keep a house as a rental, then later want out after vacancies or tenant damage. Our blog post on why St. Mary's County homeowners choose cash offers looks at those patterns.

Property Tax Here Is Relatively Simple

Leonardtown, the county seat, is the only municipality in the county with its own property tax. Everywhere else, owners pay the St. Mary's County rate of $0.8478 per $100 plus the state's $0.112, a total of $0.9598 per $100 of assessed value for FY2027 (July 2026 through June 2027).

On a house assessed at $340,000 outside Leonardtown, that is about $3,263 a year, or roughly $272 a month. A house in town adds Leonardtown's rate on top.

The Homestead Tax Credit cap in St. Mary's County is 3%, meaning the taxable assessment on an owner-occupied home can't rise more than that each year. It requires that the house be the owner's principal residence, so heirs who live elsewhere usually lose it after they inherit.

Estates Go Through Leonardtown

The Register of Wills and the Orphans' Court for St. Mary's County are in Leonardtown. The personal representative appointed there has authority to sell, subject to what the will says. Maryland's small-estate option is available at $50,000 or less in probate assets, or $100,000 if the surviving spouse is the sole heir. Older family land in the county sometimes passed down without a probate ever being opened, leaving title split among many descendants; that has to be cleared before anyone can sell.
