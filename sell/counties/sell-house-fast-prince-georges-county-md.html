Selling a Prince George's County Home: Which Tax Applies and How Estates Work

The First Question Is Whether the House Is Inside a Municipality

Prince George's County has more incorporated towns and cities than most Maryland counties, and many of them levy their own property tax. Two houses a mile apart can carry different tax bills depending on whether they sit inside a municipal boundary. For anyone selling, buying or just carrying an empty house, that is the starting point.

Outside a municipality, owners pay the county rate of $1.00 per $100 of assessed value plus the state's $0.112, for $1.112 per $100 in FY2027 (July 2026 through June 2027). On a house in an unincorporated area assessed at $395,000, that comes to about $4,392 a year, roughly $366 a month.

Municipalities with their own tax

According to the state's FY2027 rate table, the places that add a municipal tax include Berwyn Heights, Bladensburg, Bowie, Brentwood, Capitol Heights, Cheverly, College Park, Colmar Manor, Cottage City, District Heights, Eagle Harbor, Edmonston, Fairmount Heights, Forest Heights, Glenarden, Greenbelt, Hyattsville, Landover Hills, Laurel, Morningside, Mount Rainier, New Carrollton, North Brentwood, Riverdale Park, Seat Pleasant, University Park and Upper Marlboro. Some of them set different rates by property type, so check the bill for the specific parcel.

The county's Homestead Tax Credit limits the yearly rise in an owner-occupant's taxable assessment to 3%. It applies only to a principal residence. When the owner dies and the house goes to children who live elsewhere, the credit usually ends, and the taxable value can climb faster.

Probate at the Courthouse in Upper Marlboro

Estates are handled by the Register of Wills and the Orphans' Court in Upper Marlboro, the county seat. The personal representative named by the court has authority to sign for the estate. Maryland allows simplified administration for estates with probate assets of $50,000 or less, or $100,000 if the surviving spouse is the only heir, but a house usually puts the estate above those amounts.

Prince George's estates often include a house where one sibling has been living, others who want to sell, and an older mortgage or reverse mortgage still on it. Our article on heirs who disagree about selling in Maryland covers the choices, including partition.

Other Situations Common Across the County

  • Federal and military moves. Joint Base Andrews, NASA Goddard in Greenbelt and the University of Maryland in College Park all bring people who relocate on short notice.
  • Waterfront land. Property within 1,000 feet of tidal water along the Potomac or Patuxent falls under Maryland's Critical Area law, which can limit additions and rebuilding.
  • Tax sale. The county runs its own tax sale for unpaid bills; once a lien is sold, the cost to redeem keeps growing. See selling before a Maryland tax sale.
