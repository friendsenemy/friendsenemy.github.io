Selling a Queen Anne's County Home, from Kent Island to Sudlersville

The Bay Bridge, Kent Island and Shoreline Rules

Queen Anne's County is where the Chesapeake Bay Bridge comes ashore on the Eastern Shore, on Kent Island. Much of the county is low-lying land along the Bay, the Chester River and many tidal creeks. That geography brings two issues that often come up when an owner wants to sell. First, Maryland's Critical Area law applies to land within 1,000 feet of tidal water and can limit expanding, rebuilding or clearing near the shoreline. Second, low waterfront lots may sit in a flood zone, which affects insurance and what a buyer's lender requires.

Inland, toward the Delaware line, the county is farmland and small towns, where private wells and septic systems are the norm. A buyer using a mortgage will often ask for a water test and a septic inspection, and a house that has been empty for a while may need both looked at before closing.

Seven Towns Add Their Own Tax

Outside a town, owners pay the Queen Anne's County rate of $0.80 per $100 of assessed value and the state's $0.112, a combined $0.912 per $100 for FY2027 (July 2026 through June 2027). Inside Barclay, Centreville, Church Hill, Millington, Queenstown, Sudlersville or Templeville, a town tax is added on top.

If the home is someone's principal residence, the county's Homestead Tax Credit limits the rise in its taxable assessment to 5% a year. When the owner passes away and an heir who lives elsewhere takes over, that limit generally falls away, and the bill can move up toward the full assessed value over time.

Estates and the Courthouse in Centreville

Centreville is the county seat, and the Register of Wills and Orphans' Court there handle probate for Queen Anne's County residents. Things that usually decide how fast a house can be sold:

  • Who the court appoints as personal representative, and whether the will gives a power of sale.
  • Whether the estate is small enough for simplified administration ($50,000 or less in probate assets, or $100,000 if a surviving spouse is the sole heir).
  • Whether heirs live out of state and need to sign or consent remotely.
  • Whether a family farm or waterfront parcel was never retitled after an earlier death.

When the family can't agree on selling, our page for heirs who disagree on an inherited house lays out the options. For a general walk-through, read selling inherited property in Maryland.
