Selling an Older or Inherited House in Allegany County, Maryland

Mountain Towns, Older Houses and an Estate Office in Cumberland

Allegany County sits in the mountains of Western Maryland, and much of its housing was built for families who worked the railroads, the mills and the Georges Creek coal mines. A lot of those houses are now owned by the children or grandchildren of the people who bought them, and many of those heirs live hours away. When an owner dies, the estate is opened with the Register of Wills and the Orphans' Court in Cumberland, the county seat, at the western end of the C&O Canal. That is usually the first stop for anyone who needs authority to sell.

What an Empty House Costs Each Year

Allegany County's FY2027 real property rate is $0.975 per $100 of assessed value, and the state adds $0.112, for $1.087 per $100 outside any taxing town. On a house assessed at $180,000, that comes to about $1,957 a year, or roughly $163 a month, before insurance, utilities and lawn care. It keeps running whether anyone lives there or not.

Six municipalities levy their own tax on top of the county rate: Barton, Cumberland, Frostburg, Lonaconing, Midland and Westernport. If the house is inside one of them, the yearly bill is higher than the figure above, so check the town line on the tax bill before you budget.

The county's Homestead Tax Credit cap is 4%. That cap limits how fast the taxable assessment on an owner-occupied home can rise each year. Once the owner dies and the house passes to an heir who lives somewhere else, the credit generally ends, and the bill can climb toward the full assessment.

Typical Situations We See Here

  • Inherited and far away. An heir in Pennsylvania, Virginia or farther out cannot easily drive in to deal with a frozen pipe or a code notice.
  • Several heirs, one house. When siblings disagree about selling, the estate can stall for years. Our guide to what happens when heirs can't agree in Maryland covers the options.
  • Back taxes. Each Maryland county runs its own tax sale, and Allegany is no exception. Selling before a lien is sold usually keeps more equity in the family.
  • Repairs bigger than the budget. Older frame houses on hillsides can have foundation, drainage and wiring issues that a retail buyer's lender will not accept.

Small estate or full probate?

Maryland lets an estate use the simpler small-estate process when the probate assets are $50,000 or less, or $100,000 or less when the surviving spouse is the only heir. In much of Allegany County a single house can fall under that line, which may shorten the path to a sale. The Register of Wills staff can tell you which forms apply, and a Maryland estate attorney can answer questions about your family's situation.

When a Cash Sale Is the Wrong Choice

If the house is in good condition, all the owners agree, and nobody is up against a deadline, listing with a local agent who knows Cumberland and Frostburg will usually net more than any cash buyer. We would tell you that plainly. A cash offer makes sense when the house needs work that a financed buyer cannot take on, when the title or estate needs untangling first, or when the carrying costs are eating the equity month after month.
