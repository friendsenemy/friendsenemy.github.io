Frederick County, MD: Selling a House With Town Taxes, Wells or an Estate

Frederick County stretches from the city of Frederick out to Catoctin Mountain, the Pennsylvania line and the Potomac. One detail matters for almost every sale here: twelve towns in the county levy their own property tax, so two houses with the same assessment can carry very different bills depending on which side of a town line they sit.

Which Frederick County towns add a tax

For FY2027, these municipalities charge a town or city rate on top of the county rate:

  • Brunswick, Burkittsville, Emmitsburg, Frederick, Middletown, Mt. Airy
  • Myersville, New Market, Rosemont, Thurmont, Walkersville, Woodsboro

Outside those towns, owners pay only the county rate of $1.110 plus the state rate of $0.112, a total of $1.222 per $100 of assessed value for July 2026 through June 2027.

An unincorporated house assessed at $360,000 owes $360,000 / 100 x $1.222 = about $4,399 a year, or about $367 a month. Inside one of the twelve towns, add that town's rate on top.

The county's Homestead Tax Credit limits yearly increases in an owner-occupant's taxable assessment to 5%. When the owner dies or moves out, an heir who doesn't live there generally loses it, and the bill can rise toward the full assessment over the next cycles.

Probate at the county seat

The Register of Wills and Orphans' Court for the county are in the city of Frederick. A personal representative appointed there signs the deed for the estate. Maryland's small-estate route applies at $50,000 or less, or $100,000 when a surviving spouse is the sole heir, which leaves most Frederick County houses in a regular estate. If no will exists, Maryland's intestacy statute decides the heirs; our article on what happens to a house when there's no will explains the order.

Rural houses: wells, septic and outbuildings

Much of the county outside the towns is farmland and wooded lots on private wells and septic systems. Those are often what slows a conventional sale:

  • Lenders commonly want a water test and a septic inspection, and a failed system can cost a great deal to replace.
  • Old farmhouses may have additions, barns or sheds that were never permitted.
  • Larger parcels sometimes have unclear boundary lines or old right-of-way agreements.

Families from Fort Detrick or commuters to Washington and Baltimore keep demand strong for updated homes. The houses that struggle are the ones needing a lot of work before a lender will touch them.

Is a cash buyer the right call?

Often it isn't. If the house is in reasonable shape, the owners agree and there's no deadline, a listing agent in Frederick County will likely get you more.
