Complicated situations we handle in Kent County

Most "we buy houses" companies want an easy house with one owner and a clean title. We specialize in the ones they walk away from. Here is what that looks like across Kent County.

Co-owner or sibling won't agree to sell?

A surprising number of the Kent County houses we buy are owned by two or more people who can't agree. One sibling wants to sell, one wants to keep it, one has gone quiet. Maryland law lets any co-owner sell their own share without the others' permission, and we are one of the few buyers who will purchase a partial interest. That often breaks the deadlock faster and cheaper than a partition lawsuit in Kent County Circuit Court, which can take a year and eat a large chunk of the proceeds in legal fees. Read can a co-owner sell a house in Maryland for the details.

Owner in jail or prison?

You can sell a house in Kent County while the owner is incarcerated. It takes a properly drafted Maryland power of attorney, a notary who can reach the facility (or a facility notary), and a buyer who has done it before. We handle the paperwork, coordinate with the facility and the family, and can direct proceeds wherever the owner instructs. See selling a house while incarcerated in Maryland.

House needs work you can't afford?

Roof, foundation, mold, a kitchen from 1978, a basement that floods when it rains — none of it stops us. We buy Kent County houses as-is and price the repairs into the offer, so you skip contractors, permits, and inspection renegotiations. If the house has been sitting empty, we can also handle a hoarded or vacant property without you clearing it out first.
