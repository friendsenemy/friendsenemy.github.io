Selling an Older or Inherited House in Kent County on the Upper Shore

Kent County is a small, rural county on the Upper Eastern Shore, bounded by the Chesapeake Bay to the west, the Sassafras River to the north and the Chester River to the south. Chestertown, the county seat and home of Washington College, holds the courthouse, the Register of Wills and most of the older housing stock. Much of the rest of the county is farmland, with small towns and waterfront communities such as Rock Hall and Betterton along the Bay.

Old Houses, Old Deeds

A lot of Kent County houses are old. Chestertown's historic district has eighteenth- and nineteenth-century homes, and farmhouses across the county have been in the same families for generations. That history shows up at sale time in a few predictable ways:

  • Deeds still in the name of a parent or grandparent who died years ago, with no estate ever opened.
  • Additions, wiring or plumbing that predate any inspection, which can make an ordinary buyer's lender nervous.
  • Properties in a historic district, where exterior changes may need approval.
  • Wells and septic systems on rural lots, and Critical Area limits on land within 1,000 feet of the Bay or the tidal rivers.

None of these make a house unsellable, but they do decide who can buy it and how long it takes.

The Tax Bill on a Kent County House

For FY2027, the Kent County rate is $1.022 per $100 of assessed value and the state rate is $0.112, a total of $1.134 for a house outside the towns. A property assessed at $280,000 owes about $3,175 a year, or $265 a month. Five towns charge their own municipal tax in addition: Betterton, Chestertown, Galena, Millington and Rock Hall. A house in one of those towns pays more than this example.

The county's Homestead Tax Credit caps the yearly increase in an owner-occupant's taxable assessment at 5%. It follows the homeowner who lives there. An heir who keeps a parent's house as a second home or rental generally loses that cap. When taxes go unpaid long enough, the county can sell the lien at tax sale, so an estate that is short on cash should not let the bill slide. See selling a Maryland house with liens or back taxes.

Probate in Chestertown

Estates are opened with the Kent County Register of Wills in Chestertown, and contested matters go to the Orphans' Court. The personal representative appointed there signs the deed. Maryland's small-estate process applies when the probate assets are $50,000 or less, or $100,000 if the surviving spouse is the only heir. If no estate was ever opened for an earlier owner, that has to be addressed first. Our probate house page lists the documents a buyer will ask for.

A Straight Answer About Listing Versus Selling for Cash

A Kent County house in good condition, with clear title and owners who agree, will generally sell for more on the open market through a local agent, especially a waterfront or historic property that appeals to buyers from across the Bay.
