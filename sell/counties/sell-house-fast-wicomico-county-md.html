Selling a House in Wicomico County on Maryland's Lower Eastern Shore

Eight Taxing Towns and a Lower County Rate

Wicomico County's FY2027 real property rate is $0.7799 per $100 of assessed value, one of the lower county rates in Maryland. Add the state's $0.112 and an unincorporated property pays $0.8919 per $100. On a house assessed at $225,000 outside town limits, that comes to about $2,007 a year, or $167 a month.

The picture changes inside town lines. Eight municipalities levy their own property tax on top of the county's: Delmar, Fruitland, Hebron, Mardela Springs, Pittsville, Salisbury, Sharptown and Willards. A house in Salisbury, for example, pays the county rate, the city rate and the state rate. Look at the tax bill to see which applies before estimating carrying costs on a vacant house.

Wicomico's Homestead Tax Credit cap is 5%. It limits how much an owner-occupied home's taxable assessment can increase each year. An heir who inherits but does not live in the house, or a landlord, generally does not get the credit.

Probate and the County Seat in Salisbury

Salisbury is the county seat and the largest city on the Lower Shore, home to Salisbury University. The Wicomico County Register of Wills and Orphans' Court are there, and every estate that includes a county house starts with them. Until the court appoints a personal representative, no one can sign a deed for the deceased owner.

Maryland allows a simpler small-estate process when probate assets are $50,000 or less, or $100,000 or less if the surviving spouse is the sole heir. In parts of rural Wicomico, a modest house may fall within those limits. A Maryland estate attorney can confirm which route your family's estate qualifies for. Our page on selling an inherited or probate home covers what a buyer needs from the estate.

Rivers, Marsh and the Critical Area

The Wicomico and Nanticoke rivers are tidal, and much of the western county is low-lying land near tidal water. Property within 1,000 feet of tidal water is subject to Maryland's Critical Area law, which can limit new construction, additions and clearing near the shoreline. A buyer planning to rebuild or expand a waterfront house will ask about it. Flood zones and drainage can also affect insurance costs and a buyer's financing.

Situations we see in the county

  • Rentals in Salisbury that the owner, or the owner's heirs, no longer want to manage.
  • Older farmhouses and rural homes with wells, septic systems and outbuildings.
  • Houses with back taxes approaching the county's tax sale. Our guide to selling before a Maryland tax sale explains why timing matters.
  • Heirs living off the Shore who cannot handle repairs and showings.
