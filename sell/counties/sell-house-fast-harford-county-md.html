Selling a Harford County Home Near Aberdeen Proving Ground and Bel Air

Should you sell to a cash buyer at all?

Start with the honest question. In much of Harford County, from the subdivisions around Bel Air to the newer neighborhoods near Abingdon and Fallston, a house in decent shape sells to ordinary buyers with ordinary mortgages. If yours is one of those, the owners agree, and nobody is under a deadline, list it with a local agent. You will almost certainly net more.

A cash or partnership offer from Pages of Purpose is for the houses that cannot travel that road easily: an estate stuck between heirs, a property with back taxes or judgments, a foreclosure date on the calendar, a house that needs more work than a buyer's lender allows, or an owner who is in jail. Those are the situations Dustin Ray works on, and a simple sale can close in as few as 7 days.

Aberdeen Proving Ground and the transfer cycle

Aberdeen Proving Ground on the Chesapeake shoreline is the county's largest employer, and the military and civilian staff there move on assignment schedules. Families who bought near Aberdeen, Edgewood or Havre de Grace sometimes get orders before they can sell, then end up as reluctant long-distance landlords. If that is you, read selling a Maryland house on PCS orders before deciding between renting and selling.

Three towns with their own tax

Harford has three incorporated municipalities that levy their own property tax: Aberdeen, Bel Air and Havre de Grace. Everywhere else, including large unincorporated areas like Edgewood, Joppatowne and Jarrettsville, owners pay only the county and state rates.

For FY2027 (July 2026 to June 2027), those are $0.9779 for the county plus $0.112 for the state, or $1.0899 per $100 of assessed value. On an unincorporated house assessed at $335,000, the annual bill is about $3,651, roughly $304 a month. A house inside one of the three towns pays that plus the town rate.

The county caps annual growth in an owner-occupant's taxable assessment at 5% through the Homestead Tax Credit. That cap follows the owner who lives there, not the house, so an heir who keeps an inherited house as a rental or a vacant property generally will not have it.

The county's two landscapes

Northern farm country

North of Bel Air, toward the Pennsylvania line, homes sit on acreage with private wells and septic systems. Inherited farmhouses here often need a septic upgrade or a new well pump before a lender-backed buyer can close.

The shoreline and the Susquehanna

Havre de Grace sits where the Susquehanna River meets the head of the Chesapeake Bay, and there is tidal shoreline along the bay and its rivers to the south. Land within 1,000 feet of tidal water falls under the Critical Area law, which restricts clearing and new building. A buyer planning a rebuild has to factor that in.

Estates go to Bel Air

Bel Air is the county seat, and the Register of Wills for Harford County and the Orphans' Court are there. Maryland's small-estate process applies when probate assets are $50,000 or less ($100,000 if the surviving spouse is the only heir). Most homes exceed that, so a personal representative is appointed. When the heirs are not on the same page, see what happens when heirs disagree in Maryland. None of this is legal advice; a Maryland estate attorney can answer questions specific to your family.
