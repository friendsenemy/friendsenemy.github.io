Selling an Old Brick House or Estate in Washington County and Hagerstown

Old houses in old towns

Washington County, in western Maryland between Frederick County and the mountains, has some of the oldest housing in the state. Hagerstown, the county seat, and towns such as Williamsport, Boonsboro, Sharpsburg and Hancock are lined with brick and stone houses from the 1800s, many divided into apartments over the years. Around them lie farms, orchards, the Antietam battlefield near Sharpsburg, and the C&O Canal along the Potomac.

Those old houses bring familiar issues when they come up for sale: stone foundations that take on water, plaster walls, old wiring, lead paint in pre-1978 rentals, and roofs and chimneys that need work. Out in the county, most homes rely on private wells and septic systems. Any of those can push a sale outside what a financed buyer will accept. Our article on selling a Maryland house with foundation problems goes deeper on one of the most common.

Probate in Hagerstown

When an owner dies, the estate is opened with the Register of Wills for Washington County in Hagerstown, under the Orphans' Court. If the probate estate is $50,000 or less ($100,000 when a surviving spouse is the only heir), the shorter small-estate process may apply; given that some older houses in the county are modestly valued, it is worth asking about. Otherwise a personal representative is appointed and can sell. This is general information, not legal advice; a Maryland attorney can tell you which path fits.

Nine towns, and a sample bill outside them

Nine municipalities in the county levy their own property tax on top of the county rate: Boonsboro, Clear Spring, Funkstown, Hagerstown, Hancock, Keedysville, Sharpsburg, Smithsburg and Williamsport. Houses outside those limits pay only county and state taxes.

For FY2027 (July 2026 to June 2027), the county rate is $0.928 and the state's is $0.112, for $1.04 per $100 of assessed value. A rural house assessed at $195,000 owes about $2,028 a year, or $169 a month. A house in Hagerstown or one of the other eight towns owes that plus the town's rate.

The Homestead Tax Credit cap here is 5%: an owner-occupant's taxable assessment cannot rise faster than that each year. An heir who does not live in the house, or a landlord, generally does not get that limit.

Landlords and the I-81 corridor

Hagerstown sits where Interstate 81 crosses Interstate 70, and the county has a large stock of older rental housing. A lot of the calls we get from this area are from landlords who are done: long vacancies, a tenant who stopped paying, code notices, or simply too many units to manage from a distance. If that is you, see selling a rental property in Maryland.

A fair word on agents

A Washington County house that has been kept up, owned by people who agree on selling, with no deadline, will usually bring more money listed with a local agent. That is the right choice for many owners.
