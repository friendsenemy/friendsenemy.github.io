Selling a Rural Caroline County Home: Farms, Wells, Septic and Ten Town Taxes

Most Caroline County houses run on a well and a septic system

Caroline County is the Eastern Shore's farm county: flat cropland, poultry houses, small towns, and the Choptank River running past Denton, the county seat. Outside the towns, nearly every house draws water from a private well and sends waste to a septic system. For anyone selling an inherited farmhouse or an older rancher on a country road, those two systems are often the first thing a buyer's lender wants inspected and the first thing to fail inspection.

A failed percolation test or a cracked tank does not make a house unsellable, but it narrows the buyer pool to people paying cash or willing to take on the repair. That is a large part of why problem houses here sit.

Ten towns, ten extra tax lines

Caroline has an unusually long list of incorporated towns for its size, and each one levies its own property tax on top of the county rate: Denton, Federalsburg, Greensboro, Hillsboro, Marydel, Preston, Ridgely, Goldsboro, Henderson and Templeville. A house inside one of those town limits pays three rates; a house outside them pays two.

For a farmhouse outside town limits assessed at $215,000, that comes to about $2,305 a year, or around $192 a month. A house inside one of the ten towns will owe that plus the town's own rate. The county's Homestead Tax Credit caps yearly growth in an owner-occupant's taxable assessment at 5%; an heir who does not live in the house generally does not keep that protection.

Estates are handled in Denton

When an owner dies, the estate goes to the Register of Wills for Caroline County in Denton, with the Orphans' Court overseeing it. Maryland's small-estate process covers probate assets up to $50,000, or $100,000 if the surviving spouse is the sole heir; land plus a house usually exceeds that. Farm families often have several generations of heirs on a single parcel, some of whom moved away years ago. Our guide on what happens to a house when there is no will covers who inherits under Maryland law.

Land, outbuildings and acreage

Some Caroline sales are mostly about the ground: a few acres with a collapsed barn, or a lot where the house is past saving. Parts of the county along the Choptank and its tidal creeks can fall within the Chesapeake Bay Critical Area, which limits development within 1,000 feet of tidal water, so check the county's maps before assuming a lot can be built on. For bare parcels, see our page on selling vacant land.

An honest comparison

If the well tests clean, the septic passes, the heirs are in agreement and nobody needs the money by a set date, a local agent who knows the Shore market will generally get you more than a cash buyer will. That is the right move for a lot of families.
