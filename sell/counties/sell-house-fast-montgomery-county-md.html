Selling a Montgomery County Home: Rockville Estates and 19 Municipal Tax Rates

Estates in Montgomery County start in Rockville

Rockville is the county seat, and the Register of Wills for Montgomery County and the Orphans' Court are there. Many of the house sales we hear about in this county begin with a death: a parent who bought in Silver Spring, Wheaton or Bethesda decades ago, and adult children who now live in other states. Because home values here tend to run well above Maryland's small-estate limit ($50,000, or $100,000 when a surviving spouse is the only heir), most of these estates need a full administration with a personal representative who has authority to sell.

One Maryland rule surprises families: Maryland has an inheritance tax, generally 10%, on property passing to heirs outside the close-family group, such as nieces, nephews and friends. Spouses, children and other lineal relatives are exempt. If a house is going to a niece or a family friend, raise this with a Maryland estate attorney early. This page is general information, not legal advice.

Your tax bill depends on which of 19 municipalities you are in

Montgomery County has one of the longest lists of taxing municipalities in Maryland: Chevy Chase Village, Section 3 Village of Chevy Chase, the Town of Chevy Chase, Section 5 Village of Chevy Chase, the Village of Martins Addition, North Chevy Chase, Chevy Chase View, the City of Rockville, the City of Gaithersburg, Barnesville, Laytonsville, Poolesville, Garrett Park, Glen Echo, Somerset, Brookeville, Washington Grove, Kensington and the City of Takoma Park. Each one adds its own rate.

Outside those, owners pay the county rate of $0.6706 plus the state's $0.112, or $0.7826 per $100 for FY2027 (July 2026 to June 2027). On a house assessed at $585,000, that is about $4,578 a year, roughly $382 a month. Montgomery County also bills other charges with the property tax, so the actual bill can be higher; check the county's tax statement for the property.

The county's Homestead Tax Credit limits yearly growth in an owner-occupied home's taxable assessment to 10%. An heir who does not move in generally does not qualify, so the taxable value can step up after the transfer.

When listing will beat a cash offer, which is often here

Montgomery County has a deep pool of buyers with financing, drawn by employers like NIH and Walter Reed National Military Medical Center in Bethesda and by the federal agencies along the I-270 corridor. A house in reasonable shape, owned by people who agree, with no deadline, will usually net more through a local agent. We tell people that plainly.

Up-county land and the Agricultural Reserve

Much of northern and western Montgomery County is in the Agricultural Reserve, where zoning sharply limits new houses. Homes there often run on wells and septic, and the land cannot simply be subdivided. Buyers need to understand that before they price a property.
