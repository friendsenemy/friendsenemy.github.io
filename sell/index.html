We Handle the Situations Other Buyers Overlook

Most buyers want a clean title, an empty house and an owner who can sign tomorrow. These are the situations where that isn't true, and where we look for a practical way forward.

🕊️Inherited Homes & Probate

A parent or relative passed away and the house came to the family. Maybe probate is open, maybe nobody has started it. If the heirs or the estate's representative want to sell, we can look at the house as it is and work alongside the estate's attorney on the steps needed before a sale. Where state law allows, some purchases can be arranged so that certain estate costs are handled through the transaction; whether that's possible depends on the state and the estate.

More on inherited and probate houses

🧾Unpaid Property Taxes & Liens

Back taxes, a judgment or another lien can make a house hard to sell, especially when a tax sale is getting closer. In many sales these amounts are paid from the proceeds at closing. We'll look at what's recorded against the property and tell you plainly whether a sale still makes sense.

How tax-sale timing works (Maryland example)

📜Deceased Owners & Title Problems

The deed is still in the name of someone who died, an old deed was never recorded, or a signature is missing from the chain of title. Many of these problems can be corrected with the right paperwork. We research the records, and title companies and attorneys handle the legal fixes.

More on title problems

🏚️Vacant, Damaged & Cluttered Houses

Years of belongings, a roof that leaks, a house that's been empty since the last tenant left. You don't have to clean it out or fix it before talking to us. We look at houses as they are, and cleanout and repairs become our problem if we buy.

More on houses in rough condition

✈️Out-of-State Owners & Heirs

You inherited a house hundreds of miles away and can't be there to deal with keys, contractors, cleanout and paperwork. We coordinate locally with people on the ground, and closings can usually be signed remotely or by mail where the state allows.

How a Property Review Works

  1. You tell us what's going on

    The form above or a phone call. Rough answers are fine.

  2. We look into it

    Public records, the likely value as-is, and what stands between the property and a sale. We may ask a few follow-up questions or talk with local professionals.

  3. We explain where things stand

    Whether we may be able to make an offer, what would need to happen first, and roughly how long it could take. If we're not the right fit, we'll say so.

Every offer and timeline depends on the property, who owns it, the title, and the rules in the state where it's located. Some purchases are made with an investment partner; if that's the case, we'll tell you who the buyer is before you sign anything.

Questions People Ask Before Reaching Out

Do I need to clean out or repair the house first?

No. We look at properties as they are, belongings, damage and all. You don't have to fix, clean or empty anything before contacting us.

Probate hasn't been opened. Can you still help?

Often we can still review the property and explain what would need to happen before a sale. The estate's paperwork is handled by an attorney licensed in that state. In some cases a purchase can be arranged so certain estate costs are handled through the transaction, but that depends on state law and the estate, so we can't promise it in advance.

How quickly can a sale happen?

It depends on the situation. A house with clear title and an owner ready to sign can move quickly. A house that needs probate or title work moves on the court's and the title company's schedule. We'll give you an honest estimate once we understand the property.

Are there fees to talk with you?

No. Asking us to review a property is free and there's no obligation. If we make an offer, it will spell out the price and who pays which closing costs.

Who actually buys the property?

Pages of Purpose LLC, or in some cases an investment partner we work with. You'll know who the buyer is before you sign anything.

Are you lawyers?

No. Pages of Purpose LLC is a real estate investment company, not a law firm, and we don't give legal advice. Where legal work is needed, it's done by an attorney licensed in the state where the property is located.

Do you buy land too?

Yes, including inherited and vacant land. Use the same form, or see how we look at land.
