We buy houses when the owner is in jail or prison

Pages of Purpose LLC is a home buyer that specializes in one of the hardest sales there is: a house whose owner, or one of whose owners, is incarcerated. We work out who can sign, how the signature gets done inside a jail or prison, and what has to be paid off at closing, then make an offer on the house as-is. Based in Maryland, buying in all 50 states.

By Dustin Ray, Founder of Pages of Purpose LLC. Pages of Purpose is a real estate company, not a law firm, and nothing on this page is legal advice. Speak with an attorney licensed in the state where the property is located.

🙋 Who calls us

  • 👩 A spouse or partner whose husband, wife or partner is serving time and the mortgage, taxes or a vacant house can't wait for the release date.
  • 👨‍👧 A parent, child or sibling holding a power of attorney, or trying to get one signed, for a family member who is inside.
  • 🏚️ A co-owner or heir who owns the house with someone who is incarcerated and can't sell without their signature.
  • ⚖️ An attorney or executor who needs a buyer that already knows how facility notaries, wardens' offices and title companies handle these files.

🔑 What we actually do

📜 Read the deed and the records

We pull the deed, the mortgage, tax status and any judgments, liens or restitution orders recorded against the owner, so you know what the sale has to clear before anyone signs anything.

🖊️ Work out the signing path

Power of attorney, a deed signed inside, or a sale by the spouse or co-owner alone. Which one works depends on the state and on how title is held. We map it out and get the title company to agree to it up front.

🏢 Plan the signing inside

Facilities each have their own process for notaries, witnesses and legal mail. We help the family ask the right questions of the right office so the documents go in once and come back right.

🤝 Make an offer and close

A written cash offer, or a partnership offer when the house is worth more after work, with closing at a title company or attorney's office. The family never has to clean out or fix the house.

❓ Frequently asked questions

Can you buy a house if the owner is in prison?

Yes. An incarcerated owner can still sell, usually through a power of attorney signed inside the facility or by signing the deed in front of a facility notary. Pages of Purpose LLC buys these houses in all 50 states and helps the family plan the signing so the title company will accept it.

Does the person in jail have to sign?

If their name is on the deed, yes, either personally or through a valid power of attorney. A spouse or relative cannot sign for them without one. If the house is in someone else's name alone, the incarcerated person may not need to sign at all; the deed decides.

Do you need a power of attorney before you can make an offer?

No. We can look at the house, the deed and the liens and make an offer first. The power of attorney or inside signing only has to be in place before closing.

What happens to restitution or court costs at closing?

Recorded judgments against the owner are usually paid from the sale proceeds at closing, the same way a mortgage is. We find them before the offer so the number you see is the number you can expect.

Are you lawyers?

No. Pages of Purpose LLC is a real estate company, not a law firm, and nothing we say is legal advice. We work alongside the family's attorney or a title company attorney licensed in the state where the house is.

Which states do you buy in?

All 50. We are based in Maryland and have state-specific guides for Texas, Florida, California, Ohio, Georgia, Pennsylvania and Maryland, with more added every week.
